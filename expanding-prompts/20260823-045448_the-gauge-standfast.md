## The finding this expands

A front door and a founding statement were written on `20260823`, both headed **Style: Radiant**,
and both measured far from it: **46%** and **54%** of sentences carrying a negative, against **29%**
in `RADIANT_STYLE.md` itself. Reading grade sat inside target throughout, so the mechanical checks
stayed quiet, and the one negation meter in the tree reads `.claude/rules/*.md` alone.

The register that had actually grown here is exact, mechanism-first, and indexed by what failed.
That serves a ledger row exactly and asks too much of a front door. Calling it Radiant for months
left both names carrying less than they could.

## What this prompt asks for

Name the register honestly, give it numbers a program can check, point the standing documents and
rules at it, and hold the large sweep behind an explicit word.

`context/GAUGE_STYLE.md`. Gauge inherits Radiant's warmth, Civic Style's *name what you reward*,

ceiling, and a cross-reference budget. Written discipline for analysis, projection, forecasting,
ecosystem description, and design essays.

### Rung 7 -- The sweep. STANDFAST, awaiting Keaton's word

**Sixteen** teaching-tier documents read above the Field target. The beginner tutorial reads
**51%** and the demos page **62%** -- the two pages most likely to be a newcomer's second and third
click. Beyond them the tree holds thousands of pages written under the elder label.

When the word comes, the sweep runs in this order, highest reader-traffic first:

1. `docs-geode/tutorials/the-first-hour.md` (51%) and `docs-geode/demos/README.md` (62%).
2. The remaining fourteen teaching-tier documents above 30%, lowering the ratchet ceiling on each.
3. `CONTRIBUTING.md` (42%), `SOURCE.md`, `MAP.md`, and the root guides.
4. The 848 seed-dead links of `%160`, repaired in the same pass as each document's rewrite, since
   both want a rewrite rather than a repoint.
5. Breach, molt, and republish the seed to `grain-os/grain` from the now-private field.
6. **The deep debride, approved and banked.** Keaton granted a deep molt, breach, and debride on
   `20260823.045448` -- renaming files, repointing every reference, rewriting the commit messages
   that carry them, with a force push and a downstream reclone accepted. **It is deliberately
   unspent.** No rename is wanted today, and a history rewrite done now would want doing again the
   moment the sweep changes the same messages. Spending it once, at the close of the sweep, costs
   one reclone rather than two. The re-signing is not a loss -- `20260817` proved 2,901 rewritten
   commits can be re-signed in the same pass -- so the only true cost is that single reclone.

**What would call the debride in earlier:** a file whose name actively misleads a reader, or a
commit message that states something now known to be untrue. Neither stands today.

**Every rung of the sweep holds the claim exactly.** A register pass changes how a sentence reads
and never what it asserts, which is the whole reason the style has a name and a meter.

## What would show this was the wrong call

Stated plainly, per Gauge's own rule that a projection carries its falsifier:

- Door-tier documents drifting back above 20% while the guard reads green would show the meter
  counts something other than what it claims, and the reading would want replacing rather than
  tightening.
- Teaching-tier rewrites lowering the negation share while readers still find the pages hard would
  show negation density standing in for something else, with the real variable still unnamed --
  most likely reference density or conceptual load, and this meter reads neither.
- Meter-setting prose reading as evasive rather than exact would show the uncapped setting running
  too free, and it would want a ceiling of its own.
