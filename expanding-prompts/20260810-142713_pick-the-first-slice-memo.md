## Why this memo exists

A season of vision named many big things at once: an ecological certification, a certified-brand marketplace, a social network for aligned artists and buyers, a token economy, on-chain payments, treasury and governance designs, and a legislative vehicle. Each is real. None can be *first*, because "first" is a discipline, not a ranking of ambition.

The `SIMPLE_LOVABLE_COMPLETE` philosophy already answers this. It says: ship the smallest thing that is *whole* — one clear scope, lovable at that scale, complete on the day it arrives — and grow the rest from that running seed (Gall's Law). This memo applies that test to the candidates and names the one slice to build first.

## The test a "first slice" must pass

Four plain questions. A first slice answers yes to all four; a north star fails at least one.

1. **Simple** — one clear scope a newcomer grasps in a sentence?
2. **Lovable** — would a real person want it *today*, at its smallest honest size?
3. **Complete** — does the loop close with what exists now, claiming nothing not yet true?
4. **Sellable and safe** — can a sales rep close it this quarter, at low legal and capital risk, without waiting on lawyers, licences, or a network that does not exist yet?

## The candidates, triaged

### Tier 3 — North stars (real, but not first)

These fail question 4 hard, and usually 2 and 3 as well.

- **Token economy, on-chain payments, treasury and governance designs.** These raise securities-law, money-transmission, and tax questions that need licensed counsel *before* design, not after. Capital-heavy, slow, and not lovable at small scale. A first slice cannot depend on them.
- **A legislative / monetary-policy vehicle.** A multi-year, low-probability political undertaking resting on contested economics. Worthy as a horizon; ruinous as a starting point.
- **A full social network.** Network effects mean it is not lovable at small scale — a network of a few users is not yet worth loving. It needs an audience it does not have. It is the *reward* of an earlier win, not the win.

Name these as north stars, keep them in the vision doc, and build nothing first that cannot stand without them.

### Tier 2 — Could be first, but heavy

- **A full certification program with unannounced randomized audits.** The market is real and your ecological values are a true differentiator. Yet it is staff- and capital-intensive (auditors, travel, standards, legal defensibility), slow to earn the trust it sells, and — critically — it must never be entangled with any incentive that could look like pay-to-pass, or the mark is worthless. Strong second lap; too heavy for the first.

### Tier 1 — The first-slice candidates (light, whole, sellable now)

- **A. A curated trust directory** — the honest, transparent shortlist of genuinely ecological / vegan brands across food, drink, and clean beauty, with your review criteria stated plainly and existing trusted marks cited where they apply. Simple (a guide), lovable (buyers want a shortlist they can trust *today*), complete (browse → trust → click/buy), sellable (a rep sells placement or membership), and low-risk (you vouch transparently; you are not yet the legal certifier). 
- **B. A small pilot mark** — a "verified aligned" seal for a hand-picked cohort of a few already-aligned brands, proving the standard and the review motion at tiny scale before any talk of scaling or audits.

## The recommendation

**Lead with A, the curated trust directory, and fold B into it as the editorial standard.**

It is the most `SIMPLE_LOVABLE_COMPLETE`-pure thing on the list: whole on the day a single web page goes live, genuinely wanted by buyers who cannot tell honest brands from greenwashed ones, and closeable by a sales rep selling transparent placement or brand membership. It carries the lowest legal and capital risk of any candidate, and — this is the point of choosing it — **it grows every north star from a running seed:**

- The directory's honest reviews *are* the seed of the certification standard (Tier 2).
- The audience it gathers *is* the seed of the social network (Tier 3).
- The trust it earns is the only currency that makes a future mark, or a future token, mean anything at all.

Start where trust is cheapest to build and compounds fastest.

## What the first shippable version is

Kept deliberately small, so it finishes:

- One page or simple site: a curated list of a modest number of genuinely-aligned brands, each with a plain-language why-it-qualifies and any existing marks it already holds.
- One stated, public review standard — honest about what you did and did not verify (the Two-Rooms discipline: name intent versus proven fact).
- One revenue motion a rep can sell: transparent placement or membership, priced in plain cash. `[company sets: the price and what it includes.]`
- Nothing else claimed. No token, no network, no audit apparatus — those earn their own page when they, too, are whole.

## The reds to respect before touching the north stars

- **Counsel before crypto or policy.** Any token, treasury, payment, or legislative step needs licensed securities, money-transmission, and tax counsel first. This is not an AI's ground, and not prose's ground.
- **Consent before naming.** Any real person or company invited to partner gives a real yes before being named publicly. Until then they are a private intention, not a claim.
- **Care with borrowed names.** Any brand-name identifier held on any registry belongs, in trademark, to that brand. Get an IP read before building anything that monetizes one.
- **Never pay-to-pass.** Whatever the eventual certification, keep it structurally impossible for money or tokens to buy the mark. The whole value is a buyer's belief that it cannot be bought.

## What this memo does not know

This recommendation rests on general strategy, not on facts only the company holds: available capital, existing brand relationships, and how close the underlying software actually is to carrying any of this. Weigh it against those, and correct it where the ground is different from the map.
