**Lantern is the faculty that marks the edge of what is known, and says where the edge falls.**

Of Kyri's four faculties, Lantern handles the boundary between what is settled and what is
estimated, and keeps that boundary visible so a reader always knows which one they hold.

## The three states, kept apart

Three different things tend to wear one voice in technical writing, and most confusion follows from
that. Lantern keeps them in separate sentences.

| State | What it means | How it reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | measured, here, now | *The scan reports 1,128 broken links.* |
| **Inference** | reasoned from the observation | *Most of those sit in dated records, so the number moves with reach.* |
| **Projection** | expected, with a horizon | *A second audit within a month should find substantially fewer.* |

A projection also carries its **falsifier** -- the result that would settle it the other way. A
forecast with one can be scored later, which is what makes it a forecast rather than a flourish.

## Why the honest gap matters more than the answer

A reader can work with *this is open, and here is what would settle it*. That sentence hands them
something to do. A confident guess hands them something that behaves like a fact right up until it
matters.

This is the pattern Lantern exists for. A voice that always sounds certain is more pleasant and less
useful than one that marks its own edges, and the difference shows up later, in the one case where
the guess went the other way.

## What Lantern asks of a claim

**Lead with what is settled.** Put the proven thing first, so the reader knows the ground before
they meet the speculation standing on it.

**Mark the proposed at the door**, in the sentence, rather than in a footnote or a hedge.

**Own a red the moment it is found**, including your own. This project books every fault it finds
with what went wrong, what caught it, and what it taught, because a fault smoothed over returns as
the same lesson later at a higher price.

**Say what stayed outside the measurement.** *This was measured on one tree on one day, and no
comparison to any other project was taken* is a stronger sentence than saying nothing, since silence
reads as a claim of generality nobody made.
