A **ratchet** is this tree's gentlest instrument. A guard counts a population it wants smaller,
writes the count down as a **ceiling**, and refuses on the lap the count rises above it. The rule
each one prints in its own header is four words long: **the ceiling only falls.** Lower it when a
repair lands; leave it alone otherwise.

Followed exactly, that rule has one arithmetic consequence, and the consequence is the subject of
this page. A ceiling lowered to the reading sits at **zero slack**, so the very next arrival reds.

## The question that decides a ceiling

> **Can this reading fall, and by whose hand?**

Three answers live in the family today, and each wants a different instrument.

**One -- the population grows by defects, and the lane holding the guard may shrink it.** Here the
next arrival genuinely is a defect, the red is the whole point, and zero slack is the ratchet
working. Most of the 21 walls at zero are this: the class has been swept to nothing and the guard
now holds the ground.

**Two -- the population grows by ordinary lawful work, and some lane may sweep it.** A ceiling
lowered to the reading here reds on a lap that did everything right, and the lap that meets it pays
for a peer's ordinary morning. The cure is already demonstrated: `say_compose_bound` was swept on
`20260910` and its ceiling set to **489 against a reading of 480**, nine per mille of deliberate
slack, sized from the measured rate at which the population grows. Slack chosen from a growth rate
is a different object from slack left behind by accident.

**Three -- the population grows by tissue the tree's own law forbids anyone to repair.** This is
the trap, and it is a countdown rather than a gate. `dated_path` gates `refs_lost` at **85** and read **105**
at `20260910.172957`, over a set that includes references standing inside dated testimony --
where accrete-never-break holds every word, so the count rises whenever a ship writes a log naming
a path, and falls for nobody.

## The law, proposed

> **Place a gate over a population somebody may lawfully shrink. Count the rest.**

And its companion, which answer two earns: **a ceiling over a population that ordinary work grows
takes its slack from the growth rate**, rather than falling to the reading each time a lap looks.

Both wait on Keaton's word, and one prior question waits with them, asked from this ship's own card:
whether a ceiling over a population its lane cannot lower may be gated at all.

## What this leaves for a hand

**Which answer any given ceiling belongs to.** The census hands a reader the slack and the scan's
own header; deciding who grows a population and who may shrink it stays a person's reading, since
both facts live in prose today.

**The decidable form, named rather than built.** A ceiling could declare its grower beside itself --
one field, read by the census -- and then the three answers above become a count instead of a
judgment. That is a convention, so it is proposed here and left for the word that seats it.

*May every ceiling stand where a hand can lower it, and may the counts we keep beside the rest stay
honest company.*
