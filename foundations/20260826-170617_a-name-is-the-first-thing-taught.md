# A Name Is the First Thing Taught

**Language:** EN
**Stamp:** `20260826.170617`
**Style:** Gauge, Door setting (see `../context/GAUGE_STYLE.md`)
**Voice:** Kyri
**Status:** Living
**Kin:** [`comlink-tendency`](../.claude/rules/comlink-tendency.md) -- [`stamp-and-name`](../.claude/rules/stamp-and-name.md) -- [`gauge-style`](../.claude/rules/gauge-style.md)

Every name in a system is a small lesson, handed to whoever arrives next. It is handed to them
before the documentation, before the tutorial, before anyone has time to explain -- because they meet
the name the moment they open the file, and they meet it again every day after.

So the question a name has to answer runs past *is it short*, past *is it clever*, past *does it
match what the elders called it.* The question is: **what does this teach the person reading it for
the first time?**

## A name teaches, or it charges rent

Two names can point at exactly the same thing and cost completely different amounts.

A name that says what the thing does is a lesson delivered free, once per reading, forever. A reader
opens `lower_conditional` and already knows what they are looking at. They spend their attention on
the code.

A name that points at a spelling -- at the shape of the punctuation, at a syllable someone assigned
to a glyph -- carries the meaning separately, in a second lesson the reader owes. It stands between
them and the code and asks to be memorised first. Every reading pays that toll again until the day it
is finally learned, and every new arrival starts the payments over.

Both names serve the person who already knows equally well. That is exactly why the difference is
easy to miss, and why it deserves a deliberate decision rather than an accumulation.

## Inheritance is a gift, and a gift may be unwrapped

This tree descends from good work, and it says so plainly and by name in `../gratitude/`. Descent
means the ideas arrive tested, and the debt is real.

Yet a name is a separate thing from an idea. An ancestor's naming scheme was fitted to an ancestor's
surface, its size, its constraints, its readers. Carrying the idea forward and giving it our own
clearest word is what the inheritance was for. **We keep what was learned and we say it in our own
voice**, which is the move `shape` made when it replaced an inherited word, and `Glow gate` made when
it replaced a bare one.

The test is whether the property that made the elder scheme good survives the renaming. Where it does
-- where the regularity lived in the structure rather than in the syllables -- the rename is free and
returns a lesson. Where the syllable itself carries the property, the elder word has earned its place
and keeps it.

## The tree usually knows already

The honest thing about this foundation is that it is mostly a description. When the vocabulary was
measured, thirty modules out of thirty-two had already reached for the plain word ahead of any rule.
That happened uncoordinated. People writing code reach for the clear name because the clear name is
easier to write beside, and the drift shows up in the few places where somebody reached past it for
the ancestor's spelling instead.

So a naming law here is rarely an instruction and usually a **ratification** -- find what the work
has been doing when it was going well, say it out loud, and close the handful of places that went
another way. A rule written that way is one people were already keeping.

## What this asks, and what it does not

It asks one question at every naming: **would someone meeting this for the first time learn
something from it?** If yes, it stands. If it only points at a spelling, reach for the word that
teaches.

It leaves sweeps alone. Dated testimony keeps every word it ever wrote -- the one-clock law and
accrete-never-break hold, and a stale reference is resolved rather than rewritten. Names people have
lived with and love keep their place. And length stays free: the clearest word is the one to reach
for at whatever size it wants to be, which is the Comlink tendency's own first rule.

May every name in this tree be a small kindness to whoever opens the file next, and may the ones we
inherited be thanked plainly, kept where they teach, and translated into our own voice where a
clearer word waits.
