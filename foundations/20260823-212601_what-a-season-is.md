A **season** is this project's unit of time and of care. It runs long enough for a real thing to be
finished, and it ends on purpose.

## Where the word comes from, and what it carries

The word is borrowed from farming, and the borrowing is exact. A growing season has a beginning
you can name, a middle where the work is daily and unglamorous, and a close where you find out
what you actually have. It also has a shape you accept rather than choose: some things ripen and the
rest wait, and the season closing is what tells you which.

A season here works the same way. It opens with a name and an intent. It fills with rounds -- the
ordinary working units, each a day or a few hours. And it closes with a reading: what landed, what
stayed a plan, and what the next season inherits.

## What a season is for, practically

**It bounds ambition to something a person can finish.** An open-ended plan keeps growing past what
anyone can hold. A season says *this much, and then we look*.

**It creates a natural place to be honest.** At a close, the distance between what was intended and
what was built stands in plain view, with nobody having to raise it. That is a gift rather than a
judgement -- the plan gets corrected while correcting it is still cheap.

**It gives custody a rhythm.** Keys, funds, signatures, and anything else a person must decide by
hand collect at a season boundary, where there is time to weigh them together.

## How a season nests

Seasons divide by fours, and the division is the same shape at every scale, so the words stay
usable no matter how far in you zoom.

| Unit | Roughly |
|---|---|
| **Season** | the whole arc, named |
| **Equinox** | one of four movements within it |
| **Journey** | one of four within an equinox |
| **Quest** | one of four within a journey |
| **Round** | one working session, with an opening and a close |
| **Lap** | one complete piece of work inside a round |

You will meet **round** and **lap** most often, since those are the units a day is made of. A lap
owes nothing to the lap before it -- it is complete in itself -- and a round is the bounded sitting
that holds several.

## What tells you a season is over

A season closes when its crux is hit: the hardest solvable problem it named at the start. That is a
different signal from a full checklist or an expired calendar. The crux is what the season was
really about, and everything else was support.

Then the close does three things: it reads the logs for what actually happened, it condenses the
finished work out of the living plan, and it names the next crux. The record stays; the plan gets
lighter.
