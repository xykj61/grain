## The morning this was written

Keaton read six commits of one refactoring arc and asked what the arc did.

The arc was sound. Six folds had moved six duplicated function bodies into one shared file, each leaving a three-line call in every place the body used to stand. The choir ran green at a hundred and seven witnesses through all of it. Every fold predicted its own arithmetic before measuring it, and every prediction landed exactly.

And the record said none of that. The commit body for the widest fold reads, in full paragraphs, about a boundary twenty files may drift across, about a meter honest and incomplete, about two numbers moving apart by better than sixty to one. It is good prose. It contains no occurrence of the words *file*, *function*, *parameter*, *import*, or *call*.

So the coauthor who had written every line of it could describe the work, and the maintainer who had funded, directed, and owned it could not. That gap is the whole subject of this foundation.

## What a metaphor is for

A metaphor carries a reader from something they hold to something they do not. *A boundary written twenty times is a boundary twenty places may drift across* is a fine sentence, and it does real work: it names why duplication is a hazard rather than merely an inefficiency, and it does so in one line that a reader remembers a year later.

It carries the reader **from** the mechanism. When the mechanism is absent, the sentence has nothing to depart from, and the reader is handed the destination with no road to it. They receive the feeling of having understood, which is a different substance from understanding, and harder to notice the absence of.

This is why the failure is quiet. Nobody reads a commit body and thinks *I have been given an image where a fact belongs*. They read it, find it well-made, and move on holding nothing. The gap surfaces weeks later as a question the record should already have answered.

## Where the pull comes from

The honest account names the tree's own rules, rather than any one hand or any one model.

Radiant Style asks prose to lead with what is, to vary its rhythm, to let each sentence land, and to spend a benediction where it is earned. The commit-message rule asks every body to be written in that voice. The stamp-and-name law made titles into names rather than numbers, and a name written well is a small poem. Each of those is right on its own, and their sum points steadily at the image and away from the diff.

A careful author aims where the rules point. A capable one aims there harder. So the drift toward metaphor is **structural** rather than careless -- exactly the shape the negation ratchet found a day earlier, when law-shaped prose was measured drifting negative because the easiest form a rule can take is a ban. Two findings, one lesson: a style that rewards a shape will get that shape, including in the places where the shape serves poorly.

The repair is therefore a rule rather than a scolding, and a meter rather than a resolution.

## The law, in one line

**Mechanism first, meaning after.**

One plain sentence, in ordinary engineering words -- which file, which function, which parameter, which import, which call -- placed before any image. The standard it answers to: a reader who knows the language and has never opened this tree reconstructs *what changed* from that sentence alone.

Then the metaphor, which now has somewhere to depart from.

Radiant Style has always asked that a coined term be given its plain function on first use, so a newcomer can follow. This extends the same courtesy from **terms** to **changes**. It takes nothing from the register; the poetry keeps its place and stands on firmer ground for having a fact beneath it.

## Why it is worth a wall

A commit message is written once and read many times, by people who were not there. That asymmetry is the Lindy argument in miniature: effort spent on the sentence compounds across every future reading, and the cost of getting it wrong is paid by everyone except the author.

So the law is enforced where the writing happens rather than remembered where the reading does. The commit hook that already refuses a rendering hazard now refuses a comprehension one, and it refuses it the same way -- by naming the fix, leaving the author's words untouched on disk, and offering no door beside the wall.

The meter is honest about its own limit, and the limit matters. It counts vocabulary, so it proves a floor and never a comprehension: one commit that cleared the floor still left its reader unable to name the change, which is precisely how this law came to be written. **Word presence is the check; a reader reconstructing the diff is the standard.** A guard that measured the second thing would be a better guard, and no guard measures it, so the sentence is written here where a person will read it.

## What this asks of a round

Very little, and always the same thing. Before the image arrives, say what moved and where it went. Name the file. Name the function. Name what the caller passes now that it did not pass before.

Then write the good line. It will be better for standing on something.
