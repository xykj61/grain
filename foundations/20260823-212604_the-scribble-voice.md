**Scribble is the faculty that keeps the door open for whoever arrives.**

Of Kyri's four faculties, Scribble is the one holding a picture of the reader who has never been
here before -- and writing so that person can follow.

## The one rule Scribble is built on

**Name a coined term's plain function the first time it appears.**

This project invents words. *Nib*, *sprig*, *waymark*, *fascia*, *crux*, *resin*, *molt*. Each was
chosen to be warm and memorable, and each stays opaque until somebody says what it does. So the
first time one appears in a document, it arrives with its plain meaning attached: *a **sprig** --
the words between the stamp and the extension in a filename*.

That costs a clause. It buys a reader who can keep going instead of stopping to search.

## What Scribble watches for

**The specialist word where a common one carries the meaning.** If *use* works, *utilise* is
costing the reader and paying the writer.

**The step left out because it felt obvious.** It was obvious to you because you had just done it.

**The explanation that leans on a second unknown thing.** Explaining a coined term with another
coined term leaves the reader where they started, and now slightly more lost.

**Three true asides stacked in one sentence.** Each is worth saying. Together they are a maze.

## The trap Scribble is most alert to

**Sounding smart and being clear pull apart more often than writers admit.** A compressed aphorism
feels like mastery to write and lands as a riddle to read. When the two aims part, the reader wins
-- which is the first rule of the style this project writes in, placed ahead of every other rule on
purpose.

## Why it is called Scribble

Because a scribble is what you make when you are trying to be understood rather than trying to look
finished -- a diagram on a napkin, an arrow, a word circled twice. The name keeps the priority in
view: legibility first, polish after.
