## The thesis

**A personal computer can be built so that two of its most expensive habits -- remembering
everything, and staying sealed -- are absent by construction.**

Absent by construction, the way a device with no radio stays off every network. A setting is a
promise somebody administers; construction is a property you can see.

## Four choices, and what each one costs

Each of these is a real trade, and the cost stands beside the gain.

### Memory that forgets

**The choice:** working memory only. What you are holding stays while it is powered, and a
deliberate act is what makes anything persist.

**Why it protects:** a device holding nothing by default has nothing to hand over and nothing to
sell. Privacy becomes a property of the hardware rather than a policy somebody administers.

**The teacher, named honestly:** Cerebras builds wafer-scale processors whose memory is SRAM
distributed across the wafer rather than separate DRAM reached over a bus, and the family is sold
as the CS series. This project studies that arrangement as a *concept* -- compute and memory in one place,
with the bus between them gone -- and writes its own. **Every line of code here is ours**, and any
statement about their current products belongs to them rather than to this page.

**The cost, stated plainly:** you give up the convenience of a device that quietly remembers for
you. That convenience is genuine and widely wanted, and this is one deliberate trade rather than a
verdict on anyone who makes the other one.

### One wire, or none

**The choice:** a single USB-C connection carries power, data, and display. There is no cellular
radio, and network access is a cable you attach.

**Why it protects:** the difference between *connected* and *not connected* becomes visible and
physical. You can see it across a room.

**The cost:** all wireless convenience, and a real accessibility question worth naming -- a
cable-only device asks something of a person for whom attaching one is hard, and that stands as an
open design question here rather than a solved one.

### Opens with a screwdriver

**The choice:** every part a person might replace comes apart with common tools, and the parts are
named in the documentation.

**Why it endures:** a device that opens gets repaired, and a repaired device stays useful for years
longer. Right-to-repair sits here as an engineering requirement rather than a compliance box.

**The cost:** thickness, some weight, and a higher bill of materials. Sealed devices are thinner
because sealing is cheap.

### Recyclable by design

**The choice:** material chosen so the device can be taken apart into streams that actually
recycle, with the fewest possible bonded composites.

**Why it matters here:** this project's other work is biocyclic-vegan farming, where a system is
designed so the soil ends up better than it started. A computer built to become clean material
again is that same idea in a different medium.

**The cost:** a narrower palette of materials and finishes than a sealed device enjoys.

## Two scales of the same idea

**The pocket device** is what a person carries: reflective and paper-like, forgetting by default,
one wire, opening with a screwdriver.

**The desk companion** is the piece that stays home and does the heavy thinking. Small-form-factor machines
from the current market -- the class this project has been researching -- are the honest near-term
shape, since a local machine you own is buyable today where a bespoke one waits on a fabrication run.

The pairing is what makes the pocket device's forgetting workable. Work happens where the memory
is, travels when you deliberately keep it, and the thing in your pocket stays light in both senses.

## The civic half, named rather than assumed

Hardware alone moves little on its own. What this is *for* is the reason it looks like this.

**Value that stays close to home.** A device connecting a person to a local economy keeps more of
what they earn nearby.

**Public financing, studied honestly.** This project researches Modern Monetary Theory arguments
for public budgets and public buyouts, including the case that a currency-issuing government faces
real-resource limits rather than household-style budget limits. **That is contested economics**, the
research here is reading and argument rather than advice, and every document in that thread says so
at its head.

**Software that belongs to the person running it**, which is the whole direction this tree points.

## What is built, and what is not

**Built and proven, today:** the software side. Bounded memory discipline, the capability enclosure,
the content-addressed store, the receipt format, and thirty-five standing guards that run over this
tree and are each proven to refuse as well as to pass.

**Studied, not built:** the memory-that-forgets firmware, proven pure in software ahead of any
metal.

**Vision:** the device itself. Every hardware statement above is a design intention rather than a
specification -- the board, the parts, and the fabrication all still lie ahead. Anything touching a real purchase, a real
supplier, or a real fabrication run is a gate a person opens by hand.

**The falsifier, so this page can be scored later:** the forgetting firmware gets simulated in
software first, and testers use it. Should the deliberate act of keeping something prove tedious
enough that they ask for the old default back, the premise fails at the ergonomic level rather than
the technical one -- and the honest response is to write that here rather than build the board
anyway.
