The elder method teaches how a language grows: stand on proven ground, wear a thin costume,
own the core by path, diverge by strengthening, live in it and gate. This page adds the clause
the method left quiet -- **when** does the language grow? -- and the answer one night proved
five times over:

> **A rune is earned by a law that wants it. The language grows a feature the day a real law
> becomes unsayable, and on no other day.**

wanted by nothing yet, and therefore closed.

## What the clause buys

**Every feature arrives with its witness born.** A growth pulled by a law lands beside a desk
that speaks the law and a witness that runs it on metal, so the feature's first day is also its
first proof. A growth pushed by symmetry lands beside nothing, and its guard is vacuous from
birth -- the shape REDS %335 taught the tree to fear: a guard the type system makes vacuous
reads exactly like a guard that holds.

**The grammar stays a closed, learnable table.** The shop's operators -- `gth`, `eq`, `mul`,
`and`, `or` -- number five after a night of growth, because each seat cost a law. A grammar
that grows by want stays the afternoon-learnable set the runes were chosen to be; a grammar
that grows by symmetry doubles every time someone notices an absence.

**Precedent compounds.** The first composed operand took two laps -- parse seated, emit
waiting, the `-a`/`-b` split -- because the pattern was being cut. The second took one lap.
The third shared the second's fields and landed in a morning. The clause makes growth cheap
exactly where it has already been proven, and expensive only at true frontiers.

**Spellings ride free, behavior never does.** When a peer spelling is wanted -- the ancestor's
rune beside the shop's word -- alias-sameness seats it as one body, one parse path, one emit,
proven byte-identical. A spelling is a name, and names are cheap; a behavior is a law, and
laws are earned.

## The test, for the next feature someone wants

Name the law that cannot be said. Point at the Rye that holds it today and the desk that would
speak it tomorrow. If both exist, the growth is a lap. If either is missing, the want is a
symmetry noticing itself -- write it as a named door and let it wait, the way nested
composition waits now.

May every rune this language ever wears be able to answer, when a newcomer asks why it exists:
*a law needed me, and here is its desk.*
