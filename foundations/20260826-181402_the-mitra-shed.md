**Mitra** is the old Sanskrit word for the friend, and in the Veda the keeper of covenants --
the one who holds agreements true. The tree wears the word for its gentlest cut: **the mitra
shed is the removal of a dated fossil whose living mutant already stands, done so carefully
that every promise the fossil ever received is kept by someone else before the fossil goes.**
A shed that keeps faith is the difference between pruning and loss, and the name says which
one this is.

## The process, whole

Six steps, each with its instrument, none skipped:

1. **Survey.** A looking pass names the candidates: dated writings whose living intent has
   moved on. Nothing is cut here; the census
   ([`../tools/fixtures/s/shed_census_scan.sh`](../tools/fixtures/s/shed_census_scan.sh)) is the
   authority on membership, never recall.
2. **Molt.** Each candidate's living content is re-seated as a **mutant** at a fresh
   one-clock stamp; the elder stays on disk as a **bannered fossil** pointing at its living
   path, and a class row lands in `../construction/SHRED_PREP.md`.
   Molt preps and destroys nothing ([`../.claude/rules/molt.md`](../.claude/rules/molt.md)).
3. **Repoint the living.** Every living citer of the fossil -- code, docs, indexes -- moves
   to the mutant *before* any cut, because a living link is a promise due now. Dated
   testimony keeps its elder spelling and is resolved on read, never rewritten
   ([`../tools/d/dated_path_resolve.rish`](../tools/d/dated_path_resolve.rish)).
4. **Circle.** The cut waits for Keaton's word, always. A prepped fossil may stand for weeks;
   a well-prepped fossil costs a directory entry and keeps a chapter readable.
5. **Checkpoint, then cut.** The walk-back nib lands in
   `../construction/CHECKPOINTS.md` first, so every cut
   page stays one `git show` away; then the shed removes the fossils in one pass and the
   SHRED_PREP block becomes the shed-complete record.
6. **The guards read the wound.** The dated-path census's lost ceiling
   ([`../tools/d/dated_path_witness.rish`](../tools/d/dated_path_witness.rish)), the living
   link scans, and the shred-prep witness all run over the cut tree -- the fascia is measured
   after surgery, which is what makes the shed a friend rather than a knife.

## The worked example, and the lesson it paid for

The first full mitra shed ran `20260826.032839`: twelve fossils cut in one circled pass,
every mutant standing, the pond application sources repointed in the same round. The census
then taught the process its hardest lesson yet: the cutting bench's dated-path census stood
dark that morning (the BSD dialect family, closed later the same day), so the cut crossed the
lost-reference gate unmeasured, and the first completed census read the crossing at 390 lost
against a ceiling of 177. The repair seated the shed basenames as deliberate absences and
repointed forty-eight living foundations links the quiet had hidden -- and the rule joined
the ledger in its own words: **a guard dark on the measuring host gates nothing; a cut that
crosses a census runs that census on the cutting bench first.** The whole account lives in
the session log `20260826-141312_the-dialect-family-closes.kyri` and the dialect-family REDS
rows of that day.

## Where mitra sits among the three depths

The tree removes at three depths ([`20260818-081438_the-three-depths-of-removal.md`](20260818-081438_the-three-depths-of-removal.md)):
**molt** preps and destroys nothing; the **mitra shed** cuts fossils whose mutants stand,
keeping history's account; **debride** removes from history itself, on Keaton's explicit word
with a cairn laid first. Mitra is the middle depth -- the only one an agent may *prepare*
end to end, and never the one an agent may *circle*. The word to cut is Keaton's in every
case; the shed's craft is making that word cheap to give, because every promise is already
kept.
