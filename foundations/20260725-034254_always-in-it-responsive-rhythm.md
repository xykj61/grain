## The season that does not close

A season boundary set in advance is a rule that stops listening. It closes while the work is alive, and it opens while the body is still tired, because a calendar cannot feel either condition. What listens is attention itself, and attention is available every hour.

So the seasons here are ongoing. The tree grows when there is growing to do and rests when rest is what is true. The compass gains a rhythm that reads the moment rather than the month. This is aparigraha reaching the calendar: carry no schedule you do not use.

When hungry, eat. When tired, sleep. Prompt when there is prompting to do, review when reviewing wants doing, question when a question is alive. The rhythm answers to the signal, rather than to a calendar.

## The two works

**The work of doing** is the lap: the witness written, the rune lowered, the memo filed, the name seated. It is visible, countable, and easy to mistake for the whole.

**The work of being** runs continuously: staying open to what is around you, noticing what makes you lean forward, gathering connections and stories and beauty as raw material long before any of it has a use. Nobody schedules this half. It happens on a walk, in a discourse, in a page of someone else's book at two in the morning.

The two halves feed each other in one direction: **being gathers, doing forms.** Material collected while simply living becomes available the next time you sit down. That is why the tree already keeps places for the gathering:

| Deposit | What it holds |
|---------|----------------|
| Study shelves (`external-research/` · `gratitude/` · kin) | What the world taught, held for reading |
| Gratitude library | Sources honored without being copied into our modules |
| Open questions (`context/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`) | Living concerns waiting their own season |
| Design sittings (`active-designing/` · `counsel/`) | Gatherings shaped toward a decision |

Those are not overhead. They are where the work of being deposits what it found.

## Undivided, rather than unbounded

The rhythm is not *do whatever you feel like*. It is *do one thing wholly*. Working at three in the morning, fully inside the work, honors this exactly. Working at three while half-tallying whether one ought to be asleep honors nothing. Resting while turning over parked decisions is not rest.

**One lap at a time, one question at a time, and the parked things genuinely parked.** A hold that keeps returning to mind was never really held. The tree writes holds down so a thought can leave the hands, and the current lap can have all of the attention.

The failure mode has a name worth keeping: not tiredness, and not long hours. **Dividedness.** Witnesses cannot catch it; only a person can notice.

## What this asks of counsel

Counsel reports state and names gates; the rhythm belongs to Keaton.

- **Report, never urge.** When the ungated queue is empty, say so plainly and once.
- **A recommendation offers; it does not press.** The closing line names what is available and stops there.
- **Timing belongs to Keaton.** Fatigue is Keaton's signal to read.
- **Where a lap wants fresh attention for engineering reasons**, name the technical reason once, plainly, and leave the timing alone.
- **A twice-repeated unasked suggestion is a red** to own out loud, rather than an argument to press harder.

Standing practice also lives in the collaboration rule and in [`../context/QUIN.md`](../context/QUIN.md).

## Gratitude close

With warmth and respect we thank **Rick Rubin**, whose pages on the artist's two kinds of work — and on staying awake to what arrives — gave this direction its spine; read closely, carried in our own words, never copied. We thank **Hyakujo**, whose answer about hunger and tiredness is the whole of the discipline in a few plain words, and **Basho**, whose haiku showed that where prose fails, a picture in words may still arrive. We thank **Osho**, whose commentary on that exchange drew out the distinction between the same act done wholly and done divided. The teaching remains theirs; the rhythm we draw from it is ours to keep honest.
