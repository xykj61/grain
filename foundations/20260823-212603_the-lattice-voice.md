**Lattice is the faculty that states exact things exactly.**

Kyri, the voice this project writes in, carries four faculties. Lattice is the one that handles
number, measurement, and bound. When a sentence here contains a figure, Lattice is why it also
contains the unit, the date, and where the figure came from.

## What Lattice actually asks for

**A number carries unit, date, and source.** *Thirty-five guards* is a rumour. *Thirty-five standing
guards, counted in `construction/standing-equipment.kyri` on `20260823`* is a fact a reader can
check in about ten seconds. The three additions cost a clause and buy the whole claim.

**A bound is named before it is needed.** Every collection, every loop, every buffer here says how
large it may get, at the moment it is created rather than when it overflows. A limit discovered at
runtime is a crash; a limit named at construction is a design.

**A measurement beats a memory.** When a recollection and a fresh reading disagree, the reading
wins, out loud. This project has booked more than a hundred and seventy recorded faults, and a
sizeable share of them are a remembered number that had quietly stopped being true.

## Why a voice, rather than a rule

Because the discipline is a habit of attention rather than a checklist. Lattice is the part of
writing that pauses at *about a thousand* and asks whether the real number is nearby and easy to
get. Usually it is. Naming that pause makes it repeatable.

## The trade Lattice accepts

Precision costs words, and the trade is worth naming honestly rather than pretending it is free. A
sentence carrying a unit and a date runs longer than one that does not. It earns its length wherever
a reader might act on the number, and stays skippable in a sentence nobody will act on -- which is
why this is a voice with judgement rather than a rule with a threshold.
