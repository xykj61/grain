# Reds First, and the Allocation That Follows

*A witness that refuses has already stopped the work. What was missing was what happens next — and until now the answer was whatever the next round felt like doing. This law names it: the remainder of the allocation belongs to the reds before it belongs to anything new.*

## One home, three citations

This law lives here and nowhere else. The code guidance cites it, the agent rules cite it, and neither restates it — because a law kept in three places becomes three laws within a season, and this house learned that at v6 when one convention was found living in five copies.

## What was already true

Two halves of this discipline have been in place for a long time, and both work.

A witness that refuses **stops the work** — loudly, at the seam, rather than passing a defect downstream. That is the older half, and it is the reason a green line here means something.

**Stop-and-park** names the moments a hand must be summoned: a parity red twice on one witness, a module-home decision, anything touching keys or custody or money, a guarded name.

**Own-reds-immediately** requires that a fault be named the moment it is found, without softening, and since `20260729.222000` requires that it be *recorded* in the ledger rather than merely spoken.

## What was missing

None of that said **what happens next**. A red was found, named, recorded — and then the following round did whatever the following round was going to do. Sometimes that was the fix, immediately, in the same sitting. Sometimes it was a new feature, with the red left to a ratchet or a note. The difference was mood rather than law.

Twenty reds in one sitting is a large enough sample to see the shape: most were fixed on the spot because the fixing was cheap and the fault was in front of us. That is exactly the case where a law is unnecessary. The law is for the other case — when the fix is expensive, the round is nearly spent, and something newer is more interesting.

## The law

**A red books the remainder of the allocation.**

When a red is found and recorded, the rounds left in the current **journey** — sixteen rounds, the unit that turns the priority cycle once — are dedicated to reds before any new work opens. Reds first, and the allocation follows.

If the journey closes with reds still open, the **next journey inside the same equinox** opens on reds. If the equinox closes, the **first journey of the next equinox** opens on reds. If the season closes, a **new season opens on reds** and nothing else.

The cascade is real and it is deliberate: a fault does not get to outlive the interest in fixing it by waiting for a boundary to pass.

## Four bounds on the cascade, so it stays a law and not a stampede

**A red is not a ratchet.** A **red** is something wrong: a claim that failed, a bound that did not hold, a path reported that does not exist, a guard that could not run. A **ratchet** is something not yet uniform: eighteen elder scans that predate a convention, one real file among fourteen symlinks. Reds book the allocation. **Ratchets turn on touch and never book anything** — because booking them would be a sweep, and sweeps across an already-clean collection cost more than they earn.

**Every boundary crossing is announced, never silent.** When reds carry past the end of a journey, an equinox, or a season, that crossing is stated in the waymark with the count of reds still open. The cascade may run for a long time; it may never run quietly.

**The bound is the season, and then a hand.** If a full season closes with reds still open, the next season opening on reds is **Keaton's word** rather than an automatic event. Four journeys of reds is a signal that something structural is wrong, and a structural problem wants a person, not another sixteen rounds of the same reflex.

**The fix is proven, not asserted.** A red leaves the ledger open until a witness shows the fix on metal — red before, green after, with a fixture where the guard blocks. A red closed by a claim is not closed.

## What this law does not authorize

Keaton's word asked for breaches to be approved automatically for the fixes. **That one stays gated, and the discipline this law is siloed from is itself the reason.**

The teaching draws its own line precisely, and by its own internal standard: a human pulling the cord is *related to* the machine-stops-itself principle yet is **not the full form of it** — it is the backup for where machine-level detection is not yet in place. And the cord's whole function is to **stop and summon**. The lantern lights; a person comes. The first self-halting machine did not rethread itself and resume; it halted and waited for hands.

So the discipline that inspired this law is, in its own account, a stopping-and-calling discipline rather than a proceeding-autonomously one. The study and the sources are named at [`../external-research/20260729-224828_the-line-that-stops-itself.md`](../external-research/20260729-224828_the-line-that-stops-itself.md).

Our own laws say the same in our own vocabulary: *counsel may design a breach and may never begin one*, and *a breach is a seating word*. A breach relocates paths wholesale, and automatic approval would remove the human gate from precisely the operation that moves the most at once.

**What is authorized automatically:** the allocation. The rounds book themselves to reds without asking, exactly as the line stops without asking. The fixes proceed under every ordinary law — accrete by default, witness before narrative, propose never seat.

**What stays gated:** any breach the fixes want. It is designed in the round, named in the waymark, and it waits. If a red genuinely cannot be fixed without a breach, then the honest output of the round is a **designed, unbegun breach** and a red that stays open in the ledger until a word arrives. That is not a failure of the round. That is the round doing its job and telling the truth about where it stopped.

## Which of our practices halts itself, and which needs a hand

The distinction we owe the teachers most, applied to ourselves.

**The self-halting machine** — stopping without anyone watching: every witness in `tools/gen/season/`, the scan seams whose exit status agrees with their verdict, the bounds that refuse rather than truncate, the six standing-exempt fixtures that keep the guards honest. These sense and they stop, and no one has to be watching. This is the full form of the principle rather than its backup.

**The lantern** — the human backup where machine detection is not yet in place: a red owned in prose, a second look that overturns a first finding, a stopwatch reached for before a sentence. Real, necessary, and *weaker* — because it depends on someone noticing.

Reading the ledger's *what caught it* column, the machine caught nineteen of twenty. So the honest direction of travel is clear: **every lantern that fires twice should become a self-halting machine.** A fault caught by attention once is a story; caught twice, it is a guard that has not been written yet.

## The measurable

Named so this law can be checked rather than admired:

- **reds open** — rows in the ledger without a witnessed fix
- **rounds booked** — of the journey's sixteen, how many the reds hold
- **boundaries crossed** — journeys, equinoxes, or seasons the cascade has carried past, each named in its waymark
- **lantern-to-machine** — faults caught by attention that have since become guards
