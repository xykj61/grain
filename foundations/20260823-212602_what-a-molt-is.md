A **molt** is how a piece of writing here gets a new skin without losing the old one.

## The problem it solves

A document written a year ago can be entirely right and still read poorly. The style moved on, or
the project learned a plainer way to say the same thing. You want the better version, and you want
the old one too, because a hundred other files link to it and because how a thing was once explained
is itself a record.

Each usual answer costs something. Editing in place spends the old reading. Making a new file and
removing the old spends every link pointing at it.

**A molt keeps both.** It writes the new version at a fresh timestamp, points the living references
at it, and leaves the original exactly where it stood.

## The four steps

1. **Seat the mutant.** Write the new version at a new one-clock stamp. It becomes the page people
   read.
2. **Repoint the living citers.** Files still being worked on get updated to point at the new
   version. Dated records keep every word they wrote -- those are testimony, and testimony stands
   as written.
3. **Banner the fossil.** The original gets a note at the top saying where the reading moved and
   what changed, so whoever lands there knows immediately.
4. **Write the row.** The change goes in a queue with its measurements, so a later reader inherits the decision
   rather than re-deriving it.

The old version is called a **fossil** and the new one a **living mutant**. Both words are
deliberate: a fossil is a real record of a real thing, and a mutant is the same creature with a
change that took.

## The two limits a molt holds

**A molt prepares; it leaves the cutting to another word.** Removing the fossil is a separate act
named **shred**, it waits until a person circles it, and it may well never happen. A fossil costs one
directory entry and keeps a season readable.

**A dated record keeps every word it wrote.** Where a file's own name carries a timestamp, it is
testimony, and its references are *resolved* -- a small tool computes where a moved file went.

## The gentle version

A **mitra shed prep** is a molt done all the way through: mutant seated, citers repointed, banner
placed, row written, and the cut still refused. *Mitra* is a name for friendship kept in order, and
the idea is that a fossil gets seen off like a friend rather than pulled like a weed.

## Why it is worth the ceremony

Because it lets the project keep improving how it explains itself, at no cost to anyone who linked
to it, with an honest record of what changed and when. The tree stays free to write better while
every earlier reading stays available.
