## Biography profile (public facts)

Helen Atthowe has worked for roughly **thirty-five years** connecting farming, food systems, land stewardship, and conservation. She holds a master's degree in horticulture from **Rutgers University** and has worked in education and research at the University of Arkansas, Rutgers, and Oregon State University, and as a horticulture extension agent in Montana (including an organic Master Gardener course). She and her late husband, **Carl Rosato**, co-owned a certified organic orchard in California where they pioneered raising tree fruit **without any pesticides**. She farmed and researched at **Woodleaf Farm** in eastern Oregon into 2023, then began a new Woodleaf Farm path in western **Montana**, while continuing to consult and monitor research. She has served as a board member for the **Organic Farming Research Foundation** and as an adviser for the **Wild Farm Alliance**. She is a contributing writer to *The Organic Gardener's Handbook of Natural Pest and Disease Control* and other books.

In **2023**, Chelsea Green published her book ***The Ecological Farm: A Minimalist No-Till, No-Spray, Selective-Weeding, Grow-Your-Own-Fertilizer System for Organic Agriculture*** (ISBN 9781645021810; foreword by Paul Muller). Public tellings emphasize reducing or eliminating outside fertilizers and pesticides — even those common in certified organic practice — by designing farms that keep living roots, feed microbial communities, and host beneficial insects.

Those facts are public. What this foundation cares about is the through-line: **attention over input thrash**, **on-farm fertility**, **minimal soil disturbance**, and **obedience to the whole rather than tinkering with the parts** (a phrase reviewers associate with Wes Jackson's praise of the book).
