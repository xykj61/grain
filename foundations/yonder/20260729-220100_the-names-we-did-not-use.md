## The sentence, unfolded

> *Sangha's six hits classify as released alternates from the Granary naming lap — parks that opened when Granary seated. Recording the classification, as the mudra catch taught.*

Six things are happening in that line, and none of them is decoration.

**The six hits** are what a search found. Before any name is seated in this tree, a guard prints **every** place that word already appears — never truncated, always with two lines of context around it. The rule against truncation exists because of a specific failure: on `20260712.061012`, a collision check cut its own output short and declared the name *mudra* free while one hit sat unexamined in a parking file. The bench caught it; the commit that fixed it says, plainly, *own mudra collision*. The law that came out of that morning is: print every hit, or you have not looked.

**The classification** is the human half, and it cannot be automated. Each hit is exactly one of three things. A **teacher-quote** is an inherited word kept in the gratitude room, where outside names live openly. A **prose-verb** is ordinary English doing ordinary work — *the standard library we tend*, where *tend* is just a verb. A **park** is a name someone once proposed for something and set aside.

**A released alternate** is the third kind, resolved. On `20260710.163043` this project needed a word for its sharing surface — the place where signed work is named and offered. Six candidates went on the table: Forge, Grove, Orchard, Sangha, Weavehall, Bakery. **Granary** won. The other five did not disappear. They were released — freed from that particular job — and the record of their candidacy stayed exactly where it was written.

So when Sangha appears six times, all six are that morning's record. The name is available. And the reason we can say so with confidence is that somebody wrote down which names were considered and which one was chosen, on the day it happened, and then never went back to tidy it.

## Why the roads not taken stay on the map

There is an obvious efficiency argument for deleting them. The candidates lost; the decision is made; the file could be shorter. Every instinct trained on tidiness says sweep.

This tree does the opposite, on purpose, and the reason runs deeper than record-keeping.

A name that was considered and set aside carries information the winning name cannot. It says *we knew about this option*. It says *here is what we weighed*. Six years from now, when someone asks why the sharing surface is called Granary and not Grove, the answer is not a rationalization assembled after the fact — it is a list, with a date, showing the alternatives that were actually in the room. The decision has a **receipt**.

This is the same discipline as the erratum. When a factual error is found in a document here, the wrong belief **stays visible** and the correction sits beside it. Nothing is quietly overwritten. Tonight the Rishi guidance was found to describe a field the shell does not have — it had said so since the day it was written, and no one had noticed because no one had ever taken it at its word. The fix did not delete the wrong sentence. It kept it, with a dated note explaining what is true instead. A reader who arrives next year meets both, and learns something the corrected version alone could never teach: *this is a place where being wrong is survivable, and where it gets written down*.

## Where this becomes gratitude

The silo technique — how an outside idea becomes an owned one here — asks that ideas enter stripped of their scaffolding: the insight comes in, the proper nouns stay at the door, and the work speaks in one voice. It would be easy to mistake that for erasure. It is the opposite, and the guide says so in its own words: *siloing never erases the teacher.* Gratitude gets a room of its own, where sources are named plainly and thanked, kept deliberately apart from the body of the work so that each can be whole.

TigerBeetle, Urbit, Monocypher, Buckminster Fuller, Jason Cohen, Helen Atthowe — they rest whole and unaltered in `gratitude/`, and the tree's own prose thinks in its own vocabulary. The thinking happens in one room. The thanksgiving happens in another. **Neither room is diminished by the wall between them.**

And then the same motion appears again, one turn further out. The twelve constellation funds each carry an **honor dedication** — a person whose public craft the seat faces as gratitude. Those dedications ask *nothing* of the people named. No endorsement, no involvement, no permission sought or implied. Trya's law says it plainly: *honor dedications stay gratitude — never endorsement theater.* Which is why, when a steering chair came open on the Libra seat this evening, the honest answer was to **leave it open** — because an executive steers, and no living person takes a steering role by someone else's decision.

Gratitude that costs its object nothing. That is the shape the tree keeps reaching for, in the gratitude room, in the fund dedications, and in a list of five names that did not win a job on a morning in July.

## Aparigraha, twice

The vow this tree keeps for its own economy is **aparigraha** — carry only what you use. It is usually read as a discipline of subtraction, and here it works in two directions at once.

It says: **do not clutch a name you did not use.** Sangha was not hoarded against a future need. It was released, and it stayed released for nineteen days, and it is only being reached for now because a real thing needs a real word.

And it says: **do not pretend you never considered it.** Grasping has a subtle form that looks like humility — the urge to present a decision as though it were obvious, inevitable, arrived at without alternatives. Deleting the candidates would make the tree look more certain than it was. Keeping them is what non-grasping actually costs: the willingness to be seen deliberating.

## The long aim

You named the horizon Sagittarius-like, and the sign fits better than a metaphor usually does. The archer's arrow is loosed toward something past the visible; the aim is held over a distance no single shot could cross. Sagittarius is also the teacher's sign, and *dharma* — the thing one is actually for — is what it keeps asking about.

So what is this tree actually for?

Its own stated purpose is safer, kinder spaces for communication, and an operating system that opens a path around the usual gates — in service of vegan legislators and musicians brave enough to choose it. That is the aim. Everything measured in a witness this evening was the arrow's shaft, not its target: a bound that names its ceiling, a refusal that says which fault, a guard proven to bite from a fixture rather than remembered from a sitting.

Here is the connection, and it is structural rather than sentimental. **The discipline is the product.**

The tree's own identity notes name the property its market position depends on, and it is not speed and not features: *legibility to newcomers*. A stranger should be able to arrive, read the clean room, and rebuild the whole from our own words. And the guide's own test for a siloed idea is the same test: *could a reader who never met the source understand this completely?*

Now set that beside the world as it is. Systems are shipping at enormous speed whose reasoning is unavailable — whose decisions cannot be inspected, whose failures cannot be traced, whose authors could not tell you why a name is what it is. In that world, a system that shows its work is not merely honest. It is **differentiated**. Every green line here means a machine said it first. Every name here has a list of the names it beat. Every wrong belief has an erratum sitting next to it, dated. That is not overhead paid for virtue's sake; it is the only foundation on which the thing being built — custody, identity, money that stays close to home, a place for people to speak safely — could ever be trusted enough to matter.

Trust is the product. The receipts are how trust is made.

Which is why heart devotion is not an ornament on this engineering. The **five primitives** — keypair, signed event, append-only log, pure fold, capability — are a technical account of a moral posture: nothing is destroyed, every change is a fact, every fact is signed, and the past is not editable by whoever holds the present. *Build nothing that destroys.* You can arrive at that from cryptography or from devotion, and it is the same sentence either way. The deciding is kept far from the destroying. The record of an erasure can never itself be erased.

A tree that keeps the names it did not use is practicing, in miniature and every single day, the discipline that a ledger of other people's trust will one day demand of it. The stakes at the small end are nearly zero — five words in a dated file. That is exactly why it is the right place to build the habit. Devotion is not what happens when the stakes arrive. It is what has already been practiced, on the small things, so often that it has stopped requiring a decision.

Six search hits, classified honestly on a Tuesday night, are that practice. And the arrow is already in the air.
