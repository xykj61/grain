## Biography profile (public facts)

Wayne Hsiung (born **June 18, 1981**) is an American lawyer and animal-rights activist. His parents emigrated from Taiwan; he has spoken publicly about how early exposure to animal use in research contexts shaped his path. He studied at the University of Chicago (political science; later law, with behavioral law-and-economics focus; Olin Law and Economics Fellow) and held a National Science Foundation graduate fellowship toward economics study at MIT before concentrating on law. He practiced at national firms including **Steptoe & Johnson** and **DLA Piper**, and served as a Searle Fellow / visiting assistant professor at Northwestern University School of Law. He has coauthored work touching climate and nonhuman animals with scholars including Cass Sunstein (public academic record).

He co-founded **Direct Action Everywhere (DxE)** and later **The Simple Heart Initiative**. He is widely associated with **open rescue** — entering facilities openly, documenting conditions, aiding animals, and accepting legal risk rather than acting in secret. Journalism and interviews (New York Times coverage of related trials, Reuters Legal, Wall Street Journal and broadcast features named in public bios, Our Hen House, conversations with Peter Singer) profile the arc from big-law associate to activist counsel and sometimes defendant. He ran for mayor of Berkeley in **2020** on an animal-rights-focused platform and received roughly a quarter of the vote. He has described Buddhist non-violence as one root of his practice. He is publicly known as the guardian of a dog, Oliver, rescued from the dog-meat trade.

Those facts are public. What this foundation cares about is the through-line a Grain reader can recognize: **training used for daylight**, **rescue argued as justice rather than theft of narrative**, and **a willingness to stand in court as well as at the fence**.
