## The Claim

Everyone downstream drinks what the first user leaves.

A basin is a ladder. Water enters at the top as snow and spring, gathers a town, a farm, a mill, a second town, and arrives at the mouth carrying everything each rung handed it. The rung nearest the sky holds a power the rungs below can only answer: **it decides what the whole ladder starts with.**

This document names that power plainly, says where a treatment works earns its keep, and shows how the tree's own habits -- a bound declared in advance, a receipt signed at the moment made, a fold anyone can recompute -- read as headwaters discipline. It stays place-neutral on purpose: the mountain changes with the region, and everything else stays the same.

## Three Places a Basin Offers

A person who wants to help a river has three places to stand, and they reward completely different work.

**The source.** The spring, the snowfield, the first hundred metres of creek. Here the water arrives as clean as it will ever be, and the highest-value act is to leave it be and take a reading. A source needs a gauge and a boundary far more than it needs a plant. Where the source sits inside a refuge, a wilderness, or a park, that boundary already exists, and the honest contribution is a long, public, unbroken record of what the water reads before anybody touches it.

**The first load.** The first town, camp, resort, or works whose used water rejoins the stream. This rung is where a treatment facility does the most good per dollar spent anywhere in the basin, for three reasons that compound. Headwater streams run small, so a given load arrives at a higher concentration than the same load would downstream. Cold, clear, low-nutrient water supports the life that is most sensitive to a change. And every improvement made here is inherited, unaltered, by every rung below -- one plant, many beneficiaries.

**The mouth.** The last plant before the water leaves for somewhere else. Necessary, well understood, and the place where the same money buys the least change, because the water arriving has already been used by everyone.

Read that ladder once and the siting question answers itself: **put the works at the first load, put the gauge at the source, and let the mouth keep doing its steady work.** A facility proposed at the literal source is a facility with nothing to treat.

## What the First User Owes

Four promises this project already keeps read cleanly as obligations the upstream seat carries.

**Do no harm** is the return. Water leaving the first user reads at least as clean as the water that arrived. That is a measurement rather than an intention: a concentration, at an outfall, on a date, taken by somebody with no stake in the answer.

**Tell the truth** is the record. Every withdrawal, every return, every laboratory reading is a fact somebody signed when it was made, appended to a log that only grows. What arrives unsigned is a story.

**Hold lightly** is the share. The first user takes the water it can account for and leaves the rest moving. In a basin where the claims already exceed the flow, holding lightly is the whole of good citizenship.

**Be kind** is the neighbour. The town downstream, the irrigator, the angler, and the fish each have a stake in what the first user does, and each of them can read the log.

## The Closed Basin Changes the Arithmetic

Many basins reach a point where the existing claims cover, or exceed, the water that actually flows. A state then closes the basin: new appropriations stop, and anyone wanting water either buys an existing right or offsets what they take.

A closed basin looks like a wall and works like an invitation, because it moves the whole reward from *finding* water to *using water twice*.

**In a closed basin the cheapest new water is the water already in the pipe.** Treated water put back to work -- irrigating a park, recharging an aquifer through infiltration basins, cooling a process, growing a crop -- is water that arrives with no new claim attached. The purple pipe is a water right nobody had to buy.

So the works that belong at the first load in a closed basin are **reuse works** rather than discharge works, and the design question changes from *how clean before we let it go* to *how clean before we use it again, and where does the second use send it*. Both questions want the same equipment and the same receipts. The second question adds an aquifer, a field, or a park to the ledger, and it answers the town's growth question at the same time it answers the river's.

## Why the Gauge Comes Before the Plant

A treatment works costs tens of millions and lasts twenty to thirty years. A monitoring record costs a fraction of that and lasts forever, and it is the thing that tells anyone whether the works did what it promised.

This ordering matters most where a river is visibly troubled and the cause stays open. A basin can carry a genuine impairment -- an algal bloom that anglers and neighbours can see from the bank -- while several years of careful sampling decline to name a single culprit. That combination is common, it is honest, and it is exactly the condition under which a confident program does the most damage: money spent against the wrong cause buys goodwill and changes nothing in the water.

The Civic reading is direct. **Name the outcome: a stream that reads healthy on aquatic life and recreation. Name what the reward measures: the reading itself, taken by an independent hand, published whole.** A program that rewards nutrient reduction when the binding constraint turns out to be temperature, light, or flow has aimed its whole budget at a number that was never the point.

So the sequence is: gauge, then diagnosis, then works sized to the diagnosis, with the gauge left running the whole time so the works can be graded on the water rather than on its own paperwork.

## Keeping Books on a Basin

Three habits from the code, read as water discipline.

**A bound declared in advance.** A works, a farm, or a mill states its water budget before it starts: this much withdrawn per season, this much consumed, this much returned, at this quality. A budget declared in advance is a promise a neighbour can hold.

**A receipt signed at the moment made.** The five primitives that carry a payment in this tree -- a keypair a person guards, a signed event, an append-only log, a pure fold, a capability -- carry a water fact with no change of shape. A withdrawal, a return, a laboratory result, and a soil reading are each one signed event.

**A fold anyone can recompute.** The state of a water account is a pure fold over that log: withdrawn, minus consumed, minus returned, equals what the basin is still owed. A skeptic runs the fold on their own machine and gets the same number, needing neither the operator's software nor the operator's permission.

Put those three at the first load and the upstream seat becomes something new: **a place that can prove it left the water better, to anyone, at any time, forever.** That proof is worth more to the towns below than any promise, and it is the one thing a headwaters town can offer that a downstream town structurally cannot.

## Where This Meets the Money

Public money is bounded by what is real -- the crews ready, the pipe on the ground, the water in the basin -- more than by accounting, and a basin is the plainest test of *real* there is. A region can fund the works, the crews, and the verification, and a second river sits beyond any budget.

So the civic instruments carry a headwaters clause each. **Procurement preference on measured performance** names the outfall reading beside the throughput, verified independently, at the contract and at each renewal. **Long performance bonds** carry past commissioning, so a works that drifts in year eight bears the cost of putting it right. **Transition support that names the soil** funds infiltration and organic matter, which are the land's own water accounts, with unannounced spot verification funded as load-bearing rather than as a line item that thins in a lean year.

And a fibre crop enters this conversation as the quiet ally. **A crop that lives on the rain its region already gets, retted on the field where it grew, asks the closed basin for nothing.** In a place where every drop is claimed, that is the strongest agricultural argument available, and it is a hydrology argument rather than a fashion one.

## What This Refuses

An honest vision names its limits, and this one has three.

**It ranks places rather than dismissing them.** A plant at the mouth of a basin is good work and keeps a river alive. The claim here is about where a *marginal* dollar buys the most change, which is a different question from where the work matters.

**It waits on the diagnosis.** Naming the first load as the right rung says where to stand. What to build there follows the measurement, and the professionals come ahead of the prose.

**It seats no program anywhere.** These are instruments a place could adopt, described place-neutrally. A basin whose three forks each carry a gateway town, a resort, and a university city, all inside a closed basin above a famous confluence, is exactly where the ladder shows itself in daylight -- and that is a place to *read* the ladder, in a study filed under its own name.

## Why It Belongs Here

Because *ahimsa*, followed upstream far enough, arrives at a spring.

A town at the top of a watershed that returns its water clean, proves it with a log anyone can fold, and grows its fibre on the rain that falls on it -- that is *do no harm* expressed as elevation. The first user has the least water, the smallest budget, and the largest leverage in the whole basin. Meeting that responsibility gladly is the same discipline this project already keeps in code, standing where the water starts.

*May the source keep its quiet and its gauge. May the first town return more than it borrowed. May every rung below inherit a river in better condition than the ledger promised. And may the fold that proves it run on anybody's machine.*
