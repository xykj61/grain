## The Claim

Clean at the top.

The cheapest place to treat water is the door where it enters the basin: a spring, an outfall, a well field, the front of a plume. Every mile downstream mixes the load with more water, spreads it across more soil, and multiplies what it costs to take back out. A basin held clean at its entrances stays clean by construction. A basin cleaned at its mouth spends its whole length paying for one reach.

This document names that rule plainly, says why a dry-country river makes the rule easy to see, and shows how the tree's own habits -- a bound on everything, a receipt for everything, a fold anyone can recompute -- read as basin discipline. It stays place-neutral on purpose. The river changes with the region, and everything else holds.

## Three Headwaters

In wet country a river begins where rain gathers on a hill. In dry country it begins in three places at once, and only one of them is a hill.

**Rain on a draw.** A draw is a channel that runs a few days a year and rests dry the rest. The mapped source of a long dry-country river is usually the head of a draw on a high plain, and a visitor standing at it sees grass.

**An aquifer's yield.** Under the plain lies the water the fields and the towns actually pump, older than the river above it, refilling on a clock measured in centuries. The plain's real headwater is a well.

**A city's return.** After a town uses its water once, it cleans that water and sends it back. In many dry basins the reclaimed return is the only flow that runs every day of the year, and the river's first perennial mile begins at an outfall.

Read plainly, this turns a duty into a design. **In dry country the treatment plant is a headwater.** What the plant lets through, the whole river carries; what the plant holds back, the whole river is spared. A source deserves a source's care, and an outfall that is a source deserves the same.

## Where the Load Enters

Three kinds of load come through three kinds of door, and each door has its own honest works.

**Salt enters where old seabeds seep.** A few springs at the edge of a plain can carry a third of a river's salt in a base flow of a cubic foot per second or two. Intercept the spring with a handful of shallow wells and the river sweetens for hundreds of miles below it. Treat the same salt at a city intake instead and the city treats a million gallons to remove what one well field could have kept out.

**Chemicals enter at a plume.** A pond of firefighting foam that leaked into the water table moves through the aquifer a little each year, toward the draw and the dairies below it. The place to hold it is the plume's leading edge, where it is still one thing in one place.

**Nutrients enter at the barn and the outfall.** A dairy basin's wastewater carries phosphorus and nitrogen that a field can use and a stream cannot. Recovered at the barn as a slow-release mineral, the nutrient becomes fertility with a receipt; released at the outfall, it becomes an algae bloom with a cost.

A basin, read this way, is a ledger of entrances. Name each door, name what comes through it, and put the works at the door.

## The Pumped Loop

*The water and the work* says every unit of water carries an energy shadow. In a pumped basin the shadow is the whole picture.

Every foot the water table drops adds a foot of lift to every gallon that follows, and the bill compounds for as long as the pumping lasts. An aquifer's decline reads, in the electric meter, as a rising price on the same water. This is why **reclaimed water is the cheapest headwater a dry town has**: it has already been lifted once, cleaned once, and delivered once, and every reuse spends only the treatment and none of the lift.

A surface-water pipeline is a loop with a long tail. Water is lifted from a reservoir, carried over a caprock, treated, delivered, used, and returned. What the works removes from imported water -- the salts, the metals, the trace chemistry -- is the loop's ash, and the ash needs a home. A deep well is one answer. A salt yard that sells what it evaporates is another. Reinjection to the aquifer the water came from is a third. The honest plant **names its residual before it is built**, because a residual that goes into the next basin down has moved a harm rather than ended it, and that is the custody-first principle meeting hydrology.

So the order the tree already keeps -- water first, energy second, yield third -- gains one clause in a pumped basin: the residual is named before the plant.

## Keeping Books on a Basin

The tree keeps three habits in code, and each one reads as basin discipline the moment a works or a well field adopts it.

**A bound on everything.** A basin declares its budget by reach: this much withdrawn, this much consumed, this much returned, at this quality, at each door, stated before the season starts. A budget declared in advance is a promise a downstream neighbour can hold.

**A receipt for everything.** A withdrawal, a return, and a laboratory reading at each door are facts somebody signs at the moment they are made, appended to a log that only grows, with corrections that point at what they correct. The five primitives that carry a payment in this tree -- a keypair, a signed event, an append-only log, a pure fold, a capability -- carry a door reading unchanged.

**A fold anyone can recompute.** The state of the basin is a pure fold over that log, reach by reach: what entered, what left, and what each door read. A skeptic downstream needs neither the works' software nor its permission to check that the water leaving the top of the basin is cleaner than the water that came in.

The outfall that is a headwater signs what leaves it every day. That single habit, kept for a decade, is the difference between a river that is trusted and a river that is tested.

## Where This Meets the Money

Public money builds works, and Civic Style asks what the money rewards. A pipeline rewards miles laid. A treatment plant rewards gallons treated. A grant rewards a purchase. None of these, alone, rewards the basin clean.

The instruments that do are plain. **Pay on the reading at the door:** tons of salt kept out of the river, mass of chemistry captured at the plume front, acre-feet returned to the aquifer with a quality receipt attached. **Fund the verifier as load-bearing,** an independent laboratory with no stake in the reading, budgeted so that it holds through a lean year. **Prefer the long contract to the one-year line,** because a twenty-year purchase of treated water rewards durability while an annual grant rewards a ribbon-cutting. And keep the smaller currency turning inside the basin -- grower, dairy, works, and utility in one circle -- so the value the water carries stays where the water is.

## What This Refuses

An honest vision names its limits, and this one has three.

**It picks no technology.** Membranes, wells, salt yards, and wetlands each earn their place on a basin's own readings. The document names the door and the receipt, and leaves the machine to the engineers.

**Its figures are the basin's own.** A cubic foot per second at a spring, a plume four miles long, a return of four million gallons a day -- each belongs to one place, dated, in a study filed under that place's name. This page carries the shape and none of the numbers.

**It seats no program anywhere.** A high plain where three rivers rise as dry draws, where a base leaked foam into the water table, where a city's return runs perennial into a canyon, and where a pipeline over the caprock is waiting on its treatment plant, is exactly where the rule shows itself in daylight -- and that is a place to *read*, in its own study, rather than a program this page proposes.

## Why It Belongs Here

Because *ahimsa*, followed far enough upstream, reaches the well.

A river kept clean at its doors, a plant that names its ash before it pours a footing, a city whose return is a headwater worth the name, and a ledger of entrances anyone can fold -- that is *do no harm* expressed as a basin. It is the same discipline this project keeps in code, meeting the water where it enters.

## Gratitude

*The water and the work* thanks the three teachers of the nexus by name, and this page adds its own, by role, in the room kept for thanks: the river-authority engineers who put seven shallow wells at a salt spring and quietly sweetened a reservoir a hundred miles away; the survey geologists who mapped which creek carries the salt and how much; the water-district researchers who measured what a fibre crop drinks on a high plain; and the dairy families who opened their land so a plume could be measured where it moves. Their work enters here siloed, in this tree's own words; their names stand in the study that carries the numbers.

*May every river know its doors. May the works stand where the load enters. May the water a city returns be a headwater worth the name, and may the receipt at the outfall be one a skeptic can fold.*
