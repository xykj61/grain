## 3. Lens reading -- what each figure actually claims, and where it stands

- **Tesla.** Told the AIEE in May 1891 that electricity and ether phenomena are one; wrote in
  "Man's Greatest Achievement" (drafted 1907, published later in the Milwaukee Sentinel) that all
  perceptible matter comes from a primary substance filling all space, the akasha or luminiferous
  ether, acted on by prana -- Vedic terms he took from his 1896 acquaintance with Vivekananda
  (LinkedIn/Kak; Swarajya; academia.edu reads, 2026-08-26). His engineering (polyphase AC,
  induction motor, resonant RF) is foundational and fully mainstream; his substantial-ether
  ontology was set aside by physics after Michelson-Morley (1887) and special relativity (1905),
  and he declined relativity to the end.
- **Steinmetz.** GE's chief consulting engineer; the phasor method and the law of hysteresis are
  his, and both are textbook. His "Four Lectures on Relativity and Space" (McGraw-Hill, 1923)
  accepted relativity and treated the electromagnetic field as a condition of space itself. The
  aether-revival tradition claims him chiefly through his transient-phenomena work and his
  field-first teaching that energy travels in the field around the conductor -- a claim mainstream
  physics agrees with (Poynting flux, 1884). He is the bridge figure: entirely mainstream, and
  field-first in every line of his pedagogy.
- **Eric Dollard.** Living researcher, self-positioned heir of Tesla and Steinmetz; four-quadrant
  theory and versor algebra for AC; dielectricity and magnetism as two aspects of one aether;
  claims longitudinal magneto-dielectric waves propagating faster than light ("Theory of
  Anti-Relativity"; versoralgebra.com; gestaltreality.com reads, 2026-08-26). Publishes through
  A&P Electronic Media and Borderland Sciences rather than peer review. The superluminal claims
  conflict with established electromagnetism and carry no independent verification. The durable
  offering: send readers back to Steinmetz's originals, and take the dielectric field of a
  capacitor as seriously as the magnetic field of a coil.
- **Ken Wheeler.** "Uncovering the Missing Secrets of Magnetism" (2nd ed. 2014, self-published,
  free PDF; archive.org read 2026-08-26). Claims: magnetism is spatial and radiative,
  dielectricity is counterspatial and inertial; field lines are a perceptual artifact; the
  electron as a discrete orbiting particle is a fiction, charge being a field relation; the ether
  is counterspace, named apophatically. He leans openly on Plotinus and the Neoplatonic method.
  Status: fringe, zero peer review, and critics call the terminology idiosyncratic. Two of his
  specific denials run against measurement: charge quantization was measured by Millikan in 1909,
  and single-electron detection is routine laboratory work. The durable offering: the insistence
  on asking what a field IS, ferrocell visualization, and carrying Plotinus into physics talk.
- **Plotinus.** Enneads (3rd century): the One as simple source; emanation that leaves the source
  undiminished (Ennead V.2.1); the intelligible order in which each part carries the whole
  (Ennead V.8.4). This is the monist grammar the tree's topos doc already sings in.
- **Vedic akasha.** First and subtlest gross element, carrier of sound (Samkhya; Bhagavata cantos
  2-3) -- covered in full by the tree's own topos doc, which the drafts cite rather than restate.
- **The mainstream anchor that makes the lens usable.** Einstein's 1920 Leiden address allowed
  that general relativity's spacetime, endowed with physical qualities, may be called an ether,
  with the one caveat that it has no state of motion. And quantum field theory's textbook
  ontology (Weinberg, The Quantum Theory of Fields, 1995) holds that fields are the fundamental
  entities and particles are their quanta. So the field-monist INTUITION lives inside the
  mainstream at the level of ontology; the fringe divergence is in specific testable claims.
