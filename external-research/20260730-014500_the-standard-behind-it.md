## What she says

Every distribution center she walks into is buying robots or budgeting for them -- and almost none can say with confidence whether supervisors execute the standard the same way on second shift as on first. AMR fleets, AI-driven slotting, predictive replenishment: none of it holds if the humans around the automation aren't consistent, because automation still needs to be **fed, checked, and recovered** by people -- *"and 'recovered' is where most networks fall apart."* Her thesis in one line: **automation doesn't fix an execution gap; it makes the gap more expensive when it shows up.** The leaders she respects ask *"what's actually happening on the floor today, and can I see it the same way across every site?"* Her closing question: is your team spending more on the tech stack, or on **the standard behind it**?

## Why this lands squarely on our grain

She has independently written three of this house's laws from the warehouse floor.

**"Recovered" is survivability.** Our v12 crossing said it as: correctness asks *is it right?* -- survivability asks *what happens when it is not, and who is left able to act?* Her recovery gap is that second question wearing a safety vest.

**"See it the same way across every site" is a pure fold.** The only known way for every site to see the same thing is a shared append-only log of signed facts that anyone recomputes -- never a per-site report. Her wish is, precisely, `copy_sameness` for human process: one canonical standard, every site a symlink, drift loud.

**"The standard, not the stack" is values apart.** In most floors the standard lives *tangled inside* the vendor's automation -- reading it requires holding the whole product. Kept apart, the standard becomes its own artifact: versioned, signed, checkable per shift, portable across vendors. That separation is this round's own priority, and it is her point exactly.

## What crosses (the silo)

**The execution record as the product.** Grain's warehouse-shaped offering is not a robot -- it is the standard-behind-it: **Mantra** holding the living standard, **Amber** sealing each shift's execution facts, the fold answering "same on 2nd shift?" with arithmetic instead of confidence. The v12 *record-that-stands-alone* finds its second industry here, which by our own rule is when a concept earns its keep. **Mand's authority-never-subtracted** fits her floor too: the check never hard-stops the supervisor; it makes the supervisor's judgment auditable. And the v19 hardware basket is the small honest end of the same stack -- a rover fed, checked, and *recovered* by a garden allocator whose every bound is named.
