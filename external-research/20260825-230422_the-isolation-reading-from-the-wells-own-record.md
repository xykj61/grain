## What this is

The Brazos study closed on *four readings and one room*. The four readings are taken; this is
the document the room reads. It answers one question from public record: **how isolated is the
authority's proposed injection zone, and what remains to be shown?** It argues from a single
well's sworn record rather than from regional inference, it names what the public record holds
and what it lacks with equal weight, and it supports either verdict honestly. It is a reading
for a table in Clovis. It files nothing, and it has been sent to nobody.

## The one well that reaches the depth

One well inside the authority's 2.5-mile area of review was ever drilled to the proposed depth:
Texas Gulf Producing's **Garrett No. 1** (API 30-009-60003), 1.7 miles from the proposed IW-1
site near Grady, spudded 1962-10-12, plugged dry 1962-12-15 at 7,537 ft on Precambrian basement.
Its Form C-105 well record, sworn 1963-01-10, carries the formation tops -- and those tops are
the isolation argument's spine (OCD well file, read in full 2026-08-25):

## What the sworn record supports

**The separation is real, and now it is counted on a sworn document.** The proposed zone at
~6,270 ft sits **4,630 ft below the base of the red beds** (6,270 minus 1,640) and roughly
5,700 ft below the Ogallala the irrigation wells draw -- the authority's own stated figure, now
consistent with the well record beside it. Between the two stand, in order: the upper Dockum
mudstones, an **840-ft anhydrite section**, and two thousand feet of San Andres-Glorieta-Yeso
dolomites -- the Permian confining stack, named bed by bed in the 1963 record rather than
sketched from a regional map. The state's brackish resource (the Santa Rosa, base 1,640 ft
here) sits wholly above that stack. On separation alone, the isolation reading writes itself.

**The precedent runs a hundred miles north.** CRMWA's Logan injection well (NMED DP-1054) has
taken 49,300 mg/L brine a little over 3,000 ft down since September 2001, permitted to 648,000
gal/day -- a quarter century of at-source injection in this same corner of the state.

## What the public record still owes

**Nobody has published a salinity number for the zone.** The USGS produced-waters database
(v3.0, Dec 2023; 10,927 New Mexico samples) holds **zero** samples from Curry County -- checked
by county field and by bounding box. The Garrett file names three drill-stem tests and records
no recoveries. The state's brackish assessments stop at the Ogallala here. The nearest analogs
cut both ways: Roosevelt County San Andres waters run 9,775 to 382,198 mg/L, while one
shallower Quay County Yeso sample reads 7,663 mg/L -- **below** the 10,000 mg/L line that
separates a protected aquifer from a lawful disposal zone. And the authority's own Q&A asserts
"very poor-quality brine... in the proposed injection zone" with no number and no citation
behind the sentence.

**The old borehole is itself a question for the room.** Garrett No. 1 was plugged in 1962 with
three straddling cement plugs and a bridge plug, and its casing to 2,660 ft was left at the
landowner's request for a future water well. A basement-deep borehole 1.7 miles from the
injection site, sealed to 1962's standard, is the area of review's one deep penetration -- both
the record's best gift and its most concrete conduit question. (A small accuracy note the room
should carry: the Q&A counts four plugs below the casing shoe; the C-103 shows three below and
one at it.)

## The reading, stated for the room

Three sentences carry the verdict shape.

1. **Separation is shown.** On the sworn record, the zone sits under an evaporite-and-dolomite
   stack thousands of feet thick, far beneath both the Ogallala and the state's brackish
   horizon -- the geometry the study asked for is on file.
2. **Salinity is owed.** The >10,000 mg/L reading that makes the zone a lawful home exists
   nowhere in public record; the permit's own test-well-first structure is exactly the
   instrument that produces it, and the room should ask that the test well's water analysis be
   published, not merely filed.
3. **The study's ordering stands until that number exists.** Residual named first, the salt
   yard preferred, the deep well earning its place by measurement -- this document changes
   none of that; it shows the measurement is one authorized test away.
