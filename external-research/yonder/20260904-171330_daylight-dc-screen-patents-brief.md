## The question

Daylight Computer (a public benefit corporation) ships the DC-1 tablet with a fast reflective
daylight-readable display -- marketed around a paper-like sunlight-first screen at video frame
rates. The maintainer reads on one and prefers it to emissive glass. The research question, in
one line: **what intellectual property actually covers that display, who manufactures the panel
itself, and could a Grain open-hardware device -- a Grainphone, a Mantrapod, or a candidate not
yet named -- lawfully source the same or equivalent panels?**

## What the round would establish, in order

1. **Patents.** Search USPTO and Google Patents for assignee "Daylight Computer" (and founders'
   names as inventors), plus continuation chains. Separate what covers the *panel* from what
   covers *system integration* (backlight-free driving, frontlight, firmware) -- a company often
   holds the integration while the panel is a merchant part.
2. **Trademarks.** TESS search for the marketing names (the "Live Paper" family and any sibling
   marks) -- trademarks bound what we may *call* a screen, never what we may build.
3. **The actual panel.** Identify the manufacturer and part family behind the DC-1 display --
   reflective/transflective LCD lineages (RLCD) have known makers; a teardown citation or FCC
   filing usually names the module. A merchant panel with a datasheet is a sourcing door;
   a custom-fabbed exclusive is a wall.
4. **Prior art and expiries.** Reflective LCD patents from the elder generations may already be
   expired -- an expired patent is a recipe in the commons.

## Bounds and custody

- Reading public filings is study; the gratitude-licenses clean-room discipline governs any
