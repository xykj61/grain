## What this is

The Brazos study closed on one sentence: *four readings and one room -- then the authority, the
state, the base, and the dairies at one table before the state rules on the residual.* This note
takes the four readings from public record, dated 20260825, and adds the fifth the study implied:
whether the state has ruled. It has, as of this date, ruled on nothing -- the window stands open.

## Reading 0 -- the docket, and the open window

**No NMED decision on the deep-well injection application exists in any public source as of
2026-08-25.** The authority's board heard on 2026-07-23 that a decision was expected "sometime in
August" (Eastern New Mexico News, 2026-07-29). NMED's news releases (latest 2026-08-24) and
public notices carry nothing on it. The authority's own site (self-dated 2026-08-21) posts
nothing, and lists its next board meeting for 2026-09-17. **The treatment plant itself remains
unfunded.** The pipeline money is complete -- $77M federal (May 2026), $18.7M state, an $8.5M
local match (June 2026), and a $66M Raw Water 1A contract awarded 2026-07-23. The board
chairman said in July the board "can now concentrate on finding the money to build a water
treatment plant." Both facts together are the strategy's timing. The room can still convene before the
ruling. A campus reading arrives while the authority is actively hunting plant money.

## Reading 1 -- the isolation, writable on the authority's own numbers

The authority's proposed well IW-1 (northern Curry County, near Grady) names an injection zone at
about 6,270 ft below surface. That sits **5,720 ft below the base of the Ogallala** the
irrigation wells draw from. Precambrian basement was hit at 7,537 ft in the Garrett No. 1 well,
1.7 miles away (authority's own Q&A, posted ~March 2026). The state's brackish resource near Clovis is the
Dockum/Santa Rosa section, whose base sits on the order of 1,500-2,000 ft. So the injection
zone stands roughly 4,300+ ft below even the deepest state-brackish target, across the Permian
confining section. The well needs a **UIC Class I (non-hazardous)** permit. New Mexico itself issues that class
(primacy since 1983-07-11, NMED Ground Water Quality Bureau). It would be **Curry County's
first**. The Logan precedent the study leaned on reads sharper now. CRMWA's one injection well (NMED
DP-1054) has run since September 2001 at ~125 gpm. It is permitted to 648,000 gal/day, taking
49,300 mg/L brine a little over 3,000 ft down. **The written isolation reading is assemblable.** Two site numbers finish it: injection-zone
TDS above 10,000 mg/L (so the zone is no underground source of drinking water), and
confining-layer continuity from the Garrett No. 1 log rather than regional inference. The study's salt-yard-preferred stance can now be argued with
depths instead of caution.

## Reading 2 -- the reuse permit, undemanding for a fiber crop

New Mexico irrigation reuse runs through an NMED Ground Water Quality Bureau discharge permit
(20.6.2 NMAC) read with the 2007 reclaimed-water guidance. A non-food fiber crop sits in the undemanding classes. Class 1B asks BOD5 and TSS 30/45 mg/L
with fecal 100/200 per 100 mL -- levels any modern plant clears. The loading cap is **200 lb
total nitrogen per acre per year**, spray setbacks to dwellings run 100 ft, and the permit
enforces the groundwater standards (TDS 1,000, nitrate-N 10, chloride 250 mg/L). The permit clock is short. Completeness takes 15 days, public comment 30, and the decision
comes 30 days after the record closes, for a term of five to seven years. On the Texas side, Lubbock's land system holds Water Right 3985 for up to 18,430 acre-ft/yr on
up to 10,000 acres. Chapter 210 Type II (BOD5 20 mg/L, fecal 200/800) covers non-food
irrigation there. Neither
state's hemp program names irrigation-water source in its rules as searched. The regulatory door
the study called "reuse-grade water to a fibre field" is, on these parameters, the easy door.

## Reading 3 -- the nutrient anchor is the cheese plant, and the digester chair is empty

The barn-door arithmetic inverted cleanly: **Southwest Cheese alone roughly equals the whole
county herd's wastewater stream.** The plant (the largest cheese plant in the US) holds NMED permit DP-1508 for up to 2.7 million
gal/day of process and RO wastewater, with 1.9 MGD actual as of 2020. Its own $25M train -- a
15-million-gallon covered anaerobic lagoon, activated sludge, a clarifier -- feeds center-pivot
land application on up to 1,016 adjacent acres. It recovers about 1.2 million gal/day **from the milk itself**. It calls itself a net water
generator of 900,000 gal/day. Around it stand 73,000 milk cows on 22 Curry County dairies (2024). Their permitted parlor
flows bound county-wide dairy wastewater at roughly 1.5-2.9 MGD, all lagoon-and-pivot, none
municipal. And the digester chair is empty. **EPA's AgSTAR database lists zero operational livestock
digesters in the whole state.** The 2019 Dominion-Vanguard entry that named the Clovis dairies
never verifiably built a farm. The nearest operating dairy digester is at Friona, Texas, thirty
miles east. The PFAS wall now has doors in it too. Four NMED-dairy remediation agreements were signed
2026-03-12, the Air Force funds off-base testing since May 2026, and a $12M waterline
appropriation stands. The sleeve's "digester-and-nutrient plant at the barn door" has a named
anchor tenant candidate, a named vacuum, and a named precedent thirty miles away.

## Reading 4 -- the haul, and what it does to the fiber lead

Clovis to the Wichita Falls gin is ~309 road miles. At August 2026 published rates (DAT flatbed $2.72/mi linehaul, ~$0.40-0.50/mi fuel), a baled
load quotes near **$1,000-1,200 all-in**. At 17-22 tons a load, that reads **$50-60 per
delivered ton, ~$0.17 per ton-mile**. Set against USDA's stalk-value references ($60-125/metric ton at the conservative end),
**freight consumes a third to all of farmgate value**. Clovis sits at or past the economic haul
radius for raw hemp stalk unless the buyer pays delivered premiums or arranges freight. The gin's own sourcing map
says the same thing: its contracted growers cluster in North Texas and southern Oklahoma, with
farther partnerships only in Kansas and Colorado. It runs ~17,000 acres in 2026, pays Pay-to-Grow farmers three times with free seed, and buys
spot straw from anyone. It publishes no freight term. **Whether the program carries freight is
the one question a phone call answers and no search can.** The reading strengthens the study's residue-first lead. Cotton and sorghum residue already
lying within the county carries no such haul. The hemp lap belongs on reclaimed water only
where delivered economics are someone else's problem.

## What narrows, strategy-wise

1. **The window is real and open.** The room -- authority, state, base, dairies -- can still meet
   before the residual ruling, and the ruling could land any day. Convening it is a human hand.
2. **The isolation reading is now a drafting task, not a research task.** The authority's own
   numbers carry it; two site figures (injection-zone TDS, confining continuity from the Garrett
   log) complete a document that supports either verdict honestly.
3. **The campus pitch gains a funding face.** The plant is the unfunded piece exactly when the
   Strategic Water Supply fund ($75M, brackish-only, HB 137) and the beneficial-reuse argument
   (Reading 2's easy permit) are live -- a campus that helps fund the plant knocks at an open door.
4. **The anchor conversation is with one company, not twenty-two farms.** Southwest Cheese holds
   the permit, the lagoon, the acres, and the water surplus; the dairies join through the four
   remediation agreements already signed.
5. **The fiber lap stays residue-first**, and the single Panda question -- freight terms -- gates
   whether hemp enters lap one at all.

## What this does not do

It contacts nobody, proposes no document to any agency, and moves no money. Every reading is
public record with its date and source in the round's session log; the four hands named in the
study stay the hands that act.
