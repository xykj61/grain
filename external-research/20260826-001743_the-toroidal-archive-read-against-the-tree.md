## Per-Document Reads and Critique

The register here is warm and the verdicts are plain. Agreement is given where it is
honest, and a compliment stays separate from a verdict.

### The two January whitepapers (silicon torus hardware; toroidal information geometry)

The strong parts still stand, and the tree's existing silos already state them well.
Uniform topology, bounded known distances, memory beside compute, and
coordinate-as-address are geometry and engineering. They survive the removal of every
contested sentence around them. The natural-gradient-on-a-compact-domain technique is
real mathematics and a genuinely TAME-shaped optimizer.

Where I disagree, constructively. First, the aether register is more than optional
decoration. In the hardware paper it does argumentative work (signal "reinforcement,"
propagation claims) that the geometry alone leaves unsupported. The better shape today
is the one the tree's silo already took: lead with the geometry, file the field theory
as the author's inspiration. Second, the hardware paper's roadmap (a monolithic 3D
silicon torus prototyped in months 1-6) was never credible as scheduled engineering.
The better shape is the rehearsal pattern: prove the abstraction in software now, and
let fabrication remain the far horizon it is. Third, the ML paper's composite multipliers
are arithmetic on estimates about unbuilt silicon. The honest use of that paper is the
optimizer, and the tree's silo drew exactly that line. One idea sat outside both silos
and now has a draft: the bound carried in the shape, with the wrap-versus-assert correction.

### The Grain Point whitepaper

The finest of the old set. The tree's `the-state-at-center.md` silo already keeps
its heart: one transition function, state at the center, allocate-once, flow rather than
objects. Three critiques with today's better shape. **Wrap as safety:** the claim that
toroidal wrapping eliminates buffer overflow describes masked faults. The better rule is
wrap-where-periodic, assert-where-linear (now in `draft-the-bound-in-the-shape.md`).
**The fixed-point argument:** Brouwer genuinely does not apply to the torus, and the
essay's example (a rotation has no fixed point) is true. Yet "the fixed point is the
topological root of memory fragmentation" is an image rather than a mechanism.
Fragmentation comes from allocation patterns, and static allocation removes it on any
topology. Pleasant surprise from checking rather than assuming: the antipodal claim (any
continuous scalar field on the torus has a pair of opposite points sharing a value) is
actually true, by a simple sign-flip argument. The cited theorem's proper home is
the sphere. The mirror-pair redundancy image is filed as vision in the wafer draft.
**The graincard** (code shaped 128x64 to match the geometry) is charming and idle. The
tree's 100-column rule stands on typography, which is the honest reason.

Where agreement is honest: the primitives-plus-fuser reduction is right and TAME-shaped.

operator's time. Its strongest line was always the physical custody story, and the

Where I disagree with the original: prescribing a nine-section article with couplet
bookends is production direction rather than design. Grounding the technical case in a
specific foreign toolchain dated it immediately. The draft keeps the debt and the
witness, and leaves the register with its author.
