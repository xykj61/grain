## A receipt, in one line

Kyri 6 is a small formula this tree already seats in [`../../../context/CHEMICAL_FORMULAS.md`](../../../context/CHEMICAL_FORMULAS.md):

```
digest + stamp + kind + subject  ->  receipt
```

A digest is the short name the bytes compute. A resin is the bytes a vessel carries. A stamp is the one-clock time the note was written. Kind and subject say what sort of note it is and what it is about. A session log wears the receipt's stamp, kind, and subject. Its `file` lines name paths, not a digest. That path list is the useful compression. You can ignore the mood of a paragraph and still learn which files the day moved.

## Why the old notes are a question

## What the branching-log talk is allowed to teach

In the summer of 2026, Ram Alagappan (University of Illinois, the Distributed and Storage Systems Laboratory) spoke at a TigerBeetle gathering in the Systems Distributed conference series. The recording is `https://youtu.be/nxTPMqaSkWc`. The title on that recording is about forkable shared logs and letting agents work on live data. This page's account comes from a Gemini viewing summary supplied with the recording link. The talk is not yet a file in `gratitude/`. The numbers below are the talk's reported results, read on `20261003` from that summary. They are not measurements on this pier.

The talk's situation is easy to recognize. A single linear log was built for programs that do the same thing every time. An agent explores, guesses, and tries several paths. Four troubles follow.

An unsafe write lands in the history everyone else reads. The log has no gate that refuses a bad fact before it becomes the shared past.

One line cannot hold several futures. An explorer wants to try more than one next step.

A realistic test wants real data, and writing the test into the live log can break every reader that trusted the log.

A heavy reader, scanning and rewinding, can slow the writers who are doing the day's real work.

The proposed abstraction makes a branch a first-class act. One kind of branch splits off and stops seeing the parent's new facts. Another kind keeps seeing the parent's new facts as they arrive, while the branch's own writes stay private until someone checks them and merges, or throws the branch away. The implementation described beside it keeps the durable bytes in an object store, points a child at the parent's index instead of copying that index, and updates the child lazily when the child is read. The talk reports a branch created in about 10 microseconds, and a parent carrying more than 1,000 live children of the second kind.

Set that design against this tree's own laws.

The insight we keep is small and already native. A reader may look at a history without writing into it. A private trail may follow new facts from a parent and keep its own notes off that parent until a hand says the notes may join. That shape will matter later, when many hands share one Mantra stream and a Mycelium room has to let an explorer try a future. It is a study until that build is booked. A suggested concept name, also unseated, is **private trail**: a reading that follows the living log and stores its own tail apart from the parent.

The implementation we leave on the study shelf is the rest. Session logs are files in the tree, testimony with stamps, not a streaming bus and not an object-store service. TAME puts safety before performance and bounds every collection. A diskless store, a lazy index, and an unbounded table of branches are a different architecture with a different failure story. An agent must not squash history. Mitra prep writes a row. Shed and deep debride wait for the maintainer's word, and a deep debride rewrites every hash. One writer per checkout already refuses two hands in one tree. Custody gates already refuse a publish, a force-push, and a key. The crawl runs while the fleet is quiet so a heavy read does not land in a lap that is trying to commit.

Copying the talk's microsecond figure onto a Grain page would dress their bench as ours. The figure stays here, with its date and its source, and it does not become a fascia number.
