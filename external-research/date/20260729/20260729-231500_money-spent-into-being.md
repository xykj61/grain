## The five load-bearing claims

**Taxes drive money.** A unit gains demand because the issuer requires it in payment of an obligation. The tax is not revenue the issuer needs; it is what makes the token wanted.

**The issuer spends money into existence and taxes it out** -- Wray's "creationism versus redemptionism." Supply at any moment is an accounting identity: everything issued minus everything redeemed.

**Functional finance** (Lerner, via Wray): issue to achieve the named public purpose, never to hit an arbitrary balance or an arbitrary quantity. Soundness is the wrong test; function is the test.

**The anchor is a price, not a quantity.** The buffer-stock insight: the issuer fixes the price of one real thing (in the ELR/Job Guarantee, an hour of labor at the program wage) and lets quantity float. Price stability comes from the fixed point, and full employment enhances it rather than fighting it.

**Inflation is demand exceeding real capacity**, and real-world hyperinflations trace to supply collapse -- wars, embargoes, institutional destruction -- rather than issuance per se. So the discipline is a capacity question and a measurement question, not a cap.
