## The finding, first

**The farthest upstream source of the Brazos is the head of Blackwater Draw, a dry channel about eleven miles southwest of Clovis, New Mexico, and the water-treatment facility that belongs at that source is already designed, sited, and waiting on its money.** The Eastern New Mexico Rural Water System will lift Canadian River water from Ute Reservoir, carry it over the caprock, and treat it on the plain where the Brazos's upper draws begin. Its pipelines are funded through the last large-diameter segment; its treatment plant is the next thing the authority must pay for; and the plant's residual -- what it removes from the water, planned for deep-well injection -- is the open question before the state this summer. The optimisation this study offers is a Civic reading rather than an engineering drawing: **name the residual first, build the plant as a campus at the basin's doors, and let a fibre field on reclaimed water be the beneficial use that makes reuse water worth its permit.**

On the fibre, the ground answers before the market does. The high plain sits on the semi-arid row of the foundation's own table, so the honest first material is the stalk residue already lying in the fields. Hemp earns a trial on reclaimed water only, textile flax stays off this ground, and the neighbours who grow the majority of the country's organic cotton on rain alone teach the corridor its bale-level receipt. The nearest decortication line is the largest in the hemisphere, three hundred road miles east at Wichita Falls, which turns the mill's first lap into contract acres before any building.

## 2. The aquifer under the plain

The plain's real headwater is a well. The Ogallala under eastern New Mexico and the South Plains is pumped faster than it refills; the regional water authority was formed in 2010 because the aquifer showed depletion, and the federal climate assessment's reading is that the aquifer should be treated as non-renewable (senator's office, 2020). The plain receives 350-550 mm of rain a year against 1,500-1,750 mm of evaporative demand (university, 2024), which is the arithmetic that puts every fibre choice below on reclaimed water or on residue.

## 3. The works at the source

**What is already true.** The Eastern New Mexico Water Utility Authority holds an allocation of up to 16,415 acre-feet a year from Ute Reservoir on the Canadian, a compact right owned by the state and worked with the Bureau of Reclamation since federal authorisation in 2009, with a federal share of up to 75%. The system runs about 130 miles of pipe to Clovis, Cannon Air Force Base, Portales, Texico, and Elida. In May 2026 the bureau awarded USD 77 million, bringing recent funding to USD 95.7 million and completing the last 16.3 miles of large-diameter raw-water pipe; in July 2026 the board approved the bid package for that final segment and said the next step is the money for the treatment plant. Delivery is promised within five years, and the system's own completion date reads 2031. The plant stands about fifteen pipeline-miles from the northwest corner of the base, on the plain where the Brazos's upper draws begin, with a 21.7-mile raw-water line running north from it to the caprock.

**What is open.** The plant's plan includes deep-well injection of the contaminants it removes, including heavy metals, and that plan is pending before the state environment department, with an update expected in August 2026 (local paper, 2026-07-01). The pipelines were funded first; the residual is being decided last. *Where the water enters* names this exactly: a works whose residual reads *open* after the pipelines are funded has been built from the wrong end, and the honest move is to name the residual now.

**The reading, in the Civic way.** Three homes for the residual, read against the custody-first principle:

| Residual home | What it rewards | What it costs | Custody reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| deep-well injection | the lowest capital and operating line | a permit, a well, and trust that the formation stays sealed | passes only if the receiving formation is isolated from the Ogallala *and* from the brackish aquifer the state's own 2025 act now funds treating -- the study asks for that isolation reading in writing |
| a salt yard (evaporation with salt recovery, zero liquid discharge) | a product sold from what the plant removes; the regional plan already imagines salt products from Salt Fork brine | energy for evaporation, land, and a buyer for the salt | passes: the residual becomes a receipt and leaves no basin holding it |
| reinjection to the source aquifer with a quality receipt | the loop closed where it opened | the tightest treatment of the three | passes where the quality reading meets the aquifer's own |

Counsel reads the second as the one the tree's own principles prefer and the first as the one the money prefers, and names both plainly so the place can decide with the bill in view.

**The campus.** The plant stands at one door. Three more doors sit within a few miles of it, each with its own load, its own public funder, and its own product, and a plant built as a campus treats them together on one site with one laboratory and one ledger.

## 4. The city's return as the river's first mile

Two hundred draw-miles below the plant, Lubbock shows what a return does when it is treated as a source. The Jim Bertram lakes turned an intermittent draw into a perennial stream by discharging reclaimed water at its head (journal, 2021). The city holds a water right to land-apply up to 18,430 acre-feet a year on 10,000 acres east and southeast of town, irrigating about 5,000 of them under 31 centre pivots from a 412-million-gallon reservoir, growing wheat, bermuda, and rye; it may also send up to 9 million gallons a day down the North Fork. And in January 2026 the council carried a permit to divert up to 50,000 acre-feet a year from a new Lake 7 on that same reclaimed flow -- the return becoming supply (permit 5921, council record, 2026-01-27).

One line in the city's own code is the Civic finding of this section: the land-application system "is designed for inefficiency in order to ensure that the greatest volume of wastewater possible can be disposed of" (ordinance, 2024). The permit rewards *disposal by the acre-foot*. A fibre field on the same water rewards *a product by the acre-foot*, and the difference between those two rewards is the whole reason a corridor belongs beside a works. Lubbock's northwest plant, master-planned from 3 to 15 million gallons a day with a 30-million-gallon expansion in design and a phosphorus goal of 0.1 mg/L, is the second-newest headwater on the Brazos and the cleanest.

**What the trials say.** Texas Tech and Texas A&M AgriLife have grown fibre hemp at Lubbock since 2020 on dryland, 6-inch, and 12-inch seasonal irrigation, funded by the High Plains water district. Early planting at higher seeding density read as the water-efficient practice; establishment in hot soils was the hard part, with an 80%-germination seed lot giving stands under 25% in a June planting; and the Chinese fibre lines that grew best (Jinma, Yuma) tested above 0.3% THC while two Italian lines (Eletta Campana, Fibranova) stayed under (university, 2022-2024). A fee-based variety trial for fibre and grain runs at Plainview under irrigation from 2025. Read the Civic way: the federal definition that now protects industrial hemp by name also measures *total* THC, so on this ground **the cultivar is a licence question before it is a yield question**, and the trial plot chooses the line.

**The neighbours who grow on rain.** Around Lubbock, a cooperative of roughly 35-40 family farms plants 17-20,000 acres of organic and transitional cotton a year and markets 7,000-17,000 bales, the majority of the organic cotton grown in the country, much of it with limited or no irrigation, and tracks every bale from the field to the customer by the farmer's name (cooperative, undated pages). This tree keeps cotton off its own list of fibre metaphors because a crop that drains an aquifer and dyes a river fails the water column; a dryland organic bale grown on rain and classed by the government is a different reading, and it is named here as a **teacher rather than a metaphor**: the bale-level receipt those growers keep is exactly the receipt this corridor wants, and they have kept it since 1993. The corridor's own fibre stays hemp and linen.

## 6. Retting on a dry plain

Dew is scarce on the Llano, and its rain comes in two short seasons, May-June and August-September. Field retting is possible after an August cut into the fall rains, and the timer already in the tree logs whether it worked. The largest domestic line answers the question a different way: it decorticates field-dried straw mechanically and cottonises the fibre with no water retting at all, marking every bale with its field, farmer, genetics, and the rain the field received (processor, 2026). ## 10. Hemp or linen?

Neither leads. **Residue leads, and hemp on reclaimed water follows as a trial.** Fibre flax wants a cool damp spring and is pulled before summer heat; on this plain a March sowing meets heat by June and a fall sowing meets a hard freeze, so linen enters here only as cloth the brand buys from a maritime corridor. Hemp enters on the city's return and nowhere else, with the cultivar chosen for its licence reading before its yield, and the corridor's name keeps both fibres in it on purpose, because the brand wears both.

## 11. The brand, read by role

## 13. Base rates, before the story

Most rural water systems of this size take two decades from authorisation to delivery, and this one was authorised in 2009 and promises water by 2031. Most at-source salt works are small and cheap beside the intake plants they spare, and the region's one running example cost the federal side about USD 3.7 million and the cities up to USD 5 million. Most first hemp fields on a new ground miss their planning yield in year one, and the plain's trials say establishment is where they miss it. None of this is a reason to wait; all of it is a reason to name the residual first, contract the acres before any line, and put the laboratory ahead of the prose.
