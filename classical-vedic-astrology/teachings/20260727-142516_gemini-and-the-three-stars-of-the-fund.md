## One Sign, Two Frames, Three Stars

The house method reads the sky twice and lets both readings be true: **tropical signs** for the seasons' geometry, **sidereal nakshatras** for the stars themselves. Between them stands the ayanamsa -- measured near **24 deg** in our own transit study -- and the arithmetic is simple enough to do aloud: tropical Gemini spans 60 deg to 90 deg; subtract the ayanamsa and the same arc lies over sidereal 36 deg to 66 deg, which is exactly **the last portion of Krittika, all of Rohini, and most of Mrigashira**. So when the Gemini fund walks with three nakshatras, that is not poetry layered on top of astronomy -- it is the two frames agreeing about one sky, the same reconciliation the nodes taught us in July.

## The Three Lords, Named Rightly

Classically, each of the three stars has its presiding deity, and a teaching essay owes the classical names before any devotional weaving: **Krittika belongs to Agni**, the fire that purifies and cooks -- the star of the razor's decisiveness and the flame that consecrates beginnings. **Rohini belongs to Prajapati, the Brahma** -- the growing, the fertile, the beloved of the Moon; the star of things that ripen. **Mrigashira belongs to Soma**, the moon-nectar -- the searching deer's head, the star of the quest that gives Gemini's span its curiosity. And in this house's own devotional frame -- offered as bhakti, not imposed as doctrine -- these lords are honored as aspects within one wholeness: the sustaining face called **Vishnu, Krishna, Hari** with **Lakshmi, Sri** beside; the dissolving face called **Shiva, Rudra** with **Kali, Sati, Parvati** beside; and the creative word itself, **Brahma with Sarasvati** -- she whose name this project's earliest voice once carried. A fund named for service walking with fire, ripening, and the quest: the curriculum writes itself.

## What the Gemini Seat Learns From Its Stars

Three lessons, one per star, in the plainest words. From Agni's Krittika: **cut cleanly and consecrate what you keep** -- a fund that cannot say no cannot bless its yes. From Prajapati's Rohini: **tend what is planted until it actually ripens** -- patient capital is Rohini's whole personality. From Soma's Mrigashira: **stay a seeker** -- the deer's head is always turned toward the next question, which is the right posture for the wheel's most communicative seat.

## A Gentle Aside for the Sorting of Houses

Popular stories sort temperaments too, and one beloved school's houses rhyme with the elements our wheel already speaks: the eagle-marked house of wit and air reads like the air signs' own common room -- Gemini among them -- while the lion-marked house burns fire-red and gold, and the serpent-marked house keeps to earthy green. It is a resonance offered with a smile, honored by name in the gratitude below, and held no tighter than a good aside should be.
