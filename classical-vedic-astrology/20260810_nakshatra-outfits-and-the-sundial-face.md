## Sundial has two faces

Sundial is one **indicator** -- a bounded set of readings shown at a glance, like the model or effort marks a baton carries. It wears two faces:

- **Public face -- the health of the day.** Module readings (each witness green or red) -> green of total, the health percent, the fascia. This ships in the seed (`sundial/sundial.rye`), clean and generic.
- **Nakshatra face -- the outfit of the point.** *Which of the 27 nakshatras a point wears* as its **d27 outfit**. Same mechanism, a different roster. The roster is below; a pilot's own outfit is theirs to record privately.

The `council_sky` is a **d27** (`comlink/topology.rye`): fifteen galaxies, three stars each, nine planets each, a galaxy leading a circle of twenty-seven -- one seat per nakshatra. Sundial is the indicator that reads which nakshatra a point wears.

## The source, and the clean-room discipline

The roster is the **Taittiriya Brahmana nakshatra sukta** -- the ancient Vedic hymn naming the twenty-seven lunar mansions and their presiding deities. The text is public-domain (millennia old); the **names and deities are public facts**, listed below. Any *rendering* of the hymn's verses that lives here must be the project's **own Radiant translation, authored clean-room from the public-domain original** -- never a reproduction of a copyrighted modern translation. The glosses below are one-line essences in Riyo's own words, not translated verses.

## The twenty-seven outfits (name  -  deity  -  a one-line essence, our own)

**The count starts at Krittika and closes at Bharani** -- corrected `20260823.201533` on Keaton's word,
and it is a factual repair rather than a preference. The roster this note cites is the **Taittiriya
Brahmana nakshatra sukta**, and that hymn enumerates the lunar mansions beginning with Krittika. The
Ashvini-first order familiar from modern almanacs is the later convention, so a table claiming the
Taittiriya source while counting from Ashvini named one source and followed another. The names,
deities, and glosses are unchanged; only the seat numbers move, by a rotation of two.

The two **water seats** -- Purva Ashadha (Apas) and Shatabhishak (Varuna) -- are the ones pinned publicly, because they belong to the waters that **Basin** serves. When the d27 sky grows its outfit theme, Sundial reads which of these twenty-seven a point wears.

## A point's outfit

A pilot's point wears its own nakshatra as its d27 outfit, from its own chart. That reading -- which nakshatra, for which point -- is the pilot's own, recorded in their private identity record, never computed into the public seed, and never carried by Sundial's public health face.
