## What this is

**Constel** is the tree's own answer to elder Urbit's fake-galaxy dev networks — a way to stand up many local piers on one machine and let them meet over Comlink, none of them a real point on the live network. Where Urbit dev nets ran fake galaxies for local testing, Constel runs fake piers with a stronger guarantee: **a Constel name is structurally incapable of being a real `@p`**, so no dev command a newcomer copies could ever reach a stranger's ship.

That guarantee is one clean property, checkable at a glance. Every one of Urbit's 512 real syllables — the 256 three-letter prefixes and 256 three-letter suffixes that compose every galaxy, star, planet, moon, and comet — carries exactly one vowel, and `y` never appears in the table at all. So a name that carries **no vowel** — abjad, Hebrew-style — can never be assembled from real syllables, and therefore can never parse as a real address. The missing vowel *is* the whole safety proof, and it fails closed in a single bounded pass. This is stronger and simpler than counting segment lengths against the syllable table (the `~acme-…` length trick the placeholder law uses for docs illustrations); Constel names are the *runnable* fake piers, and their guarantee is the missing vowel.

Everything here is purely **local** — a string predicate and a bounded in-memory registry on the bench, siloed to `constel/`, run from inside the jailed pier. No network, no keys, no funds, no real address ever formed. The local handshake and the real Comlink transport cross the Comlink seam and stay their own later round; the rungs below give that round names and a constellation already proven safe to greet across.

## The rungs

- **`name.rye` — the name that can never be a real ship (FORA0).** The naming law and the name primitive. `check_ship` / `is_valid_ship` prove a single fake ship lawful (bounded to `max_ship_len`, drawn only from the consonant-and-digit alphabet, vowel-free); `never_a_ship` states the safety invariant positively; `check_constellation` proves a bounded hyphen-joined run of fake piers; and `generate(index)` draws a distinct vowel-free name per `u32` index — a two-letter `xn` silo prefix over a base-21 consonant palette. Every real `@p` example (`zod` · `sarlev` · `sampel` · `palnet` · `sampel-palnet`) refuses `VowelPresent` by name; 512 generated names are deterministic, collision-free, and vowel-free by construction. Witness: `tools/fora_name_witness.rish`.
- **`roster.rye` — a bounded registry of fake piers (FORA1).** The roster every handshake leans on first: who is in the constellation. A `Roster` holds up to `max_piers` (= `name.max_ships`, eight) distinct fake piers, each a `Pier` carrying its `u32` draw index and generated name. Membership is held as an **invariant, not a hope** — a pier enters only through `generate` + `never_a_ship` (asserted on every join), a duplicate name refuses `DuplicatePier`, a ninth pier refuses `RosterFull`, and the hyphen-joined `render` always passes `check_constellation` (`joined_bytes` refusing `ConstellationTooLong` before a join could overflow the byte bound). The API seats `init` · `join` · `stand_up` (join `0…n-1` deterministically) · `find` · `contains` · `member` · `render`. An empty roster reads and renders by name, `stand_up` draws a deterministic constellation, lookup finds the present and refuses the absent, and the full roster renders a lawful whole with no vowel end to end. Witness: `tools/fora_roster_witness.rish`.
- **`handshake.rye` — two piers greet, proven pure (FORA2).** The one thing every real multi-pier sync leans on: **agreement before exchange.** Two piers confirm they are distinct members of the *same* constellation before a single payload byte moves. Three pure steps, each an immutable value the next validates — `open` (the initiator offers its name and constellation), `accept` (the responder confirms same-net membership and answers), `finish` (the initiator confirms the ack — a `Session` `COMPLETE` only when both are distinct never-a-ship members of the one sky) — plus `is_member`, an exact segment scan that names a whole member and refuses a mere substring. A clean handshake completes end to end; a different constellation refuses `ConstellationMismatch`, a non-member refuses `NotAMember`, a self-greet refuses `SameParty`, and a vowel-bearing (real-`@p`-shaped) party refuses `VowelPresent` at open and at accept — the naming law's safety reaching all the way into the greeting. Witness: `tools/fora_handshake_witness.rish`.
- **`wire.rye` — the handshake, framed for a local wire (FORA3).** The three proven values carried across a self-describing frame so a real multi-pier sync can move them — **verify-before-trust**, exactly as DREY1 proved the Mikrophone's committed payload before any real wire. One tagged layout is the single source of truth: `magic` (`CNST`) · `version` · `kind` (Greeting · Ack · Session) · `payload_len` · a Sha256 `digest` over the payload · the payload itself. `frame_greeting` / `frame_ack` / `frame_session` serialize each value's own length-prefixed fields; `deframe_*` prove the frame whole and lawful before handing back a value. Deframing is **two independent gates** — the Sha256 proves the payload *whole* (no byte flipped), and the naming law re-run at the border proves it *lawful* (no vowel-bearing name smuggled in). A short, bad-magic, bad-version, bad-kind, overlong, truncated, tampered, wrong-kind, or field-overrun frame each refuses by name; and — the FORA-specific proof — a vowel-bearing (real-`@p`-shaped) name smuggled into a payload *whose digest was fixed up* still refuses `VowelPresent`, so integrity alone can never carry a real address across a Constel channel. Witness: `tools/fora_wire_witness.rish`.
- **`exchange.rye` — the handshake, carried end to end across frames (FORA4).** The rung that turns three proven values and a proven frame into a proven *conversation*: **two piers reach a COMPLETE `Session` communicating only through frames** — bytes — never once sharing an in-memory value. This is the real pier boundary. A `Party` is a pier's own local view (its name and the constellation it claims, a non-member refused `NotAMember` at construction); the initiator never sees the responder's `Ack` value, only its frame, and the responder never sees the initiator's `Greeting` value, only its frame. Three crossings — `initiator_greets` · `responder_answers` · `initiator_seals` — pass only `wire.Frame` bytes, and `exchange` drives all three and reads the sealed `Session` back *from the final frame*, so even the completed value crossed the wire. A clean exchange completes end to end; a mismatched sky refuses `ConstellationMismatch`, a non-member `NotAMember`, a self-greet `SameParty`, and a tampered greeting frame (a smuggled vowel with a fixed-up digest) refuses `VowelPresent` at the responder's border — the naming law guarding every crossing, so a real-`@p`-shaped party can never enter the exchange even over the wire. Witness: `tools/fora_exchange_witness.rish`.
- **`channel.rye` — a bounded byte channel, one frame read at a time (FORA5).** The last purely-local rung before the socket, and the one that isolates the single hard thing a real transport adds: a **byte stream**. Bytes arrive continuously, a frame may split across two arrivals, and two frames may sit back to back. A `Channel` is a bounded in-process byte FIFO — `push` appends (refusing `ChannelFull` rather than overwriting), and `read_frame` cuts **exactly one whole frame** out of the stream: a header that has not fully arrived, a payload tail that has not arrived, and a wild declared length each fail closed (`NeedMoreBytes` · `BadFrame`) *without moving the cursor*, so a caller retries after more bytes. The channel's only job is delimiting — it never re-checks a frame, so the two FORA3 trust gates (Sha256 integrity + the naming-law border at `wire.deframe`) stay the only trust boundary. A whole exchange runs over two channels to a COMPLETE `Session`; a partial frame waits then completes on its last byte; two back-to-back frames read one at a time, exact and in order; a wild length refuses `BadFrame`; an overfull channel refuses `ChannelFull`; and a frame read cleanly off the stream but carrying a smuggled vowel still refuses `VowelPresent` at the border. The socket is this same channel with a kernel file descriptor standing in for the buffer. Witness: `tools/fora_channel_witness.rish`.
- **`switchboard.rye` — a whole local constellation, routed by name (FORA6).** Every rung before this joined exactly two piers; yet *Constel* is named for a **constellation**, many piers. A `Switchboard` is a bounded set of per-pier byte queues — one `channel.Channel` mailbox per member of an FORA1 roster — the first rung that lets three or more fake piers meet through **one shared medium**. Its one new concern is the one a real network of many piers forces: **route a frame to the right pier by name.** `deliver(to, frame)` pushes a frame's bytes onto a pier's inbound mailbox and `receive(from)` reads exactly one whole frame off a pier's own mailbox; a name no roster holds refuses `NoSuchPier` before a byte moves. The switchboard only routes — it never re-checks a frame — so the two FORA3 trust gates (Sha256 + the naming-law border) stay the only trust boundary. A constellation of three stands up with three empty mailboxes; two members reach a COMPLETE `Session` through the switchboard as their only shared medium; the bystander's mailbox never holds a byte; a stranger's name refuses `NoSuchPier`; a pier reads only its own mail (a frame for one pier is absent from the others); and a greeting routed through a mailbox but carrying a smuggled vowel still refuses `VowelPresent` at the border. The real Comlink socket is this same switchboard with each mailbox backed by a kernel file descriptor. Witness: `tools/fora_switchboard_witness.rish`.
- **`gossip.rye` — one frame reaches the whole constellation at once (FORA7).** The switchboard routes to *one* named pier; yet the shape a real network leans on for a shared fact — a member joining, a name claimed, a block proposed — is **one-to-many**. `broadcast(sb, from, frame)` fans a single frame out to every member **except** the announcer, refusing `NoSuchPier` before any mailbox is touched if the announcer is not a member, and returning the count reached (`size - 1` on a clean fan-out). Each recipient reads its own copy with the ordinary `receive`, so a broadcast is **many honest deliveries** — the two FORA3 gates (Sha256 + the naming-law border) still run per recipient, never shortcut. A broadcast in a sky of four lands one whole frame in each of the other three mailboxes and never the announcer's own; each recipient reads its copy byte-for-byte, independently; a broadcast of two degenerates cleanly to a single delivery; a stranger's announce refuses `NoSuchPier` with no partial fan-out; and a broadcast frame carrying a smuggled vowel is delivered to every mailbox yet each recipient's `wire.deframe` still refuses `VowelPresent`. This is the primitive every gossip and flood protocol stands on — the honest bridge toward the constel consensus track. Witness: `tools/fora_gossip_witness.rish`.
- **`census.rye` — announce, collect, and know who is present (FORA8).** An announce is only half a round; a real protocol needs the other half — **collect the replies and know who answered.** A heartbeat, a roll call, a quorum vote all share one shape. `announce` broadcasts the caller's greeting (the ping); `respond` has a member drain the ping it heard (proving it was reachable) and reply its own greeting as a "present" token; `hear` reads one reply off the caller's mailbox at the naming-law border and returns the responder's slot; and `take_census` **interleaves** respond and hear — so the caller's inbox holds one reply at a time and a full constellation of eight censuses without a `ChannelFull` — tallying a fixed `[max_piers]bool` by slot. A full census in a sky of four sees exactly the three other members present (the caller never itself); a **silent member is detected by slot** (the tally counts one fewer and the absent slot stays `false` — *which* pier is silent, not only *how many*); a reply proves the ear that heard it (an un-announced member refuses `NeedMoreBytes`); a vowel-bearing reply refuses `VowelPresent` at the caller's border; and a stranger cannot open a census (`NoSuchPier` before any announce). This closes the gossip round — the liveness primitive every consensus stands on. Witness: `tools/fora_census_witness.rish`.
- **`quorum.rye` — from a census, decide whether a majority may act (FORA9).** A census counts who is present; it does not yet *decide*. Every consensus — a leader election, a block commit, a name claimed once across the whole sky — leans first on one bounded question: do enough piers agree that the constellation may act at all? `majority_of(n)` is the strict-majority threshold (`n/2 + 1`), and its one asserted property is the whole safety of a quorum — `2·threshold > n`, so any two quorums of the same constellation must **intersect** in at least one pier and can never each decide a conflicting value. A `Quorum` records each heard reply by slot (refusing `DoubleCount`), `seal`s into a live count (the caller plus every responder), a threshold, and `reached`; `require` refuses `NoQuorum` below a majority; and `in_quorum(slot)` names *which* piers form the deciding set, not merely how many. A full sky of four is quorate (live four, threshold three); one silent member still holds a majority (live three, the silent slot outside the deciding set, yet the sky may act); two silent lose it (`NoQuorum`); two of three is a majority yet a lone pier never is (`NoQuorum`); a stranger cannot open a quorum (`NoSuchPier`); the naming-law border still guards every vote (a vowel-bearing reply refuses `VowelPresent` at `hear`); and a full constellation of eight stays bounded (live eight, threshold five, no `ChannelFull`, because the round interleaves respond and hear). Witness: `tools/fora_quorum_witness.rish`.
- **`elect.rye` — from a quorum, elect one leader (FORA10).** A quorum knows the sky *may* act; it does not yet name **what** the sky acts on. This rung spends the quorum's intersection property on the first real decision every consensus makes: a single leader, chosen once. A `Ballot` records one vote per roster slot (`vote`, refusing `DoubleVote` — a pier votes exactly once); `tally(candidate)` counts a candidate's votes; and `elect` returns the candidate a strict majority named, else refuses `NoLeader`. The crux is not the counting but the **safety carried over from the quorum**: the seal asserts that **no second candidate also reached the majority** — because any two majorities intersect and a pier votes once, at most one candidate can ever win, so an elected leader is unique. `run_election(sb, choices)` drives a real round over the switchboard — announce, then each member's `respond`+`hear` proves its right to vote at the naming-law border before its own honest choice is recorded. A clear majority elects (three of four name slot 1); a split refuses `NoLeader` (two and two, a minority never leads); **uniqueness holds across every arrangement of single votes** in a sky of four (checked directly); a unanimous sky elects unanimously and a lone vote never does; `DoubleVote` refuses a second cast; a stranger cannot open an election (`NoSuchPier`); the border still guards every vote (`VowelPresent` at `hear`); and a full constellation of eight elects bounded (no `ChannelFull`, the round interleaves respond and hear). Witness: `tools/fora_elect_witness.rish`.
- **`decree.rye` — from an election, commit one value (FORA11).** An election decides *who* may propose; a decree decides *what* the sky holds. Every replicated ledger — a block committed, a balance moved, a name bound once across the whole constellation — leans on one value being committed **once**, so no two piers ever hold a different value for the same decision. A `Decree` records each member's `accept` of the proposed value (refusing `DoubleAccept` on a second cast and `WrongValue` on an off-value accept — a decree gathers support for a single value, never a mixture); `support` counts the accepts; and `commit` returns the value iff a strict majority accepted it, else refuses `NoDecree`. The seal asserts the safety: every recorded accept is for the one proposed value, so a second value has **zero support by construction** and two values can never both commit — the ledger's consistency resting on the same intersection that made the leader unique. `run_decree(sb, leader, value)` drives a real round over the switchboard — announce, then each member's `respond`+`hear` proves its right to accept at the naming-law border. A strict majority commits (three of four accept 7); below it refuses `NoDecree` (a minority never decides); only the proposed value can commit (an off-value accept refuses `WrongValue` and is never tallied); a unanimous sky commits and a lone accept never does; `DoubleAccept` refuses a second cast; a stranger cannot open a decree (`NoSuchPier`); the border still guards every accept (`VowelPresent` at `hear`); and a full constellation of eight commits bounded (no `ChannelFull`, the round interleaves respond and hear). Witness: `tools/fora_decree_witness.rish`.
- **`log.rye` — a sequence of decrees the whole sky agrees on, in order (FORA12).** A ledger is not one decision but a **sequence** of them, in order — the output every consensus protocol exists to produce: entry 0, then 1, then 2, each committed by quorum and the same at every pier. A `Log` is a bounded, append-only `[max_entries]u32`: `append(value)` places a committed value at the next index (refusing `LogFull` at the cap), `get(index)` reads a committed entry (refusing `NoEntry` past the prefix), and `matches(other)` proves two logs identical entry for entry. `run_and_append(sb, leader, value)` runs one FORA11 decree and appends **only on a clean commit** — any error (`NoDecree`, `NoSuchPier`, `VowelPresent`) propagates and leaves the log unchanged, so it never carries a gap or an uncommitted value. The safety carries up from FORA11 by construction: because each index commits **at most one value**, two logs replicating the same committed decrees are identical — **no fork is possible.** `append` grows in order and reads back, bounded; a real sky commits a sequence (`11, 22, 33`) in exact order; two logs driven through the same sequence match while a log one entry short honestly does not; a failed decree (a stranger leader, `NoSuchPier`) does not grow the log; and a full constellation of eight appends bounded. Append-only, exactly Mantra's own order. Witness: `tools/fora_log_witness.rish`.
- **`reconfig.rye` — change the constellation safely, through joint consensus (FORA13).** Every rung from FORA9 on reads the roster size *once* and treats it as fixed — so FORA12's log can never fork only as long as the membership never changes. Yet a real network gains and loses piers, and reconfiguration is the single hardest move any consensus protocol makes: during a switch from an old config `C_old` to a new one `C_new`, two **disjoint** groups — one still believing the old membership, one already believing the new — could each form a lawful majority and commit a *conflicting* value, a fork born of the change itself. **Joint consensus** closes it with one bounded rule that spends the FORA9 intersection lemma directly: a transition decision requires a strict majority in **both** `C_old` and `C_new`. Because any two strict majorities of a config intersect, a joint decision overlaps any pure-old and any pure-new decision, so the reconfiguration **inherits** the no-fork guarantee. A `Config` is a bounded membership mask plus its population (`EmptyConfig` refused at construction — a majority of nobody is undefined); a `JointBallot` records each participant's single yes-vote (`DoubleVote` on a second cast, `NotInEitherConfig` from a slot in neither config) and `seal`s a decision that reaches only with a strict majority in each; `run_reconfig` drives a real round over the switchboard, every union member responding+hearing at the naming-law border before its vote. A transition with a majority in both configs commits; an old-only (`{0,1}`) or new-only (`{2,3}`) majority refuses `NoJointMajority`, holding the split-brain door shut from each side; and — the FORA13 proof, enumerated directly as FORA10 checked leader uniqueness — every joint-majority yes-set intersects every pure-old majority inside the old config and every pure-new majority inside the new, so **no fork across the switch is possible**, shown exhaustively rather than argued. The border still refuses a vowel-bearing reply (`VowelPresent` at `hear`), and a full transition over eight stays bounded. Witness: `tools/fora_reconfig_witness.rish`.

- **`snapshot.rye` — compact the log so it can serve forever (FORA14).** FORA12 built the append-only log every consensus exists to produce; FORA13 let the sky change its own stars. Both leave one honest bound: the log is a fixed `[max_entries]u32` that refuses `LogFull` at the cap. A ledger that can only ever hold sixty-four decisions is a demonstration, not a ledger — a real replicated log runs for years and must **compact**, folding its settled history into a small summary and reclaiming the room, or it stops. The state a log accumulates is a **deterministic, order-sensitive fold** over its committed values (`state_n = state_{n-1} *% fold_mul +% value_n`, wrapping) — order-sensitive on purpose, so the same values in a different order fold to a different state and a snapshot preserves the log's *ordering*, not merely its multiset. A `Snapshot` is `(count, state)`; because two piers that committed the same prefix apply the same values in the same order (FORA12: each index commits at most one value), **their snapshots are identical** — the no-fork guarantee carries straight into compaction rather than being re-argued. A `CompactLog` keeps a snapshot plus a bounded live `tail`: `total()` is `snap.count + tail_len` (which climbs past the tail cap as compaction reclaims), `append` grows the tail (`TailFull` at the cap), `compact` folds the whole tail into the snapshot in order and empties it, `get` reads an effective index (refusing `Compacted` for a folded-away index, `NoEntry` past the end), and `equivalent` judges two logs as ledgers by their committed history regardless of when each reclaimed its tail. The selftest proves: the fold is order-sensitive; append/get bounded by `TailFull` and `NoEntry`; `compact` preserves `total()` and `effective_state()` and turns a folded index into `Compacted`; **no fork across compaction** — an eager log and a lazy log fed the same committed sequence are equivalent while a different sequence honestly is not; compaction carries the effective log past its original bound (twice `max_tail`, tail still bounded); and over a real sky `run_and_append` commits a sequence in order past the cap, a stranger leader refusing `NoSuchPier` and leaving the log untouched. Witness: `tools/fora_snapshot_witness.rish`.

- **`catchup.rye` — a lagging pier brought current from a leader's snapshot + tail (FORA15).** FORA14 lets the log serve forever by compacting; the reason a real system compacts at all is the pier that **fell behind** — offline, or joined late — now holding fewer entries than the leader. A follower lacks the effective entries `[follower.total(), leader.total())`, and the leader can *serve* an index only at or past its snapshot boundary, so the follower's position chooses the method. **Tail catch-up** (`follower.total() >= leader.snap.count`): every lacked entry is still live in the leader's tail; the leader serves them in order, the follower appends each, auto-compacting its own tail so it stays bounded however far behind it was — and because the overlap is verifiable, catch-up **verifies before it appends**, refusing `Diverged` on a real disagreement and changing nothing. **Snapshot install** (`follower.total() < leader.snap.count`): the lacked entries are already compacted on the leader with nothing to replay, so the follower adopts the leader's snapshot **wholesale** — its count and order-sensitive state — empties its tail, and takes the leader's whole live tail; this is Raft's own InstallSnapshot, resting on FORA14's proof that two piers committing the same prefix hold the *same* snapshot. Every non-refusing `catch_up` ends with `follower.equivalent(leader)` asserted. The selftest proves: tail catch-up appends the missing entries and reads them back; snapshot install reaches a follower far below a compacted leader's boundary; an already-current follower returns zero untouched; a divergent overlap refuses `Diverged` unchanged (the check is real, not a constant success); a longer follower refuses `Ahead`; **no fork across catch-up** — two followers caught up from one leader are equivalent to each other and to the leader, and a caught-up follower advances with the sky and stays equivalent; the follower's tail stays bounded catching up past `max_tail`; and a real decree-built sky drives it end to end. Witness: `tools/fora_catchup_witness.rish`.

- **`term.rye` — leadership made safe across re-election: an old leader can't commit (FORA16).** Every rung from FORA10 on assumes **one stable leader**. Yet a real network re-elects — a leader crashes, a partition heals, a new leader rises — and the single hardest safety question is what happens to the **old** leader, the one superseded yet still believing it leads and trying to commit a value the sky has moved past, forking the ledger in *time*. A **term** is a `u32` that only ever climbs; every pier tracks the highest it has seen, a leader is elected *for* a term, and a decree carries *its* term. The whole safety is one refusal: a voter whose current term **exceeds** a decree's term refuses `StaleTerm` — it has moved to a newer leader and will not lend its vote to an older one; a voter at or below adopts the term (monotonically) and accepts as in FORA11. The safety follows from FORA9's intersection lemma — once a strict majority has advanced to term `T` (exactly what electing a leader in `T` does), any decree in a term `< T` gathers accepts from at most the un-advanced minority, below the threshold, so it **can never commit**. The old leader is not forbidden by fiat; it is **out-voted by construction**. A `TermTable` is a bounded per-slot term (`observe` advances to the max and never lowers; `at_least(t)` counts the advanced majority); a `TermedDecree` is an FORA11 `Decree` plus its term (`accept` refusing `StaleTerm`, inheriting `DoubleAccept`/`WrongValue`); `run_termed_decree` drives the round over the switchboard, a stale member simply not gathered while a real fault still surfaces. The selftest proves: the table is monotonic; a ground decree commits and its accepters adopt the term; a moved-on voter refuses `StaleTerm` unrecorded; **an old leader can't commit** — proven at the value level and again over a real sky (a term-1 decree commits, a majority advances to term 2, the old term-1 leader gathers only its own vote and refuses `NoDecree` while the new term-2 leader commits); and a full constellation of eight runs bounded. Witness: `tools/fora_term_witness.rish`.

- **`repair.rye` — the leader heals a follower's divergent suffix (FORA17).** FORA15's catch-up heals only a follower that is *behind* with a *matching* prefix — a disagreeing overlap it can only refuse `Diverged`, because a value-log cannot tell a conflict from a lag. FORA16 proved an old leader can never *commit*, yet before it is out-voted it can still have *appended* uncommitted entries to its own log. When such a pier rejoins under a newer leader it carries an **uncommitted divergent suffix**, and this rung heals it — Raft's AppendEntries consistency check and its **Log Matching Property**. The missing ingredient is the per-entry term FORA12's value-log lacked: a `TermedLog` of `(term, value)` entries, terms non-decreasing along the log (a leader appends only in its own term, and terms climb by FORA16). `append_entry` refuses `TermRegressed` on a backward term and `LogFull` at the cap; `consistent_at(match_len, boundary_term)` is the AppendEntries gate a real socket also calls; `agree_upto` finds the highest index where two logs agree; and `repair` truncates the follower's divergent suffix at that point and appends the leader's true entries, ending `follower.matches(leader)`. The **Log Matching Property** — a shared `(index, term)` implies an identical prefix — is proven by enumeration; the heal makes a divergent follower match exactly with the common prefix byte-unchanged; only the uncommitted stale-term tail ever moves (a behind follower repaired by pure append, an already-current one with zero changes, a longer one shed to the leader's length); **no fork across repair** — two followers with different divergent suffixes both converge onto the one leader and thus onto each other; and over a real sky the FORA16 out-voted old leader's uncommitted term-1 scribbles are precisely the tissue repair removes before installing the new term-2 tail. The committed prefix is never rewritten — the only entries repair removes are the tissue FORA16 proved could never have committed. Witness: `tools/fora_repair_witness.rish`.

- **`commit.rye` — a leader commits an old-term entry only through a current-term one (FORA18).** The single most famous safety rule of replicated consensus, the scenario Raft's paper draws as **Figure 8** (§5.4.2): *a leader may not conclude that an entry from a previous term is committed merely because it is stored on a majority.* The intuition "replicated on a majority = committed" is **false for an old-term entry** — a later leader elected on a log that lacked it can still legally overwrite it (FORA17's repair is exactly that mechanism). The safe rule is narrow: a leader advances its commit index over an entry only once it has replicated an entry from its **own current term** on a majority; committing a current-term entry commits every earlier entry beneath it *transitively* by the Log Matching Property (FORA17). A `MatchView` is the leader's bounded record of how far each pier has replicated (`replicated_len(n)` counts piers holding at least `n` agreeing entries, the leader counting itself); `safe_commit_len` is the whole rule — the largest `N` whose top entry carries the current term and whose prefix a majority holds; `naive_commit_len` is the *tempting wrong rule* (majority-replicated, term-blind), kept only so the witness shows, side by side, the entry it would wrongly commit. The selftest proves: `MatchView` counts replicas; a single-term log commits by majority exactly as FORA11 did (a strict generalization, not a regression); **the Figure 8 refusal** — an old-term majority entry is *not* committed by `safe_commit_len` while `naive_commit_len` would commit it, the two rules disagreeing exactly where Raft says they must; **the transitive commit** — once a current-term entry above it reaches a majority, `safe_commit_len` jumps to include both; **the danger made real** — the term-2 entry the naive rule would have committed is then overwritten by FORA17 repair under a new leader whose log lacked it, the exact fork the safe rule prevents by never calling it settled; and the rule runs bounded. Witness: `tools/fora_commit_witness.rish`.
