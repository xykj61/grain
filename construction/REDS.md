## Why this file, and what belongs in it

Own-reds-immediately has been a house law for a long time, and it worked: reds were named the moment they were found, out loud, without excuse. What the law never said is **where**. So the ones that happened to land inside a witness header or a spec erratum survived, and the ones that happened in a sentence did not.

Three fields per row, because a red with fewer teaches nothing:

- **What went wrong** -- stated plainly, no softening.
- **What caught it** -- a compiler, a stopwatch, a guard, a second look. Never "I noticed."
- **What it taught** -- the transferable rule, which is the only part that outlives the incident.

A red enters this ledger when it is found. Rows are never edited or removed; a later correction accretes as a new row pointing back.

## What the pattern says

Read down the *what caught it* column and almost nothing was caught by thinking harder. A compiler, a stopwatch, a file-type check, a guard's first run, seven witnesses at once, a pack catching its own author. **The machine caught nineteen of twenty.**

That is not a complaint about judgment; it is the argument for the whole apparatus. Witnesses exist because the author is the last person able to see their own gap, and this column is twenty lines of evidence for that claim rather than one more assertion of it.

Read down the *what it taught* column and four rules recur: **measure before claiming**, **scope before shipping**, **fixture rather than remember**, and **narrow the objection**. Those four have paid for themselves repeatedly in a single sitting.

**REDS %213 (`20260824.234500`) -- the instruction was to seat 456 labels, and 361 of them would have been false.** *What went wrong:* the invariant meter reported that **456 uncovered asserts already carry an unlabelled comment above them**, and named the remedy as *label-seating rather than reason-writing* -- cheap work, one pass, gap 468 to roughly 12. Keaton gave the word. **Reading the comments before stamping them showed 361 of 377 are not reasons at all.** They are test-case narration inside proof functions: *"VFS may read system paths, not write them"* above `assert(!table.allows("vfs", "/etc", right_read))`; *"The three rye paths in held order"* above `assert(resp.tokens_out == 3)`; *"The dark sheet lowers to Bark paper"* above a colour comparison. Seating `// invariant:` on those would have written **361 false invariants into the tree's most-cited room**, each one telling a later reader that a test's description is a contract's reason. *What caught it:* sampling the data before acting on the instruction, twice -- the first twelve looked wrong, and a second sample spread differently confirmed it rather than explaining it away. *What it taught:* **a count of a SHAPE is not a count of a MEANING.** The meter counted comment lines standing above asserts, which is a shape it can see; whether a comment is *about* the assert beneath it is a meaning only a reader holds. Every earlier correction in this arc moved a number that was wrong; this one would have moved a number that was right and made the tree wrong doing it. **The cheapest-looking sweep was the only one that could have done harm.** *The root, measured:* **361 of the 377 sit inside a `pub fn` that a proof entry in the same file calls** -- `caravan/regions.rye` exports `check_declared_access`, `check_reads`, `check_refusals` for a witness to call, and its own `run_self_test` calls them too. The reachability bin cannot reach them because of the **`pub` guard added at REDS %211**, which exists because a selftest calling the real API is what a selftest is for. That guard was right for `image/photos.rye` and is wrong here, and the two cases differ by something no in-file reading can see: **`crop` is called from other modules, `check_reads` is called from nowhere but its own proof.** *Not fixed, deliberately:* a cross-file reachability pass is the honest discriminator and it is a real change to a meter that has now been corrected nine times, several of which needed a second fix. It waits for a round of its own rather than a fast patch at the end of a long one. **Nothing was stamped.** *Landed instead:* the 16 genuine ones carry `// seam:`, TAME's own word for the inherited-std boundary, above asserts that guard exactly that boundary -- *bufPrint returns usize; it never exceeds the slice it wrote into*.
