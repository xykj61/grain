## Why this file, and what belongs in it

Own-reds-immediately has been a house law for a long time, and it worked: reds were named the moment they were found, out loud, without excuse. What the law never said is **where**. So the ones that happened to land inside a witness header or a spec erratum survived, and the ones that happened in a sentence did not.

Three fields per row, because a red with fewer teaches nothing:

- **What went wrong** -- stated plainly, no softening.
- **What caught it** -- a compiler, a stopwatch, a guard, a second look. Never "I noticed."
- **What it taught** -- the transferable rule, which is the only part that outlives the incident.

A red enters this ledger when it is found. Rows are never edited or removed; a later correction accretes as a new row pointing back.

## What the pattern says

Read down the *what caught it* column and almost nothing was caught by thinking harder. A compiler, a stopwatch, a file-type check, a guard's first run, seven witnesses at once, a pack catching its own author. **The machine caught nineteen of twenty.**

That is not a complaint about judgment; it is the argument for the whole apparatus. Witnesses exist because the author is the last person able to see their own gap, and this column is twenty lines of evidence for that claim rather than one more assertion of it.

Read down the *what it taught* column and four rules recur: **measure before claiming**, **scope before shipping**, **fixture rather than remember**, and **narrow the objection**. Those four have paid for themselves repeatedly in a single sitting.

**REDS %205 (`20260824.183000`) -- the room bound and the pin bound cannot both hold, and no sentence anybody writes can make them.** *What went wrong:* two numbers were seated separately and never checked against each other. A room folds past **256 flat files** (`tools/r/room_bound_witness.rish`), and a living pin stays under **24,576 bytes** (`living_pin_max_bytes`). The index must hold one row per flat file, so at the room's own ceiling the pin must hold 256 rows. **A row costs about 123 bytes before it says anything** -- 21 for the stamp cell, roughly 97 for the title-and-filename link, 5 for the pipes -- so 256 rows need **~31,500 bytes**, above 24,576 with the meaning column entirely empty. The two bounds are incompatible by arithmetic rather than by neglect, and shortening a sentence cannot reach it. *What caught it:* doing the arithmetic while choosing a row bound, rather than picking a round number and shipping. The chain that led here is worth reading: REDS %204 found the pin 5,421 bytes over after a correct fold; Keaton's word was **shorter rows**; and deriving the row bound from the pin's own capacity is what made the second constraint visible. *What it taught:* **a derived bound tests the bounds it is derived from.** Two ceilings agreed with each other for as long as nobody computed the number that has to sit between them, which is the same shape as REDS %199 and %201 -- a constant that stays true until somebody reads it twice -- arriving now between two constants rather than inside one. *Repaired:* the row bound is seated at **192 bytes** and gated by [`../tools/in/index_row_bound_witness.rish`](../tools/in/index_row_bound_witness.rish) over [`../tools/fixtures/index_row_bound_scan.sh`](../tools/fixtures/index_row_bound_scan.sh), proven across seventeen behaviors on planted pins with the bound shown free at exactly 192 and bitten at exactly 193. The pin reads **7,563 bytes** for 36 rows, from 291,781 before the fold and 30,224 after it. **The incompatibility is named rather than papered over**, in the mark law and in this row: 192 bytes carries a **median** day -- 61 logs across 61 measured days, mean 75 -- while **15 of those 61 days ran past the ~116 rows the pin holds**, the peak at **223**. On such a day the remedy is a fold rather than a shorter sentence, and the fold is exactly what the room bound is for. *The choice, taken the same day:* Keaton's word was **raise the pin bound for this one page**. `living_pin_max_bytes[session-logs/README.md] = 57344` -- `256 x 192` rows plus 8,192 for prose -- accreted into the seated law beside the general 24,576, which is unchanged for every other pin. The argument for an exception rather than a new general number is that the two bound different readings: 24,576 is *a pin an agent reads in one breath* and bounds a page read **whole**, while an index is read from the **top**, which is exactly what a lap is instructed to do. [`../tools/fixtures/living_pin_max_bytes.sh`](../tools/fixtures/living_pin_max_bytes.sh) now answers **per page**, so the exception costs no second reading to disagree with the first (REDS %199's own lesson, honoured rather than re-learned), and the five meters that measure a page were each taught to ask about the page rather than about the tree. Proven by seven new checks in [`../tools/fixtures/declared_ceiling_control.sh`](../tools/fixtures/declared_ceiling_control.sh) -- 57 in all -- including both anchoring failures a substring match would have caused: a longer path borrowing a shorter page's bound, and a shorter one borrowing a longer page's.
