## Why this file, and what belongs in it

Own-reds-immediately has been a house law for a long time, and it worked: reds were named the moment they were found, out loud, without excuse. What the law never said is **where**. So the ones that happened to land inside a witness header or a spec erratum survived, and the ones that happened in a sentence did not.

Three fields per row, because a red with fewer teaches nothing:

- **What went wrong** -- stated plainly, no softening.
- **What caught it** -- a compiler, a stopwatch, a guard, a second look. Never "I noticed."
- **What it taught** -- the transferable rule, which is the only part that outlives the incident.

A red enters this ledger when it is found. Rows are never edited or removed; a later correction accretes as a new row pointing back.

## What the pattern says

Read down the *what caught it* column and almost nothing was caught by thinking harder. A compiler, a stopwatch, a file-type check, a guard's first run, seven witnesses at once, a pack catching its own author. **The machine caught nineteen of twenty.**

That is not a complaint about judgment; it is the argument for the whole apparatus. Witnesses exist because the author is the last person able to see their own gap, and this column is twenty lines of evidence for that claim rather than one more assertion of it.

Read down the *what it taught* column and four rules recur: **measure before claiming**, **scope before shipping**, **fixture rather than remember**, and **narrow the objection**. Those four have paid for themselves repeatedly in a single sitting.

**REDS %207 (`20260824.193815`) -- a number reported without its bins meant three things at once, and stood 2.8x the truth for a day.** *What went wrong:* the comment-dial round of `20260824.170904` reported **13,235 asserts with no stated reason** against the seated `// invariant:` law, and that figure was carried into the living card, named to Keaton twice as the standing crux, and used to argue that a wall wanted his word before it could go up. The real figure is **4,757**. Two faults compounded, and neither was an arithmetic slip. **First, three promises shared one denominator.** A **contract** assert inside an ordinary function is what the law is about; a **selftest** assert inside `main` or a `selftest` function has the test's own name as its reason; a **witness** assert inside a `*_witness.rye` has the witness header as its reason. The three sum exactly to the old figure -- `4,757 + 3,944 + 2,568 = 13,235` -- so nothing was ever miscounted, and three different things were added together. **Second, 211 symlinks were counted as modules.** Zig refuses an import that escapes the root file's directory, so a module several rooms need is symlinked into each; `image/photos.rye` is the file and two rooms hold links to it, and counting the links tripled its 185-assert gap to 555. **11% of the 1,891 modules were links.** *What caught it:* binning the reading before acting on it, and then **three identical rows in the worst-ten table** -- 234 contract, 49 covered, 185 gap, three times over. A symlink looks like a coincidence to a counter. *What it taught:* **care about the method does not substitute for care about the population.** The round that produced 13,235 said so explicitly in its own log -- *counted directly, walking up from each assert rather than dividing two line counts* -- and that sentence was true, careful, and beside the point, because the question it never asked was *what am I counting over*. This is the denominator lesson of `20260824.170904` arriving at the numerator: **a share is a claim about its denominator, and a count is a claim about its population.** *Repaired:* [`../tools/fixtures/invariant_coverage_scan.sh`](../tools/fixtures/invariant_coverage_scan.sh) bins the three promises, skips symlinks, and reads in two seconds.
