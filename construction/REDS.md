## Why this file, and what belongs in it

Own-reds-immediately has been a house law for a long time, and it worked: reds were named the moment they were found, out loud, without excuse. What the law never said is **where**. So the ones that happened to land inside a witness header or a spec erratum survived, and the ones that happened in a sentence did not.

Three fields per row, because a red with fewer teaches nothing:

- **What went wrong** -- stated plainly, no softening.
- **What caught it** -- a compiler, a stopwatch, a guard, a second look. Never "I noticed."
- **What it taught** -- the transferable rule, which is the only part that outlives the incident.

A red enters this ledger when it is found. Rows are never edited or removed; a later correction accretes as a new row pointing back.

## What the pattern says

Read down the *what caught it* column and almost nothing was caught by thinking harder. A compiler, a stopwatch, a file-type check, a guard's first run, seven witnesses at once, a pack catching its own author. **The machine caught nineteen of twenty.**

That is not a complaint about judgment; it is the argument for the whole apparatus. Witnesses exist because the author is the last person able to see their own gap, and this column is twenty lines of evidence for that claim rather than one more assertion of it.

Read down the *what it taught* column and four rules recur: **measure before claiming**, **scope before shipping**, **fixture rather than remember**, and **narrow the objection**. Those four have paid for themselves repeatedly in a single sitting.

**REDS %175 (`20260823.204456`) -- 237 dated logs were classified as living, and open for writing.** *What went wrong:* the tree's canonical dated/living test, `DATED_RE='(^|/)[0-9]{8}-[0-9]{6}_'` in `tools/fixtures/dated_classify_seam.sh`, requires an underscore after the stamp. The session-logs law makes the sprig **optional** -- *when two logs share a second, add `_short-sprig`* -- so **237 tracked logs are named `YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.ext` with no underscore at all**, and every one of them read as **living**. `tools/fixtures/dated_path_repoint_scan.sh` builds its writable set from that same test, so a repointer whose header promises *a dated basename is testimony and is never opened for writing here* had 237 pieces of testimony in its write set. *What caught it:* a hand-written sweep in this same lap, using the same too-narrow pattern from memory, rewrote two of them -- `20260619-214612.md` and `20260621-065312.md` -- while repointing a molted foundation. Both reverted with `git checkout` before the commit. **The slip and the canon shared one error, which is why the slip was worth reading rather than merely fixing.** *What it taught:* **a rule with an optional part is a rule two readers will implement differently**, and the reader most likely to get it wrong is the one working from memory. The law says the sprig is added on collision; every regex written since has treated it as mandatory. *Repaired:* the seam, the repointer, and `tools/fixtures/readme_reach_scan.sh` now accept `_` **or** `.` after the stamp; `dated_path_repoint_witness` is GREEN with dated testimony untouched and idempotence held. *Open, and named:* **eight more scans carry the same narrow pattern** -- `one_clock_shape`, `dated_path`, `tracked_link`, `exec_bit`, `room_bound`, `prose_register`, `seed_link`, `tool_path_repoint`. None of them writes, so a miscount is the whole exposure there rather than a rewrite, and widening them is its own lap with its own re-measurement of every ceiling they hold.
