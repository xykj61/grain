**REDS %258 (`20260826.075023`) -- the witness the one-clock law calls blocking stood on no roster, and ran on no lap.** *What went wrong:* `.claude/rules/session-logs.md` names `tools/o/one_clock_witness.rish` as the law's guard and calls its mode **blocking**; `context/specs/20260722-125845_edt-framework-host-convention.md` says the same. It was absent from all sixty-nine rows of `construction/standing-equipment.kyri`, so no lap ran it -- cold, hot, or cadence. That is why the clock half of %250 went unseen on this pier for a day and had to be reported by the macOS bench, which ran the witness by hand. *What caught it:* grepping the roster for `one_clock` while checking which guards this round's edits would move, and finding nothing. *What it taught:* **a doc calling a guard blocking is a claim about a schedule, and the schedule lives in the roster.** REDS %219 named this class for a choir standing off the roster -- a refusal nobody receives -- and this is the same fault reaching the guard for the tree's most-cited law. The stronger reading is that the two are one obligation: a repair to a guard is finished when the guard runs, so a lap that repairs one asks where it is scheduled. *Repaired:* `one_clock` seated on the every-lap tier at `20260826.075023`, roster **69 -> 70**, `guards_path_missing=0`. *Surfaced, not crossed:* whether every doc-declared blocking guard is rostered is a census this round did not take, and it is one grep away for the lap that wants it.

**REDS %259 (`20260826.083012`) -- a guard pinned a constant one paragraph after quoting the rule against pinning constants.** *What went wrong:* the `prove-date` leg added this round counts `PROV_OK` lines from `tools/fixtures/one_clock_provenance_scan.sh` on two dialects, and I taught `tools/s/shell_dialect_witness.rish` to assert `living_bsd_shaped=2`. The provenance scan weighs every dated artifact the working tree **and index** hold, so pinning the base at `HEAD` kept the branch's merged history out and did nothing about the round's own staged files. The witness read 2 and passed on the clean tree, then read **3** and refused on the staged one -- the moment the round staged its own session log. The elder `prove-portable` leg beside it says the answer in its own comment: *"Comparing readings rather than pinning a constant keeps both halves true as the tree grows."* I copied its shape and left out its lesson. *What caught it:* the hot roster, run after `git add` exactly as REDS %174 asks, on the tree the commit was about to ship. The cold roster could never have caught it -- the fault only exists once an index is dirty, which is the state `--hot` was built to measure. *What it taught:* **a leg that compares two readings must assert the comparison, never either reading.** The equality is the property under test and the count is an accident of the day; asserting the count turns a guard into a file somebody edits every round, and a guard people edit to make green is a guard that has stopped refusing. The scan already refuses under `host_changed_the_reading` when the dialects disagree, so exit status carried the equality the whole time and the witness needed only to read it. *Repaired:* the constant is gone; the witness asserts `dated.ok`, `planted_elder_bsd_shaped=0`, and `verdict=ok`, and the scan's comment now says plainly that the base pin bounds the history rather than the number. Proven on metal from the refusing side as well: run under a host whose `date` refuses `-d` outright, the leg answers `verdict=subject_reads_nothing` and exits 1 rather than passing on a comparison of zero against zero. **CLOSED.**
