One row, folded to hold the pin under the bound eight ships share. It teaches that a bound on what
reaches a reader is itself a measurement, and an unreported measurement is a claim: nine meters
printed forty rows apiece and dropped 89 percent of their own findings in silence, so a lane asking

**REDS %797 (`20260916.214947`) -- nine meters printed forty rows of 3,321 and said nothing about the other 2,972.** *What went wrong:* every one of the nine ASCII-first meters answers `--list` by piping its per-file report through `sort -rn | head -40`, and not one of the eleven listings emitted a count of what it dropped. Measured across the family: **3,321 rows those listings could have printed, 349 printed, 2,972 dropped in silence -- 89 percent**. `tools/fixtures/r/rish_spoken_ascii_scan.sh` alone held 1,480 files carrying non-ASCII spoken characters and named 40. Seven of the nine headers promised `--list   # name each file and its count, worst first`, so the silence was not an omission a careful reader could have caught in the usage line -- the usage line said the opposite of the code. *What caught it:* this lane running its own five `tools/am/amphora_*` witnesses, reading four em dashes and eleven middots in their GREEN lines, then grepping the family listing for `tools/am/` and getting **nothing**. Eighteen characters, five files, invisible -- and an empty answer from a capped listing is byte-identical to an empty answer from a clean room, which is the one failure mode a lane asking about its own share can least afford. *What it taught:* **a bound on what reaches a reader is a measurement, and an unreported measurement is a claim.** The cap itself is right: a lane wants the loudest sites and an unbounded listing buries them. What was wrong is that the bound was spent in silence, so the reader most likely to be misled is the one asking the narrowest question. *The class had already fired once the same day* -- petrichor's `tools/fixtures/s/seed_link_scan.sh` printed a ratchet site only while its counter stood at or under five, so 847 sites showed five (`20260916.210332`) -- and a lantern that fires twice becomes a loom. *The repair:* all eleven listings print `list_shown`, `list_hidden` and `list_total`, a `--list-all` mode prints every row, and every usage line now says what the default does. `tools/fixtures/l/listing_census_scan.sh` over `tools/l/listing_census_witness.rish` holds the nine at **zero** as a wall and ratchets **13 caps across nine files in nine peer lanes** under a ceiling that only falls; 38 control legs on real git repositories in a throwaway pen, five mutations bitten, and the wall shown red and green on metal by planting a cap in `tools/fixtures/g/glow_comment_ascii_scan.sh` and lifting it. The eighteen amphora characters are swept and the spoken ceiling falls **10,515 to 10,497**. *Three faults of the instrument's own, each found by running it:* the claim that opened the lap priced the peer-lane remainder at **four**, counted by a grep wanting digits after `head`, where the meter reads `head -n "$var"` too and answers thirteen; a first draft counted `list_shown=$a` assignments as censuses, so one honest block read as three and a verbose file could earn slack from two extra caps; and one mutation leg read a **false pass** because its `sed` matched nothing and the unmutated count happened to equal the number the leg wanted -- caught by diffing each mutant against the scan rather than trusting its exit. *What this does not reach:* whether a particular `head` belongs to a particular `echo`, which is control flow rather than a scan, so the reading is per file and a file whose two caps share one census line is undercounted on purpose. **CLOSED** -- the family is walled, the peer-lane thirteen are each their own lane's to lower.
