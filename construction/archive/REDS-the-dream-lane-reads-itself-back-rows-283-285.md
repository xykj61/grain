# REDS %283-%285 -- three reds the Dream pier found by reading its own work back

*Written straight onto this shelf on `20260826.213000`, all three **CLOSED**. They were booked on
the Dream pier as `%278`-`%280` and **re-seated here** when the round rebased onto `xy`, which had
already published `%278`-`%281` from the Sound pier. **The push to `xy` is the allocation**, so the
numbers upstream held stand and these three moved. That is the same resolution `%233`-`%237` took to
`%271`-`%275`, arriving a third time; the door is named on the live card and the seat is Keaton's.*

**What the three teach together.** Each was found by a hand reading back work that had already been
called finished, and none of them could have been found by a guard:

- **%283** -- five witnesses in one chain were red together because two of them grepped a head count
  out of a **prose sentence**, and no roster clock reached any of the five. Found by baselining
  before a rename, which is what a baseline is for.
- **%284** -- a rename sweep could not tell a live reference from a **record of the elder name**, and
  overwrote the one column a fossil row exists to hold. Three counts written mid-lap reproduced under
  no instrument. Found by closing a lap another hand had ended at `git add`.
- **%285** -- the tree's named width lint held a roster and **no body**, so it reported green for its
  whole life while reading nothing. Found by following the loop seed's instruction literally and
  noticing that the silence had the wrong shape.

**The shared shape:** a green is worth what its refusals are worth, and three of these greens had
never refused anything. The habit that found all three is the cheap one -- **read the thing back,
and ask what population the number came from.**

**REDS %283 (`20260826.195014`) -- a count that lived in a sentence, grepped by two witnesses, and five went red where nobody was looking.** *What went wrong:* `tools/g/glow_rune_alphabet_worker.sh` counts the rune head rows and asserts the total, and its witness announced the number in a prose line -- `GREEN: Glow rune alphabet G1+G2 -- 28 heads`. Two other witnesses read that number by grepping the sentence: `glow_digraph_twin_witness.rish` asserted the output `contains "28"` and `glow_barket_tokenize_witness.rish` asserted it `contains "27"`. When the table grew from 27 heads to 28 the sentence was updated and the second grep was not, so tokenize refused -- and `parse` calls tokenize, `lower` calls parse, `horizon` calls lower, so **five witnesses in the barket chain were red together**. *What caught it:* baselining those very witnesses before the rune renames, because a rename promising byte-identical output needs a before-reading and six of eleven had none. Nothing else would have: **not one of the five sits on a roster clock**, so `standing_equipment_run` read 70 of 70 green the same evening while this chain was broken. That is the unreached population `witness_reach` counts at 1,154, biting for the first time in view. *What it taught:* **%277 and this row are one lesson from two ends** -- there, a guard read one file where four existed; here, five guards read nothing at all because no clock reaches them. A guard that cannot be reached cannot go red in time to matter, and its green is a memory rather than a reading (REDS row 59's own words, arriving again). The second fault rode beneath the first: a bare number in prose is a **loose match**, so `contains "28"` would answer yes to a `STOA128` in the same line, and the passing assertion was passing by luck as much as by fact. *Repaired:* the worker publishes `rune_heads=$n` as a named field beside its `OK`, the witness echoes the worker's output so the field reaches a caller, and both readers assert on the field rather than on prose -- tokenize on `rune_heads=` and the digraph twin on `rune_heads=28`. The stale `27 heads` in tokenize's own GREEN line is corrected to 28, since a witness announcing a count it no longer measures is the same fault one layer up. **And a sixth was red for a different reason, found in the same baseline:** `glow_bartis_horizon_witness.rish` named `active-designing/20260720-033340_stoa99-bartis-horizon-wording.md` and the brief had moved into `yonder/`, so the witness refused on a file standing one directory over. That is REDS %231 exactly -- a fold repoints links and leaves a guard's citation, because the citation is code. Repointed with `tools/d/dated_path_resolve.rish` (`verdict=recovered-by-index`) rather than guessed, and GREEN. **Six of eleven rune witnesses were red before this lap touched anything**, which is what a baseline is for. CLOSED on the chain. *Surfaced, not crossed:* none of the six has a roster row, and seating them is its own lap with its own cost -- the chain compiles and runs Glow, so it is minutes rather than seconds.


**REDS %284 (`20260826.201500`) -- the rename sweep overwrote the one column that holds the elder name, and wrote three counts nobody read back.** *What went wrong:* the rune rename ran `bartis -> shop_gate` and `barket -> shop_nest` across the tree by substitution, and the substitution reached `construction/SHRED_PREP.md`'s own Class M row -- the row whose Fossil column exists to record what the module used to be called. It came out reading `glow/lower_shop_gate.rye` in both columns, a fossil row naming the mutant as its own fossil, which is the record erasing itself. In the same block three figures were written from the middle of the lap and none reproduced on the closing tree: *42 living files repointed* measured **46** (19 renamed and repointed, 27 repointed only), *847 substitutions* measured **538**, and *the emitted identifiers stand untouched -- `bartis_gate` 234, `barket_gate` 144, `bartis_pair` 28* measured **45**, **28**, and **18** inside the emitter modules. *What caught it:* closing another hand's unclosed lap. The roster refused to open cold over 19 paths staged and never committed -- `run_verdict=lap_unclosed`, the guard REDS %223 seated -- and claiming them with `--hot` meant reading the whole diff first. Nothing else was looking: every witness the rename touched was already GREEN, because a witness reads code and this fault was in prose about code. *What it taught:* **a bulk rename cannot tell a live reference from a record of the elder name, and the one place the elder name is load-bearing is the ledger row recording the cut.** The sweep is right 538 times and wrong twice, and the two are in the file whose whole job is remembering. Second, **a number written into a ledger before the lap closes is a number nobody has read against the tree it describes** -- the lap that stops at `git add` stops before its own arithmetic is checkable, which is REDS %188, %220, and %223's cost showing up in prose rather than in a stale page. Third, and the one worth carrying: **a tree-wide string count is the wrong instrument for an emitted identifier.** `bartis_pair` lives inside the filenames `glow_bartis_pair_*_witness.rish`, so counting it across the tree mixes code with paths and reported movement where the emitters stood still -- this reading made that mistake first, called it MOVED, and the content diff corrected it. The right instrument was reading the four emitter diffs, which are two lines each: the self-naming header comment and the `@import` path. *Repaired:* the Fossil column restored to `glow/lower_bartis.rye` and `glow/lower_barket.rye`; the three counts replaced with measured ones, each naming the instrument and the closing tree it was measured on; the wrong-instrument note written into the block so the next hand checking a rename does not reach for the count first. Twenty-one witnesses GREEN on metal after the rename -- twelve rune, two nest-type, two tally, five STOA emit. CLOSED.

**REDS %285 (`20260826.211422`) -- the tree's named width lint was fourteen lines and no body, and reported green for its whole life.** *What went wrong:* `tools/w/width-check.rish` held a thirteen-line comment header and one binding -- `let files = [...45 paths...]` -- with no code after it. A `let` with no consumer does nothing, so the tool exited 0 in silence every time it ran. It has been fourteen lines since the root commit `ff870e7825`, and it sits on the standing roster as `guard width_check`, seated `20260620.210812`. So the roster read it green every lap of its life while it read nothing at all. `.claude/rules/tame-guidance.md` names it *"Live lint"*, `CLAUDE.md` names it on the lint surface, and the unattended loop's own seed names it in the sentence *"before any round claims GREEN, run ... `tools/w/width-check.rish`"*. Forty-five module names were added to that list one TAME-tidy round at a time, across nine commits, every one of which left the file at fourteen lines, and not one of those files was ever opened. *What caught it:* following that loop-seed instruction literally while closing another lap. The command printed nothing and exited 0, which for a lint over forty-five modules is the wrong shape of silence. `wc -l` said fourteen. *What it taught:* **a guard on a roster is not a guard that reads; the roster proves it ran, and only its own output proves it looked.** This is REDS row 59's sentence arriving from a new direction -- there, a green was a memory because no clock reached the guard; here, a clock reached it every lap and there was nothing behind it. The second half is the more useful one: **the real lint existed the whole time, written inline three times over.** `tools/w/width_check_th1.rish`, `th3`, and `th6` each spell the same `grep 'usize' | grep -v '@intCast' | grep -v '@as(usize'` pipeline in their own bodies, covering seven files between them, and not one of them reads the forty-five-file roster. A lantern that fires three times is a loom nobody built. *Measured before repair, on 1,891 authored sources:* the declared roster reads **44 of 45 clean**; the discovered hosted corpus reads **359 files and 1,365 lines** carrying authored `usize`. The roster covered 2.4% of the corpus and was itself unread, which is REDS %277's hand-typed-roster lesson at scale. *The one flagged roster file is not a violation:* `rishi/src/main.rye` carries five `usize` locals -- `gi`, `start`, `i`, `total`, `pos` at lines 1886, 1887, 1920, 1937 and 1945 -- each indexing a Zig slice or holding a length against `.len`, with `const size: usize = @intCast(size_i)` standing directly above the first. They are the seam value carried onward, and the filter is line-scoped so it cannot see where a local came from. Converting them to `u32` would add an `@intCast` at every slice site, which is more casts rather than fewer, against TAME's own words that a seam cast is correct code and not debt. So the count is **pinned at five** rather than waived: these five stand and a sixth refuses. *Repaired:* [`../tools/fixtures/width_check_scan.sh`](../../tools/fixtures/width_check_scan.sh) gives the meter a body and holds three readings -- the declared roster **gated at zero**, the named seam exemption **pinned at 5**, and the discovered corpus as a **ratchet under ceilings that only fall** (359 files, 1,365 lines). The roster stays inside `width-check.rish`, read out of its own declaration line, because naming those modules is the promise that file makes; what changed is that something now reads it. `tools/w/width-check.rish` keeps its name, its path, and its roster row, and becomes the witness that runs the scan and the control. [`../tools/fixtures/width_check_control.sh`](../../tools/fixtures/width_check_control.sh) proves **thirteen behaviors** on real git repositories in a throwaway pen, every one from both sides: a seam cast passing free and an authored `usize` refused and named, a roster naming a vanished file refused, the exemption standing at its pin and refusing a sixth, `aurora/` and `vendor/` and `gratitude/` and dated testimony left outside, a stranger no roster names discovered by the corpus reading, both ceilings shown passing at the ceiling and refusing one past it, and a tree with no roster refusing rather than reading zero and calling it clean. Proven on metal in the living tree as well: a planted `var planted_index: usize = 0;` in `tally/seed.rye` reads `declared_roster_flagged=1` and names the file, and removing it returns the scan to `verdict=ok` with the file byte-identical.
