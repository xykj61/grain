**Language:** EN
**Stamp:** `20260830.153100`
**Status:** Fold shelf -- rows %356-%358, all CLOSED

Three guards read less than the artifact they claimed to judge. A bounded reader truncated Pond's
policy before its own length check, three witnesses pinned borrowed document bytes without hearing
their siblings, and the quality card knew two comment marks while 446 tracked programs spoke a
third. Each repair widened the reading to the whole subject and planted the direction the old shape

**REDS %356 (`20260830.032600`) -- a buffer sized AT the bound made the bound's own guard unreachable, and the record was read in part.** *What went wrong:* `pond/enclosure_policy.rye`'s `run_check` read the record into `var buf: [max_policy_len]u8` and handed the slice to `inspect_policy`, whose very first line is `if (text.len > max_policy_len) return error.PolicyTooLong`. That check could never fire: `readFile` fills the buffer it is given and stops rather than refusing, so the caller could not hand it a value large enough. This lap's own header carried the record from 6,610 to 8,468 bytes past a bound of 8,192, and the checker answered **place=26 hold=2 refuse=3** for a document of **34** declarations -- three lines never judged at all, and the half-line at the byte cut parsed as a declaration that does not exist and counted as a refusal. The receipt's whole promise is to name the line an operator opens first, and it was describing a file nobody wrote. *What caught it:* arithmetic on the receipt. The counts summed to 31 against a record measured at 34 one command earlier, and the difference was three rather than none. Nothing printed a warning; the verdict, the offender, and the exit code were all the shapes a working run makes. *What it taught:* **a guard the caller cannot reach is not a guard, and sizing a buffer AT a bound is how a length check gets quietly disconnected from the length it checks.** The bound and the buffer look like the same number and do opposite jobs -- one names the largest lawful document, the other names how much will be read -- so they must differ by at least one byte or the refusal is decorative. The broader form, worth carrying past this module: **a truncating read is a silent narrowing of the input, and every count downstream describes the truncation rather than the file.** *Repaired (`20260830.032600`):* the buffer takes `max_policy_len + 1` with the said-why naming the measurement, and `inspect_policy`'s `PolicyTooLong` is caught and printed as a named refusal at exit 2. Proven on metal from both sides -- an 8,317-byte record answers `is longer than max_policy_len=8192` and one trimmed under the bound reads whole -- by a `TOOLONG` leg in `tools/fixtures/p/pond_enclosure_policy_scan.sh` and a selftest assertion that hands `inspect_policy` a value one byte past the bound. The live record now reads **place=30 hold=2 refuse=2**, summing to 34. **CLOSED.**
