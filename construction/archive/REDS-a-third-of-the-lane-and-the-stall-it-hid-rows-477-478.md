**REDS %478 (`20260906.093709`) -- the vessel fetch path stalled forever, one run in six to eight, and it took rostering the guard that runs it to find out.** *What went wrong:* `amphora/vessel_fetch_delivery.rye` opened, bound, received on, and closed a fresh UDP socket for every single datagram, so the receiving end was unbound in every gap between datagrams -- and a datagram arriving at an unbound port is discarded by the kernel with no trace at either end. Two races followed from that one habit: a response overtaking the fetcher's next bind, and chunk two of five arriving while the fetcher reopened its socket. Either loss then met a `recvfrom` carrying no `SO_RCVTIMEO`, which waits forever. The 50-millisecond `sleep` in the fixture and the 50-millisecond `nanosleep` in `run_chunkdemo` are both that race wearing a band-aid. *What caught it:* running `amphora_resin_chunk` four times in a row while measuring what to roster -- **one of the four hung**, where a single run had passed twice before it. Isolated to 1 in 8 for `chunkdemo` alone and 1 in 6 for the fixture, on a quiet pier with nothing else touching the ports. *What it taught:* **a guard nobody runs hides its subject's flakiness as completely as its subject's rot** -- `%477` found the coverage gap by reading, and only the running found what the gap had been holding. And a second lesson, on the shape of the elder code: **an unbounded wait is not a slow failure, it is the absence of one.** *The elder form did red, and named the wrong thing:* a failed `rye build` overwrites the emitted binary with a 5,547-byte artifact, so `tools/gen/amphora/amphora_resin_chunk.rish`'s *preferring warm binary* fallback had nothing to fall back to and the next assert blamed a selftest that was fine. *Repaired, three instruments:* the module binds before it sends and holds one socket across an exchange, every receive bounded at `recv_timeout_seconds` 10 with a named `RecvNoDatagram` and a bounded `max_request_attempts` 3; `tools/fixtures/a/amphora_vessel_port_lock.sh` holds the pair at a **host** path around every step that speaks it, since a port belongs to the machine and eight trees reach for one; and both fixtures bound their long-lived processes with `timeout`. *Proven both ways on metal, same pier and hour at load 8.5:* HEAD's own bytes built in a pen stalled **2 of 20**, the repair **0 of 40**; two concurrent `chunkdemo` runs abort at 134 in 31s unlocked and pass in 1-2s through the lock, three trials each; a type error planted in a **reached** function reds by name through the shim the roster runs, and the same plant in an **unreached** `pub fn` rides free, which is `%463`'s lazy-analysis lesson met from the other side. *One row of this lap withdrawn rather than landed:* it booked the `standing_equipment` self-red latch, which `%475` published first, measured the same way and repaired where mine only named the fix. **CLOSED.**
