**REDS %817 (`20260917.164918`) -- a reader capped its own map at sixty of nine thousand seven hundred, and a law page told every ship the map was the whole journal.** *What went wrong:* the `--keys` branch of `tools/l/loom_trend.sh` ended its pipeline `| sort -rn | head -60`, printed no count of what it dropped, and described itself in its own header as *the keys actually written* and *the map of what the journal actually holds*. `.claude/rules/session-logs.md` carried the same claim to every ship. The journal held **9,767** keys; the mode showed **60**. Its BOUNDS line named two other caps exactly and not this one. *What caught it:* a lap comparing the vocabulary 407 rostered scans emit against the vocabulary `--keys` reports, which concluded that **2,558 of 2,588 emitted key names had never once been recorded** -- wrong by two orders of magnitude, since the journal in fact records 1,912 of 3,599 and **292 of 316** rostered guards with a scan already have an arc. *What it taught:* **a cap is lawful here and a silent one is not, because a reader cannot tell a small journal from a truncated reading of a large one.** This tree bounds everything by law; what no law permits is a bound contradicting its own tool's account of itself.  **It reached a peer, and the reach is the sharper half.** `tools/fixtures/l/loom_name_scan.sh` landed the same morning and reported, with its population named honestly, *over the 60 keys `--keys` lists*: `name_shared` 60 times, `kind_scopes` never, `name_scopes` never -- and **a top-N by frequency can return no other verdict, since frequency selects for the many writers that `name_shared` means.** Sampling the 9,707 keys the cap withheld, every 48th by rank, 203 keys: `name_scopes` **138**, `name_shared` **39**, `undeclared_only` **20**, `kind_scopes` **6** -- the distribution inverts. *What was NOT wrong, and it is the finding worth keeping:* **that paper's inference survives its own corpus intact.** It explains the scarcity of well-named keys as *a key earns `name_shared` by being useful; the well-named keys are the ones nobody has had a second use for* -- and out of sample **131 of the 138** `name_scopes` keys carry one occurrence, the 7 exceptions carrying two. Ninety-five percent singletons, on a population that paper could not see. What the cap did reach is narrower: two verdicts it reported absent from the live journal stand at 6 and 20 there. *What holds the finding still:* `--keys` prints the same listing byte for byte -- proven against the shipped reader before the claim -- followed by `keys_distinct` beside `keys_shown`, with `--keys --all` lifting the cap; BOUNDS names all three; and the law page says what the mode does. **`--keys` was the one mode its control never exercised** -- 13 legs, none on the mode a law page called the map of the journal, which is why the cap stood unseen. **21 legs from 13, 0 failing, two mutations bitten:** striking the `keys_distinct` print returns the silence (4 legs), and striking the clamp lets a journal under the cap claim 60 rows while printing 4 (1 leg). The witness asserts both counts. *What this does not reach:* whether the other **271** tracked tools carrying a `head -N` are honest about it -- named rather than counted, since that is a judgment per tool rather than a grep. **CLOSED** -- `loom_trend` GREEN on metal, `loom_name` and `loom_sitting` GREEN beside it.
