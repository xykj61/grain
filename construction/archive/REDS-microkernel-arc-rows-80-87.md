**REDS #82 (`20260816.201310`) -- ML-DSA `decompose` panicked on a residue its random selftest never drew.** *What went wrong:* `crypto/mldsa_encode.rye`'s `decompose` (FIPS 204 Algorithm 36) asserted its centered low part as `r0 > -gamma_2` (strict), yet FIPS's top-step borrow reaches `r0 = -gamma_2` exactly at the single residue `r = q - gamma_2` -- the assert `reached unreachable` and killed signing whenever a message's `w` hit that value. The reconstruction was already correct (the code computed the right `r0`); only the range check's lower bound was one notch too tight, so the valid FIPS range `[-gamma_2, gamma_2]` was rejected at its floor. *What caught it:* building the new `hybrid_signed_carry` witness -- its BLAKE2b digest, signed with ML-DSA-44 (`pq_small`), drew a `w` coefficient equal to `q - gamma_2` and the primitive panicked on the first metal pass. The module's own decompose selftest had swept 64 pseudo-random polynomials and missed that one residue in `q`. *What it taught:* a rejection-sampling primitive must **tolerate the boundary its own math produces, then reject on the norm bound** -- the assert was defending an invariant FIPS does not hold; the real guard is the reconstruction check (unchanged) plus the signer's `||r0||inf` rejection, which retries such a `w`. A random spread is not a boundary proof: the fix plants `r = q - gamma_2` in the selftest and checks `decompose(q - gamma_2) = (0, -gamma_2)` directly, so the exact residue is proven, never merely sampled. Closed the same round -- the assert relaxed to `r0 >= -gamma_2`, all four ML-DSA modules and the whole hybrid ladder re-GREEN on metal, the boundary now witnessed.
