# REDS %411, %412, %413 -- what a wrapper inherits, and what a loop hides

*Folded from the living pin [`../REDS.md`](../REDS.md) on `20260905.011727` to bring it back under its bound as
`%414` landed. Three **CLOSED** rows from one long session, and the thread between them is what a
thing inherits without being told. `%411`: four behaviors every ship performed every lap and not one
of them was a rule, invisible because every ship was handed them at launch. `%412`: a third of the
roster's clock spent forking rather than reading, invisible because the scripts read correctly.
`%413`: three more guards of the same shape -- and a repair that twice taught a guard to report a
clean tree it had never read, because an empty answer from a failed instrument is byte-identical to
an empty answer from a clean one. Link depth moved with the text.*

**REDS %411 (`20260904.215903`) -- four behaviors every ship performed every lap, and not one of them was a rule.** *What went wrong:* the fleet's shared opening lived in six seat prompts, each restating it in its own words, and the `.claude/rules/` roster -- the thing a fresh clone reads to learn how this tree works -- carried **no rule at all** for four of the eleven things that opening asks. Measured by grepping the 49 rules for each claim: **one writer per checkout** appeared once, in passing, inside `derived-spine.md`, which is a rule about ledger numbers; **claim-as-override** appeared **zero** times; the **council rota** appeared **zero** times; **GATES-ONLY** appeared **zero** times. Each is load-bearing. One writer per checkout is REDS `%291`, a wound that bit four times in four spellings in a single day. Claim-as-override is what stops an idle ship. The rota is a reading habit that has caught a sleeping doorway guard and a silent page in two commanded laps. GATES-ONLY is how an unattended loop stops at a custody gate instead of circling it all night. *What caught it:* Keaton asking, in plain words, whether the rules match what the launch loops load. The honest answer was no, and it took one grep per claim to say so -- the question was better than the tree's own instruments, none of which compare a seat prompt against the rule roster. *What it taught:* **a behavior carried only in a prompt is a behavior a clone cannot learn.** The rules roster is the tree's answer to *how does this place work*, and four of its load-bearing answers lived in a launcher argument and a design essay instead -- readable by an agent already running with that prompt, invisible to one reading the rules cold. The sharper half is why it stayed invisible: every ship behaved correctly the whole time, because every ship was handed the behavior at launch. **A gap that nothing misbehaves over is a gap no meter finds.** Kin to `%410` in the same session, where an exception no input reached was tested by nothing; here it is a rule no reader missed because no reader was ever without it. *Repaired (`20260904.215903`):* `tools/l/fleet_baton.txt` holds the shared opening once and `tools/l/fleet-loop.sh` prepends it, so a seat prompt is now its **lane stanza** alone -- REDS `%409`'s repair applied one room over, to prompts instead of to a seat table. `.claude/rules/the-baton.md` maps every baton section to the rule that governs it and **seats the four that had none**, each written out rather than pointed at. Six control legs hold the shape: a stanza restating the baton, a looping seat with no stanza, and an invocation reaching the agent without the baton are counted at zero, and a `field` seat is exempted by its own engine word rather than by a name. *Found while proving it:* two of those legs passed for the wrong reason on their first draft, because the marker string they grepped for is **line-wrapped inside the baton** and so appears in neither file. A leg that greps for a phrase across a line break tests nothing and reports green; both now key on a line-anchored marker, and one compares line numbers rather than substring order. **CLOSED** on `tools/f/fleet_roster_witness.rish`, 37 behaviors GREEN on metal.

**REDS %412 (`20260904.225911`) -- a third of the roster's clock went to forking, not to reading, and the two worst offenders were checking cheap things expensively.** *What went wrong:* a full pass measured **1,510 seconds** across 111 guards with a **median of 2s against a mean of 13** -- and a mean six times its own median is never a rate, it is a handful of guards carrying everything. **Five held 46%.** Two of those were slow by accident rather than by nature. `tools/fixtures/l/living_card_ascii_scan.sh` validated UTF-8 by calling a shell function once per file over **14,709 tracked text files**, and that function forked `mktemp`, `iconv` and `rm` each time -- roughly **44,000 processes** for a reading the bytes make trivial, at **137s**. `tools/r/reds_row_present_witness.rish` proved the ledger's spine complete by asking `reds_row_present.sh` for row 1, then row 2, to the end -- **411 invocations, each re-reading the whole spine** of the pin plus forty archives, at **177s**. *What caught it:* Keaton asking whether six ships could share one pier, which turned a vague sense that the roster was slow into a question with an answer. The run card had recorded per-guard seconds since `%388` and nobody had sorted it. The first move was nearly the wrong one: the obvious repair is to move slow guards to `tier cadence`, and a **137-second ASCII check over nine small files is not slow, it is broken** -- reading the script rather than reaching for the tier is what found the fork loop. *What it taught:* **before you make an expensive thing rare, find out whether it is expensive on purpose.** Two of the three biggest costs on this roster were accidental and one was inherent, and only measurement tells them apart -- a cadence tier applied to the first two would have hidden 277 seconds of pure waste behind a slower clock, and left the waste. The sharper half is where the waste hid: neither guard reads anything expensive. One validates bytes, the other greps a ledger. **The corpus was never the cost; the loop around it was**, and a per-item subprocess is invisible in a script that reads correctly. *Repaired (`20260904.225911`):* `tools/fixtures/l/utf8_valid.awk` validates the whole corpus in **one process** under `LC_ALL=C` so `substr` counts bytes, refusing a lead byte with too few continuations, a bare continuation, and an overlong or out-of-range lead; the list is **NUL-delimited**, because `git ls-files` hands back paths with spaces in them and a whitespace-split list drops them in silence. **137s -> 25s**, proven both ways in a five-file pen -- clean and valid-multibyte pass, orphan and bare-continuation and overlong refuse -- and again on the real tree by planting an orphan lead byte and watching the guard name its path. `reds_row_present.sh` grows an `--all` mode answering from **the same sed expressions over the same spine file set**, so the witness keeps that script as its authority rather than growing a second reader beside it, and calls it once: **177s -> 12s**. The single-row path a scan actually calls stays proven on first, middle, last and past-the-end, because a completeness leg running only `--all` would leave that contract tested by nothing -- `%410`'s own shape, one room over. *And one moved rather than mended:* `sow` at **230s** is expensive on purpose -- it projects the whole public seed and scrubs every name from it. It sits at `tier cadence` now, honest because the act it guards is **custody gate %1**, a manual publish that runs this witness in its own flow, so no lap can take the thing it protects. A tier is a cadence rather than an exemption. **Measured together: 1,510s -> 1,003s routine, 1,233s on the cadence lap.** **CLOSED** on `tools/l/living_card_ascii_witness.rish` and `tools/r/reds_row_present_witness.rish`, both GREEN on metal.

**REDS %413 (`20260905.001835`) -- three more guards forking per item, and a repair that twice taught a guard to report a clean tree it never read.** *What went wrong:* the same shape `%412` found in two guards stood in three more, and each was reading something cheap expensively. `tools/fixtures/t/tracked_link_scan.sh` forked an `awk` and a `grep` **per link**, plus three more per file -- about **47,700 processes** across 4,289 documents and ~17,400 links, at 72s. `tools/fixtures/p/phantom_path_scan.sh` forked an `awk` per source file and then a `grep` per literal -- 2,859 files and **8,793 literals**, at 58s. `tools/fixtures/d/declared_model_scan.sh` walked all **14,709 tracked files** forking a `basename` and one or two `grep`s each -- roughly 35,000 processes to find the 1,127 files that name a model id at all, at 75s. *What caught it:* the previous lap's own recommendation, taken literally. Three guards were named on a hunch that they shared `%412`'s shape; all three did. *What it taught, and this is the half that cost a repair rather than a reading:* **a guard that cannot run its instrument must refuse, never report a clean tree.** The first rewrite of `tracked_link` resolved its new awk helper from `$(pwd)` and sent awk's complaint to `/dev/null`. The control runs that scan by absolute path after `cd`-ing into a throwaway repository, so the helper was not there, awk failed, the candidate list came back empty, and the scan printed **zero unresolved links and passed**. Four control legs caught it. Then the same two mistakes were found sitting in the `living_card_ascii` repair landed an hour earlier under `%412` -- `$(pwd)` and an unchecked pipeline -- where no control pens the scan, so nothing would have caught it until someone did. **An empty answer from a failed instrument is byte-identical to an empty answer from a clean corpus, and the second is the reading everyone wants to hear.** *Repaired (`20260905.001835`):* `tools/fixtures/t/tracked_link_duty2.awk` resolves and tests every link in one process, emitting only the residue that misses the tracked set -- 636 of 17,400 -- for the shell's `-e` test, where `[` is a builtin and costs nothing. `phantom_path` reads every source in one awk and filters 8,793 literals against the known set and the symlink map in a second, leaving a residue for `-e` and `git check-ignore`. `declared_model` asks `git grep -l` once for the 1,127 files naming any model id and loops only over those. Every one now finds its helper from `$(dirname "$0")` and **refuses loudly when its pass fails**, proven by hiding the helper and watching `instrument=failed` where a green had stood. **Measured: 72s -> 5s, 58s -> 6s, 75s -> 5s**, each output byte-identical to its elder and every control leg unchanged -- `phantom_path`'s nine cases including three refusals, `tracked_link`'s twelve. *And two standing guards bit this repair on the lap it entered, which is the roster working rather than a second fault:* `shell_dialect` refused `xargs -0 -a FILE` -- the argument-file flag is a GNU extension and this fleet spans a Mac. The tree had **already written that trap down**, in `tools/fixtures/s/shell_portable.sh`'s own header and in a comment inside `geode_libraries_scan.sh` that says it was *paid for twice*; a third payment was avoided only because a guard was listening. Piped instead. `vocabulary_collection` refused a bare `corpus` in the operator card's reader prose, a seated retirement this lap's own summary walked straight into. Swept to **collection** in the card and in both new instrument headers. **CLOSED** on the three witnesses, GREEN on metal.
