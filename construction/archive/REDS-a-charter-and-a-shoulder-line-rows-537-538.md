# REDS %537 and %538 -- two claims a ship read aloud every lap, and nothing in the tree could check either

*Folded off the living pin [`../REDS.md`](../REDS.md) on the round that booked the second of them,
by their own author, because that row ran the pin to 42,309 against its 40,960 bound. Both read
**CLOSED**, each on its own witness GREEN on metal. No peer's row was moved to make this room.*

*Both rows are one fault at two ranges.* A **seat prompt** told a ship how big its own lane was --
`10 Rye modules and 3,861 lines` -- and no instrument read that sentence, so a figure that matched
no reading of the room on the day it was written was recited at the head of every lap for two days.
A **vessel** told its reader that an unnamed kind is refused whole -- the wreck rule, in its own
header line and its own selftest -- and no witness read that claim either, so the one field of the
three-field manifest line that says *what rides inside* went unchecked for eight weeks.

*The sentence worth carrying forward:* **prose a program never reads is a claim with no way to
fail, and it decays silently in exactly the places a reader trusts most** -- the charter a ship
opens with, and the header of the module that names the rule. The repair is the same in both:
give the sentence an instrument, or replace it with the command that derives the answer fresh.

*The second row's own sharper half, which the first did not have:* Amphora and Pond were handed
the SAME vow by the same design page, and Pond kept it while Amphora did not -- **a vow written
across two modules is kept by whichever one has a witness.** Worse, the vessel's seal disguised
the gap perfectly: every plant that could test the mark also broke the Kumara signature, so the
refusal always arrived and always for the wrong reason. A correctly sealed vessel carrying
`executable` rode straight through. **An integrity guard reads exactly like a type guard until
somebody asks it the question directly.**
