**REDS %647 (`20260907.223749`) -- a bound named for memory, one function over: `apply` walked the whole weave three times a call, and two of the three walks were asserts the release build hides.** *What went wrong:* `Weave.apply` in `mantra/src/weave.rye` found each delete by scanning every line, proved each insert's identity fresh by scanning every line, and re-read the whole weave in its postcondition. So a diff cost `|diff| x |lines|` and a weave built one line at a time paid the sum. `max_weave_lines = 1 << 20` is checked at the edge of every operation and speaks only of memory -- the same two-claims shape `20260907.210632` closed in the merge fold, standing one function over. *What caught it:* that lap's own recommendation, measured rather than assumed. *Measured on this pier, the CHECKED build a witness runs, one-line applies:* 2,048 lines **116ms**; 8,192 **1,696**; 16,384 **7,244**; 32,768 **24,432**; 65,536 **98,994** -- 4.05x per doubling. At **1,048,576, exactly `max_weave_lines`, it did not finish inside ten minutes.** Deleting every line of a 16,384-line weave: **3,415ms**. *What it taught:* **an invariant re-proved by walking is a cost a release measurement cannot see.** Asserts compile out under ReleaseFast, so two of the three walks were free where nobody was checking and quadratic in the only build that checks -- the build that must reach the declared ceiling for the invariant to be checkable at all. The delete walk was real work in every build. *Repaired (`20260907.223749`):* a weave has always held its lines in **identity order** -- `apply` appends above every position held, `merge` sorts its union -- and the module now states it and reads it. Deletes binary search; an insert's uniqueness is one comparison against the last held line, which carries the order forward; the postcondition reads the lines the call placed, since `next_pos` and `next_run` only rise. `merge` sorts its union by identity rather than place (document order stays `current`'s reading, over a copy) and drops the sort that put its held side in order. Same ceiling: **1,048,576 lines built in 189ms, every one deleted in 731ms**; 16,384 **7,244 -> 2ms**. Claim 11 proves a merged weave still deletes by name where the two orders disagree -- nothing else edited a merged weave -- and an eleventh control phase plants the elder place sort. Merge, annotate, model, diff witnesses GREEN; `tame_style_check` and `width-check` GREEN. **CLOSED**
