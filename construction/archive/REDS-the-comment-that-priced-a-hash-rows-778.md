**REDS %778 (`20260916.185437`) -- a comment priced the build receipt's own key at nanoseconds beside a read, and the hash costs eleven times the read.** *What went wrong:* `rye/src/main.rye` opens the receipt key by hashing the toolchain's bytes, the standard library's bytes, every closure source, and this binary's own bytes, and the comment introducing that walk told a reader the hasher's cost was *nanoseconds beside a read*. Measured on this pier over the pinned **172,641,672-byte** toolchain with a warm page cache: `cat` **18 ms**, `sha256sum` **194 ms** and **198 ms** on two runs -- **eleven times** the read, and coreutils reaches the CPU's SHA extensions where this standard library's SHA-256 runs in software at roughly **120 MB/s**, so the cost inside rye is higher again. *What caught it:* a **recovered session log**. `stash_record` read RED at the cold open with `unlanded=2`, and the `20260915.182427` record it named -- a Bakery lap that read GREEN before a send it never made -- had timed the thing from inside the program: a receipt HIT on a one-file program cost **1,492 ms**, of which hashing this toolchain was **1,420 ms** and reading the same bytes **124 ms**. The same ratio, two days and two methods apart. No guard reads a performance claim in a comment, and none can. *What it taught:* **a comment's why-sentence is a claim, and a performance claim is the kind that decays without anyone editing it.** This one did not merely go stale -- it was inverted by an order of magnitude, and it is exactly the sentence a reader would use to decide that no cheaper key path is worth designing. `docs-implementation-sync` already rules that a doc's behavioral claim is an assertable invariant rather than a belief; a comment is a doc that ships inside the thing it describes, and a figure with no unit, no date, no source, and nothing holding it still is what Gauge's fifth thing exists to refuse. *The repair:* the comment carries the measurement, both figures named **FREE** with the two commands beside them, and the reasons for taking the compiler's bytes rather than its path are left whole -- the key stays correct, and only the claim that computing it is free falls. *What this does not reach:* the repair that recovered lap designed -- deciding whether a key is wanted before reading, and remembering the compiler's digest under inode, size, mtime and ctime -- stands **unbuilt at HEAD**, its 217 lines in `stash@{12}` against a family peers have since moved to `ryekey-v7`; re-deriving beats replaying a stash against a design that moved. **BOOKED** -- the false claim is gone and measured from both sides; `ryekey` **22 legs GREEN**, with `width-check`, `tame_style_check`, `ascii-comment` and `stash_record` (**105 legs**, `unlanded=0`) GREEN on metal.
