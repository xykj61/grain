**REDS %796 (`20260916.224600`) -- a ledger row quoted a peer row's prose, and the consistency guard read the quotation as a closure.** *What went wrong:* `%792`, written by this ship one lap earlier, cites `%730` by quoting its final sentence -- `` `%730`'s own closing line -- *seven firings by six hands...* `` -- to say that the written habit had been read and walked around anyway. `tools/fixtures/r/reds_status_consistency_scan.sh` decides a closure claim by **word proximity**: a row number standing near one of `close`, `closes`, `closed`, `closing`, `closure` on another row's line. The noun phrase *closing line* carries the word, so the scan reads `%792` as claiming to close `%730`, `%730` still reads **OPEN**, and the guard answers `verdict=ledger_contradicts_itself` with `contradicted_rows=1`. Nothing in the ledger is actually inconsistent: `%730` is open, `%792` says so, and both rows are true. *What caught it:* the hot scoped endurance run of `20260916.222001`, seven laps after the row landed and on the first pass that reached this guard -- `reds_status_consistency red`, then the scan's own detail line, which names the pair outright. No hand saw it, because the ledger reads correctly to a reader and the fault is only visible to the proximity rule. *What it taught:* **a guard that admits its own heuristic in prose still needs the exclusion written into its pattern.** The scan's header already says *word proximity rather than [parsing]* and already carries one exclusion, for a `gate %5` standing in a sentence with a closing word -- so the shape was known and the class was drawn one case too narrow. The distinguishing fact is grammatical and cheap: `close`/`closes`/`closed` are verbs a row uses to claim a closure, while `closing` followed by `line`, `sentence`, `word`, or `paragraph` is a noun phrase about PROSE, and a ledger that cites its own peers will write that phrase again. **OPEN** -- the ledger keeps every word it wrote, so the repair belongs in the reading rather than in the row. **Specified, not built:** hold the proximity rule to verb forms, exclude `closing <line|sentence|word|paragraph>`, and prove it from both sides in a pen -- one planted genuine closure that must still refuse, and this real pair replayed from history, which must walk free. **CLOSED** (`20260916.231355`) -- both exclusions stand in `tools/fixtures/r/reds_status_consistency_scan.sh` and the guard reads `contradicted_rows=0 verdict=every_status_agrees` on the living ledger. **The specified repair closed half the fault, and the row booking it tripped the same guard on its own account.** The noun exclusion freed the quoted phrase; this row then wrote the verb beside the row it was defending, one sentence later, and the reading still refused. So a second phrase is read past: a window carrying the bold open marker is a row ASSERTING a peer is open, which is the opposite of claiming to have closed it. Measured across the whole spine before it was seated -- 55 closure claims over 716 rows, and exactly **one** carried the bold marker in its window, this false one. **Proven:** the pen stands at **47 legs, 0 red**, each exclusion planted and then lifted, three mutations bitten -- the noun exclusion removed, the marker exclusion removed, and the marker test loosened to a bare lowercase word -- and every new leg quoted by name in `tools/r/reds_status_consistency_witness.rish`, so none can flip in silence. **The honest limit:** a row claiming a genuine closure while quoting the bold open marker of its peer inside the same sixty characters would walk free; that prose contradicts itself and stands nowhere in 716 rows.
