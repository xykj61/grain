# REDS -- a postcondition over the outputs

**Language:** EN
**Style:** Gauge, Meter setting
**Voice:** Kyri
**Status:** Shelf -- one folded row, immutable once written
**Room:** checkable -- a ledger row, closed on two witnesses and two controls run on metal
**Folded:** `20260915.233000` from [`../REDS.md`](../../REDS.md)

One row, born onto its shelf because the pin stood 211 bytes above its bound with the row in it and
`reds_pin_capacity_scan.sh` read `verdict=over_bound_foldable`. It teaches that a function may assert
its whole declared postcondition over what it RETURNS and still be blind to a fault in what it was
HANDED -- and that the two plants which look alike are told apart only by running them: a minimum
reds on every claim and proves the general law, while a derivation equals the law on every tight
input and parts from it on the one input a record admits by design.

**REDS %751 (`20260915.220400`) -- a merge carried two counters through the max builtin and stated no postcondition about either, so a counter derived from the result's lines passed every claim in the witness.** *What went wrong:* `Weave.merge` in `mantra/src/weave.rye` sets `next_pos` and `next_run` to the maximum of the two sides, then walks the merged lines asserting each stands BELOW those numbers. That walk reads the LINES, and it is exactly as tight as the tightest side: every weave `apply`, `planted`, `wide_weave`, `from_v1` and the witness's `seeded` can build stands one above its highest line, so a fallen counter is caught there by a line that reaches it. `V2Record` admits a weave that does NOT -- its own head says the record declines to inherit the tightness, and `from_v2` refuses only a counter at or below a row it holds -- so a restored weave may declare `next_pos` 5 while holding position 2. Merge it and a counter DERIVED from the result answers 3: every line still stands under it, every place still rises, the walk passes, and the next `apply` hands out a position that record already declared spent. *What caught it:* the card's own handoff line -- *merge and annotate still reach the two counters through the max builtin alone* -- then plants in a pen. A minimum is the WRONG plant: it reds on the elder claims, so it proves the maximum's general law rather than this gap. The derivation equals the maximum on all ten and parts from it only on a slack side -- with the new claim removed it exits **0**, with it present **134**. *What it taught:* **a postcondition over the outputs can be strictly weaker than one over the inputs, and nothing in the shape says which you have.** Ten claims and four constructors all read the result; the one reading that could see a counter fall had to compare the merged counter against the two it came from. The module had ALREADY WRITTEN the hazard, three hundred lines up in `V2Record`'s own head, and held it in no assert. *Repaired (`20260915.220400`):* four counter asserts in `merge`, named honestly as restatements holding for every caller rather than as an isolated catch -- claim 11 reds with them deleted, measured. `Note.gen` gains a precondition and a `merged >= 1` postcondition where it had none; two further asserts were taken out the same lap as tautologies of the maximum above them. Claim 11, `prove_slack_counters_carried`, restores a slack weave through `from_v2` and reads the merged counters BOTH orders. The merge control gains `pos_derived`; the annotate control's `derive` plant was repointed after `plant_apply` refused it by name -- `%519` doing its whole job, since `Note.gen`'s new binding retired the literal that sed carried. *Booked `%743`, re-booked `%750`, then `%751`:* upstream published a row against each while this one stood unshared, so it derives above both and the key stays the stamp. **CLOSED** on `mantra_weave_merge_witness` and `mantra_weave_annotate_witness` GREEN on metal, both controls `control_verdict=ok`, `tame_style_check` and `width-check` GREEN.
