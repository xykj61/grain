One row, and it is the far side of the seam its own kin landed one lap earlier. The compile-reach
census asks a resolver what a harness assembles and grants that credit **only** when the assembling
script invokes the Rye compiler -- a condition its header states twice. The residue beside that
credit, the sites the resolver could not read at all, was published with no condition on it whatever.

So the same seam was crossed twice and only one crossing carried its rule. The consequence is not a
wrong count; it is a **wrong frame**. `harness_unresolved=10` answers *how many sites could the
resolver not read*, and a reader weighing this census's floor takes it for *how much of my floor is
in doubt* -- ten times the true figure, because nine of those ten sit in scripts that never invoke
the compiler and could therefore have credited nothing whether resolved or not.

The lesson generalizes past this instrument. **A number published in the resolver's frame is read in
the consumer's**, and the two frames differ exactly where the consumer applies a condition the
resolver does not know about. That is `%472`'s shape one room over, where 108 copies were counted
against themselves, and `%453`'s before it. The cure is the same both times: teach the reading to
carry the condition, rather than teach the reader to discount the number.

**And a smaller finding worth keeping, because it argues against the obvious repair.** Most of the
residue is not a harness at all. A roster scan iterating a directory it has just listed spells
`"unrostered: $DIR/$m.rye"` inside a **diagnostic message**, and a candidate grep cannot tell a
message from an assembly. Widening the resolver would lower a number without touching a fact -- so
the honest instrument is the conditional weighing, and the wider parser is a lap nobody should spend.
