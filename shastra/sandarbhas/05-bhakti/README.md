## One question

The first four Sandarbhas answer *what is true* -- one non-dual reality, disclosed fully as
Bhagavan, reaching as Paramatma, named directly as Krsna. This file turns to a different kind of
question, the abhidheya entry in [`../../glossary.md`](../../glossary.md) already names: **once
that truth is known, what does a person actually do, and why does this doing stand apart in kind
from ordinary ethical action or contemplative knowledge?**

## The definition the argument rests on

> *anyabhilasita-sunyam jnana-karmady-anavrtam*
> *anukulyena krsnanu-silanam bhaktir uttama*
>
> "The highest devotion is the favorable cultivation of activity connected to Krsna, free of
> desire for anything else, and unclouded by the pursuit of abstract knowledge or of action done
> for its results."

Three qualities, held together, define bhakti here: it is **favorable** (done with warmth toward
Krsna, rather than as cold obligation), it is **free of other desire** (aimed at Krsna alone,
rather than at some separate reward), and it stands **uncovered** by two rival pursuits -- jnana,
the path of abstract knowledge seeking impersonal liberation, and karma, action performed for its
earned results. Each exclusion sharpens what remains: an activity done purely for Krsna's own sake.

## The argument, kept plain

**Bhakti as both the path and the destination.** Every other classical path treats its practice as
a ladder to be climbed and then set aside once the goal is reached -- karma's rituals end when
liberation dawns; jnana's inquiry ends when the self is finally known. Bhakti Sandarbha argues
bhakti works differently: the practice and the perfection share one continuous nature, so a
devotee's service in this life and a liberated soul's eternal loving relationship in the next are
the same activity, ripened rather than replaced. This is why bhakti earns its own category among
the paths, standing apart from a rung on a ladder that gets kicked away once climbed.

**The angas -- what devotion actually looks like, done.** The tradition names nine primary
practices (navadha bhakti): hearing about Krsna, chanting his names and glories, remembering him,
serving his feet, worshipping his form, offering respect, serving as his servant, befriending him,
and surrendering the self entirely. Bhakti Sandarbha treats these as concrete, learnable practices
-- each one plain enough that anyone can begin today, and deep enough that a lifetime keeps finding
more inside it.

**Vaidhi and raganuga -- two honest starting points, one destination.** Some practitioners begin
under **vaidhi bhakti**, guided by scriptural rule and injunction, because their own spontaneous
longing for Krsna has yet to fully awaken. Others move by **raganuga bhakti**, following the
spontaneous mood of Krsna's own eternal associates in Vraja, drawn by attraction rather than by
rule. Bhakti Sandarbha honors both as genuine doorways, each fitted to where a person actually
stands rather than to where theory says they should stand.

**Association as the quiet engine underneath every practice.** Across every anga and both starting
points, one condition recurs: sadhu-sanga, association with those already established in devotion.
Bhakti Sandarbha treats this less as one practice among nine and more as the medium every other
practice grows in -- warmth passed from a person already warm, rather than kindled alone.

## What this file opens next

Priti Sandarbha takes up what bhakti, practiced fully, ripens into: **prema**, love in its complete
and final form, and the different flavors -- the rasas already named in
[`../../glossary.md`](../../glossary.md) -- that love can take depending on the relationship a
devotee holds with Krsna. That file, the sixth in this room's order, still ahead, is where
abhidheya's long practice finally becomes prayojana's stated goal.

## What this file leaves open

Whether bhakti is best understood as a fourth path standing genuinely apart from karma, jnana, and
yoga, or as their fulfillment folded into one continuous practice, is argued differently across
Vaishnava schools even where the definition above is broadly shared. This file states the Gaudiya
position as Bhakti Sandarbha makes it, leaving the comparative question for a reader's own further
study.

---

*A path asks to be walked, and admiring it from where a person already stands is only ever the
first look. Bhakti Sandarbha spends its whole labor naming the ground underfoot plainly enough
that the first step stays open to anyone -- willing to begin exactly where one is, and to keep
good company along the way.*
