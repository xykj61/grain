## One question

Three files back, Bhagavat Sandarbha named Bhagavan as the fullest disclosure of one truth,
leaving whose face that fullness wears for a later file to name. Paramatma Sandarbha traced how far that
fullness reaches, leaving its source itself still to be named directly. This file asks the question both were
building toward: **who, specifically, is Bhagavan?**

## The verse the claim rests on

> *ete camsa-kalah pumsah krsnas tu bhagavan svayam*
>
> "All of these are either full expansions or portions of full expansions of the Supreme Person,
> yet Krsna is Bhagavan himself, the source and origin."

This verse follows a long list, earlier in the same chapter, naming the great avataras -- Rama,
Nrsimha, Vamana, and others -- each a genuine and complete manifestation of the Absolute for its
own purpose. The verse then draws one clear line: every name on that list descends from Krsna,
while Krsna descends from nothing prior to himself.

## The argument, kept plain

**Amsa, amsa-kala, and svayam -- three degrees of the same source.** Krsna Sandarbha reads the
avatara list as a hierarchy of genuine, complete expansions -- amsa, a full portion, and
amsa-kala, a portion of a portion -- each carrying real divinity for its own task, standing in
relation to one still point the whole hierarchy radiates from. Krsna alone is named **svayam
bhagavan**, Bhagavan in his own original person, the source every other name in the list already
presupposes.

**Why this reading serves rather than diminishes the other avataras.** Naming a source honors
rather than demotes what flows from it -- a river's tributaries keep every drop of their own water
however plainly the source spring is named. Krsna Sandarbha's whole argument treats Rama and Nrsimha as fully divine, fully
worthy of worship, and fully effective for what each descended to accomplish, while still holding
that one among these many faces is the face the others all express.

**Two aspects of the one source: aisvarya and madhurya.** Even within Krsna's own person, the
tradition distinguishes two modes of disclosure. **Aisvarya** is majesty -- the opulent, awe-inspiring
sovereignty shown in Dvaraka and Mathura, where Krsna rules as king and commands as Lord.
**Madhurya** is sweetness -- the intimate, playful presence shown in Vrindavan, where Krsna is met
as a cowherd, a friend, a son, a beloved, stripped of formal grandeur so that love, rather than awe,
becomes the whole relationship. Gaudiya theology holds madhurya as the fuller disclosure exactly
because it costs the Absolute more to set majesty aside than to display it -- a king wears his
crown easily; a king who sets it down to herd cows for love holds something rarer.

## What this file leaves open

The claim that Krsna specifically, among all forms of the divine named across scripture, is the
original source rather than one expression among equals is a Gaudiya reading of a shared verse,
held distinctly from other Vaishnava traditions that honor the same avataras while ranking their
source differently. This file states the Gaudiya argument plainly, leaving the wider comparative
question for a reader to weigh on its own terms.

---

*A source asks to be found rather than argued into existence -- the argument only points toward
where to look. Krsna Sandarbha spends its whole labor pointing, so that whatever a reader finds at
the place it points to is met directly, rather than through the pointing itself. The pointing ends
here; what it names begins wherever a heart is ready to receive it.*
