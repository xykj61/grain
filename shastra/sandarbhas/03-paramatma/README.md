## One question

Bhagavat Sandarbha named Paramatma as the second disclosure, the sun's own disc rather than its
distant shine. This file asks the question that naming leaves open: **what, specifically, does
Paramatma do -- as the witness seated inside every soul, and as the ground the whole world of
matter rests upon?**

## The argument, kept plain

**Two jobs, one localized presence.** Paramatma Sandarbha is read as doing cosmology and
psychology in the same breath, because Gaudiya theology treats them as one continuous fact rather
than two separate subjects. The same localized presence of the Absolute that pervades the material
universe as its ground is, at a smaller scale, seated within every living being as an inner
witness -- close enough to know a soul's every thought, yet distinct enough to remain untouched by
that soul's own confusion.

**Jiva-tattva -- what a soul actually is.** Each individual soul (jiva) is described here as an
eternal, atomic spark of consciousness, one in essential quality with the Absolute -- both are
consciousness itself -- while remaining infinitesimally small and eternally distinct in identity.
The classical formula for this relationship is **acintya-bheda-abheda**, inconceivable
simultaneous oneness and difference: the soul is genuinely continuous with the Absolute in
quality, and genuinely its own separate self in quantity and experience, at once, with each side
kept fully intact rather than either one collapsing into the other.

**Cosmology -- how the witness becomes the ground.** The tradition describes the Absolute
expanding, through a series of purusa forms, into the very substance the material universe rests
upon and unfolds within -- present as the ground beneath creation in one expansion, and present as
the indwelling witness inside each creature in another. Paramatma Sandarbha is reported to trace
this expansion carefully, holding the same one Absolute steady across every scale it appears at,
from the cosmic to the intimately personal.

**Why this is Bhagavan's own particular reach, standing apart from a lesser deity's.** Read
against Bhagavat Sandarbha's own argument, Paramatma is Bhagavan's own expansion -- one and the
same source, reaching as far inward as a single heart and as far outward as the whole created
order, however far the reach extends.

## What this file leaves open

Acintya-bheda-abheda is presented here as Gaudiya Vaishnavism's own settled answer to a question
that other schools of Vedanta answer differently -- some collapsing the soul fully into Brahman,
others holding the two eternally and simply separate. This file states the Gaudiya position
plainly rather than adjudicating between it and its rivals.
