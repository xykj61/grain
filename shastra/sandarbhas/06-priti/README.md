## One question

Five files have carried one argument to its very edge: truth is trustworthy, its fullest
disclosure is Bhagavan, Bhagavan reaches as Paramatma, Bhagavan is named as Krsna, and a person
moves toward Krsna through bhakti. This last file in the six-work core asks what that whole chain
was for: **what does practiced devotion ripen into, and why does Gaudiya theology hold that fruit
higher than liberation itself?**

## The claim, stated plainly

Prema -- love, in its complete and mature form -- is named here as prayojana, the final goal, and
the tradition makes a striking claim about its rank: a soul offered liberation from suffering may
set that offer aside, choosing continued loving relationship with Krsna instead. Liberation ends a
soul's bondage; prema gives a soul something to do, and someone to be with, once that bondage ends.
The tradition reads this preference as evidence of prema's own higher standing, rather than as an
oversight liberation's own logic would correct.

## The argument, kept plain

**Five rasas, five ways of loving the same source.** Priti Sandarbha, working alongside Rupa
Goswami's own rasa-theology, names five primary relationships a soul can hold with Krsna, in
ascending intimacy: **santa**, peaceful reverence, seeing Krsna as the vast and awesome Absolute
from a respectful distance; **dasya**, service, relating as a devoted servant to a beloved master;
**sakhya**, friendship, relating as an equal and a companion; **vatsalya**, parental love, relating
as Yasoda cared for Krsna as her own child; and **madhurya**, romantic love, relating as the
gopis and Radha loved Krsna without reservation. Each rasa is held as fully real and fully
satisfying to the soul who holds it -- the ranking names depth of intimacy, and every depth is a
complete relationship in its own right.

**Structured like art, yet realized rather than performed.** Sanskrit poetics already had a
developed theory of rasa -- the aesthetic flavor a well-made drama evokes in an audience capable of
receiving it. Priti Sandarbha borrows this vocabulary deliberately, and then makes one careful
distinction: where a drama's rasa is felt by an audience watching someone else's story, prema's
rasa is a soul's own actual, lived relationship. The borrowed structure explains how the different
flavors of love can be named and compared at all; the difference explains why prema stands as
lived relationship rather than very good theater.

**The circle closes.** The Uttara Veda claim, the very first idea this room opened with, said the
Bhagavata Purana is the ripened fruit of the Vedic tree. Priti Sandarbha names, at last, what that
fruit tastes like once it is finally in hand: not a doctrine to be understood and set down, but
prema itself, the love every earlier Sandarbha was quietly building toward and never once named
outright until this file.

## What the six-work core leaves complete

Sarva-samvadini, named next in this room's order, stands apart from this arc rather than
continuing it -- an autocommentary answering technical objections a careful reader of the first
four Sandarbhas might raise, rather than a seventh step in the chain sambandha-abhidheya-prayojana
already completes here.

## What this file leaves open

The claim that prema outranks liberation is a distinctly Gaudiya position, held differently by
schools of Vedanta that treat liberation itself as the highest good attainable. This file states
the Gaudiya argument as Priti Sandarbha makes it, leaving the comparative weighing to whoever reads
both positions honestly and decides for themselves.
