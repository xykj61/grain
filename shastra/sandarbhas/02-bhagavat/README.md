## One question

Tattva Sandarbha earned the Bhagavatam its trust. Bhagavat Sandarbha spends that trust on its
first real claim: **granted that one non-dual truth exists, what is the fullest way that truth
discloses itself, and how do its lesser disclosures relate to that fullness rather than compete
with it?**

## The verse the claim rests on

> *vadanti tat tattva-vidas tattvam yaj jnanam advayam*
> *brahmeti paramatmeti bhagavan iti sabdyate*
>
> "Knowers of the truth call that non-dual reality by three names: Brahman, Paramatma, and
> Bhagavan."

Three names for one truth, offered by the text as a settlement rather than a puzzle. Bhagavat
Sandarbha's whole labor is showing how three real disclosures can differ in fullness while
remaining, at root, one and the same reality -- so that affirming Bhagavan's fullness leaves
Brahman and Paramatma exactly as real as they always were.

## The argument, kept plain

**Three names, one light, seen from three distances.** The tradition offers a steadying image for
this: Brahman is like sunshine -- real, radiant, everywhere, yet undifferentiated, carrying
only the glow itself, apart from any feature a person could relate to directly. Paramatma is like the sun's own disc, localized and
nearer, the Self seated within every heart and every atom. Bhagavan is like the sun's indwelling
person, the source both the shine and the disc depend on and radiate outward from. Each image
names something true about the sun; only the third names someone a person can actually know.

**Fullness, measured by qualities rather than by size.** Bhagavat Sandarbha's technical move is to
define fullness as **possessing infinite auspicious qualities** (kalyana-guna), rather than as
merely more of the same undifferentiated glow -- qualities that stay outside Brahman's own nature
by definition,
since a feature-carrying quality would already differentiate it into something more particular. Bhagavan
is read as the reality in which every such quality is present at once, in full measure; Brahman
and Paramatma are read as genuine, partial glimpses of that same fullness, true as far as they
reach and complete only in what they point back toward.

**Why this settles rather than ranks.** A ranking would set the three against each other; this
argument instead sets them in relation, each one true at its own depth, with Bhagavan named as the
depth at which the other two are recognized as expressions of a single, personal, and infinitely
qualified reality rather than as impersonal ground alone.

## What this file opens next

Two later Sandarbhas take up exactly what this file leaves as an open naming: **who**, specifically,
is Bhagavan -- a question Krsna Sandarbha answers directly and in full, arguing Krsna as
*svayam bhagavan*, the very source these three names all describe -- and **how a person moves
toward that fullness in practice**, prefigured already by the abhidheya entry in
[`../../glossary.md`](../../glossary.md) and awaiting Bhakti Sandarbha's own file, further down this
room's order, still ahead.

## What this file leaves open

Whether the sun-and-sunshine image fully answers the philosophical question it illustrates is a
live and old debate inside Vedanta itself, and this file states the Gaudiya argument as that
tradition makes it rather than as a settled verdict every school shares. A reader is free to weigh
the image on its own honest terms.

*Light carries what its own source already holds, rather than competing with that source,
nearer or farther depending on where a person happens to be standing. Bhagavat Sandarbha asks only
that the nearest, warmest, most personal disclosure be recognized as fully real as the glow
that reaches everywhere. What that disclosure is called, and what it asks of anyone who meets it,
waits in the files still ahead.*
