## One question

The six-work core states a positive teaching, one Sandarbha at a time. This file asks a different
kind of question, the one a careful reader always eventually asks of any argument: **when a
skilled philosophical opponent raises a genuine technical objection to Tattva, Bhagavat,
Paramatma, or Krsna Sandarbha, what does Jiva Goswami actually say back?**

## Where this file sits, structurally

Sarva-samvadini is Jiva Goswami's own autocommentary -- written by the same author, addressing his
own earlier work directly, taking up the first four Sandarbhas specifically rather than all six.
Bhakti and Priti Sandarbha teach practice and its fruit; Sarva-samvadini defends the philosophical
ground the practice stands on, anticipating objections a rigorous critic might bring against the
sambandha argument before that critic has the chance to raise them unanswered.

## The argument, kept plain

**Written in the opponent's own idiom.** Jiva Goswami composed Sarva-samvadini in the technical
style of **Navya-Nyaya**, the "New Logic" school that dominated formal philosophical debate across
sixteenth-century Bengal, centered in nearby Navadvipa. This was a deliberate choice of terrain:
rather than restating doctrine in devotional language and hoping it would satisfy a logician,
Sarva-samvadini meets a logician's own objections using the very apparatus that logician trusts.

**Anticipating the strongest objection, not the easiest one.** The most serious challenge Tattva
Sandarbha's argument faces comes from Advaita Vedanta, which holds impersonal Brahman as the final
and complete truth, with any personal quality read as a lower, provisional stage on the way to
that impersonal realization. Sarva-samvadini engages this challenge directly rather than around
its edges, working through the specific logical moves an Advaitin would make and answering each on
its own terms.

**Rigor as its own form of respect.** A treatise that only asserts its conclusion treats an
opponent's objections as unworthy of a real answer. Sarva-samvadini's whole method assumes the
opposite -- that a serious critic deserves a serious reply, argued with the same precision that
critic would bring, rather than dismissed or answered only in the language of the tradition being
defended.

## What this file closes

With Sarva-samvadini, the seven works this room names as the Sat Sandarbhas -- six that teach, one
that defends -- stand complete: sambandha secured twice over, abhidheya and prayojana taught in
full, and the whole argument's hardest technical seams shown their own careful answer rather than
left for a critic to find unguarded.

## What this file leaves open

Whether Sarva-samvadini's specific answers to Advaita's objections persuade a committed Advaitin
reader is, honestly, a live question this file cannot settle from a secondhand summary -- that
judgment belongs to whoever reads both sides of the debate in their own technical depth, which
this room's own honesty note keeps naming as work still ahead.

*A teaching that welcomes its hardest questions is a teaching confident enough to survive them.
Jiva Goswami spent a whole treatise proving exactly that confidence was earned, meeting every
serious challenge on its own ground rather than asking it to soften first. The six works before
this one taught what to love; this one simply made sure the teaching could stand.*
