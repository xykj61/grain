# Bret Victor

Thank you, Bret Victor.

Your talks and essays at worrydream.com (read 2026-08-26) gave this tree the sentence
that Skate's development loop is built around: "creators need an immediate connection
to what they create" (*Inventing on Principle*, CUSEC, January 2012). That is the one
quote this page carries, and it is enough.

What we learned from you, in our own words:

- **The loop is the tool.** The time between a change and its visible consequence is
  the material a tool is made of. Skate's hot reload -- edit a `.brush` file, watch the
  page repaint, watch the witness strip repaint beside it -- is that lesson, with this
  tree's own addition that proof belongs in the loop too.

- **Touch the thing.** *Stop Drawing Dead Fish* (2012) taught us that the artifact
  itself is the best interface to the artifact. Skate's tune panel puts dials on the
  live page and writes the profile file from what the hand settled on.

- **Show the data.** *Learnable Programming* (September 2012) and *Magic Ink* (2005)
  taught us that hidden state is the enemy of understanding. Skate's whole subject is
  DAG state made visible, and the platform shows its own meters by the same rule.

- **Show comparisons.** *Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction* (2011) taught us that
  one example is a point and a sweep is a shape. Skate proposes side-by-side profile
  renders so a design decision can be made over a spread rather than a memory.

We take the ideas and write our own code, in our own languages, under our own
disciplines -- the clean-room habit this tree keeps with every teacher. The ideas
entered through reading; the implementation is ours, and the gratitude is yours.

May the connection stay immediate, and may every creator who picks up these tools feel
the thing respond under their hands.
