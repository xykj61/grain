## The Gratitude

Our thanks to **ArchiLabs** — Brian and William, Carnegie Mellon classmates turned co-founders, Y Combinator Fall 2024 — and to the classmate whose LinkedIn line carried the news across twelve years. ArchiLabs builds AI-native CAD for architecture, engineering, and construction: they began as an AI copilot writing transaction-safe scripts inside the incumbent tools, then took what the market taught them and set out to build **a better CAD engine from scratch** — web-native, code-first, every component defined via code, heavy computation server-side — opening with **data center design** as the first bounded domain. We honor the road as much as the product: a homebuilding factory, a transcription company built and sold, homes raised by hand in Texas, a private CAD tool built alone before the shared one — practice before platform, twice over.

## Siloed Along Our Grain

Five learnings, restated in our words and bound to our laws:

**One — code is the component.** Their platform defines every component via code, which is our desk law wearing another industry's clothes: a `.glow` desk *is* its definition, and the lowered artifact is derived, never authored. The teaching sharpens our beta bar — the day a Grain design can only be reproduced by clicking is the day we have broken this law.

**Two — the beachhead is the chapter.** They opened with data centers because the domain is standardized, bounded, and urgent — exactly how PLEAC opens each chapter with the narrowest desk that can be whole. Simple, Lovable, Complete already says this; ArchiLabs shows it holding at company scale.

**Three — build the engine when the moment ripens, not before.** They spent a deliberate season atop the incumbents, and only when their own hands and the new tools made a from-scratch engine feasible did they cut one. That is our thin-frontend patience and our fork-horizon law confirmed from outside: the compiler fork waits for its season, and waiting is a strategy rather than a delay.

**Four — the seam belongs where the weight is.** Web-first with server-side computation is a seam-placement decision: put the heavy fold where the resources live, keep the edge light and honest. Our worker-and-runner split, and the far-day Pond and Comlink lanes, read the same way.

**Five — the chat bar is a witness of ergonomics.** Their first wedge — plain requests becoming transaction-safe scripts — teaches that automation languages fail on learning cost, not power. Our answer stays different in kind (witnesses and generators over conversational mutation), and the lesson still lands: every Grain tool should be invocable in one remembered line.

## The Difference We Keep

ArchiLabs races to market inside a venture arc; Grain accretes inside a dharmic one. We take their craft lessons and keep our own order — safety first, the tree append-only, every green proven able to red. The gratitude is real precisely because the paths differ: same era, same tools, two grains.
