# Pablo Picasso -- the theft that honors, and the angle that multiplies

**Language:** EN
**Style:** Gauge at the Door, in the Twilight register (see `../context/GAUGE_STYLE.md`)
**Voice:** Kyri
**Status:** Living gratitude, seated `20260826` on Keaton's word
**Kin:** [`../cubist-bhakti-astrology/README.md`](../cubist-bhakti-astrology/README.md)

Grain thanks **Pablo Picasso** by name, for two gifts this tree now lives in.

The first is the line tradition hands him -- that good artists copy and great artists
steal. Held in the best radiant way, the saying names a kind of love: to steal a teaching
is to take it all the way in, make it answer to your own materials, and stand accountable
for what it becomes -- where a copy only borrows the surface and hands the weight back.
This tree steals like that on purpose, and says so out loud. From **Vic Dicara** it takes
the measured, source-honest reading of the classical sky; from **Parisa Yazdi** the warmth
that keeps a chart a conversation rather than a verdict. Both teachers are thanked here by
name, their teachings carried into our own words and our own witnesses, the debt recorded
rather than hidden -- theft in Picasso's sense, gratitude in ours.

The second gift is Cubism itself: the discipline of painting one subject from several
sides at once and trusting the viewer to hold the faces together. That discipline now has
a room of its own here -- `cubist-bhakti-astrology/`, where each classical teaching is
shown beside its measurement, its working habit, and its palette pair -- and a wider
movement behind it: the Grain and Skate surfaces, the social layer, and the twilight
astrology register, each rendering one subject from its own angle, none pretending to be
the whole. A tree that reads the sky, the ledger, and the field at once needed a painter's
permission to show all three faces on one canvas. Picasso gave it a century ago.

No canvas is reproduced here and no estate is claimed; the gratitude is for the method and
the nerve. The clean room holds for paint exactly as it holds for code: the understanding
crosses, the brushwork stays his.

*May every honest theft here honor its teacher, and every angle multiply the light.*
