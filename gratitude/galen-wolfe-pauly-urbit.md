# Galen Wolfe-Pauly -- the personal server, made a thing you could hold

**Honors:** Galen Wolfe-Pauly, who spent years turning a dense set of ideas into something a
person could actually run, look at, and own. Grain descends from that work in spirit, and this
note pays the debt by name.

**Role for us:** We study the shape and write our own code, per
[`../context/SILO_TECHNIQUE.md`](../context/SILO_TECHNIQUE.md). What we owe here is less a
technique than a **conviction** -- that a personal server is worth building for ordinary people,
and that it has to be pleasant before anyone will keep it.

**What we carry**

- **The personal server as a product rather than a thesis.** Someone had to insist that the thing
  boot, show a screen, and be worth returning to. That insistence is why **Pond** and **Skate**
  exist here as surfaces rather than as diagrams.
- **Design as a first-class concern in systems work.** A systems project that treats the interface
  as someone else's problem builds something only its authors can use. We took the opposite lesson.
- **Owned identity as the centre, rather than an accessory.** An address you hold, which nobody
  grants and nobody can revoke, is the foundation everything else rests on. **Kumara** is our own
  answer to that question, and the question came from here.
- **Patience with a long build.** A decade of work before the world was ready is its own teaching,
  and this tree is early enough to need the example.

**What we leave**

- **The network, the address space, and the economics.** Grain runs its own **d12-d60** topology
  and its own settlement, reasoned from our own strands.
- **Any claim of affiliation.** We are grateful readers, thanking a builder by name.

**Sources (public study doorways):** public talks, essays, and product documentation published
over the project's lifetime. Concepts only.
