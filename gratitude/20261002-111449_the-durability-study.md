A thing that does not spoil the way bread does can carry its age as evidence. A book still read after many years is a kinder bet to be read again than a book that arrived this spring. People who looked at that fact with care taught us to trust what has already lasted, and to spend our hours on work a stranger can still use when the season has turned.

We thank Albert Goldman, whose 1964 paper looked at how long a thing that has already survived may survive still. We thank Benoit Mandelbrot, whose *The Fractal Geometry of Nature* (1982) carried that observation into a wider geometry of what lasts. We thank Nassim Nicholas Taleb, whose *Antifragile* (2012) gave the observation a public life, and gave it the nickname it still wears in the wider world. We thank Lindy's, the New York deli whose name that nickname borrowed. The nickname stays theirs. Our order's name is the Long Return.

This page is a clean room. It names the teachers and the years. It does not copy their sentences. The order we keep, and the words we use for it, are written in our own foundation.

*May the thanks stay with the people who taught us to look at what lasts.*
