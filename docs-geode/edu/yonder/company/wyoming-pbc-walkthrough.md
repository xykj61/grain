## The Eight Stations

**One — the name.** Search availability at wyobiz.wyo.gov's search tool. W.S. 17-16-401 governs names. Bashō PBC searches "Basho." The real filings search "Siya Fund" and "Grain Energy." One state quirk is noted with a smile: entity names beginning with the letter **A** must paper-file for manual review. None of ours do. The name may carry "PBC" or spell the designation the statute asks. Confirm the exact required suffix on the current state form (*verify*).

**Two — the registered agent.** Wyoming requires one with a Wyoming street address. A **signed consent to appointment** files with the articles. A commercial registered agent, for a modest yearly fee, keeps home addresses out of the public record. The same instinct already switched on whois privacy for the domains. **Never a home address here.**

**Three — the public benefit statement.** This is the heart of a PBC's articles under Chapter 31. Name the specific public benefit plainly. Bashō PBC's template line reads:

> *"the specific public benefit of supporting open, contemplative, and ecological literary education."*

Siya Fund's real line names patient capital for regenerative, open-source, service-first technology. Grain Energy's line names open computing built and licensed for every hand. One sentence each, radiant, exact.

**Four — the articles, filed online.** At wyobiz.wyo.gov, choose the profit-corporation formation. Choose the benefit-corporation election the form offers too (*verify the checkbox or article text the current form uses for Chapter 31*). The articles carry:

- the name
- the registered agent and signed consent
- shares authorized (a simple common-stock start is typical; the attorney confirms counts and par)
- the incorporator's name and signature
- the public benefit statement

The filing fee is approximately **$100** (*verify on the fee schedule at filing*). Corporate existence begins when the Secretary of State files the articles.

**Five — the first acts on paper.** The incorporator names the board. The board then adopts bylaws and appoints officers by written consent. The template consent line, filled for the real company, reads:

> *"RESOLVED, that Sara Livermore is appointed Chief Executive Officer and Keaton Dunsford is appointed Chief Technology Officer."*

Bylaws follow a standard benefit-corporation skeleton the attorney blesses. Chapter 31 expects a benefit officer or benefit-report duty. It is assigned here (*verify the chapter's exact reporting cadence*).

**Six — the federal minute.** An **EIN** comes from the IRS: online, free, in the founders' own hands. It never comes through a paid intermediary. Then comes the bank account in the company's name. Its books open from day one. The CPA's first hour is cheaper than the CPA's hundredth.

**Seven — the annual rhythm.** Wyoming's **annual report** is due the first day of the anniversary month. It files online. Its license tax runs a minimum around **$60**, asset-scaled (*verify*). The PBC's **benefit report** rides the cadence Chapter 31 sets. Calendar both. The day the articles come back stamped sets the clock for each.

**Eight — the investment step, separately and carefully.** Siya Fund may lead Grain Energy's seed one day. That would be a securities transaction between related parties. Instrument choice and board approvals on both sides belong there. So do disclosures and any exemption filings, all inside the **attorney's and CPA's confirmed territory**. This walkthrough stops at the door and says so in bold.
