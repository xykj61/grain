## Fun has a rank

The order in `context/TAME_GUIDANCE.md` reads: **safety first, performance second, joy of the craft
third.**

When those three pull against each other, safety wins. When safety and performance already agree,
**joy earns the vote.**

That is a surprising sentence for a beginner, and it is seated on purpose. A bound exists so the
program runs on a small machine a household can afford. **Joy exists so a person returns tomorrow
and writes the next clear function.** Devotion that drains the hand is devotion that ends.

Fun, here, is the gladness of making something true and watching it stand.

## Gladness is an instrument

Lesson 3 gave you the test: a path with heart makes for a joyful journey, and makes you stronger as
you go.

You use gladness the way a carpenter uses a square. If the next lane feels like a grind with no one
served, the heart has already spoken. If a small witness turns green and your shoulders drop, that
is data.

The four promises keep the fun clean. The tool serves the one holding it. What it knows, you can
see. The door out stays open. It rests quietly in a life.

**Fun that hooks a person for someone else's gain has left the path.** Fun that tells the truth and
then lets you walk away is the kind this house builds.

## The happy zone: where play lives in the code

Picture a kitchen.

The **happy zone** is the counter you control: bowls, knives, a recipe you finish in minutes. Every
piece is checked by a fast, isolated witness. You stir, you taste, you know.

The **thin edge** is the dining room where guests arrive: devices, networks, other machines. A few
honest checks live there, because only the world can tell the truth about the world.

At every **seam** -- the pass-through between kitchen and dining room -- this tree wants two small
families of answers. One asks: did we pose the right question, and can we handle every answer that
comes back? The other asks: did the other side honour the agreement?

They mirror each other. Multiplication of surprises becomes addition of known shapes. Correctness
climbs the way a well-made game does: one fair rule, then another, each checkable.

The blessing that closes that brief is the play-sentence:

> May the design feel the gentle pressure of its tests, and grow clearer for it, chapter after chapter.

**That pressure is the fun.** A witness cheap enough to run often is a toy you are allowed to use.
Coverage theatre -- numbers that look like care and act like none -- stays outside. A few real-world
moments are budgeted in the open, like the cost of a good ticket.

## Green is the first toy

Spend an hour, and watch something turn green.

A **witness** printing green is the beginner's prize. The machine ran. The claim held. You saw it
with your own eyes.

That is the oldest fun in computing, and it needs almost no vocabulary:

1. You write a small instruction.
2. The machine follows it.
3. The world answers.
4. You grin, or you adjust, and you try again.

`first.rish` in the first-hour path is that toy in this house. Count the rooms. Hear the tree speak
a number. Feel the assertion hold.

Later toys sit on the same shelf: the Glow desk hopping down to Rye and running green; Rishi
answering in a voice you can read aloud; a supervisor's ladder where each rung proves one new
property, like levels in a game that takes its rules seriously; a bounded frame grid, a tiny stage
with walls you can see.

**Play inside a bound is still play.** The bound is what keeps the stage from eating the house.

## A day that leaves room for the grin

Learn in the morning. Build in the working hours. Gather in the evening. Rest at night.

Fun needs the fourth beat. Meditation as pace, from the five pillars, is the same kindness:
attention regenerates when a chapter is allowed to end. **A lap that closes at commit is a game with
a final whistle.** Infinite overtime is how joy leaks out.

Daily service includes the small glad act: run the cheap witness, read one foundations page, write
one honest sentence in the log. Love as a schedule still gets to enjoy the hour.

| Prema | Fun on this tree |

| Heart-test | Gladness as a measuring tool |
| Seva | A rhythm that includes rest |

Bhakta Style already knew this. It **encourages by being accurate**: *here is the step, and here is
what you will see when it works.* That sentence makes room for delight without flattery.
