Two pocket dreams, one shopping honesty, and three words that keep a thing worth loving.

---

## First, the three words: SLC

From `context/SIMPLE_LOVABLE_COMPLETE.md`. The teacher is Jason Cohen; the tree keeps his essay in
`gratitude/` and speaks the idea in its own voice.

**Simple** means narrow on purpose. One job. An honest edge. A mind can hold it.

**Lovable** means a person wants it today. It feels good in the hand. It runs on a machine you hold.

**Complete** means the loop closes. Whatever the work claims, it finishes, inside the bound it
named. You ask no one to forgive a hole you left in the middle.

Three questions before a thing is offered:

1. Does it hold one clear scope, honestly bounded?
2. Would a person genuinely want to use it today?
3. Does the loop close fully inside that scope?

When all three hold, the work is ready to share, whatever its size.

Gall's Law sits beside it: **a working whole grows from a smaller working whole.** The first such
whole named here is Rishi and Mantra as one loop -- type a command, watch it run, keep a version,
call it back. Simple, because it is one circle. Lovable, because it is yours on your own metal.
Complete, because the circle closes.

A pocket device that tried to be "the whole future of computing" on day one would fail SLC. A pocket
that does *one* closed loop beautifully already passes.

## Two rooms for two devices

**Running room -- what you can touch today**

- An installable Android package whose grants follow the phone's own permission model (`docs/TUBE.md`).
- A GrapheneOS path on a supported Pixel.
- Rishi on a desk you already own -- the first whole, in software form.

**Designing room -- what the pitches describe**

- **Mantrapod**: a pocket computer with a paper-like screen, no radios, memory that forgets when you
  set it down, a key you hold, a body that opens with a screwdriver.
- **Grainphone**: the phone track. Today that track *runs* as the Android package on GrapheneOS. The
  name points at a future pocket that speaks Grain as its native tongue. Treat the finished
  Grainphone as design until a witness says otherwise.

Both dreams serve the same love: a tool that hands attention back, keeps little, and belongs to the
person holding it.

---

## How a Mantrapod would be safer

Safer in the sense Lesson 5 taught: person, machine, records.

**No radios of any kind.** A device that is not listening is hard to reach against your will, and
hard to follow across a street. It speaks through a single wire, only when you plug that wire in.
**The thin edge of the world becomes a cable you chose.**

**Memory that forgets.** Working memory holds the page while power is on. Set the pod down, and the
work dissolves. What you keep, you keep on purpose. *A tool that keeps nothing cannot leak what it
never held.*

**A key that stays.** One seed on a small secure element. One half never leaves the device; one half
can be shown so another person can confirm your word.

**Repair you can see.** Screws rather than glue. Battery on a pull-tab. Screen in a quarter hour. A
card naming each part and its recycling path. **Safety includes years of use and a clean end** --
harming a landfill is still harm.

**A reflective screen.** Room light by day, warm amber when the room is dark. Attention safety is
safety too, and joy takes the vote once the floor is down.

That is the Mantrapod's safety story, **told as design.** It becomes true when boards exist you can
hold.

---

## How a Grainphone would be safer

A phone has a harder job. People need maps, a call, a bus ticket. Radios stay. So safety here is
*permission, enclosure, and a smaller ask*.

**The platform already practised saying no.** GrapheneOS on a Pixel is an elder on this road:
sandboxes, sensors you can switch off, a boot hash you can read. The Grain package walks with that
model -- grants for storage, network and sensors, named in a closed table, given or taken whole.

**Grain still wants less.** Collect little. Encrypt with keys held elsewhere. Prefer a fold that
stays on the device. Enclosure for helpers, so an assistant works in a room with walls.

**Two Rooms on the home screen.** What runs stays labelled running; what is still a Grain-native
phone stays labelled design. **A person deserves that honesty in a pocket.**

Courtesy for the neighbours: an ordinary iPhone or Pixel already carries a decade of lock screens,
app sandboxes and secure elements. Those are real gifts. Grain's claim is a stricter diet and a
readable stack, grown from those gifts rather than thrown against them.

## Buying and assembling, with the rooms kept honest

### Path A -- the pocket you can buy this month

The running-room path.

| Piece | What you are buying | Rough household budget, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Pixel that GrapheneOS supports | Used 8a / 9a class, or a current small Pixel | about $200-$550 |
| GrapheneOS install | Your time, a cable, another computer | $0 plus an evening |
| The Grain tree | Clone and build on a desk | $0 for the software |
| Optional Faraday sleeve | Pocket discipline when radios should sleep | $15-$40 |

**Difficulty:** an afternoon to a careful weekend if the model is on the support list. The hard parts
are unlocking, verifying the boot hash, and living with a quieter app store. A beginner can follow a
written guide.

This is SLC-complete **as a hardened phone plus a Grain app.** It is still a phone with radios, and
it is the honest Grainphone-shaped thing you can hold in 2026.

### Path B -- a learning cousin of the Mantrapod

A small board, an e-ink or cheap screen, a battery pack, a case.

| Piece | Cousin stand-in | Rough parts budget |
|---|---|---|
| Board | A documented RISC-V or low-power ARM single-board computer | $50-$150 |
| Screen | Small e-ink module or simple LCD | $30-$150 |
| Power | USB battery brick and a cable | $15-$40 |
| Storage | The card the board already uses | $10-$20 |
| Case | Printed or tin | $10-$40 |
| Keyboard | USB or a small deck | $20-$80 |

Parts often land near **$150-$450** before mistakes.

**Difficulty:** medium-hard for a patient beginner; high if you want it to *feel* like one object.
Weeks, rather than an evening. You will meet drivers, power budgets, and a case that survives.

What you get is a teaching pod. It will likely still carry a radio unless you choose a rare module
without one, and it will store files on a card. **It is not yet the forgetful, screw-locked pod in
the pitch** -- and it can still be SLC: one job, run a shell on a quiet screen from a battery,
complete at that scope.

### Path C -- a true Mantrapod from parts

The pitch's early assembled figure is **$350-$550** at small batch, aimed at a decade of life.

**Treat that number as a projection.** Horizon: once boards and cases exist in a small run.
Assumption: a workshop that already knows the design. Confidence: plausible, still unproven in a
public shop.

What a household cannot buy as a kit today: a mainboard with *no* radio traces; the keyed
working-memory module; a secure element provisioned with *your* seed in your hands; the metal body
and pull-tab battery sled; a reflective display bought in ones at a kind price; a workshop already
holding that parts list.

**Difficulty: high.** A single home-lab attempt would likely cost more than $550 and still miss the
no-radio and forgetful-memory promises, because those live in the board layout and the memory
architecture rather than in a shopping cart.

**SLC's kindness here is to refuse a half-built pod that asks forgiveness.** Ship the cousin that is
whole. Keep the pitch in the designing room until the loop closes.

## Holding both to SLC

| Question | Mantrapod (design) | Grainphone path (running cousin) |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | One quiet pocket for writing and keys; a wire when the world is needed | One hardened phone plus Grain's grants |
| Lovable | Paper-like light, a screwdriver, attention handed back | A phone you already know how to carry, quieter |
| Complete | When type-run-version-recall and commit-to-wire all work on that hardware | When the OS boots, grants are visible, the package installs |

Joy stays third. A gorgeous screen that phones home has failed the floor. A brick that is perfectly
silent and miserable to type on has failed lovable.

## A picture for the budget

You want a quiet room and a street door.

The **Mantrapod** is the quiet room: no window on the street, a single keyed door. Expensive to
build well. Cheap to live in, once it exists.

The **Grainphone** is the street door with a good lock and a chain. You can buy the door this month.
You still choose when it opens.

A household on a budget builds the door first. A household that wants to learn the room builds a
cousin and keeps a written list of which pitch promises the cousin still lacks. **That is
custody-first shopping: spend first on what you can verify.**
