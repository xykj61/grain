## Two old words, given once

**Bhakti** is devotion: a path walked with the heart turned toward what it serves. A *bhakta* is one
who walks that path. This tree borrowed the name for its open-door register. Bhakta Style is Radiant
teaching written for a reader who has never written a line of code, and who is trusted with the
whole truth anyway. The path is open. Scholarship is welcome. It is not the ticket in.

**Prema** is that devotion ripened into love. In the older teaching, bhakti is the practice and the
turning; prema is the love the practice matures into -- steady, personal, glad to serve. *Prema
bhakti* is loving devotion: service that has become love, and love that still shows up as service.

Grain keeps this in its own tongue so a reader of any household can enter. Teachers are named in
`gratitude/`; the foundations keep the teaching and leave the roll call to that room.

**The farm sentence, said once.** This software is built so a household can keep its own records,
grow food from what the land returns, and run on a machine small enough to afford. Devotion here
faces that life.

## The test before the tools

A path with heart makes for a joyful journey. You travel it gladly. It makes you stronger as you go.
The disciplined hand and the devoted heart are one practice.

That is the beginner's first instrument in this room. Before a language, before a supervisor, before
a key: **does this work have heart?** If it does, take it up. If the heart leaves, release it.

You will use that test on a supervisor, on a commit message, on a feature that wants a new radio.
The question stays the same size.

## The ground under the specifications

Four stones, in the order the foundations lay them.

**Work as offering.** Each function carries attention: a clear name, a trustworthy shape. The care
is the gift, and the gift is the work itself. Results are held lightly so joy can stay in the craft.

**Building as service.** At every level the question is *how does this serve?* Competence turns into
care when the architecture lets another person verify what the tool did. An append-only log is
service you can see.

**Freedom as what we build toward.** Freedom *for*: to repair, to understand, to change, to create.
Root keys you hold. Whole histories you can read. A door out that stays open.

**Grace as the ground.** The work begins on gifts: open instruction sets, teachers, a commons
already paid for by other hands. Gratitude is how grace stays in view. That is what `gratitude/`
is for.

Technical excellence and devotion are unified in serving people and something larger. That unity is
prema bhakti in this tree's mouth: love that still writes a clear function, service that still knows
it stands on gifts.

## Five pillars of direction

These are the standing directions this tree steers by.

1. **Non-harming as the ceiling.** A feature is measured by whether a person is safer. Custody
   doctrine lives here: keep little, encrypt with a key held elsewhere, delete by destroying the
   key. The motto is *build nothing that destroys*.

2. **Devotion as service.** The work faces outward and stays with a person's good.

3. **Meditation as pace.** Slowness that names bounds and closes seasons. Attention is scarce. It
   regenerates when a chapter is allowed to end.

4. **Sound as the spoken edge.** A name has to survive being read aloud. Warm, clear, sayable. This
   is why Radiant asks you to read a draft out loud, and why Rishi is built as a shell a voice can
   live in.

5. **Scripture as compass and silo.** Compass pages guide. Silo discipline keeps an idea coherent in
   one room so it can be found again.

Read the five as one motion: harm no one, serve someone, keep a pace a human can love, speak names
that land, return to the pages that remember why.

## Love as a schedule

**Nitya seva** means service that is daily and unending -- a small act performed always.

The profundity is structural: **devotion is a schedule, love is a rhythm, and the sacred is whatever
you refuse to skip.**

That is the bridge from prema to the working day. The council grid in the loop seed crosses five
elements with three modalities: fifteen seats, three documents a lap. A ship deep-reads one row per
lap, so the canon returns to living awareness within a working day.

Prema that never returns to the work is a mood. Here it becomes a cadence: open the roster, read the
canon row, take reds first, commit at the close of the lap.

You already know this shape from Lesson 2. `SOURCE.md` puts keys in Part Two. The itinerary puts
reds before new making. **Custody first is devotion with a bound on it.**

## Four promises a tool can keep

This is prema written as an ethic a program can honour.

1. **Do no harm.** The tool serves the one holding it, and only them.
2. **Tell the truth.** What the tool knows, the user can see. Witnesses are this promise made runnable.
3. **Hold lightly.** Carry what is used. Modular parts. The door out stays open.
4. **Be kind.** Reach every body and language. Stay quiet by default. Rest easily in a life.

When Lesson 5 reaches TAME, these four will still be the reason those laws exist. Bounds keep
promises 1 and 3. Witnesses keep promise 2. Bhakta Style keeps promise 4.
