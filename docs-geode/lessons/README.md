## What these are

Reading walks, written for someone who has **never written a line of code** and is trusted with
the whole truth anyway. That register has a name here: **Bhakta**, the open door.

They are **reading walks** rather than exercises. Hands come in the first-hour tutorial. These
build the map a pair of hands will want.

Each lesson does three things: shows a concrete thing before naming it, gives every coined word
its plain meaning the first time it appears, and marks whether what it describes **runs today** or
is **designed and intended**. That last habit is [Two Rooms](../../context/TWO_ROOMS.md), and it is
the courtesy that lets a beginner trust a page.

## How to walk them

**In order, one an evening**, is the shape they were written for. Each ends by offering two or
three honest next walks, so you choose the branch rather than being marched down one.

**Skip ahead freely.** Lesson 6 stands alone if you came for the hardware question. Lesson 3
stands alone if you came for the reasons.

**Read a lesson beside the thing it describes.** When a lesson names `foundations/`, open
`foundations/`. The lessons are a companion to the tree rather than a replacement for it.

---

## What these lessons keep honest

**A number in a lesson carries its date.** Counts of witnesses and modules move every week. Where
a lesson gives one, it says when it was read, and the standing habit is to run the measurement
rather than trust the sentence.

**A design says it is a design.** Several beautiful things in this tree are drawn and not yet
built. The lessons name which is which, every time, because a beginner has no way to tell from
the prose alone and deserves to be told.

**Teachers are named.** Ideas that arrived from elsewhere say so, and the people who found them
are named in [`gratitude/`](../../gratitude/).
