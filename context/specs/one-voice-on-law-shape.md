# One Voice on a Law's Shape

*A patch either applies or it does not, so two benches may trade patches all day without harm. A law is different: its whole value is its steadiness, and trading positions on one destroys the very property being edited. This spec names how a law-shape question moves through the workshop, so that a question is answered once rather than answered repeatedly in opposite directions.*

## The rule

A question about the **shape of a law** -- what a spec permits, forbids, or names -- moves in exactly three beats:

**One proposal.** A bench raises the question once, with its reasoning and its measurement in the same breath. It proposes; it does not act.

**One word.** Keaton rules. The ruling is the end of the question, and the tree is changed once to match it.

**Then bench silence.** Neither bench re-opens, re-argues, praises, critiques, seats, or returns the shape. Whatever the tree holds is what both benches write against.

## When a bench changes its mind

A bench may of course learn something after it proposes. What it does with that is fixed: it writes **one sentence, marked *proposal to revisit***, and then falls silent until Keaton rules again.

A change of mind is never an action. It is never an undo, never a second seating, never a return of a spend. **A withdrawal executed by one hand while an approval is executed by the other is precisely how a tree oscillates** -- both hands faithful, the record swinging.

## The approval clause

*"Seat yes all recommendations"* approves the **leans standing at that moment**. If a bench withdraws a lean after such an approval, the withdrawal is a **new proposal**, not an undo of the approval. The seating that already happened stands until Keaton's next word.

## What this does not cover

Code, tools, witnesses, and content move by the ordinary crossing: propose, apply, witness, land. This spec binds **law-shape questions only** -- specs, gates, and the words that govern the workshop. Trading patches remains safe and welcome.

## The measure of success

A law that reads the same at the end of a long day as it did at the start, unless Keaton moved it once on purpose. Steadiness is the property; the rule exists to protect it.
