## What Teacher Style is

Teacher Style is Bhakta Style with the temple door left as a classroom door. Everything Bhakta asks, Teacher keeps: lead with what is, active voice, one new idea at a time, a coined term named in plain words on first use. Gauge's first rule still sits at the front: **don't be too smart about it.**

The prose is plain American English. A reader who has never written code, and who has never met this tree, can still take the next step. Kyri names the play. Kyli keeps the huddle kind.

## Sports metaphors, our own

This register borrows the *feel* of a practice, never a copyrighted playbook.

- **Film.** After a sitting, watch what actually happened. The session log is the film. Read it before the next prompt.
- **Huddle.** A short expanding-prompt is the huddle. Everyone hears the same next play.
- **One possession.** Finish one green line before you stack another season.
- **The bench.** Tools and witnesses sit ready. You send in the one the play needs.
- **Conditioning.** Fascia health in this tree is the same idea as keeping connective tissue ready: links that resolve, fossils that still point, pages a newcomer can walk without a tear.

A metaphor earns its place after the mechanism sentence. "We ran `git log -1 --format=%s` in `seed/` and read `incense`" is the play. "The seed now wears the team's name" is the color.

## What Teacher never does

Teacher Style never pastes another coach's sentences, slogans, drills, or book chapter. The Silo Technique is the wall. Gratitude lives in [`../gratitude/american-coaching-craft.md`](../gratitude/american-coaching-craft.md). If a line still sounds like a famous locker-room poster, it has not been siloed yet. Write it again in this house's words.

## When to write in Teacher Style

Acme employee pages, docs-geode tutorials that teach a sitting rather than a compiler, Cursor CLI walkthroughs, and any first-hour page whose reader may meet both computing and this tree on the same morning.

Bhakta remains the door for devotion-shaped manuals. Teacher is the door for the same care in civic, workplace, and classroom English.
