## Why one page rather than seven

The plan this comes from said *a living index page per cluster*, and one page is better. Two
reasons, both measured rather than felt.

**Seven pages would be seven near-empty rooms.** The smallest cluster here holds two documents. The
same arithmetic already decided the tools fold this week: grouping 1,891 files by first word gave
296 rooms of which 205 held one or two files, and that was rejected for trading one crowded room for
two hundred thin ones. Seven index pages averaging five links each is the same trade at a smaller
scale.

**Gall's Law says start with the simple thing that works.** A complex system that works is invariably
found to have evolved from a simple system that worked. One page with seven sections works today,
and any section that outgrows it can leave and become its own page, carrying its links with it. The
reverse move -- seven thin pages merging later -- costs seven sets of inbound references.

**Single-strandedness holds either way.** The dated documents are the record; this is the map.
Neither tries to be the other, and nothing here restates what a document says -- it says what the
document is *for*, in one line, so you can choose.

## What this index promises, and what it does not

**It promises that every link resolves**, which is now a checked claim rather than a hope:
`tools/r/readme_reach_witness.rish` gates broken links in living documents at zero, and this page is
living.

**It promises nothing about completeness.** Thirty-nine documents out of 629 in this room, chosen
because they are the ones a reader actually opened when walking the public seed. A document's
absence here says only that nobody has walked to it yet.

**The dated documents keep every word they wrote.** Nothing was edited to build this map, and no
cross-links were added to any of the thirty-nine. That was the whole reason to make a map instead.
