## The three laws the re-map seats

- **One tree per star.** Every loop body works its own clone, born whole by
  [`../tools/l/birth_a_clone.rish`](../tools/l/birth_a_clone.rish) -- remotes, tracked hooks,
  named submodules, toolchain, its own rye and rishi, signing inheritance, one witness GREEN
  inside before the first lap. The interactive Sound keeps the field `~/grain` as the primary;
  a shared checkout between a loop and anything else is the retired shape.
- **Claim-as-override.** When a lane's agent-doable queue is empty, the loop takes the oldest
  unclaimed booked lap from any lane rather than stopping: the claim is named in the session
  log and its day-shelf row, the lane's owner reviews at their next sitting, custody gates
  still stop the lap, and a claim never touches a peer's in-flight work. Seated in the three
  Claude-loop prompts; the Codex seats are walled by their pinned lane scanners instead, and a
  walled seat parks what its wall refuses.
- **The ruling seats stay put.** Custody gates, Glow language rulings, and design words still
  stop every loop and wait for the interactive stars -- which is what makes desktop interaction
  optional rather than load-bearing: the fleet builds around every gate all day, and a sitting
  is where rulings land, never where work resumes.

## What the supervised-Codex progression teaches

Carried from the cardinal seat into every walled loop: **the outer jail bounds the inner
sandbox**; **a jailed inner lap proves scoped witnesses only** (the cold and hot roster is a
bench instrument -- inside a jail it reads its own walls as reds, which answers nothing); **a
lap ends in one bounded outcome** -- one staged candidate the supervisor alone signs, or one
written custody or parked line. And from `20260829`'s three walls in one day: **a bound sized
to an instrument is re-measured when the instrument versions**, and **a wall that fires and
parks can stay forever, where a wall that fires and strands gets removed** -- the mind-park
stash rung is that sentence made mechanism.
