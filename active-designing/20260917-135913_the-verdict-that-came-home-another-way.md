A page of this lane runs an elder page's falsifier and reaches a verdict. The verdict is worth
something to exactly one reader: the person who opens the **elder** page and reads a claim that has
since been graded. So `falsifier_verdict_home_scan.sh` asks a question about **position** -- when a
child declares `row N of <elder>`, does the elder carry a `Row N erratum` line? -- and it has held

number, so it reached neither declaration form, and the scan's own header priced that blind spot

## What the instrument does now, and what it refuses to do

`rowless_gradings`, `rowless_answered`, `rowless_unanswered`, `rowless_elder_missing`,
`rowless_outside_roster`, `rowless_outside_keys` and `rowless_answer_shapes` are **reported, never
gated.** The reason is the founding paper again: the declaration side rests on a typed three-key
roster, which is a spelling, and a gate whose population is drawn by a roster reds the lap a lane
reaches for a sixth key nobody typed. So the reading is a **floor** on its population, and
`rowless_outside_roster` **prices the roster's own blindness** in the same pass -- 150 today, with
all 22 outside keys named -- so the roster can never quietly become the population.

**The kill condition, in one command.** Read the next twenty pages of this lane that grade an elder
whole. If the three-key roster names fewer than half of them, the roster is the wrong
instrument and the evidence is already sitting in `rowless_outside_keys`. **Horizon:** twenty pages,
which this lane writes in roughly a fortnight. **Confidence:** moderate. The roster held six of
six today, and six is a small number to seat a rule on.

**Assumption, named because it is load-bearing:** that a whole-page verdict is worth carrying home
at all. A reader on the elder page meets the graded claim either way; what the erratum buys is that
they meet the grading beside it. Should 155 lines of census buy too little, the honest move is to withdraw this half as its
founding paper withdrew the ledger.

## Where this reading stops

**A grading in prose alone**, under no door key at all, is invisible to both halves and always will
be by this method. **Whether a verdict, once home, is any good** is the other instrument's job.
And **whether `rowless_unanswered` should become a wall** now that it reads zero is a question this
page raises and hands up rather than answering: the reading is clean today, a wall would red the lap
a lane grades an elder and walks away, and the same wall would stay silent on the sixth key. That
trade is Keaton's word, and it is the same shape Bakery handed up one instrument over this morning.

May the verdict find the reader who needs it, and may the shape it arrives in be one nobody had to
remember.
