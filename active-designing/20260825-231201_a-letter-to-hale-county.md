## What this is, and what it is deliberately not

Keaton asked for a plain-English letter carrying the Brazos study's fixes to Hale County's own
leadership -- the County Judge, who presides over the Commissioners Court, with the county's
other civic offices beside them. This is that draft. In Texas the county judge is the county's
highest elected officer and its natural convener, while the water reclamation plant itself
belongs to the City of Plainview -- so the letter asks the Judge for the county's *table*, and
keeps the city's works the city's. It follows the works-town study's first-lap order -- the
receipt, then the reuse lease, then the retrofit, a new plant nowhere -- and the readings of
2026-08-25 (the permit current to 2030 with 1.5 MGD of headroom and the reuse authorization
still unclaimed; the sludge landfilled next door; the city's own $125,000 reuse feasibility
study in this year's budget). It offers a conversation and a gauge board alone: the fund, the
sleeve, and every allocation stay parked behind their own gates.

Two blanks stay blank on purpose: the sender's affiliation line and the reply address are
Keaton's to fill. The cc line is written wide -- Keaton trims it to taste. Sending, editing, or
discarding this is one person's choice, and it is recorded here so the tree remembers either way.

## The letter

```
To:      The Honorable County Judge, Hale County, Texas
Cc:      The Hale County Commissioners Court
         The Mayor and City Manager, City of Plainview
         The Superintendent, Plainview Independent School District
         The County Extension Office, Texas A&M AgriLife

Subject: A small idea for the water our county returns to Running Water Draw

Dear Judge,

I am writing with one small, concrete idea about something this county
already does well, and mostly without credit.

Plainview's water reclamation plant, beside Running Water Draw, returns
about 1.8 million gallons of treated water every day, against a permit
that allows 3.3 million. That return matters more than most people
know. Below the draw lie the White River and the reservoir that
supplies drinking water to Ralls, Spur, Post, and Crosbyton -- our
neighbors in the counties east of us. In a real sense, four other
towns drink what Hale County finishes. The plant's record is clean --
no violations across the last three years of federal reporting -- and
I believe that record deserves to be visible, and celebrated, at the
county's own table.

I am bringing this to your office rather than only to City Hall
because the idea is bigger than one city's fence line: the draw
crosses county land, the growers who could use this water farm county
ground, and the towns downstream sit in our neighbors' counties. A
county judge's table is where all of those can sit at once.

Here is the idea, in three steps, smallest first.

1. A public reading. One sign -- a gauge board -- in Runningwater Draw
   Regional Park, updated with the plant's daily numbers: gallons
   returned, and the basic quality readings the city already reports
   to the state. Families at the soccer fields would walk past the
   county's own honest number every weekend. Cost: a sign and a
   habit. What it earns: trust, and a county that can see its own
   good work. The park and plant are the city's; the county's voice
   beside the city's would make the sign a county pride rather than
   a utility notice.

2. A working use for the water. Texas law (Chapter 210) lets a city
   authorize its treated water for irrigating non-food crops, and
   records show that authorization is still unclaimed here, with
   about 1.5 million gallons a day of permitted headroom at the
   plant. A modest lease of land beside the plant, irrigated with
   reclaimed water, could host a fiber-crop trial -- the kind the
   AgriLife station here already runs under irrigation. Water the
   city has already cleaned would grow a product for county growers
   instead of only leaving town. The city has budgeted a reuse
   feasibility study this year; this would be a small, visible first
   proof of the same idea, and the county's blessing would help it
   land.

3. Down the road, worth studying together: the plant's solids --
   about two thousand tons a year, now landfilled -- hold nutrients
   that modern recovery equipment can turn into fertilizer product
   for the same county ground. That is a bigger conversation with
   its own engineering and costs, and I raise it only as a horizon,
   after the first two steps prove themselves.

I am not selling equipment and I am not asking the county or the city
for money. Step one is nearly free. Step two uses water and land the
city already owns, under a state rule made for exactly this. If any
of it interests you, I would welcome thirty minutes with you -- and
with the Mayor or City Manager at the same table, if you would have
them -- in person or by phone, at your convenience.

Thank you for the work the city's utilities staff and the county's
growers already do. Their record is the reason this letter was worth
writing.

Respectfully,

[NAME]
[AFFILIATION -- Keaton's to fill]
[REPLY ADDRESS AND PHONE -- Keaton's to fill]
```

## Why the letter says what it says

**It leads with a strength the Judge can verify.** The plant's clean record is real (federal
compliance reporting, read 2026-08-25), and the four downstream towns give a county judge the
regional-neighbor framing that office actually holds. **It respects who owns what.** The works
are the city's; the letter says so twice and asks the county only for what a county judge
uniquely has -- the table where city, growers, schools, and downstream neighbors sit at once.
**It asks for the smallest thing first**, keeping the study's receipt-lease-retrofit order as
three numbered steps. **It names no company, no fund, and no purchase**, because the money
parks and a first letter that smells of a pitch is a letter that gets binned. **Every number
traces**: the 1.8 and 3.3 MGD to the permit and the city's own page, the four downstream towns
to the reservoir's service record, the unclaimed Chapter 210 authorization and the
feasibility-study line item to the readings of 2026-08-25. **And it tells the truth about who
is writing**: a person with an idea, asking for thirty minutes.

## What would make this letter wrong

A fresh violation on the plant's record between now and sending. A Chapter 210 authorization
that exists in program files the databases miss. A county-city relationship where the Judge's
convening would read as stepping on the city's toes -- a local judgment only the sender can
make. Or a decision that Post, not Plainview, opens the corridor -- in which case this draft
molts to Garza County's judge rather than being sent as-is. Check all four on the day of
sending; the letter's claims are dated to 2026-08-25.
