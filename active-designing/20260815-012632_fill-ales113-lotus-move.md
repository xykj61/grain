## The crux, chosen Lindy-first

ALES112's **duplicate** repeats a span in place — the clip grows by exactly the span. This rung names the other gesture a keeper reaches for when a bar is in the wrong place: **move.** Circle a bar, drag it, and the span lifts out of one spot and lands at another — the verse pulled ahead of the chorus, the stray breath slid to a rest, the loop nudged whole to a new beat. Where duplicate *copies* a span (the clip grows), move *relocates* it (the clip's length holds). Naming it beside duplicate completes the copy/relocate pair on a selection: repeat it where it is, or carry it somewhere new.

## The rule, stated once

`move(clip, start, count, dest)` lifts the `count` samples starting at `start` out of the timeline and reinserts them at `dest`, where `dest` names a position in the timeline **after the span is removed** (`0 ≤ dest ≤ len − count`). It stands on the two proven timeline primitives: the span is first copied into one bounded scratch buffer (`max_clip` samples, its budget named), then ALES2's `cut(clip, start, count)` removes it in place (the clip shrinks by `count`), then ALES2's `splice(clip, dest, span)` reinserts it (the clip grows back by `count`) — so the net length is unchanged and no third primitive is invented. Every range is refused **before any write**, reusing `timeline.EditError` whole. One bounded scratch buffer, no other allocation.

Choosing post-removal coordinates for `dest` keeps the law crisp: because the head `[0, start)` has length `start`, `dest = start` is the identity, and the inverse is exactly `move(dest, count, start)`.

## The four laws

1. **THE MOVE LAW** — after `move(clip, start, count, dest)`, the clip holds the span relocated: the removed timeline `original[0..start] ++ original[start+count..len)` with the span reinserted at `dest`, checked against a hand-built vector at a forward move, a backward move, and a move to the head.
2. **THE INVERSE LAW** — the crux tying this rung to ALES2's own cut and splice. `move(start, count, dest)` then `move(dest, count, start)` returns the original **byte for byte**, the span carried out and carried home — move is its own inverse under swapped endpoints, proven side by side.
3. **THE PRESERVATION / PEAK LAW** — move relocates existing samples and invents none: the length is unchanged and the multiset of samples is unchanged, so the peak magnitude re-measured through ALES13 is **unchanged** at any move (the loudest sample simply sits at a new index).
4. **THE DEGENERATE / REFUSAL LAW** — `count = 0` is the identity, and `dest = start` is the identity (the span reinserted exactly where it was removed); a `start` past the samples, a `count` past the remainder, or a `dest` past `len − count` each refuses `BadRange` — each before any write, the clip untouched.

## Honest scope

Software only, purely local. A bounded in-process buffer of i16 PCM on one bench, siloed to `lotus/`. It copies a span into one bounded scratch buffer and reinserts it through the proven `cut`/`splice` — it invents no sample, changes no value, and reads no byte past `len`. No real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real speaker.
