## What the center holds

The clean-slate README carries three promises under the heading *What Grain gives you*, and every
one of them is a gate rather than a feature:

1. **Bound everything.** Every allocation, collection, and loop names a maximum.
2. **Witnesses on metal.** A thing earns the word *working* when a program has checked it.
3. **Two rooms.** Checkable and vision, so a reader knows which claims a witness holds still.

These three are the center. They stay true whether the tree holds ten files or ten thousand, and
they are stated in Bhakta at the Door setting -- plain words a newcomer grasps without a tour of
the codebase. The clean-slate tree proves the point by standing on exactly this register and
carrying a metrics block that measures itself against it.

The `docs-geode/` voice is the second weight at the center. Eight lessons, a first-hour tutorial,
an API reference, a library index, a study guide -- each page declares `Style: Bhakta at the Door
setting, with Radiant warmth` and keeps it. The register IS the product documentation, and the
register is what makes the front door legible to someone who has never read a codebase before.

SOURCE.md is the third center piece: the whole path from nothing to a signed, sandboxed home, in
two parts (the first day, and the signed home). A reader following it ends with a working machine
that signs its own commits and proves its own claims.

## What the periphery holds, and why it stays

The fleet machinery -- eight ships, the baton, the roster, the claim board, the endurance runs --
is scaffolding. It exists to carry the center forward across many hands and many days. It is real
and load-bearing, yet the clean-slate tree carries none of it on its front door, because a newcomer
meeting the work for the first time never needs to know how eight clones coordinate.

The rule rooms (`.claude/rules/`, `.cursor/rules/`) are scaffolding too. They govern how agents
write in this tree, and they are invisible to a reader who arrived through `docs-geode/`.

The REDS ledger, the checkpoints, the waymark registry, the derived spine -- each is
infrastructure a maintainer reads daily and a newcomer never meets. The clean-slate tree mentions
the reds once, as a reading habit worth borrowing, and leaves the machinery out of sight.

## The lesson for this molt

The clean-slate tree is what a newcomer sees. The gate, the docs-geode voice, and the Bhakta
register are what make that first meeting legible. Everything else in the tree exists to keep those
three promises honest. When this checkout takes a step toward the clean-slate shape, the question
to ask is: does this step make the center clearer, or does it add periphery a newcomer would have
to read past?

The `study/` room and its three-condition gate sit at the center. A newcomer who wants to bring
a text into the tree meets the gate on their first attempt, and either the gate is plain or it is
not. A docs-geode page teaching the gate in Bhakta at Door is center work.
