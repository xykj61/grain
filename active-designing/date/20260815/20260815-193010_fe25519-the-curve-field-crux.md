## Why this is the next crux

Season G named five primitives GREEN — BLAKE2b, SHA-512, ChaCha20, Poly1305, and
the ChaCha20-Poly1305 AEAD — and each of those five stood on integer arithmetic
alone. The AEAD's own header states the honest boundary plainly: *"it adds no new
field math (the curve, edwards25519, still wants its own design round)."* Every
one of the remaining Monocypher-parity primitives that the decision wave points
at — **Ed25519** signing (the Lotus signed carry, Kumara identity, Vault,
Comlink) and **X25519** key agreement — stands on one shared foundation: the
finite field **GF(2²⁵⁵ − 19)**, the base field of Curve25519.

Lindy-first, crux-first says: take the durable stone every later thread reaches
for, and take the hardest *still-tractable* move first. The field is exactly that
stone. It is pure arithmetic — no key, no signature, no identity, no network, no
custody gate — so an agent can build it, prove it, and send it. Once the field is
parity-GREEN, both Ed25519 and X25519 become composition rounds over a proven
base, the same way the AEAD was a composition over ChaCha20 and Poly1305.

## What this round builds

`crypto/fe25519.rye` — a field element over GF(2²⁵⁵ − 19), carried in **five
51-bit limbs** (radix 2⁵¹), the natural sibling of Poly1305's five-26-bit-limb
scheme over 2¹³⁰ − 5. The operators:

- `from_bytes` / `to_bytes` — unpack a 255-bit little-endian value (top bit
  masked) into limbs; pack back with a final conditional subtract of *p* so the
  output is the true canonical residue.
- `add` / `sub` — limbwise, `sub` adding a folded 2*p* so no limb goes negative.
- `mul` — schoolbook radix-2⁵¹ product with the **19-fold reduction**: every
  term past limb 4 carries a factor of 19, because 2²⁵⁵ ≡ 19 (mod *p*), exactly
  as Poly1305 folds ×5 because 2¹³⁰ ≡ 5. `u128` intermediates; carry chain back
  to reduced 51-bit limbs.
- `square` — `mul(a, a)`, kept as its own named contract.
- `invert` — Fermat's inverse, *a*^(p−2) = *a*^(2²⁵⁵ − 21), by the documented
  square-and-multiply ladder (254 squarings, skipping the two multiplies the
  exponent's bit pattern omits).

## How parity is proven (two references runnable now)

1. **Known-answer** — canonical round-trips and small products anyone can check
   by hand: `1·1 = 1`, `2·3 = 6`, `to_bytes(from_bytes(x)) = x` for a canonical
   *x*, `a − a = 0`, and `a · invert(a) = 1` for nonzero *a*.
2. **Independent-implementation parity** — Zig's own
   `std.crypto.ecc.Edwards25519.Fe` (radix-2⁵¹, an independent codebase) run
   beside ours over a deterministic spread of field elements: `add`, `sub`,
   `mul`, `sq`, and `invert` compared **byte-for-byte** on `toBytes()`. Because
   the comparison is on canonical bytes, the two implementations may carry limbs
   differently and still must agree exactly.

## Honest scope

Software only, purely local. No key, no signature (a field element is arithmetic,
not an identity — Ed25519's *signing* stays the custody gate), no network, no
funds, no real device. Monocypher-source parity stays the same held-fetch horizon
the other primitives carry; constant-time timing-safety stays a separate named
horizon (the invert ladder is data-independent by construction, yet timing is a
measurement, not a claim). We study the public mathematics of Curve25519 (the
radix-2⁵¹ schedule, the 19-fold reduction, the Fermat inverse) and write our own
Rye; Zig's field is the parity *target*, never a copied line.
