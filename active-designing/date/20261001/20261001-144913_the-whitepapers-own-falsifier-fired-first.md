## Inference: the paper's own falsifier, read against its own conditions

Row 11 asked whether its three definitions would "pull against each other." They do, and plainly:
the wrap-as-bound definition (one axis, proven four times over) and the radius-as-distance
definition (two axes, tested five times over) answer two separate questions -- *is this quantity
bounded* and *is this quantity located* -- and this tree's own measurements favor the first every
time it was asked and have yet to favor the second once. A paper binding both under one word,
"topos," would land exactly where row 11's own falsifier pointed: two ideas sharing one name.

This reads as the ladder succeeding rather than stalling. Row 11's falsifier was written to catch
exactly this outcome, and a falsifier that fires is the method doing its job. The honest paper here
is the one row 11 would have wanted all along once the measurements came in: a report that names
where the synthesis holds and where it still wants separate words.

## What survives, named plainly

- **Periodic wraparound, as a one-axis discipline, is real and worth keeping.** Four rows prove it
  on four different structures (a ring buffer, a witness cycle, a Glow loop bound, a queueing
  backlog), and `caravan/queue.rye`'s own `// invariant: wrap-as-periodic` already lives in the
  tree independent of this ladder. Reach for a ring, a cycle, or a bounded period wherever a
  quantity genuinely repeats.
- **A deliberately non-avalanching key buys locality, at a named cost.** Row 5's closing finding --
  `tools/fixtures/l/locality_key_scan.sh` -- is the one place in the whole ladder where two axes of
  structure (grouping and distance) showed up together, and they arrived as one quantity read three
  ways: a key that helps a reader find neighbors is a key that tells an observer which group a file
  belongs to (**0.5353** correct placement against a **0.3471** no-key baseline, over 44 rooms).
  This stands as a privacy trade-off, a different find from the torus the ladder went looking for,
  and one found honestly while looking.
- **A quorum trades coverage for a square-root cost, and that trade is real and separable from
  "consensus."** Row 8's cost curve (exponent **0.5237** over its two largest grids) is a genuine
  find, mislabeled at first and correctly labeled now.
