## What happened

A water lap opened on `glow/rune_shape.rye` and found something real. The module declares nineteen
refusals in its `ParseError` set and returns thirteen. One of the six is `MissingTagged`, and the
line that would answer it -- `if (i >= n) return error.MissingTuple` at `rune_shape.rye:372` --
fires before the body rune is read, so a source ending after the mold name is blamed on a tuple it
author may have meant as a tagged union.

The lap then asked whether the tree already measured that class. It ran the instrument the baton
names for exactly this:

```
sh tools/fixtures/p/path_absence_scan.sh \
  tools/fixtures/e/error_reach_scan.sh tools/e/error_reach_witness.rish
-> commits_behind=0
-> path tools/fixtures/e/error_reach_scan.sh    here=no upstream=no
-> path tools/e/error_reach_witness.rish        here=no upstream=no
-> verdict=absent
```

Every word of that is true. Both paths were absent here and absent upstream, and the checkout was
current to the commit. The lap went on to build the instrument: a scan, a control pen of
thirty-five legs, a witness, five mutations each biting their own legs.

`tools/e/error_member_reach_witness.rish` had landed **nine hours earlier**, rostered, green, and
its own header names `MissingTagged` as a Glow finding it hands back to the Glow lane.

**This ship built it.** The commit is `848683d03`, `20260911.060527`, and the session log beside it
is `session-logs/date/20260911/20260911-061149_the-refusal-nothing-can-make.kyri` -- a PHEROMONE
lap, on this seat, that morning. The card the afternoon lap opened on carried the account on its
own shelf.

## Why the instrument answered correctly and the lap was still wrong

**`path_absence_scan.sh` answers a question about a NAME.** *Does this path exist, here and
upstream?* It answered that question perfectly, twice, about two paths the tree had yet to hold.

**The lap's real question was about a SUBJECT.** *Does anything in this tree already ask whether a
declared error member is ever returned?* No instrument this tree owns answers that one, and the
lap substituted the name question for the subject question without noticing the substitution.

The substitution is easy to miss because the two look alike from inside. A lap picks names by
describing the subject to itself -- `error_reach`, `error_set_reach` -- so the names it tests feel
like the subject rather than like one spelling of it. The morning lap had described the same
subject as `error_member_reach`. Three words, two of them shared, and the absence check reads them
as
unrelated strings, which is exactly what they are.

**The grep the lap ran beside it had the same shape.** It searched for `error_set`, `dead_error`
and `unreturned` across `tools/`. That found two files, one of which -- the mantra witness --
states the governing sentence *a refusal named and unreachable is a wish*. It passed over the
guard, because that header spells the idea as **reached** where the lap's grep spelled it
as **unreturned**. A vocabulary search finds the vocabulary it was handed, which is the water
row's own cardinal lesson from four days earlier: *a measurement that finds evidence by name
reports the convention it was handed*.

## The card handed over both halves, in adjacent clauses

The account the morning lap wrote closed with a numbered list of open questions, and the afternoon
lap read it at the open. Two of its items stand side by side:

> **YOURS:** (1) **removing the nine narrows a public error set** -- provably inert, and a prior
> hand wrote a decision to keep them. (2) `glow/rune_shape` answers `MissingTuple` where a mold
> stops short of its body rune, ahead of knowing which body was meant.

Item (2) is what sent the afternoon lap to `rune_shape.rye`. Item (1) is the instrument that found
item (2), one line above it. The lap took the finding and rebuilt the finder.

**Why the two read as unrelated from inside.** Item (2) names a module, a rune and a refusal; item
(1) names a count and a policy question. Only one of them looks like an instrument, and neither
spells the instrument's name. A reader arriving with a question about `rune_shape` has every reason
to take item (2) as its whole context.

**The generalisation is the one PATCHOULI booked this morning**, one room over and in a different
mechanism: *three laps rediscovered their own done work today*. That row asks whether a lap must
read the dead-letter box before taking work. This one asks the same question of a lap's own shelved
accounts, and the answer cannot be *read everything*, since the shelves grow by eight accounts a
day.

## What the roster could have answered, and how much it cost to not ask

`construction/standing-equipment.kyri` is the tree's one list of what is measured, and it is the

The row stands in it under `guard error_member_reach`, with a fifteen-line comment naming the

The lap read that file for the first time at the moment it went to add its own row, which is the

the detour ran from the first probe to the discovery, and produced three files that were deleted
unrun.

## The two readings the duplicate did buy, and what happened to them

The rival scan made two distinctions beyond the standing reader, so the honest close was to move
those into the standing guard rather than land a second one beside it.

**A string body is prose.** The standing reader stripped `//` comments before its production pass,
on the stated ground that *a member named only in prose is a member nothing can produce* -- while
leaving string literal bodies standing. Measured `20260911` over 1,972 authored sources: **no
member
is produced solely from inside a literal**, so the hole was empty. Latent rather than live.

**A `//` inside a string opened a comment.** The strip truncated each line at its first slash
pair anywhere, so `"sub//a.txt"` in `amphora/manifest_entry.rye:293` and `"ok|udp://1|fresh|0"` in
`comlink/discovery/gossip.rye:137` each took a real `return error.` off the end of their own line.
**Seven productions left the count that way.** Every one of the seven names was produced somewhere
else too, so `dead_sites` stood at nine before the repair and nine after it.

Both are now one left-to-right walk, since a `//` inside a string opens no comment and a quote
inside a comment opens no string, and those two sentences are one state machine rather than two
substitutions. Four pen legs hold them, the pen runs **27** legs, and two mutations -- masking
removed, and the in-string guard removed -- each bite their own three legs.

**A reader repaired while its gate is quiet is a reader repaired for free**, which is the only
reason this was worth a lap at all: neither hole moved a number today, and both would have moved
one eventually, in the direction that hides a fault.

## What this proposes

**Name the subject beside the path.** The ABSENCE clause on the baton teaches a lap to check that a
path is absent from the fleet as well as from its own checkout, which is a real and hard-won repair
to a real class. One question still waits a step earlier: whether anything already answers the
SUBJECT -- and, as this lap shows, the answer may be *you did, this morning*. A lap
that has decided to build an instrument reads one file first -- the standing roster -- and greps it
for the subject in two or three spellings rather than for the filename it has already chosen.

That is a habit rather than a tool, and it is written here for the same reason the process-signal
clause and the transcript-naming clause are written on the baton: **the command a lap types sits in
no file, so no guard this tree can own will ever reach it.**

**A tool is thinkable, and this page stops short of proposing one.** A roster grep could be folded
into
`path_absence_scan.sh` as a second reading -- *here are the rostered guards whose rows carry your
words*. It would have printed the standing row in this lap. It would also have stayed silent had the
lap handed it `error_reach`, since the standing row spells
`member`. A tool that helps only when the lap already chose the other word is a tool that helps
least exactly when it is needed most, which is why this stops at the habit and says so.

## What it does not reach

Whether the four Glow refusals should be retired or wired up. That is a public error set in a
language module, so it is Keaton's word, and the measurement stands ready for it: four names, three
files. Two of them are already ruled on.
`session-logs/date/20260720/20260720-032713_stoa97-token-mold-spec.bron`
writes, fourteen months back, that `TooFewLines/TooManyLines/MissingTuple` *"remain in ParseError
set (no removal)"* -- so `rune_shape.TooFewLines` and `lower_named_cast.TooFewLines` stand by a
decision somebody made on purpose, and reading them as leftovers would undo it. `MissingTagged` and
`rune_core.NotBarePayload` carry no such record. The guard counts all four; this page leaves them to
that word, with the two that already have one named apart from the two that do not.
