## The sweep that read prose and missed code

Repairing the em dash, I swept the tree for every page quoting that line and found two. The hot
roster pass then reddened `acme_dx`, and under it `tools/o/onboarding_path_witness.rish`, which
holds that exact string as **its contract's acceptance line** -- an `assert ... contains` in a
`.rish` source, which a grep over Markdown pages was never going to reach.

The law was already written and I read it too narrowly. *References are promises*, and the sweep
before a change is whole-tree **across code, docs, logs and comments** -- the collaboration rule
spells all four. A quoted output block is a reference; so is an assertion that pins the same
string. Reading one and not the other is the same fault this whole lap is about, one register
down: **a sweep that reads one spelling of a thing reads one half of it, while calling itself
whole.**

What caught it was the roster, which is what a roster is for. The repair took two characters --
the assert's operand and that witness's own spoken line, swept on touch -- so the ceiling fell
10,536 to 10,533 rather than to 10,535.

## Two reasons, and the second was the quiet one

Teaching the scan the second label made all five pairs visible and checked **none** of them. The
manual writes a one-line lead-in between the blocks -- *You should see:* -- and prose with nothing
declaring it is counted `undeclared_after_prose`: named, never checked.

The scan is right to decline it. Prose between the blocks says which of three different things the
second block is -- a subset of this output, the output of another invocation, or an unrelated
listing -- and `docs-geode/tutorials/the-first-hour.md` holds a genuine reattribution whose block
belongs to a different command. Guessing among the three would invent claims no writer made.

**The two cases separate cleanly, and by measurement rather than by taste.** All five manual pairs
carry exactly **one** line of prose; the two undeclared pairs in docs-geode carry **eight** and
**two**, and both are real reattributions. A lead-in is a sentence; a reattribution is a paragraph.

So the page declares, the way a volatile block and a selection already do, with a reason required:

    <!-- lead-in: the prose above introduces this block -->

`lead-in` names the **prose**, which is what is being declared, and it means the block is the
command's **whole** output, checked by equality. `selected` means a subset and is checked by
containment in order. Reusing `selected` here would have weakened three exact claims into three
containments -- two tokens, because they are two different claims.

## The ceiling that rose, and why that is lawful

`held` counts a pair named rather than run, under a ceiling that only falls. It rose **two to
three** on this lap, and the reason is the one case where a ratchet ceiling may honestly rise: the
**denominator widened**. `manual/guides/filevault-setup.md` runs `fdesetup status`, a macOS command
this pier has no way to run, and it entered the corpus with the room. A page that *drifts* still
falls to the gate, which stays hard at zero.

itself and hands git one top-level page where the pathspec means the whole subtree: **one page
against thirty-eight**.

## What this does not reach

It reads stdout alone; a claim living on stderr is invisible here. It proves what a command
**prints**, never that the command is the right one to teach. Two of the manual's five pairs stay
**held** and correctly so -- the FileVault command, and a nine-line cloud bootstrap carrying `pip`,
a symlink and an export. `SOURCE.md`'s pair quotes a placeholder fingerprint behind a lead-in
sentence and stays `undeclared_after_prose`, since its block is an illustration rather than a
command's output.

And it reaches two fence labels. A room writing a third would stand outside this exactly as the
manual did, which is the shape worth remembering: **a guard reading one spelling of a convention
reads one room, and reads it while calling itself whole.**
