## Why one more shape

The eleven shapes read a basin and a fibre corridor once a town is chosen. Two studies this season chose their towns by hand, and both reached the same order of reasons. This shape writes that order down so the next basin is read the same way, and so a reader can see why a town led rather than take it on counsel's word.

## The three axes, and the tie rule

A works town is read on three axes, each named for what it rewards.

| Axis | What it rewards | How it is read |
|---|---|---|
| **headwater** | the whole river below | the first town on each thread whose return runs every day; on a dry plain the honest headwater is an outfall rather than the mapped source |
| **polis** | a signature that holds | incorporated or unincorporated; county seat or not; home-rule or general-law; whether a manager sits across the table |
| **commons** | a town people stay in | soccer fields, an outdoor stage, an indoor theatre, a market -- each read as *standing*, *seed*, or *gap* |

**The tie rule: headwater first, polis second, commons third.** A river's position is fixed and a town's bones can be grown, so the axis that stays put decides the tie. A town that reads *standing* on all three leads without a tie.

## The shape

```kyri
format works-town-v1
town                   # the town, its county, its state
polis                  # incorporated (year); county seat or not; home-rule or general-law; the form of government and who speaks for it, dated
reach                  # the thread the town's return joins, and every intake below it, in order
drinks_from            # the town's own source, with the works that already stands at its door if one does
returns_to             # the outfall, the land site, or both, with the plant's average and permitted flow, dated
plant_question         # the one open question a retrofit would answer
commons                # soccer / stage / theatre / market -- each standing, seed, or gap
rail_road              # the rail line and the road, named plainly
anchor_institutions    # the university, the hospital, the employer, the park -- what holds the town through a decade
door                   # the contract shapes that fit: a services agreement, a lease, a partnership proposal, the owner's own loan
receipt_home           # where the daily reading is signed and appended, and where it is shown
residual               # what the plant leaves behind, and its home, or open
falsifier              # the reading that would move this town down the ranking
```

**Bounds.** One town per block. A reading table holds at most twelve towns; a river needing more is read in two reaches. Every figure carries its unit, its date, and its source, or reads *gap*.

**Falsifier, standing.** A town ranked first on commons and third on headwater is a town chosen for its stage rather than its river, and the shape says so in its own order. A town whose `returns_to` reads *land site only* holds no headwater rank, whatever its polis.

## How it sits with the eleven

Fill this shape first, for every town on the thread. Then fill the four headwater works shapes for the town that leads, and the seven corridor shapes if a fibre lap belongs there. The receipts shape underneath stays exactly as the corridor shapes wrote it; the `door_log` and `residual_log` rows the works shapes added carry a works town's readings unchanged.

## What this refuses

**It seats no town.** Shapes are forms; the maintainer's word seats. **It ranks no town without three readings.** A block with two axes filled is a block still open. **It names no technology.** The works shapes carry that, and the engineers carry the rest.

## Gratitude

The order of the three axes was found twice this season -- once on the Missouri's three forks and once on the Brazos's two threads -- before it was written here. The maintainer named the axes and asked for the balance; the shape only holds the balance still.
