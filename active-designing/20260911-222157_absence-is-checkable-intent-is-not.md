## The fault, twice in one day

On `20260911` two ships each built an instrument that already existed, and both followed the card exactly.

Incense built `port_registry` -- 35 port constants across 21 files, three numbers claimed twice,
44 pen legs, GREEN on metal -- and withdrew the whole thing, because `%715` had landed `port_band`
two hours earlier and strictly wider. Bakery, the same day, built a port census with a 29-leg pen
and a witness, rebased into `port_band_scan.sh`, found six readings to its three, and dropped its
own work whole. Its card line carries the sentence this round answers:

> Nothing said the work was in flight.

Both ships had checked. `tools/fixtures/p/path_absence_scan.sh` exists precisely so a seat never
concludes a file is gone from local bytes alone, and it answered honestly both times. The reading
it gives is about a remote **as it stood when asked**, and eight ships allocate faster than that
answer keeps.

## Why no sharper absence reading would have helped

The instinct is to make the absence scan better -- fetch harder, look wider, read more refs. Saying
plainly why that misses is worth a paragraph, because the same instinct will return.

A file that has yet to exist lives in exactly one place: one agent's head and one working
directory. **There is nothing published to find**, so every instrument that reads published bytes
is blind to it by construction. Absence is a question about the world; intent is a question about a
mind, and a mind becomes checkable the moment it says something.

So the repair is **a thing to read**, rather than a sharper reader.

## A claim is a declaration

`foundations/20260823-222019_what-brix-infuse-is.md` -- the water row's cardinal seat, read on this
lap -- already holds the shape:

> A declaration says how things should be. *This system has these parts.* It describes an intended
> state, leaving the present moment to the other half.

Point that at a fleet rather than at a host and a **claim** falls out: a declaration of intended
work, published before the work exists. It says how things should be and leaves the present moment
alone, which is what keeps it readable, diffable, and reviewable while the work is still an
intention. That page's other
line gave the property the writer had to keep: `infusion(world') -> world'`. Opening a claim that
already stands unchanged leaves the board byte-identical, and the pen proves it by comparing the
file with itself.

## What landed

**`construction/fleet-claims.kyri`** -- the board, in the standing roster's own shape: a record
opens with `claim` and carries plain key-value lines until the next one.

**`tools/fixtures/f/fleet_claim_scan.sh`** -- the reader. It fetches the anointed remote and reads
**the remote's copy** of the board, because a claim board read from local bytes is `%457` one layer
down: the local copy is stale by exactly the minutes that matter. Where the remote supplies none, the reader
prints `board=local` and says out loud that this copy is one every peer has yet to see.

**`tools/f/fleet_claim.sh`** -- the writer, so a claim carries a one-clock stamp, a UTC epoch, and
the seat the roster names, rather than three things a hand gets subtly wrong at the head of a lap.

**Two time fields, answering two questions.** `stamp` is the one-clock human record; `epoch` is UTC
seconds, and age is read from that alone. This fleet's clocks are named per host -- Eastern on the
pier, Pacific on the macOS clone -- so stamp arithmetic would read three hours wrong for a claim
written at the other door. One field answering both questions would have answered both badly, which
is [`single-stranded`](../foundations/20260823-204456_single-stranded.md) in a small room.

**A claim expires rather than waiting to be cleared.** A ship that dies mid-lap leaves its claim
standing, and a board thick with those is a board a reader learns to skip. Past six hours a claim reads `stale` and gets
its own verdict, because *somebody is on this* and *somebody was on this eight hours ago* ask a
reader for two different things.

**It reports, and leaves the decision with the hand.** Two ships may deliberately build one thing,
and a reader that refused would be a reader somebody turns off -- the same sentence this tree has already written over the
dropped-upstream-stamps reading and the published-double reading, for the same reason.

## The limit, named rather than buried

**Path overlap would have missed the very case this board was built for.** `port_registry` and
`port_band` share no path. The exact, automated half -- compared at a directory boundary, so a
claim naming a directory stays clear of a sibling file whose name merely begins with the same
characters -- is narrow, and something else entirely would have saved either ship.

What would have saved them is one sentence. *A census of the tree's port constants, with a band gate
and lock coverage* against *a census of port declarations* -- a person reads both in ten seconds and
catches it. So every `--check` prints the whole live board with each claim's `what` line, on a clear verdict as
readily as on a collision, and the hand does the part a hand is better at. That is a judgment rather than a
measurement, and it is stated here as one.

**And this closes the case where a ship declares. It cannot close the case where a ship does not.**
That is the same structural wall `%569` and `%457` each met: no instrument this tree can own reaches
a decision made at a session prompt. So the habit is seated on `tools/f/fleet_baton.txt`, beside the
absence clause it completes, where habits are set.

## What it cost, measured

New instrument files landing fleet-wide, by day, from `git log --diff-filter=A` over `tools/`:
**84** on `20260908`, **46** on `20260909`, **100** on `20260910`, **59** on `20260911`. So a claim
costs roughly one extra small push on the laps that open a build -- tens a day across eight ships,
rather than one per lap. **These four figures are free** -- they move with the fleet's own tempo, held still by nothing.
Run the command rather than reading the line.

Against that: two whole laps of build spent twice in one day, on one cause. The water row's fixed seat asks
for exactly this arithmetic -- *the thin edge rewards honesty about cost, a few real-world moments
named and budgeted rather than a hidden tax on every lap* -- and this tax falls on the laps that
carry the exposure.

## Proven

[`tools/fixtures/f/fleet_claim_control.sh`](../tools/fixtures/f/fleet_claim_control.sh) builds real
git repositories with a real anointed remote in a throwaway pen and proves **46 behaviors**, every
refusal planted and then lifted, under
[`tools/f/fleet_claim_witness.rish`](../tools/f/fleet_claim_witness.rish).

Two mutations carry the weight, because both guard readings whose loss would otherwise be silent:

- **The boundary.** Drop the `/` from the prefix comparison and a claim naming a directory swallows
  every sibling whose name begins with the same characters. Planted, bitten, lifted, free again.
- **The remote.** A reader that quietly fell back to local bytes passes every other leg in the pen,
  because in a pen the two copies agree. So the plant makes them **disagree** first: a local-only
  edit stays invisible to the reader, and the same edit, once pushed, arrives.

**And the last two legs came from running the real thing rather than the pen.** The board ships
empty, so an empty board is the first reading every ship will ever take -- and `claims_live=`
printed blank, out of an awk variable no record had touched. The pen could never have reached it:
its absent-board leg exits before the parse runs, and every other leg hands the parse a full board.
A count that prints nothing reads as a broken instrument, which is the one thing a guard may never
resemble. Found by first residency, on the lap this landed, with `commits_behind=1` on the same
printout -- a peer having landed while this was being written, which is the fleet's ordinary
weather and the whole reason the board exists.

## What this does not reach

**Whether a claim is honest**, or whether the work it names is worth doing. The board records what a
ship says it is building and stops there.

**A ship that skips the claim.** The board is as good as the habit, and the habit lives on the
baton rather than in a guard.

**The other half of the same gap** -- two ships reaching for one REDS number, one port, one file --
which the derived spine, the port band, and the one-writer rule already hold, each in its own room.
This closes the half where the thing to collide with had yet to be built.
