## What the Mind progression teaches every jailed loop

Three lessons, carried from `~/grain-mind` into Dream's new pier loop and any jailed lap after:

- **The outer jail bounds the inner sandbox.** Codex runs `--sandbox danger-full-access` only
  inside a proven enclosure, and the enclosure is what makes that setting safe.
- **A jailed inner lap proves scoped witnesses only.** The cold and hot roster is a bench and
  supervisor instrument -- inside a jail it reads its own walls as sixty reds, which answers
  nothing. A refusal born of the jail (a denied selector, an absent binary) is a machine fact,
  never a tree red.
- **A lap ends in one bounded outcome.** At most one candidate or commit, or one written
  custody line -- and the itinerary must **book** the crux, because an unbooked lane is a
  custody stop by the loop's own law.

Dream's loop takes the loop-shell rung of that ladder today -- the sync in the shell, Codex in

## The closed stacks

The tree is a library with two floors. The **open shelves** are the rooms a lap walks by
default -- the construction pins, the context guides, foundations, the rules, and the lane's
own module rooms. The **closed stacks** are rooms held whole and real -- nothing deleted,
nothing hidden -- that a lap enters only holding a call slip: a named path, a citation, a
resolver's answer. Every `date/`, `archive/`, and `yonder/` shelf is closed by shape; a named
roster closes the rest. Closed governs the walk, never the write. The law, the roster, and the
four habits live in [`../.claude/rules/read-scope.md`](../.claude/rules/read-scope.md), and
[`../MAP.md`](../MAP.md) is the walk that replaces the `ls`.
