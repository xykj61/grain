## What counsel proposes, and what it does not do

Counsel proposes nothing be reverted and nothing be swept. Two paths stand, and
**choosing between them is Keaton's word, not a bench's**:

**One — an auditor beside the doorway.** A small scan that lists dated artifacts
whose history shows a post-introduction modification without a living header,
run as a report rather than a gate, so the tree can *see* what the guard cannot.
Cheap to write; its first run would return roughly seven hundred names, which is
a report about the past rather than a task list.

**Two — narrow the law to what we actually practise.** If dated seats are in
truth *amendable until superseded*, the honest move is to say so in
`context/specs/living-vs-dated.md` and let the guard cover only the classes we
genuinely freeze — witnesses, goldens, receipts, key material.

The second reads truer to counsel's eye: seven hundred amendments are not seven
hundred violations, they are a law that was written narrower than the practice
it describes. But a law's shape is Keaton's to choose, and the compass says
safety first — so nothing changes until his word picks a path.
