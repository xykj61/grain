I recommended stopping the waves, and the recommendation rested on a mistaken reading of the goal. I measured the lint delta and found it flat, then concluded the passes had no return. The actual goal is different and better: **a known-passed baseline across the whole corpus, so that every future reader and every future writer inherits prose that has already met a Radiant hand.** Coverage is the deliverable, and coverage is not something a violation count can measure.

So this memo withdraws the stop, and spends itself instead on three things worth more: an honest runway, a genuine metric, and the safety gap that a fast automated pass at this scale actually opens.

## Two — Raise the Batch, and Say Why Plainly

The twelve-file wave bound was chosen for one reason: so **Keaton could read a whole wave in one sitting.** That constraint was honest when review was the safety.

Working alongside other things changes what the bound is for. If each diff is no longer read, the bound protects nothing — and the honest move is to name that shift rather than drift into it:

> **Raising the batch means the witnesses become the only reviewer.**

For style-only passes that trade is acceptable, since `claim_preserve_witness` is byte-exact on claims and both lints run every wave. So I propose **fifty files per wave**, which turns eighty-four waves into roughly **twenty**, with one PR per wave as now.

Paired with two cheap rituals that replace continuous reading:

**A spot-read every fifth wave.** One file, chosen at random from that wave, read by Keaton. Five minutes, and it catches a model doing something strange far better than skimming fifty diffs would.

**A rollback rule.** Any wave is one revert away, since each is its own PR. Naming that in advance is what makes the larger batch safe to accept.

## Three — The Metric I Said Was Missing

Here is the measurement that answers my earlier objection, and it costs almost nothing: **record whether each file actually changed.**

The ledger gains one field per row — *changed* or *clean*. Then coverage stops being a bare count and becomes something with a shape:

- A genre whose change-rate runs high is earning its waves outright.
- A genre whose change-rate falls near zero can be **exempted wholesale, on evidence rather than instinct** — two waves of a genre coming back all-clean is a fair basis for skipping the rest.
- And the final report says something worth having: *this corpus was passed, and here is exactly how much of it needed anything.*

This lets the data prune the runway while the runway is being walked. It is also the honest way to test my flat-lint finding: if change-rates come back high while the lint stays at two, then the passes were improving what no witness could see, and the waves were right all along.

## Four — The Gap a Fast Model Opens

One real safety finding, and it matters more at fifty files a wave than at twelve.

**`claim_preserve_witness` protects tokens, rather than modality.** It compares numbers, digests, fingerprints, paths, stamps, and proper nouns — so it catches a changed hash and misses a changed *obligation*. A pass could turn *we recommend* into *we require*, *may* into *must*, *some* into *all*, *proposed* into *seated*, and every token check would stay green while the document's force quietly shifted. In a tree whose memos carry proposals, holds, and stop-and-park weight, that is precisely the class of drift that would hurt.

So I propose extending the witness with a **modality set**: preserve the per-file counts of `must`, `must not`, `shall`, `should`, `may`, `never`, `always`, `all`, `every`, `none`, `require`, `recommend`, `propose`, `seat`, `seated`, `hold`, `held`, `parked`. Counts identical before and after, or the wave stops. Cheap to compute, and it closes the one hole a large automated prose pass genuinely opens.

Two smaller cautions worth writing into the pass rule:

**Guard against homogenization.** A single model passing a thousand files tends to converge every voice toward its own. The instruction that prevents it: **preserve each document's existing cadence and vocabulary; fix only what the playbook names.** A pass is a correction, never a rewrite.

**Preserve hedges deliberately.** Where a memo says *probably*, *I lean*, or *my guess*, that hedge is load-bearing counsel — it tells a future reader how much weight the claim carried. Strengthening it reads as improvement while destroying information.

## Five — What I Now Recommend

Scope the pool, exempt with reasons, raise the batch to fifty, instrument the change-rate, extend the witness to modality, spot-read every fifth wave, and walk the twenty waves. Then let the change-rate data decide whether the exempted genres ever earn a pass of their own.
