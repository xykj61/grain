## The Breach — Affirmed, Framed, and Given an Expiry

The instinct is right and the timing argument is real. Two documents carry the reasoning: the long form at `foundations/20260726-020537_the-breach.md` and the condensed law at `context/BREACH.md`. The short of it: **accrete by default; breach by declaration**, spoken as *accrete, sometimes breach*. A breach relocates and re-points and never rewrites, which is what keeps it clear of the tier law entirely — the tiers govern bytes, and a breach does not touch bytes. Six promises frame it: declared first, every byte preserved, finished inside its scope, witnessed on both sides, measurably better, standing alone.

The addition counsel makes to your framing is the **expiry**. The claim that a breach is cheaper now than later is only honest if it names the day it stops being true, and that day has a definition: **the breach window closes at the first outside consumer of a path** — a fork, a published link, an apprentice with a clone of this tree. Until then the entire cost is internal and mechanical. After then a breach owes migration and someone else's trust, and the law reverts to what it has always aspired to be. Naming the expiry is what turns a mood into a decision, and it also sharpens why the SOURCE front door and the breach are in tension for scheduling: the front door exists to create the very apprentice whose clone closes the window.

## One Correction Worth Making Before Anything Moves

The tree already seated this law, and it is better than the criterion counsel was about to propose.

`ORGANIZING.md` carries a section named **Sort as Priority**, and it already says all of it: a folder's own alphabetical order carries its priority; active work rests one level deep; deferred-yet-alive work rests in **`yonder/`**, which sorts toward the bottom and points forward; finished or retired work rests in `archive/`, which points backward. It states the active test — a document stays one level deep while it drives present implementation and moves to `yonder/` when it becomes future, dormant, consumed, or realized-as-code. It states the caution — age is a hint rather than the test, and relevance and inbound references outrank the date. It states the method — move by `git mv` so history stays whole, and update every reference. And it states the exception that protects the load-bearing pages: a canonical reference cited by many files keeps its stable name and is affirmed in place rather than moved, because renaming what a hundred files depend on trades a real promise for a small convenience.

So the breach is **enforcement of a law already seated**, rather than a new law. That reframing makes it far safer, and it corrects one detail in the plan: the destination for cooled active-designing pages is **`active-designing/yonder/`**, not `work-in-progress/`. Those are different rooms. `work-in-progress/` holds the living instruments — the now, the itinerary, the roadmap, the working inventories — and filling it with three hundred dormant briefs would break the room that the router points every fresh agent at first.

Every one of those 382 was correct on the day it landed; together they have made the room mean less than its name promises.

**Rung zero is the instrument, and it is not optional.** The only link check in the tree today is `living_docs_lint` duty two, which is roster-scoped and advisory and never fails. A breach without a completeness instrument is a break with good intentions. So the first rung builds `tools/link_witness.rish` — walk every relative markdown link in the tree, assert each target resolves, blocking, with a known-good fixture beside a known-bad one. Its first run is the baseline, and if the tree already has dangling links today, that number is the honest starting count rather than a reason to stop.

**Rung one is a census that moves nothing.** Apply the seated active test mechanically and produce a manifest: for each of the 382, its inbound reference count, its last-touch date, whether any live instrument names it, and the proposed room. Reading only. The manifest is what Keaton reads.

**Rung two is Keaton's pass over the manifest** — strike anything that must stay hot, per the canonical-reference exception. That pass is the seating word for the motion.

**Rung three is the motion, in bounded rounds.** Fifty files at a time: `git mv`, re-point every inbound reference, run the link witness, green, commit, send. Each round stands alone and each round finishes. A round that cannot finish rolls back rather than lands half.

**Rung four publishes the arithmetic** — before, target, after. Counsel's proposed target is that `active-designing/` end below **forty** files one level deep, which is roughly a ninety-percent reduction and well past the ordinary first-pass expectation. Keaton sets the real number; the discipline is that a number exists before the motion begins.

## The Variant Order, and What Order May Mean

**Reorder the README table alphabetically: Quin, Reya, Riyo, Trey, Triz.** A pure row reorder moves no claim tokens and no headings, so both gates stay green and this is an ordering pass rather than a claim lap. One caution: the Quin row reads *the fifth boot image* with stamps confirming it fifth, and those stay true as seating history even when the row sits first, though they will read oddly at the top. Rewording that cell is a claim change and wants its own small lap with a word.

**On whether the order should carry weight — no, and this is the more important half.** Decouple display order from wire representation completely. The variant selector must be a **name**, never an ordinal. Named constants over magic numbers is already root discipline, and accrete makes the case sharper: a name once given is a promise, while an index is a promise you can break by inserting a row. A signed manifest carrying a `2` means something different the day the array grows, and every old signature silently re-points to the wrong variant — the exact misuse-guard shape. If a tagged cell needs a discriminant on the wire, derive it, pin it, append only, never renumber, and let a witness assert the name-to-tag map holds across versions. With that settled, alphabetical display order costs nothing, because display order and wire order were never the same thing.

## The Glow Docs, With One Correction

Yes to the room and yes to the writing, after the breach. **`docs/` stays the proven shelf** — the fifteen compressors there are witness-backed and linked from the README, and moving them would misstate their register. The hot room is **`active-designing/docs/glow/`**, and a page **graduates** into `docs/` when its witnesses land. Prose graduation mirroring the module graduation rule, and checkable.

The correction concerns what to write. Root rule five already seats Grain's value model: a string, an integer, a boolean, a list, a record, or a composite of those. That is **not** atom and cell. A page titled *Glow's atoms and cells* would quietly re-seat another system's value model as Glow's own, against both the seated law and the decision that Nock is only an interop backend. So write two pages rather than one: **Glow's value model**, clean-room, with its RISC-V lowering; and **the Nock interop seam**, narrow, where Grain values map to atoms and cells at the boundary and only at the boundary. Full Silo in both — lineage in the gratitude close, nothing reproduced — and Two Rooms register marked honestly, since most of it is Design today.
