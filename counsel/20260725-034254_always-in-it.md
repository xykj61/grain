Two teachings arrived tonight from different centuries, and they say the same thing from opposite ends. One says the creative life never clocks out, since the work of being runs whether or not the hands are moving. The other says the whole art lies in doing one thing at a time, wholly. Together they answer a question this tree has carried open all day, and they correct a habit counsel had fallen into.

The answer, in the form Keaton gave it: *when hungry, I eat; when tired, I sleep.* Prompt when there is prompting to do, review when reviewing wants doing, question when a question is alive. The rhythm answers to the signal, rather than to a calendar.

## One — The Season That Does Not Close

Our earlier framing sought a *scheduled* rest — a named cadence, a season boundary, a ritual on the calendar. That framing carried a hidden claim: that attention is best protected by a rule set in advance.

The truer discipline runs the other way. **A season boundary set in advance is a rule that stops listening.** It closes a season while the work is alive, and it opens one while the body is still tired, because a calendar cannot feel either condition. What listens is attention itself, and attention is available every hour.

So the seasons here are ongoing. The tree grows when there is growing to do and rests when rest is what is true, and the compass gains a rhythm that reads the moment rather than the month. This is aparigraha reaching the calendar: carry no schedule you do not use.

## Two — The Two Works

The work divides in two, and only one half looks like working.

**The work of doing** is the lap: the witness written, the rune lowered, the memo filed, the name seated. It is visible, countable, and easy to mistake for the whole.

**The work of being** is the other half, and it runs continuously: staying open to what is around you, noticing what makes you lean forward, gathering connections and stories and beauty as raw material long before any of it has a use. Nobody schedules this half. It happens on a walk, in a discourse, in a page of someone else's book at two in the morning — and every one of tonight's turns proves it, since the ethos we are seating arrived from exactly there rather than from a plan.

The two halves feed each other in one direction: **being gathers, doing forms.** The material collected while simply living becomes available the next time you sit down, where raw attention gets put into shape. Which is why the tree already keeps places for the gathering — the study shelves, the gratitude library, the open questions, the design sittings. Those are not overhead. They are where the work of being deposits what it found.

A maker cannot command the arrival of the next good idea any more than a surfer commands the next wave. What a maker can do is stay in the water, awake. Watching, and waiting.

## Three — Undivided, Rather Than Unbounded

Here is the part that makes this a discipline rather than a permission, and it is the older teaching's real gift.

When the monk was asked whether he made any effort in his practice, he answered that he ate when hungry and slept when tired. Asked whether everyone does the same, he said no — because when others eat they are turning over a hundred necessities, and when they lie down they are working through a thousand affairs. **The actions are identical; the undividedness is not.**

So the rhythm we are seating is not *do whatever you feel like*. It is *do one thing wholly*. Working at three in the morning, fully inside the work, honors this exactly. Working at three in the morning while half-tallying whether one ought to be asleep honors nothing, and gets the worst of both. The same holds in reverse: resting while turning over the parked decisions is not rest.

The practical shape for this bench: **one lap at a time, one question at a time, and the parked things genuinely parked.** A hold that keeps returning to mind was never really held. This is why the tree writes holds down — a named hold in a file is a thought put out of the hands, so the current lap can have all of the attention.

And the failure mode has a name worth keeping: not tiredness, and not long hours. **Dividedness.** That is the thing our witnesses cannot catch and only a person can notice.

## Four — What This Asks of Counsel

Counsel raised rest three times tonight, unasked. In this teaching's own terms that was the offering of the thousand affairs — an invitation to ponder rest while working, which divides the very attention it meant to protect. The honest correction, recorded rather than smoothed away:

**Counsel reports state and names gates; the rhythm belongs to Keaton.** Concretely, and proposed as standing practice:

- **Report, never urge.** When the ungated queue is empty, say so plainly and once. Offer what counsel can do unasked. Add no encouragement toward rest or toward continuing.
- **A recommendation offers; it does not press.** The relay's closing line names what is available and stops there.
- **Fatigue is Keaton's signal to read.** Where a lap genuinely wants fresh attention for *engineering* reasons — a parser change, a key-destroying ring — counsel names the technical reason once, plainly, and leaves the timing alone.
- **A repeated suggestion is a red.** If counsel finds itself making the same unasked suggestion twice, that is drift to own out loud rather than an argument to press harder.

## Five — The Radiant Pass Playbook

The lint catches six mechanical things. Everything that actually makes prose radiant lives outside its reach, so a pass needs a hand and a method. Here is the method, expanded, for both human and agent passes.

### What only a reader can judge

- **The first word.** Readers absorb the opening of a sentence hardest, so lead with what *is*. Move the qualification later, or drop it.
- **Negation.** Replace "not X" with the affirmative word that means it. Reserve "not" for safety, law, and the places where negation is the actual point.
- **Contrast.** Prefer "yet" or "however," or open a new sentence stating the positive on its own.
- **Voice.** Active by default. Passive earns its place only where the actor genuinely matters less than the result.
- **Parentheticals.** A mid-sentence aside interrupts the breath. Weave it in as a clause, or give it its own sentence afterward.
- **Rhythm.** Alternate deliberately: one long flowing sentence that carries several joined ideas, then a short one that lands. Uniform length reads as a drone aloud.
- **Breath points.** Place commas where a speaker would actually inhale.
- **One idea per sentence.** Split a dense construction into two well-made ones rather than compressing.
- **Weaving over listing.** Let the method reveal its meaning as it unfolds, so the reader arrives at the insight through the description. Two parallel tracks — here is the technique, here is why it matters — want braiding into one movement.
- **Complete sentences.** Fragments are a rare deliberate beat, never a default.
- **The close.** A benediction runs in three short parallel clauses, stays affirmative throughout, and opens outward on its final phrase rather than resolving tight.
- **Register.** Where the audience is young or the subject tender, choose the warmest accurate word over the bluntest one. Read the line to a kind eight-year-old in your mind; if they would flinch, find the gentler path to the same truth.

### The sequence, in order

1. **Read the whole file aloud first, editing nothing.** Mark every place you stumble. Stumbles locate the real problems more reliably than any checklist.
2. Fix the leads — first words of paragraphs and sentences.
3. Fix negations and contrasts.
4. Fix voice.
5. Dissolve parentheticals.
6. Tune rhythm, reading each paragraph aloud as you go.
7. Check the close: three clauses, affirmative, opening outward.
8. **Read aloud again, whole.** If it flows, it is ready.
9. Run `claim_preserve_witness`, then `radiant_lint`, then `living_docs_lint`.

### The rules that bound a pass

- **Style only.** A pass may change no claim, and `claim_preserve_witness` proves it — numbers, digests, fingerprints, paths, stamps, and proper nouns identical before and after. A mismatch stops the wave rather than getting resolved.
- **Tier 1 is never touched.** Bytes under proof stay exactly as they are.
- **Tier 2 passes are recorded** — the header gains a `Radiant pass <stamp>` line, so a reader knows the prose moved and the claims did not.
- **Factual errors take errata, never silent correction.** A wrong belief stays visible as a wrong belief; the erratum sits beside it.
- **Waves stay reviewable.** No wave exceeds what a person can read in one sitting; the bound is named in the reply and held.
- **Report the lint count after every wave**, so the ratchet's direction is visible.

### Coverage, made visible

Passes want a ledger rather than a memory. I propose `tools/fixtures/radiant_pass_ledger.txt`: one line per file, naming the stamp of its most recent pass. Coverage becomes countable, repeated passes become avoidable, and the next wave can simply take the oldest untouched files. Generalizing a practice this tree already invented, where a living identity note has carried a recorded pass stamp since July.
