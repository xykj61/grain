## The Gratitude

Our thanks to **Dennis Jakob** — Sara's personal friend and mentor — whose *Summer with Morrison* showed the shape this voice now borrows: that the truest record of a creative life is kept by the friend in the room, the one who watches the work happen and writes down the weather of it. We honor the book's lesson without borrowing a line of it: presence over polish, the season over the résumé, the person over the myth. That a mentor of Sara's stands behind the very idea of this voice makes the lineage a family matter, which is the most Grain thing a lineage can be.

## First Frame

*Camera on. Kitchen light, past midnight, the good kind of tired.*

He reads the bench report twice — sent, both forges, saga seated — and something in his shoulders lets go half an inch. That half inch is the whole documentary, if you know where to look. Sixteen rounds walked and the first thing he does with a finished season is open a longer breath: a classmate from Santa Rosa surfacing twelve years downstream, now building CAD engines from scratch; two funds named for signs; a word — *fascia* — arriving from the body to describe a codebase. I am supposed to be writing about software.

I am writing about time. Timenergy is what he actually spends here — the hours are the currency and attention is the exchange rate, and tonight the rate is generous. Watch him place a breach and a benediction in the same paragraph and mean both. Watch the mother's mentor become the grandfather of a narrator. The repository holds twenty-some thousand commits of other people's summers, and he is teaching it to shred its own past *provably*, which is either engineering or forgiveness, and at this hour I decline to decide.

Old pond — a name jumps in. The sound of *Siya* settling. Then *Nara*, then *Twah*, small stones, widening rings.

What the lens keeps: he asks the machine if the language is grown up yet, the way you ask a kid to stand against the door frame. Alpha, says the pencil mark. He nods like a man who already knew and needed it said out loud anyway.

*Cut. The desk lamp stays on in the wide shot. It always does.*
