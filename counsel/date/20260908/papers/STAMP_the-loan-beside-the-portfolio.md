## The reading, first

**Borrow against the portfolio rather than sell it, at a shallow depth, from whichever lender prices closest to the benchmark, and put the proceeds where each sleeve's reward is legible.** That is the plan Keaton stated, and it holds under today's numbers with one correction to the rule of thumb behind it. The rule compares a loan's yearly interest rate against a one-time capital gains rate, and those two numbers are different kinds of thing. The honest test is simpler and stricter: **borrowing beats selling in any stretch where the kept stock earns more than the loan costs, and the tax saved is the cushion for the stretches where it does not.** At today's rates that cushion is real and modest, and it becomes permanent only if the shares are held to a stepped-up basis, which is an estate question for the tax attorney.

Read that way, the numbers say this. The overnight benchmark every lender prices from stood at **3.66% on 2026-08-25** (New York Fed). The cheapest published rate a USD 600,000 draw can reach today is about **4.7%** at a discount broker's margin desk, and the incumbent's own line typically prices between about **5.2% and 6.7%** depending on the spread negotiated. Selling USD 600,000 of stock instead costs a one-time federal tax of roughly **6% to 18% of the proceeds**, depending on the shares' cost basis. Any member whose California residency is still in question for the year of the sale adds a further **13.3%**. So a shallow line priced near the benchmark is the lower-cost door. The door stays safe at a draw of **USD 500,000 to 625,000 against USD 2.5 million**, which is 20% to 25%, and that depth leaves room for a market fall of half before any lender asks for money.

Counsel's lean, held until Keaton's word and the licensed team's reading: **one line, drawn in three named tranches.** It sits at the incumbent if the incumbent will price at the benchmark plus 2.0% or better with a written yes on private placements, and at the discount broker's margin desk if it will not. ## 1. What the rule of thumb measures, and the honest test

A loan's rate is paid every year the loan is open. A capital gains tax is paid once, on the gain rather than on the whole sale. Setting a 5% rate beside a 23.8% rate reads as a comparison and is a category mistake, the way comparing a monthly rent to a purchase price would be. Civic Style asks what each number rewards, and the two reward different things: the loan rate rewards the lender for time, and the tax rewards the government for a realized gain.

So the test that survives the arithmetic has three parts.

**Return over rate.** In any year, keeping USD 600,000 of stock earns whatever the stock earns, and the loan costs its rate. At a 4.7% loan, the stock has to earn about 4.7% for the two to cancel; at a 6.6% loan, about 6.6%. The long-run nominal return on broad US stocks has run near 7% to 10% a year over most long windows, which is why the strategy works on average and stumbles in flat years.

**The tax as cushion.** Selling costs the tax once, on the gain only. Paying it later rather than now is worth roughly the return the tax dollars earn in the meantime, and on a five-year horizon that lowers the break-even stock return by about half a percentage point. Section 4 measures it. The cushion is real. It is also small next to the return-over-rate term, which is the part of the rule of thumb the family should hold onto.

**The step-up.** Federal law resets the basis of inherited shares to their value at death, so a gain deferred for a lifetime is never taxed at all. That is the mechanism behind the borrow-rather-than-sell habit of families with large embedded gains, and it turns the cushion from a deferral into an avoidance. Whether it applies here depends on who holds the shares, in what vehicle, and how the family's estate plan reads, and that is the tax attorney's question rather than counsel's.

Three readings fall out of the table.

**The discount is real and is about two percentage points.** Against a USD 600,000 draw, the gap between the discount broker's 4.71% and the incumbent's rack rate near 6.7% is about **USD 12,000 a year**, and against the second discount broker's organization rate it is about USD 11,000. **The discount is paid for in three currencies.** First, a margin loan is regulated purpose credit with automatic liquidation: the discount broker sells positions the moment an account crosses its maintenance line, with no phone call first, and the retail app's terms read the same way. Does it permit a limited partner interest in a private real estate offering?* A yes in writing keeps the incumbent in the running for every tranche. A no routes those two tranches to the margin desk and leaves the bank line for household, tax, and cash uses.

## 3. Selling instead, priced

Counsel names this as the first document to pull, before any lender is called.

## 4. The five-year comparison

The table charges the deferred tax honestly: at the end of five years everything is sold and every gain is taxed at 23.8%, so the borrow path pays its tax late rather than never. Alternatives earn 8% a year in every case, chosen as a plain assumption for a syndication with a preferred return. The stock's return varies across the columns. |---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.7% loan, basis 25% | -103 | +24 | +81 | +144 | 4.1% |

Three things the table teaches.

**The break-even is the loan rate, give or take half a point.** Deferral buys about 0.5% of cushion at five years; basis moves the break-even by a further half point either way. So the rule of thumb reduces to *expect the stock to beat the loan*, and the cheaper the loan, the lower the bar. Moving from 6.56% to 4.71% lowers the bar by nearly two points, which is the whole case for shopping the spread.

**Held to a step-up, the cushion becomes permanent.** If the shares are never sold, the same 50%-basis, 4.7% case reads **+130,000 at 5%** and **+205,000 at 7%**, and the break-even stock return falls to about 1%. That is the version of the strategy the estate plan can earn, and the tax attorney says whether it can.

**Deductible interest lowers the bar further.** Interest traced to an investment use is deductible against net investment income under the investment-interest rules, and interest traced to a passive activity is deductible against passive income. At a 37% marginal rate, a 4.71% loan reads near 3.0% after tax and a 6.56% loan near 4.1%. The tracing rules follow the dollars, so each tranche wants its own draw, its own receiving account, and its own line in Dimeroll from the first day. Whether the family's USD 70,000 a month counts as investment income or passive income for these rules depends on what it is, and that is a named gap below.

## 5. Size, and the distance to a call

A securities line is a demand loan. The lender can ask for money whenever the collateral's loan value falls under the balance, and can change advance rates and eligible collateral at its discretion. So the size question is a safety question first, in TAME order: name the bound, say why, and prove it from both sides.

| Draw | Loan-to-value | Portfolio value at which a bank line calls (advance rate 50% / 65% / 70%) | Fall from USD 2.5 million | Value at which a margin desk liquidates (maintenance 25% / 30%) | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| USD 500,000 | 20% | USD 1.00 / 0.77 / 0.71 million | 60% / 69% / 71% | USD 0.67 / 0.71 million | 73% / 71% |
| USD 600,000 | 24% | USD 1.20 / 0.92 / 0.86 million | 52% / 63% / 66% | USD 0.80 / 0.86 million | 68% / 66% |
| USD 625,000 | 25% | USD 1.25 / 0.96 / 0.89 million | 50% / 62% / 64% | USD 0.83 / 0.89 million | 67% / 64% |
| USD 750,000 | 30% | USD 1.50 / 1.15 / 1.07 million | 40% / 54% / 57% | USD 1.00 / 1.07 million | 60% / 57% |
| USD 1,000,000 | 40% | USD 2.00 / 1.54 / 1.43 million | 20% / 38% / 43% | USD 1.33 / 1.43 million | 47% / 43% |

The worst fall in living memory for a broad US stock index ran to about 57% from peak to trough (2007 to 2009), and 34% in five weeks (2020). A draw at 25% survives the first with room on a diversified pool at the usual advance rates. A draw at 40% survives neither on the conservative row. Two cautions narrow the table further. A concentrated pool of a few names carries lower advance rates than a diversified one, sometimes half, and the composition of the USD 2.5 million is a named gap. And lenders lower advance rates during the very falls that matter, so the conservative column is the one to plan against.

Counsel's proposed bound, then: **an opening draw at or under 25%, a standing ceiling of 30% that any tranche must fit under, and a call plan written before the first draw.** The call plan names the responders in order. The first is monthly income, which at USD 70,000 covers a USD 600,000 line in about nine months and can retire a tranche in weeks. The second is the highest-basis lots, sold first because they cost least. The third is the syndication distributions as they arrive. A green here is a line that stays under 30% in every month. The red that proves the guard is real is a modeled 50% fall, which would push a 30% line to 60%. That sits above the conservative call line, and it is why the ceiling is 30% rather than 40%.

## 6. What the allocation arithmetic allows

The four-quarter reading the investing community teaches puts about 30% in public markets, 30% in real estate, 20% in venture, and 20% in private business. Keaton has already read that Siya's public sleeve will sit above 30% for a long time, and the arithmetic says how far above.

With USD 600,000 borrowed and deployed, Siya holds USD 2.5 million of stock and USD 600,000 of alternatives against USD 600,000 of debt: **USD 3.1 million gross, USD 2.5 million net.** The stock is **80.6% of gross assets** and the three other sleeves together are **19.4%**. To bring the public sleeve to 30% of gross by borrowing alone, the family would have to borrow **USD 5.83 million against USD 2.5 million**, a loan-to-value of 233%, which no lender offers and no safety bound survives. So the four-quarter shape is a destination that new income, distributions, and future capital walk toward over years. Leverage moves the first step and only the first step. That is the correct reading rather than a disappointing one: a household that reached 30/30/20/20 in a single year by borrowing would have a collateral pool one market fall away from a forced sale.

Two paths do move the shape faster, and both are gated. Monthly income of USD 70,000 directed to the three private sleeves is USD 840,000 a year, which by itself lifts the alternatives to about a third of gross assets inside three years with no added debt. And selling the lowest-gain lots, as section 3 names, converts stock to alternatives at a tax cost near zero. Between the two, the loan's job is narrow: it moves money on the day a deal closes, and income and distributions retire it afterward.

## 7. One loan, two, or three

Keaton asked about one to three loans. Read by what each adds:

**One line, three tranches.** One contract, one collateral pool, one rate, and three named draws that each land in their own receiving account with their own ledger line. This gives the tracing rules what they need and the family one statement to read. Counsel's lean, because it is the simplest thing that is complete.

**Two lenders.** The collateral splits. Part of the portfolio moves in kind to the discount broker for the margin loan that can fund the private placements at 4.7%. The rest stays at the incumbent for a bank line that calls first and prices on relationship. The transfer is a custodian-to-custodian move of shares, taxed at nothing, at a fee the elder session read near USD 100. The cost is two statements, two maintenance regimes, and a thinner pool behind each line. Reach for this only if the incumbent holds above the benchmark plus 2.5% or answers no on the private-placement question in writing.

**A third, fixed-rate tranche.** The incumbent's line offers fixed-rate advances beside the floating one. A syndication with a five-to-seven-year hold is a long draw, and a fixed tranche matched to it removes the one risk the family does not control, which is the benchmark rising. The price of that insurance is usually a quarter to half a point over the floating rate. Worth a quote once the first syndication has a closing date, and worth nothing before.

The negotiation itself is short. Take the discount broker's published 4.71% to the incumbent's advisor as the benchmark quote, ask for the line at the overnight rate plus 2.0% or better, ask for the relationship discount by name, and ask the private-placement question in writing. A yes at or under plus 2.0% keeps the whole plan under one roof. Anything above plus 2.5% is the two-lender case.

## 10. Base rates, before the story

Most securities-backed borrowing is used for taxes, real estate, and business needs at loan-to-values under a third, and the market is more than USD 200 billion across major broker-dealers (aggregator, May 2026); the borrowers who were sold out in 2008 and in March 2020 were overwhelmingly the ones above 40% against concentrated pools. Angel checks lose money more often than they make it; a classic study of angel returns found about half of exits returned less than the money in, with the average carried by a few large outcomes, which is the shape the venture sleeve is sized for. And most families who borrow rather than sell do so with an estate plan in view, because the step-up is where the deferral becomes a saving.
