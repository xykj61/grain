## Two — The First Principle of Custody: Build Nothing That Destroys

A real case sets the frame. As of the day before this stamp, a Cop City protester faces federal prosecution for allegedly giving a border officer a duress passcode that wiped a GrapheneOS phone; security and civil-liberties experts noted they had not seen a comparable charge, and voiced concern that it paints the operating system as suspect by default. The statute behind the theory, 18 U.S.C. § 2232(a), reaches anyone who — before, during, or after a search or seizure — takes any action to prevent or impair the government's lawful custody of property, up to five years. GrapheneOS on Pixel is our stated demo base. Duress-wipe is the "death switch" pattern by another name.

So the founding rule of everything we build for other people's devices and data:

> **Build nothing that destroys, and build so there is nothing to destroy.**

A destruction trigger fires wrong far more often than right, it manufactures an intent artifact a prosecutor can hold up, and it makes every future user of Glow OS inherit that exposure. The strictly stronger posture is architectural, rather than reactive: **data minimization** and **per-record encryption with the key held elsewhere**, so a seized device carries ciphertext that was never plaintext there. *Nothing was destroyed because nothing was ever kept* wins on engineering and legal grounds at once, and it needs no clever switch. This is aparigraha reaching all the way down to the threat model: carry only what you use, so there is little to seize and nothing to erase.

One companion caution from the same statute: subsections (c) through (e) criminalize giving advance notice of a search, a seizure warrant, or an authorized interception in order to obstruct it — which places a real-time warrant canary in fraught territory. The design is **aggregate, delayed transparency reporting, reviewed by counsel** — never a live tripwire.

## Three — Succession of the One Hand

A will executes after an authority declares death; a cryptographic dead-man's switch executes on the mere absence of a heartbeat — a hospital stay, a lost phone, a weak signal, a coerced silence. That is the failure to design away. Three principles hold it:

**Thresholds, rather than switches.** Succession rests on *m*-of-*n* named trustees who must **actively assert**, because active assertion creates accountability while a timer creates an accident waiting for a bad week.

**Publish, then act.** Any succession action announces itself on the append-only log and waits a named period, so a living Keaden can veto it. The adversarial-dashboard principle, turned inward on your own keys.

**Separate the three things that get conflated.** Continuity of *operations* is solved by revocable delegation — the pattern the sandbox signing key already proves. Custody of *value* is solved by threshold control with public notice. Custody of *secrets* is solved best by minimization, so that little exists to inherit.

On choosing trustees: living by ahimsa and the pillars predicts good faith, which is the hardest quality to predict and the most precious. It does **not** predict competence, availability, or independence — so the criteria want all four, plus **jurisdictional and household diversity**, so that no single event and no single legal process can reach the whole quorum at once. And the selection mechanism is **rehearsal**: name several candidates rather than one, and let each actually run a small ritual — verify a signature, clone the pier and green a witness. A trustee who cannot green a witness is not yet a trustee. This folds neatly into the stranger's-lap witness already proposed. The Zen of it, in your own framing: succession fails when it is sought late and under pressure, so trustee-growing is a practice that starts now, in calm.

## Four — Redaction: Add a Removal, Never a Silent Edit

Your state-equivalence intuition is right, and it needs two refinements to sit honestly beside accrete-never-break.

First, **equivalence is necessary rather than sufficient.** The hash chain still breaks unless the removal is itself recorded — so redaction becomes an *event* that names what left and under whose authority. Accrete holds because we **add a removal record**, rather than silently editing the past.

Second, **prune and redact are different mechanisms.** Pruning drops events whose effects are fully subsumed — a compaction concern, the elder system's own answer, where the state reached by skipping a subsumed event equals the state reached through it. Redaction destroys content that must not remain readable — a rights concern. For the rights case the primitive is **crypto-shredding**: personal content encrypted under a per-subject key, and "forget me" destroys that one key, leaving the log's shape and chain perfectly intact while the ciphertext becomes noise. The mount-style hint path — effects crossing out of the pure log into the world — is the right seam for the irreversible parts, with the log recording the request and the acknowledgment on either side.

And the dividend from Section Two applies here too: what makes deletion lawful rather than suspect is a **published retention and legal-hold regime** — routine, scheduled, announced in advance, and suspended the instant a hold arrives. Ordinary deletion must be provably ordinary.

## Six — URL-Safe Auras and the Zero-Copy Wire

The chronological version wants **two canonical forms with an asserted bijection between them**, positive and negative space at the seam.

**On the wire**, `20260724190000` fits a `u64` with room to spare — and that fixed eight-byte field is what makes zero-copy real: it packs and batches the TigerBeetle way, where a variable-length string never can. Bound named, cast asserted, per the width law.

**At the edges** — URLs, filenames, anything read aloud — the recommendation follows from our own values rather than fashion: **Crockford base32.** It is case-insensitive, it excludes I, L, O, and U to kill transcription confusion, and so it survives being *spoken*, which base64url does not — a direct fit for the Radiant read-aloud discipline. Thirteen characters, URL-safe, TTS-safe. One conversion at the seam, asserted both directions. This becomes a small Glow aura seat: the canonical `u64` inward, the base32 form outward.

## Seven — First-Responder Dispatch: The Adjacent Piece

The aim is morally serious and worth pursuing, with an honest entry point. Legacy dispatch fails precisely in TAME's domain — unbounded queues, silent failures, unclear state under surge — and an append-only signed log is a genuine gift to after-action review. The caution is real: a certified, 24/7, life-critical vendor collides head-on with the one-hand problem of Section Three. So the SLC path is **not** replacing a computer-aided-dispatch system; it is building the adjacent piece nobody owns well — a verifiable after-action log, or a mutual-aid coordination surface — and letting it earn trust long before it could ever earn liability. Lives are saved by the boring, verifiable middle layer as surely as by the sharp end.
