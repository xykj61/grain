## What this is

A picture in Grain is **verified bytes that decode, deterministically and within named bounds, to a pixel grid** — and everything a keeper does with that picture is a pure, bounded function over the grid, the source never mutated. No network is asked to read a pixel; no key, no funds, no permission. An image is yours the way a page in a notebook is yours: held in your own hand, decoded by code small enough to read whole, addressed by its own content so a tampered byte refuses **by name** rather than painting garbage.

This module is the floor beneath Season A's **parts marketplace** and its **Photos app**. It answers the plainest question a device family can ask — *can the person who holds it also hold every picture on it?* — with yes, proven on metal.

## The three arcs, each closed whole

### Open Image — the codec and its guarantees (HUNK0–HUNK3)

- **`qoi.rye`** — a whole open **QOI** codec, encode and decode, so `decode(encode(pm))` recovers a pixmap **byte-for-byte** across all six chunk kinds (rgb · rgba · index · diff · luma · run). Real compression, and five corruptions each refusing by name rather than reading past the end. Witness: `tools/hunk_qoi_witness.rish`.
- **`../pond/apps/image_artifact.rye`** — an image becomes a content-addressed **Tablecloth** artifact. `store_image` beads a picture under a name that pins a SHA3 content address; `fetch_image` reassembles it, proving **every bead against its digest first**, so a tampered bead refuses `DigestMismatch` and an unknown name refuses `UnknownArtifact` — each *before the codec reads a single pixel*. Same image under two names stores once (the dedup dividend). Witness: `tools/hunk_artifact_witness.rish`.
- **`../brushstroke/image_skate.rye`** — the decoded grid meets **Skate**: `down_map` averages each block to the nearest of a seven-color anchor palette and wears it over a full-block glyph, so a picture paints on the glass surface. Witness: `tools/hunk_skate_witness.rish`.
- **`photos.rye` — `crop`** — the first Photos verb, pure and bounded: read a rectangle, return a fresh smaller image, source untouched; coordinates checked in `u64` so a rectangle can never wrap. Witness: `tools/hunk_photos_witness.rish`.

### The parts marketplace — McMaster-Carr's one-sheet trick, with honest facts (HUNK4–HUNK7 · HUNK71–HUNK93)

One decoded sheet serves a whole catalog. `sprite.rye` binds each product name to a bounded **window** into a single decoded image and renders it by reusing `photos.crop` — every window bound-checked in `u64` at **add time**, so a render can never fail on bounds. `../pond/apps/sprite_store.rye` gives the sheet a content-addressed identity; `sprite_catalog.rye` lets the index itself travel as a flat-Bron `format sprite-catalog-v1` record, so `render(parse(render(x)))` is a fixed point. Disjoint windows never leak a neighbor's bytes; the sheet is shared and unmutated across every render.

A part is a picture *with its facts*. `part_catalog.rye` binds each product's window to a **part number**, a **material**, and a **price in cents** — recorded as a `u64` fact, never a balance the tree can move — so a `PartCatalog` holds one `SpriteIndex` (the pictures) and one record per product (the facts) in the same order, grown together so a part is never a window without facts. The whole catalog travels as `format part-catalog-v1`, a fixed point over *both* halves. `../pond/apps/part_store.rye` gives the catalog a content address: `store_catalog` beads its text, `open_catalog` fetches it back **verified before the parser reads a window or a price**, so a whole marketplace — pictures and prices — reproduces from cold out of *(sheet, catalog address)*, a tampered catalog refusing `DigestMismatch`. Witnesses: `tools/hunk_sprite_*_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_catalog_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_store_witness.rish`.

A keeper searches the catalog by its facts, and the **boolean query algebra stands whole** (HUNK75–HUNK85). `part_filter.rye` reads a single `key:value` clause over the stored facts; `part_query.rye` parses it; `part_and.rye` narrows a line of clauses to the **intersection** (a match-all empty line), `part_or.rye` widens it to the **union** (a match-none empty line) — the two true De Morgan duals, OR always a superset of AND. `part_facets.rye` joins them into the canonical faceted query — **OR within a facet** (values joined by `|`), **AND across facets** (groups by space), so `material:stainless-304|material:brass max:100` means *(stainless or brass) and under a dollar*; a `|`-free line is exactly HUNK78's conjunction, a strict superset that disturbs nothing. On glass, `../brushstroke/part_and_search.rye`, `../brushstroke/part_or_search.rye`, and `../brushstroke/part_facets_search.rye` draw exactly the narrowed, widened, or faceted page — *what a keeper types is what a keeper sees* — a price clause **comparing** a recorded cent count, never moving one (custody gate #3 untouched). Above the shelf, `../brushstroke/part_search_header.rye` (the pure AND) and `../brushstroke/part_facets_header.rye` (the whole faceted grammar) **echo the canonical query and its *N results* count**, stacked into one screen a keeper reads top to bottom — the echo a **fixed point of the parser**, so the header shows the query the machine understood, never a hopeful transcript, and its count is exactly the number of parts drawn beneath it (no drift). `../brushstroke/part_facets_paged.rye` carries the page into that header — ***page N of M*** above the very page of parts it names, a zero-result line carrying no page at all. `../brushstroke/part_facets_sort.rye` sorts the result **cheapest-first** — `sort_by_price` reorders the parts a query kept by their recorded cent count, ascending, ties keeping held order (a **stable** sort, a **permutation** of the result — same parts, only reordered), and `facets_sorted_shelf` draws that page least dear at the top, a price still **compared and reordered by, never moved** (custody gate #3 untouched). `../brushstroke/part_facets_sorted_screen.rye` closes the arc — `format_sorted_header` appends **`sorted by price`** to the paged header (only when a result exists) and `facets_sorted_screen` stacks it over the sorted shelf, so a keeper reads *what I asked · how many · which page · that it is sorted by price* above the cheapest-first parts, the word naming the order it sits over (no drift). `../brushstroke/part_facets_sort_desc.rye` turns the sort **both ways** — `sort_by_price_desc` reads the same result **dearest-first**, ties still kept in **held order** (so descending is not a naive reversal; on distinct prices it is the exact reverse of ascending), and `facets_sorted_desc_shelf` draws that page high to low, answering *what is the best they have* beside *what can I afford*. `../brushstroke/part_facets_sort_dir.rye` names the direction over the page — a `SortDir` drives both the header tail (**`sorted by price low to high`** or **`high to low`**) and the shelf it sits over, so `facets_sorted_screen_dir` can never let the label disagree with the order (the `high to low` header sits over the dearest part first, `low to high` over the cheapest), and the two directions read as exact reverses on a distinct result. `../brushstroke/part_facets_sort_query.rye` hands the direction to the keeper — a **`sort:price-asc`** or **`sort:price-desc`** directive anywhere in the typed line is lifted out (default ascending when absent, `UnknownSortValue` on a bad value, `DuplicateSort` on a second), and the stripped rest rejoins as the faceted query byte-for-byte, so `facets_sorted_query_screen` draws the keeper's chosen direction from one honest line — a strict superset of HUNK92 (a line with no directive behaves exactly as ascending). Witnesses: `tools/hunk_part_filter_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_and_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_or_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_and_search_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_or_search_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_search_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_search_header_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_header_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_paged_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_sort_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_sorted_screen_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_sort_desc_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_sort_dir_witness.rish`, `tools/hunk_part_facets_sort_query_witness.rish`.

### The Photos app — edit without ever losing the original (HUNK8–HUNK70)

Named plainly **Photos**, built entirely on the module above. Every gesture is a pure verb over the grid, added **additively** beside `crop`:

- **Orient** — `flip_h`, `flip_v`, `rotate_quarter`, proven by the algebra they obey (four quarter-turns are identity; a mirror is its own inverse).
- **Resample** — `scale` (nearest-neighbor), an area-average path, and a bilinear path, each refusing a zero-dimension or over-ceiling target before it allocates.
- **Light and color** — `adjust` (brightness · contrast around the 128 midpoint, alpha untouched), `saturate`, hue rotation, and a book of named **filter presets** that travels as a `format filter-presets-v1` record.
- **Non-destructive editing** — `photo_edits.rye` records the verbs **as data** (an `Edit` union, a bounded `EditList`, `max_edits = 64`) and `apply` **replays** them over a source to a fresh image; replay is deterministic, so an edited picture never needs its pixels stored — only the pair *(source, edit-list)*. The list travels as a `format photo-edits-v1` record; `../pond/apps/edit_store.rye` beads it content-addressed; `photo_revert.rye` reverts by simply dropping edits off the end, never losing what a keeper might redo.
- **The glass surface** — `../brushstroke/` carries the whole app onto Skate: a crop tool (drag → frame → commit → chain), a filter strip and picker (read the book at a glance, swipe to preview, keep one), a filmstrip of the edit history (jump, scrub, track), a scrolled screen view, and the **Photos library** — a wall of content-addressed tiles where a tap resolves backward through the wall's geometry to fetch and open exactly the picture under the finger. The loop closes end to end: **library → tap → open → edit → save → library**, the wall growing by one when an opened picture is saved back.

### Open Media Primitives — the color and font floor beside the codec (Season G)

The `image/` directory also holds the opening rungs of **Season G (Open Media Primitives)** — the open, bounded, permissively-licensed media family the image codec began. They finish for color and type what QOI finished for pixels: a value held exactly, every reading of it deterministic, every malformed input refusing by name.

- **`color.rye`** — the open **sRGB color algebra**. A color is four bytes (red · green · blue · alpha) held exactly; hex (`#rgb` … `#rrggbbaa`) and functional `rgb()/hsl()` texts are *readings* of those bytes, so `parse(format(c))` recovers a color byte-for-byte, and a malformed text refuses by name (`BadHexLength` · `BadHexDigit` · `BadFunctionForm` · `ChannelOutOfRange`). All arithmetic is integer fixed-point, deterministic across targets; the module states its measured HSL round-trip tolerance rather than claiming an exactness integer math cannot hold. The color model **Brushstroke and Skate read the day the design system lands** — color math waits on nothing, only rendering is paused. Witness: `tools/color_algebra_witness.rish`.
- **`color_adjust.rye`** — the **palette toolkit** over the color algebra: the everyday moves a design system makes from one seed color, written single-stranded as a consumer of color's own HSL round trip. `lighten`/`darken` steer the lightness band and clamp at white/black; `saturate`/`desaturate` steer the saturation band; `grayscale` yields an equal-channel grey (equal to a full desaturation); `rotate_hue` turns the wheel (a full 360 turn returns the hue) and `complement` is a half turn. Every amount is a bounded percentage-point step or degree turn, each verb clamps at the band edge rather than wrapping, and alpha rides through untouched — read-adjust-write over the existing algebra, so color's own proofs carry through. The scale **Brushstroke and Skate grow the day the design system lands**. Witness: `tools/color_adjust_witness.rish`.
- **`color_scale.rye`** — the **design-system ramp** one rung above the palette toolkit: grow a whole even tonal **scale** from **one** seed color — the Tailwind 50..900 / Material tonal row, tones from lightest tint to darkest shade that share the seed's hue and saturation while their lightness steps evenly down a named band (`scale_light_max` 95 down to `scale_light_min` 10). `tonal_scale(seed, dst)` fills a caller's buffer lightest-first; `tone_at(seed, i, count)` reads a single tone; `tone_lightness(i, count)` is the pure integer lerp beneath both. Single-stranded over color's HSL round trip — color decides the algebra, this module only sets each tone's lightness — so a red seed keeps its hue, a grey seed grows a pure-grey scale, alpha rides through every tone, and a scale is deterministic. A length under two or over `scale_max_tones` (32) refuses `ScaleTooShort`/`ScaleTooLong` by name, having written nothing. Witness: `tools/color_scale_witness.rish`.
- **`color_gradient.rye`** — the **two-endpoint ramp**, the sibling of the one-seed tonal scale: grow a whole even gradient between **two** arbitrary colors — the CSS `linear-gradient` stop list. `gradient(a, b, dst)` fills a caller's buffer with evenly-spaced stops, `dst[0]` the start color exactly and `dst[len-1]` the end color exactly; `sample(a, b, i, count)` reads a single stop; `stop_t(i, count)` is the even integer lerp of the mix parameter (0..255) beneath both. Single-stranded over `color.mix` — color decides the blend, this module only **spaces** the stops — so a red-to-blue mid stop carries both channels (a true blend, not a hard cut), a black-to-white ramp ascends strictly in luminance, a color-to-itself ramp stays flat, alpha rides the same lerp so an opaque-to-clear fade steps its alpha down, and a ramp is deterministic. A length under two or over `gradient_max_stops` (64) refuses `GradientTooShort`/`GradientTooLong` by name, having written nothing. Witness: `tools/color_gradient_witness.rish`.
- **`color_ramp.rye`** — the **multi-stop gradient**, the general form of `color_gradient` across **many** control colors — the CSS `linear-gradient(red, yellow, green)` stop list. `ramp(control, dst)` fills `dst` with an evenly-spaced piecewise-linear ramp through the `control` colors (`dst[0]` the first control exactly, `dst[len-1]` the last, and a stop that lands on a control reads it byte-for-byte). Single-stranded over `color.mix` — color decides each blend, this module only **routes** each stop to its segment and local fraction, floor-matching `color_gradient.stop_t` — so the general contains the special: a **two-control ramp equals `color_gradient.gradient` byte-for-byte**. A black-grey-white ramp ascends non-decreasing in luminance across both segments; a color-to-itself ramp stays flat; a control list under two or over `ramp_max_control` (16), or a `dst` under two or over the shared `gradient_max_stops` (64), refuses `RampTooFewControls`/`RampTooManyControls`/`RampTooShort`/`RampTooLong` by name. Witness: `tools/color_ramp_witness.rish`.
- **`color_ramp_stops.rye`** — the **positioned multi-stop gradient**, the general form of both `color_gradient` and `color_ramp`: each control carries its own **position** in `0..pos_max` (255), strictly ascending from 0 to `pos_max` — the CSS `linear-gradient(red 0%, yellow 80%, green 100%)` stop list, where the warm half can bunch into the last fifth. `sample_at(control, positions, pos)` reads the blended color at any global position (a position on a control reads it byte-for-byte; a position between carries both channels); `ramp_positioned(control, positions, dst)` fills `dst` with evenly-spaced **output** stops sampling the arbitrarily-positioned ramp. Single-stranded over `color.mix` — color decides the blend, this module only **routes** a global position to its segment and local fraction — so the general contains the special: a **two-control ramp anchored at `{0, pos_max}` equals `color_gradient.gradient` byte-for-byte**, while `color_ramp` is a **sibling** (it routes in segment units where this routes in the shared position domain, so uneven positions bend the ramp where even spacing cannot). A black-grey-white ramp ascends non-decreasing in luminance whatever the spacing; a length mismatch, non-ascending positions, a first off zero, a last off `pos_max`, a control list under two or over `ramp_max_control` (16), or a `dst` under two or over `gradient_max_stops` (64) refuses `StopCountMismatch`/`StopsNotAscending`/`FirstStopNotZero`/`LastStopNotMax`/`RampTooFewStops`/`RampTooManyStops`/`RampTooShort`/`RampTooLong` by name. Witness: `tools/color_ramp_stops_witness.rish`.
- **`color_swatch.rye`** -- the **on-colour ramp** one rung above the tonal scale: it joins `color_scale.rye`'s scale with `color.rye`'s readable ink, so every tone carries the black or white ink that stays legible on it -- the design-system "on-colour" token (Material's on-primary, Tailwind's paired text colour). A `Swatch` is a fill plus its ink; `readable_scale(seed, dst)` grows a whole row of swatches from one seed, and `swatch_at(seed, i, count)` reads a single one. Single-stranded -- `color_scale` decides each fill, `color` decides each ink, this module only **pairs** them -- so the fills are byte-for-byte the ramp and each ink is exactly `color.readable_ink` of its own fill. Because the ramp's lightness steps down monotonically and `readable_ink` flips at one luminance threshold, the crossover is clean: black on the light tones above, white on the dark tones below, never a black tone beneath a white, and both endpoints clear WCAG AA. Text ink is fully opaque while a translucent seed keeps its fill glass; a length under two or over `swatch_max_tones` (32, shared with the tonal scale) refuses `ScaleTooShort`/`ScaleTooLong` by name. And `branded_scale(seed, ink_light, ink_dark, dst)` inks the same ramp from a keeper's own brand **pair** rather than pure black and white -- each swatch's ink is `color.best_ink` of its fill among the two -- the general form of `readable_scale`: passing pure white then black recovers `readable_scale` byte-for-byte (both break a tie toward the lighter ink), so the general verb contains the special one. Witness: `tools/color_swatch_witness.rish`.
- **`color_harmony.rye`** -- the **named colour-wheel palettes** one rung above the palette toolkit: the classical harmonies a design system offers as a swatch rather than an angle -- `complementary`, `analogous`, `triadic`, `split_complementary`, `tetradic`, and `square` -- each grown from **one** seed by composing `color_adjust.rotate_hue` at the centuries-old wheel angles. Every scheme returns a fixed, small array (two to four colours, bounded by construction) that **leads with the keeper's own seed byte-for-byte**; `complementary`'s partner is exactly `color_adjust.complement`, so the module composes the toolkit rather than re-deriving it. Single-stranded -- `color_adjust` decides the hue turn (and its wrap, held saturation and lightness, carried alpha), this module only **picks** the classical angles -- so a translucent seed grows a translucent palette and a grey seed, having no hue to turn, collapses every scheme back to the same grey. The even-spacing claims (triadic thirds, square quarters) hold within a stated 4-degree integer-round-trip tolerance, stated rather than claimed exact. Witness: `tools/color_harmony_witness.rish`.
- **`color_contrast.rye`** -- the **WCAG contrast table** a design system ships beside its swatches, one rung above the on-colour ramp: for every swatch of a palette, the exact contrast of its ink against its fill (`ratio_x100`, so 4.50:1 is 450) and which conformance bars that ink clears -- `meets_aa`, `meets_aa_large`, `meets_aaa`. `reading_for(swatch)` reads a single row; `read_scale(swatches, dst)` reads a whole palette into a caller's buffer, refusing `ReportLenMismatch`/`ReportTooShort`/`ReportTooLong` by name. Single-stranded -- `color_swatch` decides each fill-and-ink pair, `color` measures each contrast, this module only **reads** one measurement per swatch -- so each reading agrees byte-for-byte with `color.contrast_ratio_x100` and `color.meets_aa`/`meets_aaa`, and the three bars **nest** every row (AAA implies AA-normal implies AA-large). The table is **honest rather than flattering**: it reports a legible pair and a deliberately low-contrast grey-on-grey pair with equal faithfulness, so a design system reads which tones are body-text legible and which need large text rather than a blanket claim. Witness: `tools/color_contrast_witness.rish`.
- **`color_names.rye`** -- the **named color table** the algebra needed: the sixteen HTML4 / CSS Level 1 color keywords (the oldest, most durable named set on the web, stable since ~1996), each bound to its canonical hex. `resolve(name)` reads a name to its four bytes -- `"red"` to `{255,0,0,255}` -- case-insensitively, refusing `UnknownColorName` by name rather than guessing; `name_of(color)` reads a color back to its exact keyword, or `null` when none matches. Single-stranded -- this module **names** each color and `color` **decides** the bytes through its own `parse_hex`, so no private parser and no stored channel byte -- every entry is proven to round-trip through `color.parse_hex` and back through `color.format_hex`. A Simple, Lovable, Complete set: bounded, sorted strictly ascending, duplicate-free, and **block-complete** at the whole HTML4 sixteen (the full CSS set is the sibling `color_names_css.rye`, never folded in here so this stays the durable sixteen); `name_of` matches only opaque colors, so it never claims a name it cannot honestly hold. `resolve_any(text)` is the **universal front door** one rung up: an all-letters text resolves through the table, anything else delegates to `color.parse` (hex or `rgb()`/`hsl()`), spaces trimmed -- so a stylesheet or theme file reads `"red"`, `"#ff0000"`, and `"rgb(255,0,0)"` through one call, names from this module and every other form from color's own proven parser. Witness: `tools/color_names_witness.rish`.
- **`color_names_css.rye`** -- the **full CSS named-color table**, the sibling that carries the whole modern set the HTML4 sixteen only opens: the **148 CSS Color keywords**, `aliceblue` through `yellowgreen`, each bound to its canonical hex. `resolve(name)` reads any CSS keyword to its four bytes case-insensitively -- `"rebeccapurple"` to `{102,51,153,255}` -- refusing `UnknownColorName` by name; `name_of(color)` reads a color back to its **earliest matching** keyword, so a color with a synonym reads the alphabetically first (the set carries nine `gray`/`grey`-style pairs -- `#00ffff` reads `"aqua"` before `"cyan"`, `#808080` reads `"gray"` before `"grey"`), deterministically. Single-stranded exactly as its HTML4 sibling -- this module **names**, `color` **decides** the bytes through `parse_hex`, no private parser or stored channel. The two tables stand apart on purpose: `color_names.rye` stays the block-complete sixteen, this the block-complete 148, and the selftest proves this a **strict superset** of the sixteen (every HTML4 name resolves here to identical bytes), so the older witness guards the overlap by construction. Values cross-read from the W3C CSS Color spec and an independent recall of the same standard, agreeing byte-for-byte -- verified two ways rather than transcribed once. `color_nearest` and `color_name_group` can read this richer table to name any color more finely. Witness: `tools/color_names_css_witness.rish`.
- **`color_nearest.rye`** -- the **nearest named color to ANY color**, one rung above the named table. Where `color_names.name_of` reads a color to its *exact* keyword and `null` for everything else, `nearest(c)` answers the wider question a design system asks -- *what do we call this?* -- for a color that need not land on a keyword: it scans the sixteen HTML4 names and returns the closest by squared sRGB distance, so a near-red reads `"red"`, a dark blue reads `"navy"`, and every color has a name. A `Match` carries the name, the exact color it stands for, and the squared distance (0 when the query *is* a named color). Single-stranded -- `color_names` **owns** the names and their hex, `color` **decides** the bytes through its own `parse_hex`, this module only **measures** distance and **picks** the least. Alpha is outside the metric (the named set is opaque), so a translucent red still reads `"red"` where `name_of` returns `null`; ties break to the earlier sorted entry, so a color midway between `black` and `navy` reads the alphabetically-first `"black"` deterministically. Bounded (the whole scan is the fixed sixteen) and integer (the squared distance of three byte channels never leaves `u32`, bounded by `max_dist_sq`). `nearest_name(c)` reads the word alone. Witness: `tools/color_nearest_witness.rish`.
- **`color_nearest_css.rye`** -- the **nearest CSS-named color** to any color, the fine sibling of `color_nearest`. Where that scans the sixteen HTML4 names and a cornflower blue reads only `"blue"`, this scans the full **148 CSS names** (`color_names_css.rye`) and reads `"cornflowerblue"` exactly -- the richer table as a finer ruler, the payoff the 148-name set was built for. Same single-stranded shape: `color_names_css` **owns** the names, `color` **decides** the bytes, this module only **measures** squared sRGB distance and **picks** the least; a tie breaks to the alphabetically-earlier entry, so the nine synonym pairs read their first name (`#00ffff` reads `"aqua"` before `"cyan"`). The selftest proves the **payoff against the sixteen-name sibling**: for the same color the CSS reading is strictly nearer and finer-named, imported and compared on metal rather than asserted. Every one of the 148 is its own nearest at distance zero; alpha stays outside the metric, so a translucent cornflowerblue still reads its name where `name_of` returns `null`. Witness: `tools/color_nearest_css_witness.rish`.
- **`color_name_group.rye`** -- the **coarse color family** of any color, a sibling of the naming pair one rung above `color.rye`. Where `color_names` reads a color to its *exact* keyword and `color_nearest` to the *closest* named swatch, `group_of(c)` answers the coarser, older question a palette organizer asks first -- *what family is this?* -- and returns one of eleven plain words: the eight hue families `red` `orange` `yellow` `green` `cyan` `blue` `purple` `magenta`, plus the three achromatic `black` `white` `gray`. Single-stranded -- `color` **decides** the hue, saturation, and lightness through its own proven `rgb_to_hsl`, and this module only **reads** those three numbers against named bands (each a constant with a why, this module's own design, not a transcribed table). Tone speaks first: a very dark color reads `black` and a very light one `white`, whatever its hue, because at the lightness edges the eye sees almost no color; a washed-out color reads `gray`; a chromatic color reads its hue family, red wrapping the wheel to own both ends. Bounded (eleven families, a fixed sorted scan), integer (degrees and percents only), deterministic -- a photo tagger buckets by hue, a palette sorts by family, a filter keeps "the blues". Witness: `tools/color_name_group_witness.rish`.
- **`color_tone.rye`** -- the **coarse tone** of any color -- `dark`, `mid`, or `light` -- and the deliberate sibling of `color_name_group.rye`. Beyond the accented capitals, **`font5x7_upper_nordic.rye`** draws the three Danish-Norwegian capitals a name reaches for at its head or in ALL-CAPS -- A with a ring, the AE ligature, O with a stroke (`U+00C5` `U+00C6` `U+00D8`), the capital twins of the lowercase Nordic corpus -- built the same three distinct ways (a hollow ring above the compressed A, a fused AE ligature with a full crossbar and no mark, an O with a stroke through its bowl), each read by its own toothed guard. **`font5x7_upper_icelandic.rye`** then draws the three Icelandic capitals a name reaches for in ALL-CAPS -- Eth, Y acute, Thorn (`U+00D0` `U+00DD` `U+00DE`), the capital twins of the lowercase Icelandic corpus -- built three distinct ways, each read by its own guard: Eth is a full-height capital D crossed by a bar (a top bar and a bottom bar closing the bowl, a crossbar on the middle row crossing the stem into it), Thorn stands a full-height stem open at top and bottom with a bowl at its middle, and Y acute leans its stroke right on the compressed capital Y. **`font5x7_upper_close.rye`** then draws the three capitals that CLOSE the uppercase set -- E diaeresis, I diaeresis, O tilde (`U+00CB` `U+00CF` `U+00D5`), the capital twins of lowercase letters the diaeresis and tilde corpora already carry -- so **every alphabetic codepoint in the whole Latin-1 Supplement block, lowercase and uppercase alike, now stands drawn** in our own open hand. Its crux is two marks read by their own guards: the E and I diaeresis wear two parted dots on the top mark row (center blank, second row clear), proven byte-identical to each other so the mark stays one hand, and the O tilde wears a two-row wave cresting right. Two **unifying fonts** finish each half the way `font5x7_ascii.rye` finishes the printable set: **`font5x7_accented.rye`** merges the four lowercase corpora into one gfa1 atlas of twenty-one accents (`U+00DF`..`U+00FC`), and **`font5x7_upper.rye`** merges the two uppercase corpora into its exact capital twin -- twenty-one accented capitals (`U+00C0`..`U+00DC`) -- each proving the merged codepoints run strictly ascending and unique and the count equals the sum of its corpora, so the sets are disjoint and no letter is lost. One level above both, **`font5x7_latin1_block.rye`** gathers **all thirteen** leaf corpora into one gfa1 atlas of the whole Latin-1 Supplement letter block -- sixty-two letters (`U+00C0`..`U+00FF`), thirty-two lowercase and thirty uppercase -- and proves **block completeness**: it walks the whole 64-codepoint block and shows every codepoint present except exactly the two that are not letters, the multiplication sign (`U+00D7`) and the division sign (`U+00F7`), which live in `font5x7_math.rye`, so the font holds the whole letter block and nothing but it, the thirteen corpora disjoint. One level above every unifier, **`font5x7_latin1_text.rye`** merges the two proven sub-fonts -- `font5x7_ascii.rye` (the 95 printable ASCII glyphs, contiguous `0x20`..`0x7E`) and `font5x7_latin1_block.rye` (the 62 Latin-1 letters) -- into one gfa1 atlas of **157 glyphs**, the single font a Western European surface loads for a document, a caption, or a name. Beyond the round-trip and disjointness, its crux is the **clean gap**: the ASCII half ends at `~` and the Latin-1 half opens at A grave (`U+00C0`), so the range `0x7F`..`0xBF` between them carries no glyph (spot-checked at DEL, the copyright sign, and the inverted question mark), the ASCII half is proven contiguous, the Latin-1 half block-complete, and the space stays the one blank glyph. The source stays plain ASCII (numeric codepoint literals) though the glyphs cover a non-ASCII range; like every corpus each proves `decode(encode(atlas))` byte-for-byte, all present and distinct, each inside an honest legibility band. Witnesses: `tools/font5x7_latin1_witness.rish` and its `_grave` / `_circumflex` / `_tilde` / `_diaeresis` / `_german` / `_nordic` / `_icelandic` / `_accented` / `_upper_accented` / `_upper_latin1` / `_upper_nordic` / `_upper_icelandic` / `_upper_close` / `_upper` / `_latin1_block` / `_latin1_text` siblings.
- **`text_layout.rye`** -- the **measure half** that makes the corpora usable: given an atlas and a byte string of codepoints, it places each glyph along a line at its running pen origin and sums the advances into a total width. `layout_line` returns one `PlacedGlyph` (codepoint, pen origin, atlas glyph) per byte; `measure_width` sizes a line without a buffer; `covers` asks whether the atlas carries every byte; `wrap_lines` breaks a paragraph into lines that stay within a max width, at space boundaries, so each wrapped span (`WrapLine`: start, len, width) lays out directly and its stated width equals a fresh measure of it -- a word wider than the max takes its own line whole (word-boundary wrapping, never a mid-word split). It is pure geometry -- no pixel is touched, so like `color.rye` it is floor code that waits on nothing, and painting the placed coverage into an RGBA grid is the rung it prepares. A byte with no glyph is a named refusal (`UncoveredCodepoint`), a zero max width a named refusal (`MaxWidthTooSmall`), never a silent hole, so a caller composing with `font5x7_ascii.rye` lays out and wraps any printable string and learns of a gap at once. Witness: `tools/text_layout_witness.rish`.

- **`text_paint.rye`** -- the **paint half** that `text_layout.rye` named as the rung it prepares: it turns a laid-out string from measured geometry into a string **seen** on an image. Single-stranded composition over four free parts, braiding none of them: `text_layout` places each glyph, `glyph.rye` hands each a coverage grid (one byte per cell, 0 no ink .. 255 full ink), `color.rye` composites an ink colour over the destination (Porter-Duff src-over), and `qoi.rye` owns the RGBA target. A coverage byte is the ink's alpha at that cell (scaled by the ink's own alpha), so full ink over an opaque ink writes the ink exactly, no ink leaves the destination untouched, and a partial cell blends true through `color.over`. `paint_placed` paints an already-placed line; `paint_line` lays out and paints in one call; `solid` mints a one-colour canvas to paint on. The line's painted box is bound-checked **once** up front, so the inner loop is proven in-bounds and an overrunning paint refuses `PaintOutOfBounds` by name rather than writing outside the buffer. Where a surface would rather show the visible part of a too-wide label than field a refusal, `paint_placed_clipped` paints only the cells that land inside the target and drops the rest, returning the count it painted; it composites an in-bounds cell **identically** to `paint_placed` (both share one `composite_cell` routine), matches the strict paint pixel-for-pixel where they overlap, and degrades to a clean no-op when the whole line sits off-canvas. `paint_paragraph` wraps a block to a max width and paints each line stepping down, all-or-nothing against the target (`ParagraphOutOfBounds` by name), and `paint_paragraph_aligned` sets that block **left, centre, or right** by a pure sideways offset within each line's slack -- alignment moves ink without adding or dropping a pixel, so the up-front box check and the changed-pixel count hold whatever the alignment, `.left` reproduces `paint_paragraph` byte-for-byte, and `.center`/`.right` match a hand-composed line-by-line offset paint exactly. And `paint_paragraph_clipped` is the paragraph's clip companion to `paint_placed_clipped`: where `paint_paragraph` refuses a block that overruns, this one paints as much of it as a fixed box holds -- a line inside lands identically to the strict paint, a line straddling the bottom lands its visible rows, a line entirely below is skipped -- so a bounded panel shows the text that fits rather than refusing the whole block, returning the count of cells painted (a box the block fits in clips byte-for-byte the strict paint; a box one line-box tall paints just the first line where the strict painter would refuse; a box below the block is a clean no-op). And `paint_paragraph_boxed` is the **vertical** companion to `paint_paragraph_aligned`'s sideways set: given a box taller than the block, it places the whole stack **top, middle, or bottom** by a pure downward offset of the origin, then delegates the wrap-stack-paint to the aligned painter unchanged -- so vertical placement moves the stack down as one, adding no pixel and dropping none (`.top` reproduces the plain paragraph at the box origin; `.middle`/`.bottom` match a plain paint at the shifted origin; the changed-pixel count holds across every set), refusing `ParagraphBoxTooShort` by name when the block is taller than the box (the strict all-or-nothing companion to the clipping painter). And `paint_paragraph_in_box` is the family's 2-D capstone -- a real text region: it positions the content block within a box on **both** axes at once, one `h` set choosing left/centre/right for the block's place in the box *and* each line's set within it, one `v` set choosing top/middle/bottom, delegating the vertical half to `paint_paragraph_boxed` unchanged so every axis stays in one place. Each corner equals a plain aligned paint at the box offset (placement moves the block as one, adding no pixel and dropping none), refusing `ParagraphBoxTooNarrow` on a box narrower than the block and `ParagraphBoxTooShort` on one too short -- all-or-nothing on both axes. No new storage, codec, palette, or geometry -- only the join. Witness: `tools/text_paint_witness.rish`.

- **`shape.rye`** -- the **geometry floor** beneath the paint rungs: two bounded rectangle verbs over an RGBA grid, the shapes a surface frames a picture with before it paints a glyph. Where `text_paint.rye` paints **glyphs** onto a `qoi.Pixmap`, this rung paints **plain area** -- `fill_rect` fills a rectangle, `stroke_rect` draws a rectangle outline (a frame) -- the shapes a panel, a selection highlight, a crop overlay, a table cell, or a progress-bar track are drawn from. Pure geometry over two public APIs, braiding neither: `color.rye` composites the paint over the destination (src-over), `qoi.rye` owns the RGBA target. An opaque paint writes the colour exactly; a translucent paint blends true through `color.over`, so a panel tint reads through to the picture beneath. Every rectangle's box is bound-checked **once** up front (in `u64` so the sum can never wrap), so an overrun refuses `RectOutOfBounds` by name rather than writing outside the buffer, and `stroke_rect` paints its border ring in a **single pass** (a pixel belongs to the ring when it lies within the thickness of any edge), so a corner is composited **exactly once** -- a translucent frame reads uniform, never a darker corner where two edge bands would overlap. `fill_rect` refuses `BadRectDims` on a zero extent; `stroke_rect` refuses `BadThickness` on a zero frame and `StrokeTooThick` when two borders would overrun a side; `ring_cells` predicts the painted ring count before a pixel is drawn. Witness: `tools/shape_witness.rish`.
- **`gradient_paint.rye`** -- the rung where the **color ramp meets the pixels**: the twin of what `text_paint.rye` did for the font family (it turned laid-out glyphs into pixels; this turns a positioned gradient into pixels). `paint_gradient_h` paints a **horizontal** positioned gradient into a rectangle -- each column is the color `color_ramp_stops.rye` samples at that column's global position (`c * pos_max / (w-1)`), painted down the whole height -- so a keeper who names a stop list reads back a gradient strip a surface can show; `paint_gradient_v` is the **vertical** twin with the axes swapped (each row the color at its global position, painted across the width, the ramp running top to bottom); `paint_gradient_diag` and `paint_gradient_adiag` are the **diagonal** pair that finish the linear-gradient family (main diagonal: pixel `(cx, cy)` maps to `(cx + cy) * pos_max / ((w-1) + (h-1))`, so the top-left corner is the first control exactly and the bottom-right the last, each anti-diagonal band one constant color; the anti-diagonal twin runs top-right to bottom-left, that painting mirrored left-to-right -- the CSS `linear-gradient(to bottom right, ...)` shape, refusing `StripTooSmall` on a single-pixel box). Compose not braid over three public APIs: `color_ramp_stops` decides each column's color, `qoi.rye` owns the RGBA target, `color.rye` composites src-over (so a translucent gradient tints the picture beneath), and this rung only **routes** each column to its position. The leftmost column is the first control exactly and the rightmost the last, byte-for-byte down the whole height; a mid column carries both channels (a true blend) constant vertically; uneven positions bend the painted strip where even spacing cannot; the strip round-trips through the open QOI codec byte-for-byte; a sub-rectangle paints only its own columns (the gutters stay ground). All-or-nothing -- the whole box is bound-checked once up front (in `u64` so a far edge can never wrap) and the ramp validated once before any pixel, so a `StripTooNarrow` (width under two), `BadStripDims` (zero height), `GradientStripOutOfBounds`, or any `color_ramp_stops` ramp refusal leaves the target untouched. Witness: `tools/gradient_paint_witness.rish`.
- **`radial_paint.rye`** -- the **circle** beside the line: the sibling of `gradient_paint.rye` for the other major CSS gradient shape. Where a linear gradient maps a pixel to its distance **along** one axis, `paint_gradient_radial` maps a pixel to its distance **from** the box center -- the center is the first control exactly and every corner the last, each ring between one constant color (the CSS `radial-gradient(circle, ...)` shape, its default `farthest-corner` extent). Compose not braid, unchanged: `color_ramp_stops` decides each pixel's color, `qoi.rye` owns the RGBA target, `color.rye` composites src-over, and this rung only **routes** each pixel to its radial position. The routing stays integer fixed-point so a disc is deterministic: coordinates are doubled (`2*cx - (w-1)`) so the center lands exactly on a pixel for odd dimensions and exactly between two for even ones without a fraction; a pixel's squared distance from the center `d2 = dx2*dx2 + dy2*dy2`, the farthest corner `max_d2 = (w-1)^2 + (h-1)^2` away, and the position `p = isqrt(d2 * pos_max^2 / max_d2)` (a bounded integer square root, no float), so the center (`d2 == 0`) reads 0 and a corner (`d2 == max_d2`) reads `pos_max` exactly. The four edge midpoints share one constant ring color (a true circle); the position grows with the radius; uneven positions bend the disc where even spacing cannot; the disc round-trips through the open QOI codec byte-for-byte; a non-square box still centers and corners exactly. All-or-nothing -- the whole box is bound-checked once up front (in `u64`) and the ramp validated once before any pixel, so a `BadStripDims` (zero dimension), `StripTooSmall` (single-pixel box), `RadialOutOfBounds`, or any `color_ramp_stops` ramp refusal leaves the target untouched. Witness: `tools/radial_paint_witness.rish`.

- **`conic_paint.rye`** -- the **sweep** beside the line and the ring: the third and last shape of the CSS gradient triad. Where a linear gradient maps a pixel to its distance **along** an axis and a radial gradient to its distance **from** the center, `paint_gradient_conic` maps a pixel to its **bearing** around the center -- the first control sits at the top (12 o'clock) and the ramp sweeps **clockwise** through east, south, and west back to the top (the CSS `conic-gradient(from 0deg at center, ...)` shape), every ray from the center one constant wedge color, the first and last controls meeting in a single seam at the top. Compose not braid, unchanged: `color_ramp_stops` decides each pixel's color, `qoi.rye` owns the RGBA target, `color.rye` composites src-over, and this rung only **routes** each pixel to its angular bearing. The routing needs no trigonometry: coordinates are doubled (`2*cx - (w-1)`) so the center is exact, and the bearing is an integer **pseudo-angle** -- the four quadrants around the center split the turn into four, and within a quadrant the position grows linearly with the ratio of the two coordinates (`(q*s + numer) * pos_max / (4*s)`, `s = |dx2| + |dy2|`), a monotonic function of the true angle. So the top reads 0, east `pos_max/4`, south `pos_max/2`, west `3*pos_max/4`, each exact. The bearings ascend strictly clockwise; each ray is one constant wedge; the top is the first/last seam; the sweep round-trips through the open QOI codec byte-for-byte; uneven positions bend the sweep where even spacing cannot; a non-square box still tops and seams exactly. All-or-nothing -- the whole box is bound-checked once up front (in `u64`) and the ramp validated once before any pixel, so a `BadStripDims` (zero dimension), `StripTooSmall` (single-pixel box), `ConicOutOfBounds`, or any `color_ramp_stops` ramp refusal leaves the target untouched. Witness: `tools/conic_paint_witness.rish`.

- **`text_panel.rye`** -- the first rung that **joins** the geometry floor with the text family: a framed text panel is the bordered card a surface draws when it wants readable text -- a dialog, a label chip, a callout, a captioned table cell. `paint_text_panel` fills the background (`shape.fill_rect`), strokes the border ring (`shape.stroke_rect`), and places a wrapped, aligned paragraph inside the inner content box (`text_paint.paint_paragraph_in_box`) -- the inner box the panel inset on every side by border thickness **plus** a padding gap. The crux is **containment**: the frame ring and the inked cells are disjoint by construction, so every ring pixel reads the border colour exactly and every ink pixel lies strictly inside the inner box (the text is sealed within the card). All-or-nothing -- the whole panel is bound-checked once up front and the wrapped block is proven to fit **before** any pixel is committed, so a panel that cannot hold its text refuses by name (`BadPanelDims` - `PanelOutOfBounds` - `PanelTooSmall` - `PanelContentTooTall`) having painted nothing; a borderless panel (thickness 0) skips the ring. - **`text_caption.rye`** -- the rung where the framed panel first composites onto **moving video**: a caption panel over video is the **lower third** a broadcast draws -- a fixed, readable card pinned to one place while the picture moves beneath it. The crux is the **static overlay** over moving video: because the panel is opaque and painted at the identical rectangle every frame, its pixels are **byte-identical** across the whole reel while the background outside it genuinely moves -- steady where the eye reads, live everywhere else, and measurable (within the panel rectangle every frame equals frame zero byte for byte; outside it at least one consecutive pair differs). The composited reel round-trips **byte for byte** through both open codecs (`frames.rye` gfv1, `frames_delta.rye` gfd1); the delta/keyframe mix is reported, never claimed, yet on a mostly-still shot the pinned caption and the still ground contribute zero delta, so the inter-frame gfd1 genuinely wins (41 frames of 96x48: gfd1 16329 bytes vs gfv1 23977). Witness: `tools/text_caption_witness.rish`.
- **`text_subtitle.rye`** -- the **timing** rung above the caption: where `text_caption.rye` pins **one** fixed card over a moving reel, a subtitle track pins a **sequence** of cards, each shown only during its own span of frames. That timing is the whole difference between a lower-third and a subtitle -- the panel place holds still while the text inside it changes as the clip plays, and between two cues nothing is shown at all. A track is a bounded list of `Cue` spans (`start`..`end`, half-open), validated ordered and disjoint before a pixel is painted (`EmptyTrack` - `TooManyCues` - `CueEmptySpan` - `CueBeyondReel` - `CuesOverlap` by name); `active_cue(track, k)` is the whole reader a surface needs. The crux is that a cue is shown **exactly** over its span and nowhere else, measured against the plain no-subtitle background as ground truth: a **gap** frame equals the background byte for byte (no panel where no cue is active), a **shown** frame differs from the background **only within the panel rectangle** (the subtitle touches the card and nothing else), within one cue's span the panel is **byte-steady** frame to frame, and two different cues render **different** panels (the text really changed). Pure composition over `text_caption`'s proven approach; invents no storage, codec, palette, glyph, or font (default track: 3 cues over 24 frames of 96x48, 17 shown + 7 gap; gfd1 8196 bytes vs gfv1 10271). Witness: `tools/text_subtitle_witness.rish`.

- **`scrubber.rye`** -- the **timed-geometry** rung beside the subtitle track: where a subtitle pins timed **text** over a moving reel, a scrubber pins timed **geometry** -- the playback progress bar a video player draws along the bottom edge, its elapsed portion growing left to right as the clip plays. `image/shape.rye`'s own docstring names "a progress-bar track" as a shape `fill_rect`/`stroke_rect` exist to draw; this rung makes it literal, braiding nothing beneath. Witness: `tools/scrubber_witness.rish`.
- **`timecode.rye`** -- the **timed-text** companion to the scrubber's timed geometry: where a scrubber pins a progress **bar** whose fill width is a pure function of the frame index, a timecode pins a **readout** whose text is a pure function of the frame index -- the "0:07" clock a video player burns into a corner, ticking one step per second. Pure composition over `paint_text_panel` and both codecs; invents no storage, codec, palette, glyph, or font. Witness: `tools/timecode_witness.rish`.

- **`player_hud.rye`** -- the **capstone** of the timed-overlay family: the scrubber and the timecode **composed** on one reel, the way a real video player shows a progress bar along the bottom and a clock in the corner at once. - **`text_grid.rye`** -- the **bridge** where the terminal grid meets the pixel family. The tree grew two families side by side and never let them meet on metal: one is the terminal grid (`pond/apps/scooter_view.rye` folds a channel into a fixed 24x80 grid of character **cells** -- bytes; `dexter_line.rye` edits a bounded line; `scooter_compose.rye` joins them into a composed frame), the other is this open-media paint family (`font5x7_ascii.rye` draws every printable ASCII glyph, `text_paint.rye` composites a laid line's coverage into an RGBA grid, `qoi.rye` owns the pixel target). This rung is the join -- it paints a monospace grid of character bytes into a real image, so a terminal face is not only bytes a reader parses but **pixels** a surface (Grainphone, Realidream, Skate) shows. `render_grid` is the verb a surface reaches for: give it a grid and two colours, it hands back the whole face as an image sized exactly to the grid (`col_pitch` -- the shared glyph advance -- per cell across, one cell height per row), bounded by qoi's own pixel ceiling. The crux is that the bridge is **faithful by measure**, not by claim: the painted ink equals the summed glyph coverage of every non-space cell exactly (no stray pixel, none missed), a blank row is the ground byte-for-byte, and a chosen cell's pixel block equals a standalone paint of that one glyph at its grid origin -- each cell paints its own glyph at `c*pitch, r*cell_h`. All-or-nothing: the grid's box is checked against the target **once** up front, so an overflow refuses `GridOutOfBounds` by name before a pixel moves, and every mis-shape refuses by its own name (`BadGridDims` - `GridCellsMismatch` - `GridAtlasMismatch` - `GridScratchTooSmall` - `GridTooLarge`). The render is deterministic and the terminal image round-trips through the open QOI codec byte-for-byte. `render_grid_fg` is the coloured-terminal twin: it takes one foreground colour **per cell** (a `[]color.Rgba` of exactly `cols*rows`, row-major), so cell `(r, c)` paints its glyph in `fg[r*cols + c]` -- a header in one colour, a caret in another, an unread badge in a third -- with the same all-or-nothing box check and monospace placement, only the ink read per cell rather than shared; a mis-sized palette refuses `GridFgMismatch` by name, and the coloured face round-trips through QOI byte-for-byte too. `render_grid_cells` gives each cell its own **ground** beside its own ink (two `[]color.Rgba`), so a status bar, a selected row, or an unread badge is a real coloured cell. `render_grid_cursor` is the abstract terminal **cursor**: it paints the whole grid plain but inverts one cell (its glyph in the ground colour over an ink-colour ground) to a solid inverse-video block -- the classic block cursor every grid surface shares, pure composition over `render_grid_cells`, a bad index refusing `GridCursorOutOfGrid` by name and the flip confined byte-for-byte to that one cell (`pond/apps/scooter_paint.rye`'s `render_session` passes a composing session's caret straight through it). Correctly layered -- `image/` composes only the image family, so no `pond` dependency inverts the stack; the pond rung that feeds a live `scooter_view.Frame`'s cells (already a `[]u8` of the same shape) into `render_grid` now stands at `pond/apps/scooter_paint.rye` (witness `tools/scooter_paint_witness.rish`), composing down into this module as pond should. Witness: `tools/text_grid_witness.rish`.

- **`glyph_compose.rye`** -- the **combining-diacritic overlay**, the primitive that collapses the accent grind. The long run of hand-drawn accent corpora (grave, acute, circumflex, tilde, diaeresis over the same 5x7 body) taught a discovery: an accented Latin letter is a **base** letter plus a **combining mark**, and the mark lives entirely in the two blank rows above the letter -- `a grave` is byte-for-byte the lowercase `a` with the grave mark added in rows 0 and 1, its rows 2..6 untouched. `overlay(base, mark, cell_area)` is the whole verb: a fresh coverage grid whose every cell is the larger of the two inputs (255 ink wins over 0 background), so ink from either survives and neither erases the other -- pure over the grid, bounded by the atlas ceiling, named on refusal (`BadCellArea` - `BaseLenMismatch` - `MarkLenMismatch`). The crux is proven on metal by **byte-for-byte reproduction of a shipped corpus**: `overlay(lowercase 'a', grave mark)` equals the hand-drawn `a grave` coverage byte for byte, and the same for `e`, `o`, `u`, so the grind was composition all along (the dotted `i` named honestly as the one vowel whose 5x7 base differs under a mark). The union is commutative and a blank mark is the identity. This retires **O(letters x accents)** hand-drawing to **O(letters + accents)** composition -- draw each base once, draw each mark once, compose to reach every accented letter. Witness: `tools/glyph_compose_witness.rish`.

- **`glyph_accent.rye`** -- the overlay primitive's **payoff**: where `glyph_compose.rye` composes one accented letter, `compose_accent_atlas(base_atlas, mark, map, advance)` composes a **whole corpus** -- it overlays one combining mark onto each base glyph named in a `base -> accented` codepoint table and returns a `gfa1`-encodable `glyph.GlyphAtlas` of accented letters. A five-vowel accent corpus is then a five-row table plus one mark, not five hand drawings -- the **O(letters + accents)** economy made literal. The crux is proven on metal by **byte-for-byte parity with a shipped corpus**: `compose_accent_atlas(lowercase base, grave mark, the a/e/o/u grave map)` reproduces the hand-drawn `font5x7_grave` coverage byte for byte for those four vowels, and the composed atlas round-trips through the `gfa1` encoder/decoder byte-identical -- so a corpus grown by composition is indistinguishable from one drawn by hand. The map is required strictly ascending by accented codepoint (the order the encoder trusts); a base the atlas lacks, a wrong-length mark, a non-ascending map, and an empty or over-long map each refuse by name (`BaseGlyphMissing` - `MarkLenMismatch` - `MapNotAscending` - `EmptyMap` - `TooManyAccents`). Witness: `tools/glyph_accent_witness.rish`.

- **`font5x7_acute.rye`** -- the first accent corpus **grown by composition** rather than drawn. It draws a single acute mark, then grows the a/e/o/u acute set (`U+00E1` `U+00E9` `U+00F3` `U+00FA`) with `glyph_accent.compose_accent_atlas` over the lowercase base and a four-row table. The crux is **byte-for-byte parity with the hand**: each composed vowel equals `font5x7_latin1.rye`'s own acute coverage byte for byte, and the composed atlas round-trips through `gfa1` byte-identical, so the corpus grown by composition is the corpus drawn by hand -- the economy proven on a second mark after the grave. Every mark is proven leaning right (the mirror of the grave); the dotted i acute is omitted honestly, its base carrying a dot the mark would not replace. Witness: `tools/font5x7_acute_witness.rish`.
- **`font5x7_circumflex_comp.rye`** -- the **third mark** grown by composition, after the grave and the acute. It draws a single circumflex caret, then grows the a/e/o/u circumflex set (`U+00E2` `U+00EA` `U+00F4` `U+00FB`) with `glyph_accent.compose_accent_atlas` over the lowercase base and a four-row table. The crux is again **byte-for-byte parity with the hand**: each composed vowel equals the hand-drawn `font5x7_circumflex.rye`'s own coverage byte for byte, and the composed atlas round-trips through `gfa1` byte-identical, so the corpus grown by composition is the corpus drawn by hand. The caret is proven **centered and symmetric** on the composed letter -- its apex on the center column, its two legs equal to either side, the mirror axis of the leaning acute/grave pair; the dotted i circumflex is omitted honestly, its base carrying a dot the caret would not replace. Witness: `tools/font5x7_circumflex_comp_witness.rish`.
- **`font5x7_tilde_comp.rye`** -- the **fourth mark** grown by composition, after the grave, the acute, and the circumflex. It draws a single tilde wave, then grows the a/o tilde set (`U+00E3` `U+00F5`) with `glyph_accent.compose_accent_atlas` over the lowercase base and a two-row table. The crux is again **byte-for-byte parity with the hand**: each composed vowel equals the hand-drawn `font5x7_tilde.rye`'s own coverage byte for byte, and the composed atlas round-trips through `gfa1` byte-identical. The wave is proven to **span both halves and crest right** on the composed letter -- the fourth lean beside the acute's right lean, the grave's left, and the circumflex's centered peak; only the two nasal vowels compose (the n tilde rides a consonant base and lives hand-drawn in `font5x7_latin1.rye`). Witness: `tools/font5x7_tilde_comp_witness.rish`.
- **`font5x7_diaeresis_comp.rye`** -- the **fifth and last mark** grown by composition, closing the accent-composition family, and the rung that proves the economy's **boundary** on metal. It draws a single two-dot mark, then grows the one diaeresis vowel the economy reaches -- the e diaeresis (`U+00EB`) -- with `glyph_accent.compose_accent_atlas` over the clear lowercase base. The crux is again **byte-for-byte parity with the hand**: the composed e equals the hand-drawn `font5x7_diaeresis.rye`'s own coverage byte for byte, and the composed atlas round-trips through `gfa1` byte-identical. What sets this rung apart is that the diaeresis reaches only **one** of its three vowels cleanly, and the witness proves why the other two stay hand-drawn rather than claiming it: overlaying the two dots onto lowercase `i` and `y` does **not** reproduce the hand-drawn diaeresis i/y -- the `i`'s tittle survives under the dots (three inked top cells, not two), and the `y`'s body reaches the mark row the hand shifts it away from -- so both are proven **uncomposable**, and `font5x7_diaeresis.rye` keeps them rightly. The mark is proven the same two-dot mark `font5x7_german.rye` drew over its umlauts, the fifth lean beside acute/grave/circumflex/tilde. Witness: `tools/font5x7_diaeresis_comp_witness.rish`.
