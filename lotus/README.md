## What this is

**Lotus** is Grain's own creative suite — one tool for composing, producing, mixing, and sound-engineering across the Linengrow musicians, filmmakers, podcasters, journalists, and editors the tree serves. The DAW itself remains a seated **horizon** ([`the Lotus DAW`](../active-designing/yonder/20260630-014012_lotus-the-daw.md)); this directory begins at the foundation every note stands on — the way audio reaches the software, proven whole.

## Honest scope — software only

The **audio-interface hardware** (real XLR, USB-C, and guitar pinouts, signal levels, the balanced/unbalanced electrical design) is a **paused hardware-research round**, yonder-bookmarked on Keaton's word. Nothing here asserts an electrical fact. The cable kinds below are **routing tags** on a buffer, nothing more.

## The rungs

- **`normalize.rye` — peak normalization: measure the loudest sample, then scale the whole clip so it lands exactly on a chosen target (ALES103).** The silence family (ALES96–102) answered *where is the sound?*; this rung answers what a keeper asks the instant the sound is found — *how loud is it, and can every take be the same loudness?* **Peak normalization is the single most-reached-for gesture in every editor and DAW** — the "Normalize" menu item, one click on a whole clip. A take recorded quiet and a take recorded hot both leave the same peak after normalize, so a keeper mixes without fighting the input level. `normalize_peak(clip, target)` — `target` a magnitude in `[1, sample_max]` — measures the clip's peak through ALES13's `measure`; a silent clip (peak 0) is left **exactly as it is** (no peak to lift, and the guard that keeps the division below from ever dividing by zero); otherwise it scales the whole clip `[0, clip.len)` by `target/peak` through ALES2's `gain`, amplifying a quiet clip and attenuating a hot one with the **same** fraction. The crux is **four laws**: **the scale law** — normalize is nothing but ALES13's `measure` followed by ALES2's `gain(clip, 0, len, target, peak)`, inventing no new arithmetic, so it can never drift (a peak of 8000 to 16000 is a clean ×2, a peak of 30000 to 15000 a clean ×0.5); **the peak law** — after normalize the clip's peak magnitude equals `target` **exactly**, for any non-silent clip at any target (the peak sample maps to exactly ±target, every quieter sample strictly inside it), proven including the i16 minimum at the meter's domain ceiling; **the silence law** — an all-zero clip and an empty clip are unchanged whatever the target; **the idempotence law** — normalize twice to one target equals once, the peak already on the target so the second gain is unity. A zero target and a target past `sample_max` each refuse `BadTarget`, the clip untouched. Its error union is the meter's `MeterError` and the editor's `EditError` (both honestly unreachable here — a bounded Clip, a measured non-zero denominator, a whole-clip span) reused by name plus `BadTarget`. Purely local — it changes sample **values** only through `gain`, fabricating none; a plain sample magnitude, **not** a decibel or a loudness model (this is **peak** normalization, the plainest reading); no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_normalize_witness.rish`.
- **`reverse.rye` — reverse: turn the whole clip end for end, the plainest EXACT edit on the timeline (ALES104).** ALES103 changed every **value** and no position; this rung is its exact mirror — it changes every **position** and no value. Reverse is the "Reverse" menu item every editor and DAW ships, and it carries real creative charge: the reversed cymbal swell, the backwards reverb, the pre-echo lifted before a downbeat. `reverse(clip)` takes no parameter and raises no fault from legal input — it walks the clip from both ends inward, swapping `buf[i]` with `buf[len-1-i]` until the two indices meet, one in-place pass of `len/2` swaps with no allocation and no second buffer. The middle sample of an odd-length clip is its own mirror and never moves; the length is untouched; the buffer past `len` is never read or written. The crux is **four laws**: **the order law** — after reverse, sample `i` holds what sample `len-1-i` held before, the first becoming the last exactly, checked against a hand-reversed vector; **the involution law** — reverse twice is the identity for any clip, the swap its own inverse, so a keeper who reverses by mistake reverses back to the byte; **the permutation law** — reverse is a pure rearrangement, the length unchanged and the multiset of samples unchanged, so every order-blind metric is invariant, witnessed through ALES13 (the peak magnitude before and after a reverse is identical, no value moving, only its seat); **the fixpoint law** — an empty clip, a single-sample clip, and a palindrome are each their own reverse, the degenerate cases landing on themselves with no special-casing. Purely local — it rearranges existing samples and fabricates none, changes no length, and reads no byte past `len`; no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_reverse_witness.rish`.
- **`invert.rye` — invert: flip every sample's sign, y = saturate(-x), the exact VALUE-mirror of reverse (ALES105).** ALES104 changed every **position** and no value; this rung is its exact twin from the other axis — it changes every **value** and no position. Invert is the "Invert" (or "Invert Phase") menu item every editor and DAW ships, and it earns real reach: a phase flip brings two mics on one source into agreement, cancels a difference signal, folds a stereo side. `invert(clip)` takes no parameter and raises no fault from legal input — it walks the clip once and replaces each sample with `saturate(-x)`, one in-place pass of `len` writes with no allocation and no second buffer, reusing ALES2's one true `saturate` so the signed rail is handled in exactly one place across the suite. The length is untouched; the buffer past `len` is never read or written. The crux is **four laws**: **the value-mirror law** — after invert, sample `i` holds `saturate(-x)`, every value flipped and no seat moved, checked against a hand-inverted vector; **the involution law, with an honest rail** — invert twice returns every sample to the byte **except** `sample_min` (`-32768`), whose true negation `+32768` is out of the `i16` range and saturates to `sample_max` (`+32767`), round-tripping to `-32767` — not a bug but the fixed asymmetry of two's-complement, the floor with no positive twin, saturated by design rather than wrapped; **the magnitude law** — off the rail `|−x| = |x|`, so for a clip whose peak is not `sample_min` the peak magnitude, re-measured through ALES13, is unchanged, the flip moving the sign not the loudness; **the fixpoint law** — zero is the unique fixed point of negation, so the all-zero clip and the empty clip are each their own invert (`−0 = 0`), landing on themselves with no special-casing. Purely local — it flips existing samples about zero and fabricates none, changes no length, and reads no byte past `len`; no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_invert_witness.rish`.
- **`rotate.rye` — rotate: cyclic left-shift the whole clip by k samples, the third pure rearrangement beside reverse and invert (ALES106).** ALES104 (reverse) and ALES105 (invert) named the two exact mirrors — position and value; this rung completes the **position** family with the other pure rearrangement every timeline owns. A cyclic shift slides the whole clip left by `k` and wraps the `k` that fall off the front back onto the tail — the loop-aligning gesture behind every "rotate the loop to the downbeat," every phase-aligned wavetable, every circular buffer read at an offset. `rotate_left(clip, by)` reduces `by` modulo the length (an over-long shift is the equivalent short one), then rotates in place by the classic three-reversal identity: reverse `[0, k)`, reverse `[k, len)`, reverse `[0, len)`, through one private `reverse_range` helper — the same swap-from-both-ends primitive `reverse` is built from — so it stands on proven ground and adds no allocation and no second buffer. The crux is **four laws**: **the shift law** — after `rotate_left(clip, k)`, result index `j` holds `original[(j + k) mod len]`, the front `k` samples moving to the tail exactly, checked against a hand-rotated vector at several offsets; **the modular law** — rotating by `0`, by `len`, or by any multiple of `len` is the identity, and `rotate_left(k)` equals `rotate_left(k mod len)`, so no over-long shift is ever out of range; **the composition-and-inverse law** — `rotate_left(k1)` then `rotate_left(k2)` equals `rotate_left(k1 + k2)`, so rotations add, and `rotate_left(k)` then `rotate_left(len − k)` returns to the byte, every rotation's inverse another rotation; **the permutation law** — rotate is a pure rearrangement, the length unchanged and the multiset of samples unchanged, witnessed through ALES13 (the peak magnitude before and after is identical, no value moving, only its seat). Purely local — it reseats existing samples cyclically and fabricates none, changes no length, and reads no byte past `len`; no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_rotate_witness.rish`.
- **`nyquist.rye` — nyquist flip: flip the sign of every odd-indexed sample, the parity-cousin of invert (ALES107).** ALES105 (invert) flips **every** sample's sign about zero; this rung names its exact cousin — flip **every other** sign, `y[n] = saturate((-1)^n · x[n])`, holding the even seats fixed. It is genuinely distinct and genuinely Lindy, because multiplying by `(-1)^n = cos(π n)` is **modulation by the Nyquist frequency** (`fs/2`): it reflects the spectrum, carrying a lowpass shape to its highpass twin — the classic **spectral-inversion** trick every filter-design text teaches for turning a lowpass kernel into its highpass twin. The operation is stated honestly in the **time domain** (a per-sample sign flip on the odd indices); the spectral reading is named as interpretation, never computed. `nyquist_flip(clip)` takes no parameter and raises no fault from legal input — it walks the odd seats only (index 1, stepping by 2) and replaces each with `saturate(-x)`, one in-place pass with no allocation and no second buffer, reusing ALES2's one true `saturate` so the signed rail lives in one place; even seats are left exactly as they are, the length untouched, no byte past `len` read or written. The crux is **four laws**: **the alternating-sign law** — after `nyquist_flip`, sample `i` holds `saturate((-1)^i · x)`, even seats unchanged and odd seats negated, checked against a hand-computed vector including both rails; **the involution law, with an honest rail** — flip twice returns every sample to the byte **except** an odd-indexed `sample_min` (`-32768`), whose true negation `+32768` saturates to `sample_max` on the first flip and lands `-32767` on the second, the fixed two's-complement asymmetry invert names, here confined to the odd seats (an even-indexed `sample_min` is never touched and round-trips exactly); **the magnitude law** — off the rail `|(-1)^i x| = |x|`, so for a clip whose peak is not `sample_min` the peak magnitude re-measured through ALES13 is unchanged, the modulation moving energy in frequency not the peak sample's loudness; **the fixed-seat / parity law** — every even-indexed sample is a fixed point (the operation acts only on the odd seats, partitioning the clip by parity), and the all-zero clip and the empty clip are each their own flip. - **`shift.rye` — shift: slide the whole clip by k samples and drop what falls off the edge, the honest twin of rotate (ALES108).** ALES106 (rotate) slides the whole clip and **wraps** the samples that fall off the edge back onto the far end — a permutation, exact and invertible. This rung names its honest twin: **shift**, which slides the clip and **drops** the fallen samples, filling the vacated end with silence — the **grid nudge** every editor and DAW ships (move a clip a few samples earlier or later so it lands on the beat, so a stereo pair aligns, so a doubled take sits behind the lead). Naming it beside rotate teaches the one distinction that matters about translating a bounded signal: **wrap or drop.** `shift(clip, by)` takes a signed `i32` — **negative** shifts left (earlier), **positive** right (later) — clamps the magnitude to the length (a shift at or past the length pushes the whole clip out of frame), moves the survivors in place in the safe direction (a right shift copies back to front, a left shift front to back, so no live sample is overwritten before it is read), then writes silence into the vacated span; one in-place pass bounded by `len`, no allocation, no second buffer, the length untouched. - **`insert_silence.rye` — insert silence: open `count` samples of silence at a position, growing the clip, the lossless twin of shift (ALES109).** ALES108 (shift) slides the whole clip within a fixed frame and **drops** what falls off the edge — a lossy translation, honest about its loss (`shift(k)` then `shift(-k)` returns the leading `k` as silence, not their bytes). Purely local — it opens a gap and fills it with silence, inventing no non-zero sample, dropping nothing, reading no byte past `len`; no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real speaker. - **`silence_span.rye` — silence span: write silence over a span in place, the length-preserving third of the trim/open/overwrite trio (ALES110).** ALES2's `cut` removes a span and closes the gap — the clip **shrinks**. Between them sits the third and last way a span meets silence: **overwrite it in place.** *Silence Audio Selection* is the button every editor ships — drop a cough, mute a bleed, clear a region to quiet **without moving anything around it**. - **`crop.rye` — crop: keep the named span and drop the rest, the exact complement of ALES2's cut (ALES111).** ALES2's `cut` removes the named span and keeps everything around it — the clip **shrinks** by exactly the span. The crux is that **split-equals-whole rises one level** — from the samples to a **derived count**: a flip can land exactly at the **seam** between two pieces, so the `ZeroCounter` carries both the trigger's rail (ALES92's hysteresis state) **and** the previous output rail (the memory a crossing is measured against); a span counted in two pieces, the second continuing the first's rail and previous-rail memory, equals the whole counted **once** — the seam flip counted exactly once, never missed and never doubled. It **composes** ALES92's public one-sample comparator `schmitt_step` — the same step the in-place trigger writes — and reads its flips instead of writing its rails, so it can never drift from the trigger it counts; its band and span are validated through ALES92's own `precheck`, its errors ALES92's `SchmittError` reused by name. A crossing is a change between consecutive output rails (the first fed sample sets the rail and counts none); `count_crossings` is the from-silence convenience, a fresh `ZeroCounter` fed once. Hysteresis suppresses chatter counting (zero crossings across a dither a sign comparator chatters on); silence and in-band-only count zero; `reset` re-opens from silence; the source clip is **byte-for-byte unchanged** (read-only, the span discipline in its strongest form); the count is bounded by `max_crossings` (refusing `CountFull`); an inverted band refuses `BadThreshold` and an out-of-range span refuses `BadRange`, the count and clip untouched. Purely local, read-only — the count a pure sample count of rail flips, **not** a pitch in Hz (no real sample rate exists yet — that conversion is a later rung); no anti-aliasing, no windowing, no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_zero_cross_witness.rish`.
- **`schmitt.rye` — the Schmitt trigger: a hysteresis comparator that gives the square a memory against chatter (ALES92).** ALES89's infinite clipper is the **memoryless** comparator — it judges each sample against one threshold on its own, keeps only the sign, a sine into a square. That is right for a clean signal and wrong for a noisy one: a signal riding near the threshold (a hum on a gate key, a slow envelope crossing zero, a sensor line trembling at its trip point) crosses that single level many times in a row, and the memoryless comparator **chatters** — the square flips on the noise, not on the event. The classic answer, a century old at the front of every zero-crossing detector, is **hysteresis**: two thresholds and a memory of which rail the output is on. It flips **high** only on a rise to or above the upper threshold `t_high`, **low** only on a fall to or below the lower `t_low`, and **holds** between — the band `[t_low, t_high]` a dead zone the noise cannot rattle. The output is `+sample_max` on the high rail, `−sample_max` on the low (ALES89's two rails, odd about the band, so no saturate is owed). The crux is that a comparator **with hysteresis carries state** — one bit, which rail — so, exactly as ALES91's carried DC blocker, a span compared in **two pieces** (the second continuing the first's ending rail) equals the whole compared **once**, byte-for-byte, no spurious flip at the seam: it is the carried-state **sibling** of the memoryless infinite clipper, the ALES91 shape (one bit where the blocker carries two i64 states). From silence the convention is the **low rail** (before a rise past `t_high` the output has not triggered), stated plainly so a fresh `SchmittTrigger` and the from-silence `schmitt` cannot drift. The band law is its own — `sample_min ≤ t_low ≤ t_high ≤ sample_max`, two signed thresholds ordered inside the rails; `t_low = t_high` is the legal zero-width band, the plain comparator (the degenerate, as ALES91's `R = 0` is the legal first difference), and an inverted band `t_low > t_high` refuses `BadThreshold`. `schmitt_carry` / `schmitt` / a `SchmittTrigger` value are three faces of one implementation; hysteresis resists a chatter a single-threshold comparator cannot, `reset` re-opens the low rail, only two values ever appear, and an out-of-range span refuses `BadRange` — each before any write. Purely local — one two-threshold compare in i32, one bit of state, one rail per sample; the band over amplitude not time (no debounce time constant), no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_schmitt_witness.rish`.
- **`dc_block.rye` — the one-pole IIR DC blocker: a drifting-offset remover with carried state (ALES91).** ALES90's `dc_remove` is the **block-mean** remover — it looks at a whole span at once, subtracts the one average, and is memoryless, the right tool for the **constant** offset a rectifier introduces over a bounded region. Yet a real offset **drifts**: a mic warms and its bias creeps, two takes spliced together each ride a different rail, the DC steps partway through a clip. A block mean cannot follow a moving offset — it subtracts one number, leaving each region offset by its distance from the whole-span average. This rung is the classic answer the `dc_remove` doc booked in as many words: the **one-pole IIR DC blocker**, `y[n] = x[n] − x[n−1] + R·y[n−1]` — a highpass with a **zero at direct current** (the difference `x[n] − x[n−1]`, so a constant settles to zero out) and a **pole just inside it** (the leaky integrator `R·y[n−1]`; `R` near one a narrow notch that keeps the bass just above DC, `R = 0` the plain first difference). It drives **whatever** offset the signal currently rides toward zero, sample by sample, and follows the offset as it moves. The crux is that a DC blocker **carries state** — the previous input and previous output — so, exactly as ALES43's carried low-pass, a span blocked in **two pieces** (the second continuing the first's ending state) equals the whole blocked **once**, byte-for-byte, no re-transient at the seam: it is the carried-state **sibling** of the memoryless block mean, the ALES43 shape. A settled constant reaches output **exactly zero** in finite steps (the feedback `@divTrunc(num·y_prev, den)` truncates to nothing once the tail is small enough); it **follows a DC step** a block mean cannot (each region driven to zero where the block mean leaves `±3000`); `R = 0` is the legal first-difference highpass (`num ≥ 0` here, distinct from ALES40's `num ≥ 1`), and the pole must sit **strictly inside the unit circle** (`num < den` — `num = den` is a marching integrator, `num > den` unstable, both refusing `BadCoeff`). The feedback reads the **saturated** prior output so the recurrence stays bounded, every intermediate in i64, the multiply bounded by `max_pole_den`; a sharp edge past the ceiling **saturates** rather than wraps (ALES2 reused). `dc_block_carry` / `dc_block` / a `DcBlocker` value are three faces of one implementation; a fresh blocker equals the from-silence `dc_block`, `reset` re-opens the transient, an illegal pole refuses `BadCoeff` and an out-of-range span refuses `BadRange`, each before any write. Purely local — one recursive difference in i64, one saturate per sample; no gain, no delay line, no frequency, no real sample rate, no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_dc_block_witness.rish`.
- **`dc_remove.rye` — the DC-offset remover: subtract the span's mean, a pure translation (ALES90).** The DRIVE family grew a whole shelf of even-harmonic generators — the full-wave rectifier (ALES84, `y = |x|`), the two half-wave rectifiers (ALES85, ALES86), the asymmetric tube drive (ALES82), the 1-bit crush (ALES81) — and each does one thing beyond adding harmonics that none of the odd maps do: it **shifts the signal's mean off zero**. Rectify a symmetric wave and every sample turns positive, the audio now riding on a large positive DC offset that eats headroom and thumps at a cut seam. Every real chain that carries a nonlinearity carries a DC remover right after it; nothing in the suite took that offset back out until this rung. `dc_remove(clip, start, count)` computes the span's **mean** and subtracts it from every sample — `y = saturate(x − mean)`. The crux is that **DC removal is a pure translation**: the *same* constant leaves every sample, so it **preserves every sample-to-sample difference exactly** (`y[i] − y[j] = x[i] − x[j]` where nothing saturates) — only the mean moves, the waveform **shape** and all its AC content carried through untouched, distinct from ALES2's `gain`, which *scales* the differences. The mean is truncated toward zero (`@divTrunc`, the suite's honest-lossy rule), so the residual mean magnitude is provably **less than one LSB** (`|Σ y| < count`): a zero-mean signal is unchanged exactly, and the remove is **idempotent** where nothing saturates. It **undoes the rectifier's offset** — a symmetric wave full-wave-rectified then DC-removed returns to a near-zero mean, the composition that motivates the rung. A sample near a rail that the mean pushes past pins to `±sample_max` (ALES2's `saturate`, reused — never a wrap); an empty span is a clean no-op (no divide by zero); a span past the end refuses `BadRange`, the clip untouched. This is the **block-mean** remover — exact, memoryless, no feedback; the one-pole IIR DC blocker (a drifting-offset remover with carried state, the ALES43 shape) is a later rung. Purely local — sum then subtract in i64, one saturate per sample; no gain, no coefficient, no delay line, no envelope, no frequency, no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real sample rate. Witness: `tools/ales_dc_remove_witness.rish`.
- **`wire.rye` — the basic audio wire shape (ALES0).** A buffer of PCM samples crosses a self-describing, Sha256-sealed frame — `magic(LOTS) · version · cable · payload_len · digest · payload(i16 LE)` — deframed **verify-before-trust**, the exact idiom proven at [`constel/wire.rye`](../constel/wire.rye) (FORA3) and [`mikrophone/wire.rye`](../mikrophone/wire.rye) (DREY1). Deframing is **two independent gates**: the Sha256 proves the payload *whole* (no byte flipped), and the **audio border** proves it *sample-aligned* (a whole number of i16 samples) — so a torn half-sample can never reach a timeline, the one correctness a generic wire does not owe but an audio wire does. A block round-trips byte-for-byte on each cable tag (XLR · USB-C · guitar); silence and full-scale i16 min/max samples carry exactly; a torn half-sample refuses `PartialSample`, a flipped byte refuses `DigestMismatch`, and an unknown cable, overlong, truncated, short, or bad-magic frame each refuses by name; a buffer wider than one frame refuses `TooManySamples`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_wire_witness.rish`.
- **`timeline.rye` — the timeline edits: cut, gain, splice (ALES2).** ALES0 and ALES1 carry and order the audio; neither changes a sample. A creative suite exists to **edit**, so this rung opens the first real DAW gesture — an editable `Clip` of samples and the three edits every timeline owes. **cut** removes a span and shifts the tail left, keeping the audio gapless and everything after in order; **gain** scales a span's loudness by `num/den` and **saturates** to the i16 range rather than wrapping — the audio-editor analog of ALES0's audio border, so a doubled loud sample pins to `+32767` instead of wrapping to a negative, and a negative `num` inverts phase (negating i16-min clamps to `+32767`); **splice** inserts samples at a position and shifts the tail right, in order. `splice` then `cut` over the same span are exact inverses — the round-trip restores the original. `load` copies a reassembled ALES1 `Timeline` into a `Clip`, so the full road reads bytes → frames → gapless timeline → editable clip. Out-of-range refuses `BadRange` (leaving the clip untouched), a zero denominator refuses `BadGain`, an overflow refuses `ClipFull` — each by name, at the edge, before any write. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_timeline_witness.rish`.
- **`mix.rye` — mix a second track (ALES3).** ALES2 edited one clip; a DAW's second real gesture is summing **two** clips into one — the mixer. `mix(a, b, out)` sums two clips sample-aligned, and the sum **saturates** to the i16 range rather than wrapping — the same owed correctness ALES2's gain keeps, **reused** from `timeline.saturate` (one true saturation, not a second copy), so a loud sum (`30000 + 10000`) pins to `+32767` instead of wrapping negative. A shorter track contributes **silence** past its end, so the mix runs the length of the longer track with the tail unchanged; mixing with an empty clip is identity; mix is commutative (`a + b == b + a`). Mix is **total** over two valid clips — every clip shares one bound, so the longer length always fits — held by an assert rather than a never-firing error. Level and mix stay separate gestures: run ALES2's `gain` to set a track's fader, then `mix`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_mix_witness.rish`.
- **`fade.rye` — a fade envelope: gain that ramps (ALES4).** ALES2's `gain` scaled a span by one constant fraction; a fade scales it by a fraction that **ramps** linearly from a start level to an end level — the fade-in that opens a clip from silence and the fade-out that closes it to silence, so a cut never clicks. `fade(clip, start, count, num0, num1, den)` interpolates the gain fraction across the span with **exact endpoints** (a fade-in leaves the first sample silent and the last unchanged) and, past unity, **saturates** — the same floor and ceiling, **reused** from `timeline.saturate`. A single-sample span takes the end level; a zero denominator refuses `BadGain`, an out-of-range span refuses `BadRange` (clip untouched). A fade is a gain whose fraction moves, so it composes: ALES3's `mix` sums a track under a fade-out with another under a fade-in — a crossfade — with no new machinery. Purely local. Witness: `tools/ales_fade_witness.rish`.
- **`transport.rye` — the transport play head (ALES7).** ALES6 rendered many tracks into one master, yet a rendered master is still samples at rest — nothing **plays** it. `transport.rye` is the read side of the suite: a `Transport` play head begins at a position and `read_block(transport, master, out)` copies the next up-to-`out.len` samples forward, **advancing by exactly what it copied** — a full block mid-master, an honestly **short** block at the end, **zero** past it (never an error, never a wrap). The end is a hard wall: the head never advances past `master.len`, `at_end` is exactly `pos == master.len`, and concatenating every block read from the start reproduces the master **byte-for-byte** (no sample skipped or repeated). Time is real — `seek_ms` places the head at a real moment through the ALES5 clock (refusing `PastEnd` past the master) and `elapsed_ms` reports it back — so a keeper drives the transport in seconds. The transport **reads only**: a replay after a rewind reads the identical bytes, the rendered audio never mutated. No audio leaves the process — a block is copied samples, not a sound-card write (that real write is the paused hardware round). Purely local. Witness: `tools/ales_transport_witness.rish`.
- **`clock.rye` — the sample clock: a real time base (ALES5).** Every rung so far measured the audio in sample **indices** — cut span `2..5`, a fade across `count` samples, a mix from index `0`. An index says *which* sample without saying *when*. A keeper thinks in **real time** — a half-second fade, two clips that begin two hundred milliseconds apart — and the one missing fact is the **sample rate**, the number of samples one second of sound holds. A validated `Clock` supplies it: `make(rate)` refuses a zero or absurd rate (`BadRate`) at the edge; `samples_for(clock, ms)` converts real time to a bounded sample count (`50 ms` at `48 kHz` = `2400` samples), refusing `DurationTooLong` past the clip; `ms_for(clock, n)` converts back, **honestly lossy** (a sample count rarely lands on a whole millisecond, so `@divTrunc` rounds toward zero). Two payoffs reuse the rungs already proven: `fade_ms` names an ALES4 fade in milliseconds and lands the **exact same samples** the index-named fade lands over the converted span — the clock changes only the units, never the audio; `place_at` aligns a second clip at a real offset — the lead-in carries the base alone, the overlap sums and **saturates** (reused from `timeline.saturate`), refusing `ClipFull` past the bound. The rate is samples per second, not a claim about any hardware clock. Purely local. Witness: `tools/ales_clock_witness.rish`.
- **`track.rye` — the track table: many clips, one master (ALES6).** ALES3's `mix` summed **two** clips from index zero; ALES5's `place_at` summed **one** clip onto a base at a real offset. A session is neither — it is **many** clips, each beginning at its own real moment, heard together. `Session` is a bounded table of at most `max_tracks` placed clips; `add_track(session, clip, at_ms)` appends one (refusing `SessionFull`), and `render(session, clock, out)` sums every track into one master at its real offset — each offset converted through the ALES5 clock, the sum accumulated in a **wide i64 per sample and saturated once over the whole** (not once per track), so three mid-range tracks clip exactly as an audio editor owes. A track whose placed end exceeds the master refuses `ClipFull` before any write. The table **generalizes** the two proven rungs rather than replacing them: an empty session renders silence, a single track reproduces `place_at`, and two tracks at offset zero reproduce `mix`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_track_witness.rish`.
- **`fader.rye` — the per-track fader column (ALES8).** ALES6 summed many placed clips into one master, yet every track entered at **full level** — the table places a clip, it never balances one. This rung opens the mixing-desk channel strip's one essential control: a per-track `Fader`, a level as a fraction `num/den`, applied to each track **before** it joins the sum. The one correctness beyond ALES6 is that the fader multiply happens **inside** the accumulation — each track's sample scaled in a wide i64 (`@divTrunc`, the ALES2 gain rule), the faded value accumulated **unclamped**, and the master saturated **once** over the true faded sum (reused from `timeline.saturate`). That is why a fader column cannot be gain-then-render: ALES2's `gain` would clamp each track to i16 before it could join the mix. A column of **unity** faders reproduces ALES6's `render` byte-for-byte (the mixer generalizes the table); a `1/2` fader halves a track, a `0/1` fader mutes one (present, silent), a negative fader inverts phase — so a clip mixed with a copy of itself faded at `-1/1` **nulls to exact silence**, the sharpest proof the fader scales in the wide domain before the single clamp. A zero denominator refuses `BadGain` at construction; a track past the master bound forwards `ClipFull`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_fader_witness.rish`.
- **`strip.rye` — mute and solo: the channel strip's two switches (ALES9).** ALES8 gave each track a level; a mixing keeper reaches constantly for two switches beside it — **mute** (silence this track) and **solo** (silence every *other* track). This rung opens them as first-class session state and resolves them the way every desk does — **solo, when any track holds it, wins**: only soloed tracks are heard; otherwise every un-muted track is heard, and solo overrides even a track's own mute (solo-in-place). The crux is that mute/solo add **no new sum and no new saturation**: a `Strip` resolves to an **effective fader** (its own fader if audible, else `0/1` silence), and the mix is ALES8's `fader.render` delegated whole. A column of default strips reproduces ALES8's mix byte-for-byte (the channel strip generalizes the fader column). A muted track goes silent leaving the rest unchanged; a soloed track silences every other; two solos are heard together; a muted-and-soloed track is still heard; a zero denominator on an audible strip forwards `BadGain`, a track past the master bound forwards `ClipFull`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device. Witness: `tools/ales_strip_witness.rish`.
- **`pan.rye` — pan, and the second channel (ALES10).** Every rung before this rendered **one** master — a mono mix. `pan.rye` opens the **second channel** with the simplest honest law: a **linear pan** that places each track between the left and right speaker, splitting its level by weights that sum to one (`den − pos` left, `pos` right). The crux is that pan is a **pure fraction fold into the fader** — a panned track is two faded contributions, so each channel is ALES8's `fader.render` run once over a pan-folded effective fader column; no stereo accumulation is invented, and the only sum in the suite stays ALES8's. Its defining invariant, proven on metal: **left + right reproduces the mono mix exactly, for any pan position** (the linear −6 dB-center law preserves the sum, as distinct from the equal-power curve a later rung may add). Center pan splits a track equally; hard left/right route it wholly to one channel; pan composes with the fader; two tracks panned opposite separate into the two channels. Bounds are proven, not trusted — `max_pan_den` bounds the fold so no product overflows. `make` refuses `BadGain` / `BadRange`; a track past the master bound forwards `ClipFull`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_pan_witness.rish`.
- **`power.rye` — the equal-power law, and the constant it keeps (ALES11).** ALES10's linear pan kept the **sum** constant (left + right reproduces the mono mix), at the cost of a **−6 dB center dip** — a centered track sounds quieter than a hard-panned one. `power.rye` places each track on the **quarter circle** instead of the straight line between the speakers, so the two channels' **powers** — their squared amplitudes — sum to a constant, and a track holds its loudness wherever it sits (each side `0.707`, **−3 dB**, at center). The crux is that "equal power" *means* the two squared weights sum to `den²`, so the law is built exact from that fact — `left_power = den·(den−pos)`, `right_power = den·pos`, summing to `den²` **exactly** before a single root — and the amplitude weights are the **floor square root** of each: the power split is linear in `pos`, and the square root bends it into the circle the ear wants. The one genuinely new arithmetic is a **bounded integer square root** proven to its contract `r² ≤ x < (r+1)²` (a twelve-digit binary descent over a `2²⁴` domain, no float, no unbounded loop); constant power then survives the truncation with a **proven bound** (`left² + right² ≤ den²`, and `den² − (left²+right²) ≤ 2·(left+right)+2`). Each channel is still ALES8's `fader.render` run once over an equal-power-folded fader — the same two-sums-are-one reuse ALES10 proved, only angular. `make` refuses `BadGain` / `BadRange`; a track past the master bound forwards `ClipFull`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_power_witness.rish`.
- **`crossfade.rye` — the equal-power crossfade, the same law swept over time (ALES12).** ALES11 opened equal power across the stereo **field** (pan); the same dip a linear pan left at center, a **linear crossfade leaves in time** — butt two clips and cross them with weights that sum to one, and the overlap sags in the middle. `crossfade.rye` carries equal power from the field into **time**, and the crux is a recognition rather than new arithmetic: **a crossfade is the equal-power pan swept across the overlap** — position across the field and position through a time overlap are one parameter. The outgoing and incoming weights are ALES11's `power.split` read at each sample of the overlap (the proven law **reused, not re-derived**), so the overlap holds constant power for free: it **begins as pure outgoing and ends as pure incoming** (a seamless splice onto the clips before and after), and its **center holds `0.707·den`** (−3 dB) where a linear crossfade dips to `0.5·den` (−6 dB) — a 1000-sample source landing `700` at center, not `500`. Crossing a flat clip with itself lifts the middle **above** the endpoints (`0.707 + 0.707 > 1`), never below, the opposite of a linear sag; a loud overlap **saturates** once to the i16 range (`timeline.saturate`, reused from ALES3) rather than wrapping; a length mismatch and a too-short overlap each refuse `BadRange` by name. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_crossfade_witness.rish`.
- **`stereo_transport.rye` — the stereo transport: one head reading two channels (ALES15).** Two arcs of the suite converge here. The **read side** — play (ALES7), meter (ALES13), loop (ALES14) — grew over a **mono** master; the **stereo side** — pan (ALES10), equal power (ALES11), crossfade (ALES12) — built the **second channel** and rendered stereo masters that nothing had ever **played**. This rung makes the read side genuinely stereo. The crux is that **one head reads both channels in lockstep, because a stereo master's two channels share one length** (ALES10's `StereoClip` invariant `left.len == right.len`) — a single sample position advances through both channels at once, copying the same span from each into its own out buffer, no second cursor. The correctness beyond running ALES7 twice is exactly the **lockstep**: both channels advance by the same count every block, so left and right never drift out of alignment, and concatenating every block reproduces **each** channel byte-for-byte. `read_block` refuses `BadBlock` when the two out buffers differ in length (a stereo block is one length); `seek_ms` / `elapsed_ms` drive the head in real time through the ALES5 clock, refusing `PastEnd` past the shared length; and the transport reads only — a `StereoClip` rendered by ALES11's `power.render_stereo` plays back its two channels exactly (a center pan's `0.707` per side read whole), the rendered stereo never mutated. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_stereo_transport_witness.rish`.
- **`stereo_meter.rye` — the stereo meter during playback: two meters read both channels live (ALES17).** ALES13 opened the peak/RMS meter and proved block-invariance over the **mono** transport; it can already `measure_stereo` a master **at rest**. What no rung yet did is meter a stereo master **during playback** — a live stereo VU fed by the very blocks ALES15's stereo head plays. This rung is that live path, completing the read side's third tool over two channels (play, loop, and now meter all read a stereo master through the same head). The crux is that **two meters read both channels in lockstep, because the one stereo head hands each block to both channels at once** (ALES15's `read_block` fills both out buffers with the same count) — so the stereo meter needs no new accumulation, feeding ALES13's proven `Meter` per channel. The correctness beyond running ALES13 twice is exactly that **lockstep**: both channels feed the same count every block, and **block-invariance holds per channel over the stereo transport** — metering a stereo master block by block off the one head gives the **identical** per-channel peak and RMS as ALES13's whole-master `measure_stereo`, at any block size. A fresh meter reads silence in both channels; a loud left and quiet right report distinct levels; a `StereoClip` rendered by ALES11's `power.render_stereo` meters its two channels exactly (a center pan's `0.707` per side, `700` on a `1000` source); metering never mutates either channel; `feed` refuses `MeterFull` past the bound through ALES13's own edge check. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_stereo_meter_witness.rish`.
- **`stereo_loop_meter.rye` — metering a looping stereo region live (ALES18).** ALES17 metered a stereo master during **forward** playback; ALES16 taught the same head to **loop**. What no rung yet did is meter that looping region **live** — the practice-loop VU a musician watches while a marked bar cycles. This rung composes the two most recent proven rungs: the ALES16 wrapping head feeding two ALES17 meters. The crux is that **metering any whole number of cycles gives the identical per-channel peak and RMS as metering the region once at rest** — the live VU never drifts however long it loops. Two facts make this exact: **peak** is a maximum, invariant under repetition; and **RMS** is a mean square, whose sum and count both scale by the cycle count K, so the common factor cancels **exactly even under integer floor division** (`@divTrunc(K·S, K·N) == @divTrunc(S, N)`) — no new arithmetic, only ALES13's one true `isqrt` read off ALES16's cycled samples. `measure_region` meters the region once at rest (the reference); `meter_loop_live` drives the wrapping head for exactly `cycles × span` samples, feeding the ALES17 `StereoMeter` block by block, and refuses `MeterFull` past ALES13's bound — the same edge guarding the `cycles × span` product from overflow. Block-and-cycle invariance holds at 1-, 2-, 5-sample blocks and 1, 3, 7 cycles; 100 cycles read the same level as one; a loud left and quiet right report distinct levels; a rendered center pan loops-and-meters `0.707` per side; metering mutates neither channel. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_stereo_loop_meter_witness.rish`.
- **`scrub.rye` — the scrub window: a small movable read dragged across a stereo master (ALES19).** Play (ALES15), loop (ALES16), and meter (ALES17/18) all read a master from a head that **runs** — forward, or round and round. This rung is that held, draggable window, and the crux is that **a scrub is a seek-then-read** — the window at position `p` is ALES15's `read_block` off a head seeded at `p`, so it returns **exactly** the master's own samples `[p, p+n)` in both channels (`n = min(win, len − p)`). Composing ALES17, **`meter_window` equals metering the master slice directly** — the level under the drag is the true level of what the window covers. `make` bounds the window length (`≤ max_scrub_window ≤ the meter bound`, so `meter_window` cannot overflow) and refuses `BadWindow`; `move_to` refuses `PastEnd` past the shared length; `nudge` carries the signed drag in `i64` and **clamps** to `[0, len]` (an overshoot stops at the edge rather than refusing); `read_window` refuses `BadBlock` on buffers that do not match the window length. - **`markers.rye` — the markers track: named positions a keeper navigates and snaps to (ALES20).** Play, loop, meter, and scrub all read a master by **raw sample index**, yet a keeper thinks in **named places** — the verse, the chorus, the edit point. `markers.rye` turns a number into a name, and every gesture already built reads better through it: a scrub **snaps** to the nearest named place, a loop is the **region between two markers**. The crux is that **a markers track keeps its named positions sorted and unique**, so the nearest-marker query returns the true nearest named place in one ordered pass, and a scrub snapped to it lands **exactly** there. - **`selection.rye` — a marked span a keeper edits as one across both channels (ALES23).** The markers track names places and the cue sheet saves them, yet a keeper does more than navigate to a mark — they **grab** the span between two marks and act on it whole: silence the count-in, halve the bridge, cut the bad take. `selection.rye` is that grabbed span `[start, end)` over a stereo master. The crux is **lockstep**: an edit touches both channels with the same `(start, span)`, so a cut shrinks both by the same count (`left.len == right.len` after) and a gain scales the same samples in both. Each channel edit is ALES2's own proven `cut` / `gain`, so a selection cut equals cutting each channel by hand — the samples outside untouched, the tail gapless, the gain saturating. `make` refuses `BadSelection` on an empty, inverted, or past-the-master span; `select_between` grabs the span between two named marks (ALES20's `region_between`, forwarding `BadPair`); `gain` inverts phase on a negative num and refuses `BadGain` on a zero denominator; `silence` is a zero gain over the span. - **`graft.rye` — a selected span copied or moved onto a second point, both channels in lockstep (ALES24).** The selection edits a marked span in place, yet the two gestures a keeper reaches for most are still missing — **copy** the grabbed span to a second point, and **move** it there: double the chorus, relocate the verse, lift the count-in off the front and drop it after the bridge. `graft.rye` is that gesture, named for the horticulturist's move the tree already lives by — take a living piece and join it whole at another place. The crux is that **a grafted span lands byte-for-byte at the destination, keeps the two channels in lockstep, and never corrupts itself when source and destination overlap.** Three facts make it exact: both channels' span samples are snapshotted into a bounded scratch **before** any `splice` or `cut`, so a copy whose destination sits before or inside its own source reads the **original** audio, never a half-shifted buffer (the classic self-overlap corruption cannot occur); the same `(at, span)` is passed to each channel with capacity checked **first**, so a copy grows both equally and a move keeps both equal, atomic across the stereo image; and a move is copy-then-cut with the destination **re-based** left by the span when it sat after the source, so the moved audio lands exactly where the keeper meant and the net length is unchanged. `copy` keeps the source and refuses `ClipFull` on an overflow; `move` removes the source, treats a destination on either edge as the honest identity no-op, and refuses `BadDest` on a destination past the master or strictly inside the moved span. Every edge refuses by name before either channel is touched; `timeline.splice`/`cut` and `selection.cut` are reused whole. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_graft_witness.rish`.
- **`clipboard.rye` — a held span pasted anywhere, any number of times, across masters (ALES25).** The graft copies and moves a span **within one master**, yet a keeper reaches across a session for an older, wider gesture — **hold** a span, then **paste** it: later, again, and into a *different* master. `clipboard.rye` is that held span, decoupled from any one master in both place and time. The crux is that **a held span pastes byte-for-byte into any master, repeatably, keeping both channels in lockstep — the clipboard itself never changes when it is pasted.** Three facts make it exact: `copy_to`/`cut_to` snapshot both channels' span into the clipboard's **own** bounded buffers, so the held audio survives after the source master is edited, closed, or replaced; `paste` is **non-consuming** and **cross-master** — a read of the clipboard, never a move out of it, so the same span pastes into three places and three masters identically; and the held channels are always one length, every paste passing the **same** `(at, span)` to each destination channel with capacity checked **first**, so a paste grows both equally and the stereo stays aligned or neither channel is touched. `copy_to` leaves the source untouched; `cut_to` lifts the span (holding before it cuts) for a move across masters; `paste` refuses `Empty` on a paste of nothing, `BadRange` past the master, and `ClipFull` on an overflow — each before either channel is touched. `timeline.splice` and `selection.cut` are reused whole. - **`join.rye` — the crossfade join, the choice of law where an inserted span meets its neighbour (ALES26).** Every edit so far butts its samples hard against the audio already there — ALES2's `splice`, and the graft and clipboard that paste through it. A butt splice is exact where the outgoing tail and incoming head already agree, yet where they disagree (a +30000 tail meeting a −30000 head) the waveform **steps**, and a step is a click a keeper hears. `join.rye` gives the seam the choice of law ALES12 already proved: instead of butting the span on, **overlap** its head with the outgoing tail and **cross them equal-power**, so the boundary moves through the −3 dB curve rather than stepping. The crux is a composition, not new arithmetic: **a crossfade join is ALES12's `crossfade` read at a splice point.** Three facts make it exact: because that crossfade begins as pure outgoing and ends as pure incoming, the join's head still continues the clip before it and its tail still continues the span after it — the seam flows into the appended remainder rather than jumping; the two neighbours **share** the overlap in time, so the new length is `clip + span − overlap` (a full overlap is a pure crossfade with nothing appended); and both overlap windows are snapshotted **before** the cross writes, so it reads the true tail, never a half-written one. `join` refuses `BadRange` on an overlap under two, past the tail, or past the head, and `ClipFull` on an overflow — each before any write, a refused join leaving the clip untouched. `crossfade.crossfade` and `timeline.splice` are reused whole; the join names no new fault. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_join_witness.rish`.
- **`tone_carry.rye` — the carried-state low-pass: a filter that spans a re-berthed region without a fresh transient (ALES43).** Every filter rung so far (ALES40 tone, ALES41 shelf, ALES42 stack) runs **from silence** — each call opens the one-pole state at zero, so a filter applied to a region always begins with its roll-on transient. That is right for a whole clip filtered once, yet a keeper filters a region, re-berths, and filters the next; each fresh transient at the boundary is a **click**. A real filter **carries its state** across successive spans, so the second span continues exactly where the first left off and the seam is seamless. The crux: **a carried-state low-pass filtered over a span in two pieces — the second continuing from the first's ending state — equals the same filter run over the whole span once, byte-for-byte.** This is exact *by ALES40's stepping law* (`lp_step` is a pure function of `(state, x)`), needing no new arithmetic — only a place for the caller to hold the state. `tone.rye` grows one public primitive `low_pass_carry`, and `low_pass` becomes its `state = 0` case — **one implementation**, so the carried and from-silence forms can never drift (the shipped ALES40/41/42 witnesses stay GREEN, proving the refactor by their green). The new `Lowpass = struct { state: i64, num, den }` holds no buffer — a tiny value a keeper carries beside a clip; `run` filters continuing from `self.state`, `reset` returns the state to silence so the next run re-opens the transient deliberately. Two properties fall out: **split equals whole** (a run over `[0, n)` then `[n, m)` matches one run over `[0, m)`); and **reset re-opens the transient** (after `reset` the next run rolls off from silence again). Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real sample rate. Witness: `tools/ales_tone_carry_witness.rish`.
- **`stack.rye` — the three-band tone stack: bass, mid, and treble from two one-pole splits (ALES42).** ALES41 gave Lotus two knobs by scaling the low band and its high-band complement; yet the tone stack a keeper reaches for on a guitar amp or a mixing console has **three** knobs — bass, **mid**, and treble — and the middle band (the vocal presence, the snare crack, the body of a take) is the one a two-band shelf cannot touch. This rung opens it. The crux: **a three-band stack scales the low band, the mid band, and the high band independently and clamps once, so unity on all three is the identity and only bass moves direct current.** For each sample the stack computes the low-pass output `L` at the bass crossover (ALES40 on a scratch copy), the high-pass output `H` at the treble crossover (ALES40 on a second scratch copy), the **mid band as the exact residual** `M = x − L − H`, and writes `saturate(bass·L/bass_den + mid·M/mid_den + treble·H/treble_den)` — each band divided in i64, the three summed in i64, the single clamp over the true sum (ALES8's fader lesson via ALES41). Three properties fall out: **unity is identity** — all three gains one writes `L + (x − L − H) + H = x` byte-for-byte, reconstruction guaranteed *by construction* (the mid is the residual, so the three always sum back to `x`, even if a band saturated); **the knobs are orthogonal at direct current** — a settled constant has `L = x` and `H = 0` exactly, so `M = 0`: neither mid nor treble can move a constant, only bass scales the still low end; and **a boost saturates rather than wraps** — a loud constant at `bass = 2` pins to `sample_max`. Each crossover is validated as ALES40's own coefficient — an illegal one refuses `BadCoeff`, a zero band denominator refuses `BadGain`, a span outside the clip refuses `BadRange`, each before a sample is touched. `StackError = tone.ToneError || error{ BadGain }`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real sample rate (each crossover sets a split over sample indices, not a frequency in hertz — a real time base is a later rung). Witness: `tools/ales_stack_witness.rish`.
- **`shelf.rye` — the two-band tone shelf: bass and treble from the one-pole split (ALES41).** ALES40 gave Lotus its first filter, yet a filter alone only *removes* a band; the tone control a keeper actually turns is two knobs — **bass** and **treble** — each *boosting or cutting* its band independently. ALES40 already splits any signal into a low band and a high band whose sum reconstructs the input exactly (`low_pass + high_pass = x`); this rung scales each band by its own gain and sums them — the everyday equalizer. The crux: **a two-band shelf scales the low band and the high band independently and clamps once, so unity on both bands is the identity and a boost saturates rather than wraps.** For each sample the shelf computes the low-pass output `L` (ALES40 on a scratch copy of the span), derives the high band `H = x − L`, and writes `saturate(bass·L/bass_den + treble·H/treble_den)` — each band divided in i64, the two summed in i64, the single clamp over the true sum, exactly ALES8's fader lesson (scale in the wide domain, saturate once, never saturate then sum). Two properties fall out: **unity is identity** — `bass = bass_den`, `treble = treble_den` writes `L + (x − L) = x` byte-for-byte (a flat EQ passes the audio through untouched); and **the knobs are orthogonal at direct current** — a settled constant has `H = 0` exactly (ALES40's proven direct-current removal), so treble cannot move a constant and only bass scales it (a constant at `bass = 2` doubles; the same constant at any treble is unchanged). The filter coefficient sets where the split falls; a zero/negative/oversize coefficient refuses `BadCoeff`, a zero band denominator refuses `BadGain`, a span outside the clip refuses `BadRange` — each before a sample is touched. `ShelfError = tone.ToneError || error{ BadGain }`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real sample rate (the coefficient sets the split over sample indices, not a crossover in hertz — a real time base is a later rung). Witness: `tools/ales_shelf_witness.rish`.
- **`tone.rye` — the tone control: a one-pole filter that shapes a clip's brightness (ALES40).** Every edit gesture so far changed a clip's **level** (gain, fade), its **arrangement** (splice, joins, mix), or its **map** (markers, racks) — none changed its **frequency** balance, and a suite that can only make audio louder, softer, or rearranged can never make it *brighter* or *warmer*. This rung opens the first filter: a one-pole **low-pass** that softens a harsh take (rolls off treble) and its exact **high-pass** complement that thins a boomy one (cuts bass). The crux is **a recursive filter that stays exactly reproducible on integer PCM**: a one-pole low-pass is an IIR filter (its output feeds back into itself), and an honest one must reach a constant input *exactly* — no drifting steady-state offset, never overshooting, never wrapping. The filter carries a full-precision **i64 state** holding the smoothed estimate scaled by the coefficient's denominator, so no fractional information is lost between samples; each sample steps the state a fraction `num/den` of the way toward the input, with one guarantee that makes direct current exact — **when the fractional step truncates to zero yet the state has not reached the input, the state advances by one**, so a constant input is reached in finite steps and held exactly rather than approached forever the way a plain shift-based smoother leaves a residual. The step never overshoots (`|step| ≤ |gap|`), so a constant input is reached **monotonically** — no ringing, the low-pass character proven. The high-pass is the **exact complement**: `high_pass(x) = saturate(x − low_pass(x))`, so where the difference fits `low_pass + high_pass = x` sample-for-sample (exact reconstruction) and a constant input drives the high-pass to exactly zero (direct current removed), while a transient past the i16 range **saturates** rather than wraps — reused from ALES2's `timeline.saturate`. The coefficient is the strength: `num = den` is the identity (pass-through), a smaller fraction smooths harder. A zero denominator, a zero or negative numerator, and a numerator past the denominator (a coefficient above one — unstable) each refuse `BadCoeff` before a sample is touched; a span outside the clip refuses `BadRange`, the clip untouched. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real sample rate (the coefficient is a smoothing fraction over sample indices, not a cutoff in hertz — a real time base is a later rung). Witness: `tools/ales_tone_witness.rish`. *(ALES43 accretes one public primitive here — `low_pass_carry`, the caller-owned-state form of the low-pass; `low_pass` becomes its `state = 0` case, one implementation so the carried and from-silence forms cannot drift. - **`session.rye` — the session file: a keeper's whole project as one sealed document (ALES39).** A keeper saves **two** kinds of thing — the **rack** (the held audio spans, ALES33) and the **cue sheet** (the arrangement map, ALES22) — yet until now they saved and loaded **separately**, two files free to drift apart: a rack loaded beside a cue sheet from a different take, a map whose marks no longer name the audio. - **`seal.rye` — the seal, made general: one verify-before-trust law both sheets call (ALES38).** ALES34 sealed the rack sheet and ALES37 sealed the cue sheet, and ALES37's own reading made the discovery plain — **the verify-before-trust seal is not specific to a rack; it is a law for any travels-as-text record.** Yet each module still carried its own copy of that law: the same `to_hex`, the same `digest <64 hex>` framing, the same header/digest-line location and Sha256 compare, differing only in the header string and which parser consumed the verified body. Two copies of one truth is a drift waiting to happen — a fix to one seal that never reaches the other. `seal.rye` lifts the whole frame into one **parser-agnostic** primitive both wrappers call. The crux: **one primitive owns the whole verify-before-trust frame, so both sheets call it and neither drifts.** Three functions carry it: `begin(out, header)` writes the sealed header line and the `digest ` prefix, reserves exactly 64 hex characters and the newline, and returns a `Frame` naming where the digest and body go; the caller renders its body directly into `out[frame.body_at..]` (whatever renders it — one sheet, or a composite of several); `finish(out, frame, body_len)` hashes exactly those rendered bytes and backfills the digest — **zero scratch**, the body written in place; and `open(text, header)` is the integrity gate before trust — it locates the header and the `digest <64 hex>` line, recomputes the Sha256 over the body, and returns the body **slice** only on a match, refusing `BadRecord` on a malformed frame or wrong header and `DigestMismatch` on a body that no longer matches its digest. An arbitrary body round-trips whole through `begin`/`finish`/`open`; a wrong header, a missing / short / non-hex digest line each refuse `BadRecord`; a flipped body byte and a truncated tail each refuse `DigestMismatch`; a seal into a too-small buffer refuses `Overflow`. A pure primitive over `std.crypto` only — no audio imports; the seal proves a record arrived **whole**, not **who** wrote it — never a signature. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_seal_witness.rish`.
- **`cue_seal.rye` — the cue sheet, sealed: the arrangement map that verifies before it is trusted (ALES37).** ALES34 gave the *rack* sheet an integrity gate, yet a keeper saves **two** kinds of thing — the rack (the held audio) and the **cue sheet** (the arrangement map: where the intro, verse, chorus, and outro fall). - **`rack_rename.rye` — a slot re-named in place (ALES36).** ALES30 files a span under a name and ALES33–35 save, seal, load, and merge whole racks, yet one small gesture a keeper reaches for constantly is still missing: a name typed in haste is wrong, or a merge brought in a stem whose name a keeper wants to change — and the only way to re-name a slot was to **drop** it and re-file it, which needs the source span it may no longer hold. `rack_rename.rye` is the rename in place: keep the held audio exactly where it is and change only the name. - **`rack_merge.rye` — two saved racks joined: a rack merge, and a sealed import (ALES35).** ALES33 let a rack travel as text and ALES34 sealed it, so a keeper can save a rack and load it back whole — yet a real session is stems gathered from many places (the count-in from yesterday's take, the chorus from an archived sheet), and loading a saved rack replaces nothing and **joins** nothing: `verify_and_parse` hands back a *fresh* rack, standing alone. `rack_merge.rye` is the missing gesture — **merge** brings the named slots of one rack into another, so a keeper loads an archived sheet's stems *into* the session they are already working, without losing what the live rack already holds. - **`rack_seal.rye` — the slot sheet, sealed: a rack sheet that verifies before it is trusted (ALES34).** ALES33 lets a rack travel as text, yet plain text carries no proof it arrived whole — a byte flipped in a copy-paste, a truncated file, a hand-edited sample all parse as **some other rack** without a word. The ALES0 audio wire already answers this for a live frame (a Sha256 recomputed by the far side and checked before the payload is trusted); `rack_seal.rye` hands that law to the saveable sheet. - **`rack_paste_join.rye` — the rack's paste, joined: a named slot crossing both its seams equal-power (ALES32).** ALES30's rack pastes a named slot with a hard **butt**; ALES31 gave the single clipboard the joined paste; `rack_paste_join.rye` hands that same law to the rack, so a keeper drops a **filed** span into the middle of a mix by name without a click. - **`paste_join.rye` — the paste that joins rather than butts: a held span crossing both its seams equal-power (ALES31).** ALES25's clipboard `paste` and ALES30's rack both drop a held span into a master with a hard **butt** (`timeline.splice`), exact where the samples already agree, yet where the span's head disagrees with the outgoing tail — or its own tail with the audio that follows — the waveform **steps**, and a step is a click. ALES26 gave a seam the choice of law and ALES28/ALES29 carried the two-sided insert into stereo; `paste_join.rye` hands that law to the **paste**. The crux is a composition, not new arithmetic: **pasting a held span with `paste_join` is exactly ALES29's stereo two-sided insert-join applied to the clipboard's own held buffers** — so the paste crosses both seams of both channels equal-power, atomic across the two channels, where ALES25's plain paste butts. The clipboard is **unchanged** (a read), so a joined paste stays non-consuming and cross-master exactly like the butt paste. A held span joined at a full-swing sign flip lands a seam sample strictly inside the extremes where a butt would step 60000; the new length shares both overlaps (`master + held − lead − trail`); the same clipboard joins into a second master with its held length unchanged; every edge refuses by name — `Empty`, a past-the-master or too-wide-lead `BadRange`, an overflow `ClipFull`, the master untouched and aligned after each. `stereo_insert_join.insert_join_stereo` and `clipboard` are reused whole; the joined paste names no new fault beyond `Empty`. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_paste_join_witness.rish`.
- **`rack.rye` — the multi-slot clipboard: a named rack of held spans (ALES30).** ALES25's clipboard holds **one** span and pastes it anywhere, across masters and across time, yet a keeper working a real session holds **more** than one — the count-in, the verse, the chorus, the clean take of the bridge — and reaches for each by **name**, not by remembering which single span the clipboard last held. `rack.rye` is that small bounded set of named clipboards. The crux is **disjoint, name-routed slots: a rack holds several independently named spans, pasting one slot by name is exactly ALES25's paste from that slot's own clipboard, and a slot's held span is never touched by a fill, paste, or drop of any OTHER slot.** Two facts make this exact: each `Slot` embeds a whole ALES25 `Clipboard` with its **own** bounded buffers, so the slots are disjoint — filling or pasting one reads and writes only that slot's audio, never a neighbour's; and every gesture routes to exactly **one** slot resolved by a **unique** name — `copy_to` / `cut_to` refuse a `Duplicate`, so a name names at most one slot, and `paste` / `held` / `drop` resolve that name to a single index and call ALES25's proven operation on that one board, so the rack adds naming and bounds and no new edit. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_rack_witness.rish`.

The crux is **atomicity across the two channels: a stereo insert grows both by the same shared count (`clip + span − lead − trail`) or touches neither.** Because a `StereoClip`'s channels are always one length and a stereo span is one length, **every one of ALES28's guards depends only on the shared length and span dimensions — never on a sample's value** — so `insert_join` on the left succeeds if and only if it succeeds on the right: a refused insert refuses on the first channel, atomically, before the second is touched, and a legal insert lands in both. `insert_join.insert_join` is reused whole; the stereo insert names no new fault. Purely local — no network, no keys, no funds, no real device, no real meter, no real speaker. Witness: `tools/ales_stereo_insert_join_witness.rish`.
- **`insert_join.rye` — the two-sided insert-join, the trailing-seam law (ALES28).** ALES26's `join` crosses only the **leading** seam — it appends a span to the very end of a clip, where there is no audio after it. Yet a keeper rarely inserts at the end; they drop a span into the **middle**, and the moment they do a **second** seam appears where the span's tail meets the audio that follows. ALES26 leaves that trailing seam a hard butt, and a butt is a click. `insert_join.rye` names the trailing-seam law: **a span inserted into the middle of a clip crosses BOTH its seams equal-power — or the clip is left exactly as it was.** The crux is a composition: **the two-sided insert is ALES26's `join` applied twice, once per seam.** Split the clip at `at` into a head `A = clip[0..at]` and a following `B = clip[at..]`; crossfade-join the span onto `A` over `lead` (the leading seam), then crossfade-join `B` onto that over `trail` (the trailing seam), so the result `A~S~B` flows through ALES12's −3 dB curve at both boundaries and the new length shares both overlaps (`clip + span − lead − trail`). The correctness beyond calling `join` twice is **atomicity**: an insert touches the middle of the clip, so the whole result is built in a **scratch `Clip`** and copied back only on full success — the real clip is never written until both seams are proven legal, and a past-the-master position, a lead wider than the head, a trail wider than the following audio, a short overlap, or an overflow each refuse by name leaving the clip untouched. `join.join` is reused whole; the two-sided insert names no new fault. - **`stereo_join.rye` — the crossfade join carried into stereo, both channels crossed in lockstep (ALES27).** ALES26 gave a mono clip the choice of law at a seam, yet a Lotus master is a `StereoClip` (ALES10) and a keeper joins a stereo span, not one channel. `stereo_join.rye` runs ALES26's `join` on **each** channel with the **same** overlap, so the seam moves through ALES12's equal-power curve in both and the stereo image stays aligned — exactly the lockstep the graft and clipboard already keep for the copy and the paste. - **`loop.rye` — the transport loop: a marked region read round and round (ALES14).** ALES7 opened the read side with a play head that reads a master forward and stops **exactly at the end** — the end a hard wall. - **`meter.rye` — the peak/RMS meter: the same root reading the master (ALES13).** ALES11 and ALES12 used a bounded integer square root to **shape** the audio — the field (pan) and time (crossfade). ## The road on

ALES25 gave the copy its full reach — a **clipboard** holds a span and pastes it anywhere, any number of times, across masters: the clipboard keeps its own copy so the held audio survives any edit to the source, paste is non-consuming and cross-master so the same span drops into three places identically, and `cut_to` lifts a span for a move between two masters. With play, loop, meter, scrub, markers (by index, by time, saveable), span editing, copy and move, and a carry-anywhere clipboard, a keeper reads the master the way they hear it, keeps the map, and rearranges the marked bars whole across a whole session. ALES26 then gave the edit its **choice of law at the seam** — a **crossfade join**: where an inserted span's head disagrees with the outgoing tail, overlap the two and cross them equal-power (ALES12's proven curve read at the splice point) so the boundary moves through power rather than stepping into a click; the neighbours share the overlap in time, and a hard butt splice's full 60000 swing between +30000 and −30000 becomes a seam sample sitting strictly inside it. ALES27 then carried the join into **stereo** — ALES26's `join` run on both channels with the same overlap, atomic across the two so the stereo image stays aligned or neither channel is touched. ALES28 then closed the seam story for mono — a **two-sided insert-join**, the **trailing-seam law**: a span dropped into the *middle* of a clip has two seams, not one, and this rung crosses both by running ALES26's `join` once per edge (the span onto the head, then the following audio onto that), so a mid-clip insert flows through power at both boundaries; the whole result is built in a scratch clip and copied back only on full success, so the middle-of-the-clip edit is atomic. ALES63 then opened a whole **wing** of the craft with one change — a **sidechain compressor**, the gain that follows *another* signal: every dynamics processor so far reads its detector from the very track it turns down, yet a console lets a kick key a compressor on the bass (the bass **ducks** each time the kick lands), a voice key a compressor on a music bed (the music steps back when someone speaks), a vocal's sibilant band key a compressor on the vocal (only the *ess* is tamed). All are one primitive — a **sidechain**, a detector that follows a **key** signal while the gain lands on a **target** signal. `lotus/sidechain.rye` re-runs ALES58's exact loop with the held follower advanced on `key.buf` rather than the target's own sample, the gain landing on the target. The grounding law: a **self-key** (the key holding the target's own samples) *is* the ordinary ALES58 attack/hold/release compressor **byte-for-byte** (ALES58 reads each sample before it writes, so an identical key reproduces its detector) — the sidechain **generalizes** the proven in-line compressor rather than replacing it. A **silent key never reduces** (the target passes whole however loud — the gain follows the key, not the signal); a **loud key ducks a quiet target** (a target below the threshold turned down where the in-line compressor leaves it alone — ducking, proven against ALES58); **unit ratio is the identity everywhere**; a loud target's magnitude reduces with the sign held; and the **state carries** — env and hold_left both — so a span in two pieces equals the whole once. A zero threshold refuses `BadThreshold`, a sub-unity ratio `BadRatio`, an illegal coefficient `BadCoeff`, a hold past `max_clip` `BadHold`, an out-of-range span `BadRange`, and a **key shorter than the span** `BadKey`, each before any write. With a keyed detector proven, the **de-esser** (a band-pass key), the **ducker** (a voice key), and the **keyed gate** become compositions of proven parts in the coming rungs; external key routing and the lookahead delay stay taken only on Keaton's word. ALES64 then built the first of those — a **de-esser**, the first sidechain **composition**: a de-esser tames the harsh *ess* of a vocal without dulling the take, listening to only the **sibilant band** (the high frequencies where the ess lives) and ducking the whole voice for the instant that band is loud, so the ess softens while the body stays bright. That is exactly a sidechain whose key is a **band-passed copy of the signal itself** — `lotus/deess.rye` splices an exact copy of the target into a caller-owned empty scratch clip, runs ALES46's `Bandpass` over the copy to isolate the sibilant band, then keys ALES63's attack/hold/release compressor on that copy over the target. The grounding law: the de-esser **is** the composition `{band-pass a copy; then sidechain-compress}` **byte-for-byte** (run by hand and demanded equal), so it adds no arithmetic on the audio path — it is ALES46's band-pass and ALES63's keyed compressor composed. The **key's band-passed level sets the crossing**: measure the band-passed copy's peak `P`, and a threshold just *above* `P` is the identity however loud the target's broadband level, while a threshold *below* `P` ducks — so the **sibilant band, not the raw level, drives the gain** (the de-esser reacts to the band, proven by measuring the copy). A loud in-band signal ducks with the sign held and the magnitude never expanding. The band-pass runs first over the scratch, so an illegal band cutoff refuses before a single target sample changes; the fault set is `tone.ToneError || sidechain.SidechainError` — an illegal band cutoff or attack coefficient `BadCoeff`, a zero threshold `BadThreshold`, a sub-unity ratio `BadRatio`, a hold past `max_clip` `BadHold`, an out-of-range span `BadRange`, each before the target is touched. This is the **from-silence, whole-span** form; a carried/spanning de-esser that can automate, the **ducker** (a voice key), and the **keyed gate** follow as compositions of proven parts, external key routing and the lookahead delay taken only on Keaton's word. ALES65 then built the third tool ALES63 named — a **keyed gate**, the exact **mirror** of the sidechain compressor: where the compressor **ducks** the target when the key gets loud (it acts *above* the threshold), the gate **opens** the target when the key is loud and **closes** it when the key falls quiet (it acts *below*), a gate triggered by a click track so a pad speaks only on the beat, a tom gated by its own close mic so the kit's bleed stays silent, a synth chopped by a drum loop. `lotus/gate_key.rye` (`gate_key_follow` / `gate_key_follow_carry`) re-runs ALES57's held-gate loop with the follower advanced on `key.buf` rather than the target's own sample, so a **self-key** reduces to ALES57 **byte-for-byte** — the keyed gate **generalizes** the proven in-line gate. The one care the compressor did not need: the gate acts below the threshold, where a keyed detector can read exact silence from the key while the target is loud (the fully-closed gate) — ALES57 divides the target magnitude through the envelope (`gat/e`), undefined at `e == 0`, yet that gain is algebraically **`den/num`** (the envelope cancels), so the `e == 0` branch applies `den/num` directly (no divide by zero, and for a self-key it writes zero over zero). A **loud key opens the gate** (a quiet target passes whole where the in-line gate would silence it); a **silent key closes the gate** (a loud target attenuated where the in-line gate would pass it — the `e == 0` case, proven against ALES57 both ways); **unit ratio is the identity everywhere** even under a silent key; a gated target keeps its sign and never expands; and the **state carries** — env and hold_left both — so a mid-hold split equals the whole once. A zero threshold refuses `BadThreshold`, a sub-unity ratio `BadRatio`, an illegal coefficient `BadCoeff`, a hold past `max_clip` `BadHold`, an out-of-range span `BadRange`, and a short key `BadKey`, each before any write. With the keyed gate, the **three tools ALES63 named — de-esser, ducker, keyed gate — all stand as compositions of proven parts**; a carried/spanning de-esser, external key routing, and the lookahead delay stay taken only on Keaton's word. ALES66 then opened a whole **second wing** with the primitive every returning effect is built on — a **feedback delay**, the echo that decays: every dynamics rung reads the *present* (a gain, a threshold crossing, a band), while this rung lets a sample **return**, heard again a while later a little quieter each time — `y[i] = saturate(x[i] + fb·y[i-delay])`. `lotus/echo.rye` (`echo`) makes **the delay line the clip buffer itself** — applied in place, left to right, `buf[i-delay]` inside the span already holds the output written this pass (true feedback), so an echo needs **no carried state** and a span split in two equals the whole once (the delay memory lives in the samples). Feedback below unity **decays to silence** within the clip (`fb_num < fb_den`, else a runaway refuses `BadFeedback`); a single impulse yields an **exact** decaying train (`8000 → 4000 → 2000 → 1000` at delay 2, `fb = 1/2`, byte-for-byte); a constructive sum past the rail **saturates** (ALES3's one true saturation, reused) rather than wrapping; and `fb = 0` is the dry identity. A zero or over-wide delay refuses `BadDelay`, an at-or-above-unity or zero-denominator feedback `BadFeedback`, an out-of-range span `BadRange`, each before any write. The single-tap slapback, the modulated delay (chorus, flanger), and the multi-tap and real-time forms follow as later rungs on this foundation. ALES67 then gave the echo its **real time base** — a delay named in **milliseconds**, so a keeper says "an echo an eighth of a second later" rather than counting samples. `lotus/echo_time.rye` (`echo_ms`) is a thin twin over ALES66 exactly as `marker_time` is over the base markers: it converts `start_ms`, `count_ms`, and `delay_ms` through ALES5's proven `clock.samples_for` and delegates to ALES66's `echo` **unchanged**, so the **clock adds only the units, never the audio** — the millisecond-named echo lands byte-for-byte the same samples as the index-named echo over the converted delay and span (the grounding law, both tools run; at 48 kHz `1 ms` converts to `48` samples and the first echo sits exactly there). A **sub-sample delay** (`0 ms`) refuses `BadDelay` rather than silently doing nothing; a delay past the clip refuses `DurationTooLong`; the echo's `BadFeedback` / `BadRange` forward by name. With a real time base under the echo, the modulated and multi-tap forms are each nameable in milliseconds from here.
