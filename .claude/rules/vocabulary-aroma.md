# Aroma -- the earth sense, and the tell that is not a smell

**Seated:** `20260904.214754` on Keaton's word -- **Status:** Living -- **Kin:** [`vocabulary-red-over-bug`](vocabulary-red-over-bug.md) - [`gauge-style`](gauge-style.md) - [`radiant-style`](radiant-style.md) - [`twilight-style`](twilight-style.md) - [`comlink-tendency`](comlink-tendency.md)

**The word *smell* retires from living prose, and it retires twice, because it was carrying two
different jobs and only one of them was ever beautiful.**

## The earth sense -- aroma, scent, breathing in

The five rows each have a sense: **aether hears, air feels, fire sees, water tastes, and earth
breathes in.** Earth's tanmatra is **aroma**, which is what the elder page reached for in its own
second paragraph even while its title said otherwise.

| Prefer | Rather than |
|--------|-------------|
| **aroma** -- the sense itself, and the tanmatra | the smell |
| **scent** -- a particular one, carried on air | a smell |
| **breathes in** / **takes in** -- the row's verb, parallel to hears, feels, sees, tastes | smells |
| **incense - petrichor - pheromone** -- the three Earth ships, each a named aroma | -- |

The ships are the argument in miniature. **Incense** is aroma offered, **petrichor** is aroma the
first rain lifts out of dry ground, and **pheromone** is aroma that carries a message between
living things. Three beautiful words for one sense, already sailing; the fleet had chosen this
vocabulary before the rule caught up with it.

## The other job -- a tell, a sign, a fault

*Code smell* is the borrowed industry phrase for a surface sign of a deeper fault, and it is where
the ugly connotation actually lived. This tree already seats **red**, **error**, and **fault**
([`vocabulary-red-over-bug`](vocabulary-red-over-bug.md)); what was missing was the word for the
*surface sign* that is not yet a proven fault.

| Prefer | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **tell** | a surface sign that something deeper is wrong -- not yet proven, worth reading |
| **sign** / **trace** | the neutral register where *tell* would be too knowing |
| **red** / **fault** | once it is proven, it is one of these and the ledger says so |

*"Redundant length-beside-slice fields carry the same **tell**"* says what it meant, and says it
without asking a reader to picture a bad odor first -- which is Gauge's own rule about comparing by
what keeps rather than by what decays, one sense over.

## G-friendly by default -- the standing rating

**New Gauge, Radiant, and Twilight are G-friendly by default.** Every register this tree writes in
stays readable aloud to anyone, at any age, with no warning attached: no crude anatomy, no slur, no
gore, no leer, and no word whose first association is unpleasant when a beautiful one carries the
same meaning. That last clause is why this rule exists at all -- *smell* is not a crude word, and it
still failed the test, because **aroma** was available and lovelier.

**A rating above G is opt-in, by Keaton's word, in the round that needs it.** An adult subject --
a legal exposure, a medical fact, a security finding written plainly, a piece of grief -- may need
words a G register would soften into dishonesty, and honesty outranks brightness every time
([Gauge](gauge-style.md): *honesty first, brevity second, delight third*). So the register adapts on
his word for a named round, in Radiant or Twilight as the piece asks, and returns to G at its close.
**Nothing here licenses coarseness as a style.** The opt-in buys precision about a hard subject,
never a change of manners.

## What this does not retire

- **Dated testimony and commit history** keep every word they wrote; the working tree was swept on
  the seating word, and history is exactly where a superseded word belongs.
- **Teacher quotes and vendored text** keep their words -- a study of an upstream *code smell*
  catalog names it plainly, once, and then speaks our own.
- **Ordinary English about a nose** is not banned. A kitchen may carry the aroma of the morning's
  cooking; what retires is *smell* as this tree's word for the sense, and as its word for a fault.

## Accrete-never-break, and the depth this took

Swept in the **working tree** on Keaton's word `20260904.214754`, across living surfaces and dated pages
alike -- the authorized break of accrete-never-break that [`debride`](debride.md) names. **Not a

history is for. The deeper cut stays available on a further explicit word naming it.
