Abstract

contents · previous: Context table · next: Status of this memo, and the shape chosen

An orbital data center has one energy problem on Earth and a different one in orbit. On Earth the constraint is grid watts and copper; in orbit it is heat, which leaves a spacecraft only as infrared, at a rate set by the fourth power of the radiator's temperature. This brief reads an outside counsel's answer to a question about which chip idea could ease those constraints on a 2026 to 2029 clock, before a lunar mass driver changes the cost of mass. The outside reading named a die: a hot-junction, memory-local inference chiplet on optical interconnect. This brief lands close to it with one change of shape and three corrections. The change is that the favorite idea is a bound before a die, four ceilings a witness can read, named before the arithmetic is chosen. The corrections are a leakage tax on hot junctions, an upset tax on static memory in low orbit, and a supervisor that holds the thermal ceiling, which is software. Running the physics on the builder's own disclosed numbers shows the hot loop is already inside the disclosed spacecraft rather than optional: 150 kW behind 110 square metres needs a two-faced panel near 89 C or a one-faced one near 145 C. The maintainer's own toroidal whitepapers, read through the tree's siloed adaptations, give the thesis its geometry: the torus enters the orbit question as a map and a manifold, the shape of a production optical interconnect and of a bounded optimizer, rather than as a radiator. Every figure carries its unit, date, and source; every projection carries its horizon and falsifier.

Status of this memo, and the shape chosen

contents · previous: Abstract · next: Executive reading

This is a draft for comment, in the sense a request for comments means it: the claims are numbered so a reader can answer one at a time, and a reply that finds a wrong number is the most useful reply it can get.

The shape was chosen by asking what each candidate register rewards, which is the Civic question. A venture pitch to limited partners rewards persuasion, and a reader cannot tell which sentence to check. A consulting deck rewards decisiveness, and it tends to hide its assumptions on the last slide. A paper with an abstract rewards checkable claims, and an RFC rewards the reply. So the body is a paper's body under an RFC's discipline, with two courtesies added for busy readers: an executive reading that says the finding in a page, and a disclosure box that tells an investment reader what this is and is not before they read further. Anyone who wants the pitch can build one from the numbers here; the numbers come first so the pitch can be checked.

Executive reading

contents · previous: Status of this memo, and the shape chosen · next: For investment readers: what this is, and is not

Three findings. First, the energy problem is three problems, and they reward different chips: tokens per joule on Earth, watts shed per kilogram of radiator in orbit, and kilograms per useful operation on the manifest. Second, on a 2026 to 2029 clock the interconnect and the thermal envelope are where the joules are, and the market has already priced that: about USD 6.5 billion of one chipmaker's money into photonics since March 2026, a USD 3.25 billion optical-fabric purchase closed in February, a USD 500 million round for co-packaged optics at a USD 3.75 billion valuation in March. Third, new arithmetic, thermodynamic sampling and all-optical logic, ships outside the window and stays a laboratory for this question.

One lineage. The tree's own earlier whitepapers on toroidal computing, cited through their siloed adaptations, turn out to hold the thesis in geometry: a closed surface has a fixed area, so a bound comes before a die; and the torus is already the topology of a production, optically switched supercomputer, which is where it belongs in this question, as a map rather than a radiator (section 10).

One number. The first orbital data-center craft discloses 120 kW average and 150 kW peak compute behind up to 110 square metres of radiator. That is 1,091 to 1,364 watts per square metre of panel, against about 166 flown on the space station's loop. The gap is bridged by temperature and by radiating from both faces, so the hotter chip the builder described in March is what makes the June spacecraft's radiator close. The number is disclosed and unflown; the witness is the first thermistor log.

One recommendation. Treat the first orbital boxes as witnesses rather than solutions, treat the hot-die-on-optics idea as the right shape under a named bound, and keep the bound in software: a supervisor that refuses a job the radiator cannot carry, provably. For a small fund, none of this is a buy; it is sector knowledge, held until a sector list admits it.

For investment readers: what this is, and is not

contents · previous: Executive reading · next: 01 The question, and the answer in one breath

This brief is a reading of public disclosures and physics. It names companies because their public numbers are the evidence, and it names their claims as claims. It is not an offering, a recommendation to buy any security, or a forecast anyone should act on without their own adviser. It was written for a small family fund whose own rules keep any single company to a small share of its portfolio and route every money move behind five gates and a licensed team; those rules are why the brief can be honest about companies it will never own. A larger firm reading this should treat it the way it treats an analyst's first pass: check the sources in place, re-run the arithmetic in section 3, and reply where a number is wrong. The reply is the point.

01The question, and the answer in one breath

contents · previous: For investment readers: what this is, and is not · next: 02 Three rooms, three rewards, and the clock

The maintainer asked the outside counsel this: what is your favorite mass-scale datacenter HPC chip idea from all of these, or others we haven't considered, or others funded by public-interest capital, for the purpose of solving the orbital builder's energy problem before the Moon mass driver is accomplished in several years, Hari iccha, Inshallah, God willing? The outside reading answered with a die, and ranked ten ideas beneath it. Counsel read the answer, ran the physics it leans on, and lands close to it.

The change of shape is this. Counsel's favorite is a bound before a die. Name the payload's ceilings first, in units a witness can read: watts shed per kilogram of radiator, picojoules per bit moved, joules per useful token, upsets per bit per day. Then choose the arithmetic that fits under them. That is the tree's own code rule, name the ceiling before the loop, applied to a spacecraft, and it matters because the ceiling is already public. SpaceX's first-craft disclosure puts 150 kW of peak compute behind 110 square metres of radiator, and section 3 shows that number is only reachable with a hot loop. So "run hotter" is the physics of the disclosed spacecraft rather than a slide.

Inside that bound, the die the outside reading named is the right die on a 2026 to 2029 clock, and the three corrections are the tax it pays. Leakage and wear rise with junction temperature, so a hotter die earns less than its radiator suggests. Static memory is the most upset-prone structure on a chip, so memory-local weights in a 600 km orbit want correction and scrubbing that cost area and watts. And a die with a thermal ceiling needs a supervisor that keeps it there, which is software, and the one part of this question an open operating-system tree can touch.

Two readings follow. The first is that the outside reading's central idea holds, with its number bounded: a hotter die shrinks the radiator by 40% to 70% depending on what the panel faces, and a knife-edge panel that sees cold sky gets the smaller of those. The second is that the disclosed first-craft figure sits eight times above the flown station figure per square metre. That gap is bridged by temperature and by radiating from both faces, and the outside reading was right to call the number disclosed, unflown. The witness is the first thermistor log.

A reply received the same day (see Comments received) re-ran the table and reports the arithmetic stands, and it named the load-bearing ambiguity plainly: the 110 square metres. If that figure already counts both faces, the two-faced row above double-counts and the disclosed spec fails even hotter; if it is panel area, the two-faced row is the honest one. The field cannot settle it; the first thermistor log can. The reply also asked for the third geometry the poetry usually skips, one face to 3 K space and one face to 255 K Earth: at the default 90 C panel and emissivity 0.9 that geometry sheds 1,559 W per square metre and clears; at 70 C it sheds 1,199 and refuses, short by 164. The witness at the top of this page now takes five inputs, junction, coolant delta, emissivity, sink, and geometry, and refuses when the law cannot carry 150 kW over 110 square metres.

04The wire, measured

contents · previous: 03 The radiator, measured · next: 05 The memory habit

The energy spent moving data between chips is the other half of the problem, and it is the half the market has already priced. Ayar Labs closed a USD 500 million Series E on 2026-03-03 at a USD 3.75 billion valuation, with Nvidia, AMD, and MediaTek in the round and USD 870 million raised in all; its TeraPHY optical engine is an 8 Tbps-class chiplet fed by a sixteen-wavelength light source, and the company claims four to twenty times the throughput per watt of copper (SiliconANGLE, 2026-03-03; Inside Deep Tech, 2026-09). Marvell closed its purchase of Celestial AI on 2026-02-02 for USD 3.25 billion at closing, USD 1 billion in cash and USD 2.25 billion in stock, and claims 75% lower power for accelerator-to-accelerator links on its Photonic Fabric (SDxCentral, 2026-05; marvell.com, read 2026-09-07). Nvidia has put at least USD 6.5 billion into photonics since March: USD 2 billion each into Coherent, Lumentum, and Marvell, up to USD 3.2 billion into Corning, and a seat in Ayar's round (TNW, 2026-05-29; CNBC, 2026-03-02). Lightmatter's Passage interposer and its USD 4.4 billion valuation are already in the tree's photonic survey.

Read the Civic way, what optics reward is a ratio on a chosen link, and who verifies it is the buyer's rack meter. Four-to-twenty is a wide claim, and the honest reading is that the low end is what a first-generation part delivers on a real board. The outside reading's "30% to 60% of a cluster's energy is movement" carried no source, and this brief leaves the percentage behind: Ayar's own framing, a power wall driven by interconnect inefficiency (its chief executive, 2026-03-03), says the direction without the number.

For orbit, the wire does two jobs at once. It moves the bits at lower energy, and it removes the copper SerDes furnace from the radiator's load. Both are mass. Both are witnessed by the same thermistor.

05The memory habit

contents · previous: 04 The wire, measured · next: 06 The ranked list, carried and corrected

The third leg is where the weights live. Static memory on the die instead of stacked high-bandwidth memory cuts the energy of fetching weights and cuts the stacks that have to be lifted, and the incumbents have said so with money. Nvidia took most of Groq's team and a licence in December 2025, in a deal reported near USD 20 billion (Hashrate Index, 2026-05; The Chip Letter, 2026-01), and its chief executive has described a data center in which Rubin handles prefill and attention while Groq-class parts handle token generation, at up to a quarter of the floor (Tech Investments, 2026-05, quoting him). Cerebras keeps the same habit on a whole wafer. OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeno on 2026-06-24, an inference-only accelerator with a first deployment planned by the end of 2026 and a roughly 50% cost claim against current GPUs from Broadcom's chief executive (SiliconANGLE, 2026-06-24; OpenAI, 2026-06-24). Tokens per joule is the scoreboard now, and it is the scoreboard the outside reading named.

The correction for orbit is radiation. Static memory cells flip under charged particles more readily than any other structure on a die, and a 600 km orbit passes through the South Atlantic Anomaly many times a day (counsel's knowledge, unfetched). So a memory-heavy inference die in orbit wants error correction on every array and scrubbing on a schedule, which costs area, watts, and some of the energy the memory saved. The outside reading asked for radiation tolerance at the circuit level rather than behind a vault of metal, and that is right; the addition here is that it is a witnessed error budget, upsets per bit per day against a ceiling, rather than a line in a brief. The reply of the same day agrees on the direction and marks the magnitude as the gap it is: static memory in the South Atlantic Anomaly wants a published error budget, and this brief carries none.

07Public-interest capital, on the public list

On the public list counsel read (Dakota, 2025-12-18), the intelligence community's venture arm holds compute and adjacent bets in D-Wave (invested October 2012), Cerebras, Groq, and ICEYE (from about January 2019), beside SandboxAQ, Databricks, Anduril, Skydio, Fortanix, and Cohesity. 08The first ship is the witness

contents · previous: 07 Public-interest capital, on the public list · next: 09 What an open operating system has to do with it

The builder's first craft. SpaceX laid out its first orbital data center craft, AI1, on 2026-06-08. The compute payload is 120 kW average and 150 kW peak at 70 kW per ton, on a 70 metre deployed wingspan, in an orbit near 600 km. Up to 110 square metres of deployable liquid radiator with redundant pumping loops carry the heat away. The payload is interchangeable and sized to about one GB300 rack, so any vendor's silicon can ride (Tom's Hardware, 2026-06-09). The company has filed with the FCC to launch up to a million such craft, has signed compute deals including one with Google near USD 920 million a month, and has unveiled an eleven-million-square-foot factory for them (same source). Its listing filing says it cannot yet secure enough chips, which is its own fab's reason for being.

Starcloud. Starcloud-1 flew an Nvidia H100 in November 2025, the first GPU of that class operated in orbit, and trained a model there. Starcloud-2 is to bring about a hundred times the power and cooling within about eight months of May 2026. The company contracted more than fifty Starlink Mini Laser terminals across at least twenty-five satellites, each link up to 25 Gbps at up to 4,000 km, with first hardware on orbit within a year. It raised a USD 170 million Series A at a USD 1.1 billion valuation in March 2026, has filed for 88,000 satellites, and plans a 200 kW class by late 2028 (Business Wire, 2026-05-26; MLQ, 2026-06; Y Combinator podcast summary, 2026-08).

Read together: commercial silicon works in orbit behind shielding, and it is a poor match for mass and heat. That is the outside reading's sentence and it stands. What these two craft reward is a witnessed number, kilowatts per ton and square metres per kilowatt, flown rather than disclosed. The first craft with an off-the-shelf rack is the correct first ship precisely because it measures the thermal loop before anyone bets a fab on it.

09What an open operating system has to do with it

contents · previous: 08 The first ship is the witness · next: 10 The maintainer's own torus, read for orbit

The tree this brief comes from designs no die and flies no panel. Three of its habits are the part of this question a software tree can touch.

The bound before the loop. The tree's code rule asks for the ceiling beside the constant, with the why. 10The maintainer's own torus, read for orbit

The tree keeps a lineage room: six prompted whitepapers from an earlier season, written under an earlier handle and held whole in gratitude, with a visionary and at times devotional register that the tree preserves as written. This section does that. It reads the lineage for what it gives the orbit question, and it finds that the torus enters as a map and a manifold rather than as a radiator.

What stands in the silo, in one sentence each. A computer shaped as a torus has no edges, every core has the same neighbors, and the farthest two points sit a known distance apart before the machine runs. A memory cell's coordinate on the torus, major angle, minor angle, radial depth, is its address, with no translation layer. Parameters that wrap or that live inside fixed limits map onto a torus, and natural-gradient descent on that compact domain is a bounded, deterministic optimizer that runs on ordinary hardware today. Local data that agrees on its overlaps glues into a global whole. Allocate once, never grow, never free. The silo also says what the aether framing costs: the geometry's advantages survive with the aether removed, and the framing is the author's inspiration rather than a premise. This brief keeps that law.

The bound before a die is the lineage's own instinct. The original wrote that every maximum constant in its code style maps to a topological constraint, and the silo restates it as TAME's root rule expressed as hardware: bound everything and know the maximum before you run. A closed surface has a fixed area, so its heat budget is a geometry. Section 1's four ceilings are that sentence with units on it.

The torus is a map, not a radiator. The lineage's cooling section claims significant surface area for heat dissipation and no edge hot spots, and on Earth, inside a liquid-cooled casing, convection makes that true. In vacuum only the surface that sees the sky counts. A closed shape's inner faces see each other, and a doughnut's hole faces itself, so part of what one face emits the opposite face absorbs; per kilogram, a flat two-faced knife-edge panel sheds more to cold sky than any closed volume can. So the torus does its work in this question as topology, and topology is where it is already real. Google's TPU v4 runs a dedicated 3D torus interconnect, 4 by 4 by 4 blocks with wraparound links through optical circuit switches; the optical components are under 5% of system cost and under 3% of system power, and the machine improved performance per watt 2.7 times over its predecessor (Jouppi et al., ISCA 2023). A torus of light rather than of silicon, and it is the shape of the wire section 4 priced.

The coordinate is the address; the address is the distance; the distance is the energy. If every hop's length is legible from the coordinates, the picojoules per bit of a move are known before the move, which makes an energy budget a compile-time bound. That is the vision room speaking, and the silo names the ordinary road to it: ring and torus topologies in the tree's sealed carrier and its supervisor need no exotic silicon.

The governor as a bounded optimizer. Duty cycles, phases, and coolant setpoints wrap or live inside limits, so they map onto a compact domain, and natural-gradient descent with a diagonal Fisher approximation is deterministic, single-threaded, and TAME-shaped there. A thermal governor that tunes a payload's duty cycle against a radiator ceiling is that technique's plainest use, and it is proposed here as a lap rather than claimed. The lineage's own speed projections, 1.7 to 2.9 times a Cerebras baseline, are entangled with hardware that does not exist and stay behind, as the silo already says.

Antipodal pairs, for the upset tax. The original reads a fixed-point theorem colorfully: because a torus has a hole, a continuous map on it need have no fixed point, and antipodal nodes make a natural mirror pair at maximum distance. Read plainly, that is one shape for the correction section 5 asks for, a mirrored copy far enough away that one particle cannot flip both. Proposed; the error budget still wants a published number.

The sheaf and the thermistors. The lineage's one engineering claim about wholes, local data that agrees glues into a global whole, is how a radiator log should be read. Each thermistor is a local section of a temperature field; where two overlap and disagree, that is a hot spot, and the disagreement is the information. That is how section 3's witness grows from one number into a field.

15Trust, and the two rooms
