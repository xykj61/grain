FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE -- Oregon - March 1, 2026

## A Fifteen-Year Vision for Oregon's Regeneration, Offered Freely to Every Community in the State

**OREGON** -- A comprehensive statewide vision titled **The Aspiring Oregon Radiance** is released today to all of Oregon: a fifteen-year framework weaving plant-based food systems, a federal Job Guarantee, regenerative agriculture, traditional urbanism housing, and open-source civic technology into one coherent whole. The vision is authored by **Keaton Livermore**, written together with **Riyo** of the **Grain Operating System (Grain OS)** project, and it is offered in the spirit the document itself names -- *seva*, service for the work's sake. Every community, every legislator, every farmer, builder, teacher, and technologist in Oregon is invited to take what serves and carry it forward, whoever they are and whatever office they hold or decline to hold.

The release begins by naming Oregon's pressures plainly, because a vision that looks away from hardship serves no one. Housing costs have outpaced wages in every Oregon metro area. Homelessness is visible in every city and in many rural communities. Wildfire seasons lengthen while snowpack thins, and working families stretch further every year. The vision holds these realities in full view, and it answers them with an equally plain observation: Oregon has never waited for permission. The state that pioneered the ballot initiative, the bottle bill, Death with Dignity, and the nation's most sophisticated land-use planning framework is ready for its next act of civic imagination. Oregon's 1.13 million non-affiliated voters -- the single largest bloc in the state -- are already asking for it. The pressures are real. So is the state that meets them.

At the root of the vision is one design discipline, applied everywhere: build systems that regenerate rather than extract, and let each element feed the others. Food systems that rebuild soil. Housing that improves with age. Technology that belongs to the communities it serves. Work that leaves every place it touches healthier than it found it.

## Work That Matters

The economic foundation is a federal Job Guarantee: a living-wage position for every Oregonian who wants one, paying eighteen to twenty-two dollars per hour, regionally adjusted for Oregon's diverse cost of living, with healthcare, childcare, and retirement included from the first day. The work is what communities themselves identify as valuable -- converting depleted farmland to food forests in the Willamette Valley, raising mass timber housing in Portland and Bend and Eugene and Medford, maintaining community computing infrastructure in Salem and Klamath Falls, restoring habitat along the Willamette and the Rogue, installing solar in the eastern high desert, teaching, providing eldercare and childcare in neighborhood centers.

The funding rests on a school of economic thought whose central observation is simple: a government that issues its own currency is constrained by real things -- workers, materials, productive capacity -- rather than by the scoreboard itself. When willing workers stand beside idle land and unmet needs, the federal government can mobilize all three for public purpose, and the real value created -- healthier soil, better homes, stronger communities -- is what keeps the system in balance. Oregon holds all three in abundance. Each position is also a doorway: a worker raising mass timber learns what the construction trades pay for; a worker maintaining open infrastructure learns what technology careers require; a worker designing food forests develops what regenerative agriculture needs.

## Land That Regenerates

Oregon's genius is its diversity, and the agricultural vision honors each landscape rather than imposing one model on all of them. The Willamette Valley -- some of the deepest topsoil on earth -- transitions toward direct-for-human, plant-based abundance: vegetables, fruits, grains, legumes, and hemp, connected to eaters through the farmers markets, food co-ops, and community-supported agriculture that the valley already sustains. The coast develops seaweed cultivation and aquaponics that regenerate marine ecosystems while producing food, carried by fishing communities whose knowledge translates directly. The Cascades grow forest gardens in harmony with conifer ecology -- berries, mushrooms, shade-tolerant food plants, and the watershed restoration that everything downstream depends on. Eastern Oregon's high desert, long overgrazed and eroded, becomes the state's greatest transformation: dryland permaculture, native grassland food forests, and sun-powered greenhouses bringing year-round growing to communities that currently import nearly everything they eat.

Hemp earns its own paragraph, because few crops match it. It thrives in Oregon soil, asks for far less water than many conventional crops, and builds the ground as it grows. Its fiber becomes textiles, insulation, and hempcrete -- a hemp-lime building material that holds carbon in the walls of a home for the life of the building. Its seeds provide complete protein; its oil serves kitchens and workshops alike. Oregon grew hemp at scale before 1937, and the soil, the climate, and now the knowledge have all returned. The outcomes the vision measures are the honest ones: soil organic matter rising year over year, water infiltrating rather than running off, habitat expanding, yields climbing as fertility rebuilds.

## Homes That Endure

The housing vision is traditional urbanism, which is Oregon's own inheritance: the beloved patterns of Southeast Portland, downtown Ashland, Corvallis, Hood River's main street, and Bend's original core, scaled with intention. Buildings line the sidewalk and rise from what Oregon grows -- mass timber, where Oregon already leads the nation; hemp-lime walls that insulate while they hold carbon; compressed earth; regional stone. Ground floors hold small commerce, upper floors hold homes of every size, fruit and nut trees shade streets wide enough for neighbors to walk together, and everything a family needs sits within a short walk. Inside, the materials protect the people they shelter: zero-VOC coatings that keep indoor air clean, natural-fiber insulation, solid-wood furniture from worker cooperatives -- every choice circulating dollars through Oregon communities while guarding family health.

For Oregonians experiencing housing insecurity, the vision proposes an Ecological Sanctuary Network: permanent, dignified buildings in Oregon's watersheds, with simple well-lit rooms, communal plant-based kitchens, gardens that feed residents while restoring soil, and workshops where trades are learned. Residents heal, teach one another across generations, and transition when ready into walkable neighborhoods and Job Guarantee positions built on the skills they have grown. The design recognizes housing insecurity as a systemic failure rather than a personal one, and it answers with pathway, purpose, and community -- treatment, peer support, shelter, and work, integrated from the start.

## Technology That Belongs to Its Communities

Grain Operating System -- Grain OS -- is the open-source civic platform that makes the rest verifiable. Public ledgers are append-only, every expenditure is signed by a responsible hand before it is recorded, community proposals are visible to everyone, and the code itself is public for any resident, student, or auditor to read. Community technology centers in every county offer patient, multilingual support: a teenager studies real public code and contributes to it, an elder learns video calling in the language of home, a family's laptop is repaired with parts designed to be replaceable. Municipal broadband reaches rural communities as public infrastructure, priced for universal access. Privacy and accessibility are foundations rather than afterthoughts -- screen readers, keyboard navigation, many languages, and data that belongs to the person it describes.

## Who This Serves

The vision is written for the whole of Oregon. Working families gain secure income with healthcare and childcare included, and homes they can afford in neighborhoods worth walking. Latino and Spanish-speaking communities -- central to Oregon's agricultural life -- gain living wages, Spanish-language support throughout, and genuine pathways toward land and cooperative ownership. Young Oregonians gain paid entry into trades and technology, learning from real systems they can shape. Older Oregonians gain paid roles teaching what decades have taught them, and patient support navigating what is new. Immigrants and refugees find services in the languages they speak at home. People with disabilities find universal design present from the first sketch -- in the software, in the streets, and in the work itself.

## How It Gets Built

The vision unfolds as a menu rather than a mandate. Communities adopt the pieces that serve them, and early successes teach through direct experience -- the most persuasive teacher there is. The first three years prove the concept in pilot communities across the state; years three through seven scale hemp processing, housing construction, technology centers, and the sanctuary network; years seven through fifteen bring maturity, until regeneration is simply how Oregon works. The soil for this growth already exists: Oregon's farmer-owned cooperatives, its food bank networks, its land-use planning framework, its extension services reaching every county, its mass timber pioneers, and its long habit of solving problems across party lines.

"Governance, at its best, is a creative act -- the creation of new possibility rather than the management of existing constraints," said Livermore. "This vision is offered as a path with heart for the whole state, laid out as structural architecture and given freely. Take what serves Oregon. The path with heart asks nothing in return."

The release closes with the message that threads the whole document: ***risk love***. R I S K names a reduced instruction set kernel -- the elegant simplicity of clean architecture. Love names compassion, sustainability, regeneration. Together: elegant simplicity with compassionate purpose.

## About Keaton Livermore

Keaton Livermore is a monk and mathematician by ten years of practice -- titles self-given and earned through study, discipline, and devotion in the Vedic *brahmana* tradition. He carries the Livermore name to honor a conservation legacy: Caroline Sealy Livermore, who protected Angel Island, and Norman Livermore, who served wilderness preservation. He brings expertise in sustainable architecture, regenerative agriculture, open-source technology, and public finance, and he is a musician and aspiring filmmaker who understands how culture shapes community. He is relocating from California to Oregon with purpose and with patience, establishing genuine roots. The vision grew alongside his own aspiration to public service in Oregon; this release offers it to the whole state, to be carried by whoever serves -- in whatever chamber, kitchen, field, or workshop the carrying happens.
