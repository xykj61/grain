## What this round actually was

It opened as *close two reds* and ran for roughly ten more hours across a stream of live direction.
That shape is worth recording plainly, because the working method it produced differs from the
method a planned round uses.

**Instruction arrived while work was in flight.** Roughly fifteen separate directions landed mid-turn
-- a rename, a room reclassification, a decision to make, six documents to write, a correction to a
style rule. Nothing waited for a round boundary.

**The response that worked** was to finish the current coherent unit, commit it green, then take the
next instruction from a clean tree. Fifteen commits, each one signed, each one landing with the full
guard roster green after staging.

**The response that cost the most** was carrying more than one unit before committing. Two of this
round's three self-inflicted faults arrived that way.

## What the round cost, honestly

**Three faults were mine, same-day.** The staging-order one shipped a build cache to `main`. The
dated-pattern one rewrote two records before a check caught them. The mirror-depth one broke eight
links in a copy while its guard stayed correctly green.

**Each was caught by an instrument rather than by care**, which is the finding the design essay next
door holds. The practical lesson for a round of this shape is narrower and belongs here: **run the
roster after staging, every time, with no exceptions for a small change.**

**A fourth cost, carrying no fault:** four separate repairs this round were *this session's own
earlier renames* arriving late in living prose -- `glow-os` in one README, `Crux` in another,
`Skate` beside a `surf/` link. A breach rename is finished when the guards go green **and** the prose
stops saying the old name, and those land on different days.
