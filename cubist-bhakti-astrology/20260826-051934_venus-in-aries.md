One placement, four faces, held together the way a cubist canvas holds a face: the teaching as
the classical room keeps it, the little its page counts, the habit the teaching feeds in a
working tree, and the pair of words it wears by day and by night. Bhakti names the love the
reading serves -- and this page reads the boldest love in the room, the one that moves before
it is sure of its welcome.

## The teaching, kept whole

The classical page sets two whole temperaments side by side. Venus it names the sky's soft
power -- the principle of love, harmony, and valuing, cool and receptive, a power that draws
things together by delight. Aries it names the opposite weather: fire, initiative, courage,
the sign of whoever moves first. Seat the gentlest planet in the most forward sign and
affection takes on ardor -- desire that advances, declares itself early, and would sooner act
than wait.

The page walks its tension kindly. Love here can arrive as a chase, swift to want and swift to
say so, restless with the slow unfolding tenderness asks for, and the shadow it names is
affection confused with victory -- the delight of pursuit crowding out the steadier tending
that keeps a bond. Yet the same heat is the placement's gift: this Venus loves with courage,
speaks its heart in the open, stands up for a bond worth keeping, and brings warmth where a
cooler heart would hover at the door. The counsel keeps the fire and aims it, rather than
putting it out. Maturing, the placement travels its one named journey, from conquest to
meeting: connection, the page teaches, is made by two rather than taken by one, and being
seen costs the armor. Courage to pursue pairs with patience to listen; the boldness to want
makes room for another's wanting; tempered so, the ardor stays while the war goes out of it.
The reading counsel holds the same order -- honor the courage first, and only then, softly,
invite the pursuit to slow into a meeting.

## The habit it feeds

A working tree knows this Venus well, because the tree's own law asks work to move first. The
day-side of the placement is the round that ships small and soon, the intent declared in the
open, the draft published before it feels finished -- love for the work shown as action while
a cooler hand would still be polishing in private. The shadow translates just as directly: a
merge treated as a trophy, the landing rush outrunning the quieter maintenance that keeps a
module alive, the wish to be seen crowding out the listening that review exists to give. The
tempering is the page's own journey worn as discipline. Courage to ship pairs with patience to
receive -- a reviewer's shape accepted, feedback allowed to want something back -- and the
counsel to keep the fire and aim it is already seated here as an ordering law: the ardor goes
to the hardest solvable thing first, so the heat lands where it serves. A merge, read this
way, is a meeting rather than a territory taken; the branch joins main, and the tending
continues after the applause -- references repointed, the witness run once more, the bond with
a module kept rather than won.

## The pair it wears

By day this placement wears **risk**; by night, **surrender** -- the fire element's dual seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the row reads *risk adapts* and the
night side carries fire's letting-go. The why is the teaching's own arc. Love that moves first
is love that risks: the early declaration, the open pursuit, warmth offered before any answer
is promised -- that is the day-word exactly. And the night-word holds the journey's far end in
Twilight's register, calm and never gloom: surrender here is the armor set down rather than
the battle lost, the pursuer consenting to be seen, receptivity given its turn at last -- the
meeting the whole placement matures toward. The cardinal seat tempted, since Aries is the
tradition's own cardinal fire and the rockstar's stage suits a bold opening; the dual seat is
truer, because the whole turning of this teaching is the tempering -- forward fire learning to
adapt to what love actually asks -- and adapting is the dual seat's own verb. A sibling page
already wears this seat, and the grid is built for sharing; this page earns it in its own
words, the risk as the declaration, the surrender as the meeting. Aim, then dissolve: the
day-word charges into the open with its whole heart, and the night-word lets the conquest go,
keeping the connection.
