and says so at the door.

## The teaching, kept whole

Sadness is the feeling that something which made you yourself has been lost. Its right answer
is to reconstitute and to heal: restore what can be restored, and rest while the healing
happens. Sadness asks for tenderness and time. Anger is the feeling that a wrong has been
done. Its right answer is correction -- justice as the aim, reform as the fruit. Anger asks
for action. The two blur because a loss so often arrives by someone's hand, so the ache of
losing and the heat of being wronged rise together, and naming which is which is the first
act of care.

The classical page then teaches three movements, each gentle and each exact. First, find the
source: the body already knows this method, since pain turns attention at once to where the
hurt comes from, and only then can the hand move away from the heat. A feeling left
unexamined perpetuates and grows; a feeling named at its source can finally be answered.
Second, weigh the signal: ask whether the loss is real or only felt real through a mistaken
belief of need, and ask whether the anger is right or mistaken -- anger born of wounded pride
asks for letting go, while anger that rises to defend someone gravely wronged asks for
standing firm. Third, master the anger without denying it: hold it when right action is
possible, set it down when it is impossible, and let a sincere apology that truly reforms
settle it -- while the memory stays honest and the mark is quietly kept, so kindness is
never confused with forgetting. In a chart, the page reads heat -- the sharp planets, the
storm-touched stars, a tender and easily moved heart -- and asks one quiet question: is this
difficulty a wound or a wrong? A wound asks for restoration and rest; a wrong asks for
correction and, in time, release.

## The habit it feeds

The distinction translates into a working tree without any forcing, because the tree already
lives by it. A red is the tree's anger: something wrong has been done, and its right answer
is correction -- name it, find its source before reaching for the fix, and close it on a
witness rather than a claim, since an unexamined failure perpetuates exactly the way an
unexamined feeling does. A loss is the tree's sadness: a broken reference, a folded room, a
fossil left behind by a molt -- and its right answer is restoration and rest, a checkpoint
marking the way back, the healing given time rather than hurried. Weighing the signal is the
reproduce-before-booking discipline: a red born of misread output is wounded pride, and lets
go; a red that stands on a real failure stands firm. And the ledger keeps the teaching's
finest line: the fix closes and the row stays, unedited, so the memory stays honest and the
mark is quietly kept -- forgiveness and forgetting held apart, in a book of record, exactly
as the page asks of a heart.

## The pair it wears

By day this reading wears **river**; by night, **tide** -- the water element's cardinal seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the river cuts and water's own sense,
at the fixed seat beside it, is taste. The why is the teaching's own shape. Anger mastered is
the river's day: water given a channel, moving with direction, cutting the wrong right --
correction as a current rather than a flood, action with justice as its aim. Sadness met
rightly is the tide's night, and the dark twin keeps Twilight's rule -- calm, never gloom.
The tide is grief moving as it was made to move: rising, resting, receding under a light that
does not burn, the wave sensed before it crests and met with preparation rather than alarm,
healing carried in by rhythm and time. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word carves the channel
the wrong requires, and the night-word lets the water widen, restoring the shore it touches.
