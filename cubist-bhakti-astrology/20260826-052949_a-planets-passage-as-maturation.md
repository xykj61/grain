## The teaching, kept whole

A planet enters a sign and, over the following weeks, meets the slow great powers of the sky
one after another. The classical page's whole turning is a single reframe: that series of
contacts is a curriculum rather than a scatter of unrelated events. Each meeting tests the
moving planet's principle in its own way, and taken in order the contacts form an arc of
maturation -- whatever that planet governs is stretched, refined, and grown up a little. Read
this way, a transit stops being a weather report of good days and bad days and becomes a
story with chapters, each one asking something and giving something in return.

Four teachers hold the arc, each with a lesson, a risk, and a gift. The dissolving power
teaches surrender: it softens edges and lifts the veil of ideals, risks confusion and
rose-tinted longing, and gives compassion and imagination -- love passing here is asked to
release its painted fantasy and meet what is actually there. The disciplining power teaches
patience and structure: it sets limits and asks for time, risks coldness, doubt, and the ache
of lack, and gives durability -- the love or work that lasts because it was built to. The
transforming power teaches depth: it reaches to the root and asks death and rebirth of an old
form, risks upheaval and the fear of losing control, and gives renewal, intensity, and
honesty about what was hidden. The expanding power teaches generosity and trust: it blesses
and widens, risks excess and easy promises, and gives grace, meaning, and the return of hope.
The craft is to lay the contacts in the order they occur and hold the whole as one
growing-up: a hard contact early is a first chapter rather than a verdict, and a gentle one
is grace offered so the harder lessons can be borne. And the whole is held gently -- the
point is a frame in which difficulty becomes meaningful, never a forecast of doom or a
promise of ease.

## The habit it feeds

The reframe translates into a working tree without any forcing, because the tree already
reads its own history this way. A chapter of work is a series of contacts taken in order,
and each lands as a lesson rather than a fortune. The dissolving contact is measurement
meeting the imagined design: the sketch releases what it painted and meets what actually
runs. The disciplining contact is the bound and the witness: limits set, time asked, the
module that lasts because it was built to. The transforming contact is the molt: an old form
ends at the root so a truer one is born beside it, with the fossil kept honest. The expanding
contact is gratitude and the widened door: room to grow, and reason to believe the work
serves someone. The reading order carries the finest part. A red early in an arc is a first
chapter rather than a verdict, and later laps may resolve what it opened; a green witness is
grace rather than a free pass, strength lent so the harder lessons can be borne. And the
holding-it-gently face is the counsel practice word for word: report the state, name the
gates, and let the frame make difficulty meaningful -- the ledger tells the person what this
arc is trying to grow, in the order it will come.
