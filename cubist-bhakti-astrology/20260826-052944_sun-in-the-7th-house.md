## The teaching, kept whole

The seventh house is the western horizon, where the Sun sets. Everything the house carries
grows from that setting: endings and partings, and the turn from self toward others --
partnership, marriage, cooperation, and exchange. The Sun is selfhood and visibility, so to
place it in the house where its own light goes down is to meet a rich and honest tension, and
the classical page names that tension kindly rather than darkly.

Three meetings unfold from it. The Sun stands for being seen, and the seventh is the place of
disappearing, so a person can feel overlooked even while accomplishing much. The Sun stands for
the self, and the seventh stands for the other, so the work of the placement is symbiosis --
learning to hold oneself as part of a pair, rather than sacrificing the self or being
sacrificed for it. And the Sun gives direction while the seventh asks for cooperation, so
friction can rise with those in charge, alongside a strong wish to lead rather than follow.
Named kindly, this is a placement learning how the self and the other belong together.

Then the page turns the canvas, and shows why one factor never decides a reading. The very same
Sun reads one way in a regal sign at ease with authority -- the setting light still carrying a
natural, comfortable command -- and quite another in a fiercely independent, rule-breaking
sign, where it leans toward the outsider and the iconoclast. One placement, two very different
lives, decided by the sign it occupies and the company it keeps. So this Sun is a single color
blended with the whole painting: holding a strong sign, joining a fortunate combination, and
sending its light back toward the first house, its wish to lead becomes a gift, and partnership
and health hold steady; with heavier factors gathered around it, the same placement can strain
relationships and vitality. The page's closing counsel is the gentlest face of all: read this
Sun as a lesson in partnership rather than a sentence of loneliness, honor the real drive to
lead and the longing to be seen, and let the whole chart carry the larger part of the story.

## The habit it feeds

The setting Sun's lesson is already a working method, and the tree wears it without forcing. A
module is a self, and its seam is a seventh house: the place where it turns toward another,
where its own light passes into an interface and is held by hands it does not command. The
symbiosis the page teaches is seam discipline -- a strong module leads through its API and
cooperates at its edge, kept whole as part of a pair rather than dissolved into its caller or
swollen to consume it. The sign that changes everything is context: the same function reads as
natural command in one module and as an outsider in another, so a reading weighs the
surroundings before it judges the line. One color, never the whole painting, is the reviewer's
own rule -- a single green witness colors a claim and the suite decides it. And the overlooked
accomplisher is every load-bearing quiet module in the tree: the light sent back toward the
first house is the habit of naming what carries the work, so partnership strengthens the self
that gives, and the seam serves both sides of the pair.

## The pair it wears

By day the seventh-house Sun wears **love**; by night, **longing** -- water's dual seat in the
fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where love flows onward and water's own sense, at the
fixed seat beside it, is taste. The fit is earned twice over. The dual seat holds two at once,
and two-at-once is this placement's whole lesson: self and other, one Sun reading as two lives,
the pair held rather than either half sacrificed -- so the teaching sits in the one column the
grid keeps for what moves both ways. And the words are the page's own faces. Love is the day
face: symbiosis, marriage, exchange, the self flowing onward toward the other the way water
finds its companion shore. Longing is the night face, kept exactly as Twilight keeps it -- calm,
never gloom: the honest wish to be seen that lives in a setting Sun, held as the tide that
draws two toward each other rather than the ache of one alone. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word
turns toward the other and gives; the night-word lets the light go down, trusting the horizon
that receives it.
