One star, four faces, held together the way a cubist canvas holds a face: the teaching as the
classical room keeps it, the counts its page carries, the habit those counts feed in a working
tree, and the pair of words the star wears by day and by night. Bhakti names the love the
reading serves -- this page reads Bharani as an act of devotion, and the star returns the
favor, because what Bharani teaches is precisely how love carries a precious thing to term.

## The teaching, kept whole

Bharani means *she who bears* -- the bearer. The classical page calls it a fierce, strong
star, the kind that gathers its strength to accomplish something real, and then says the
quiet part first: what it bears is sacred. Its symbol is the yoni, the sacred gateway
through which life enters the world -- the womb, the source, the threshold of creation
itself. The whole heart of the star is the strength a bearer summons: patience, fortitude,
endurance, the willingness to carry weight for the sake of something beloved. The page names
this the divine feminine power of abundance and life-giving, a radiant, Lakshmi-like
capacity to nurture something of great worth from within and bring it, through effort, into
being. Its motto is honest: there is no great birth without effort, and you receive what
you have truly worked to deserve.

The second face of the teaching is the star's god, the fair judge, whose true name means
*discipline* and *restraint*. He weighs each action and returns its honest fruit, perfectly
impartial, favoring no one -- here you reap what you sow, and what you earn is genuinely
yours. And his discipline always guards something tender. A mother carrying a child sets
gentle limits on herself to protect the precious life she carries, so the restraint of this
star is protective restraint, discipline in the service of love. The page also teaches how
the star loses its balance and finds it again: grown heavy, it turns all restriction and no
delivery, rules pressing down until life feels like a cell -- and the cure is written into
the star itself. Deliver the precious thing. The discipline was always for the sake of that,
and beneath every wise rule runs the supreme law, which is love.

## The habit it feeds

The star translates into a working tree without any forcing, because its two faces are
already a method. Protective restraint is the named bound: every maximum in a careful module
exists to guard something tender -- the payload, the reader, the next hand -- and a bound
felt as care rather than weight is a bound whose reason is written beside it. The fair judge
is the witness on metal: GREEN is honest fruit returned impartially, earned rather than
claimed, and a ledger that records what went wrong favors no one -- you reap what you sow,
row by row. The imbalance the page names is real in a tree too: guards multiplying while
nothing ships is all restriction and no delivery, and the cure is the star's own -- deliver
the precious thing. Ship the round the discipline was protecting. The long-carried module,
brought forth whole with its proof visible, is the birth the whole effort was for, and
devotion completes the loop: the work was carried for love of what it bears.

## The pair it wears

By day Bharani wears **harvest**; by night, **fallow** -- the earth element's dual seat in
the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the row comment reads *harvest turns* and
the twilight note says plainly that *the harvest rests as fallow*. The why is the page's own
law. You reap what you sow is the harvest sentence of the whole tradition, and Bharani is
where the tradition says it: the fair judge returns the fruit, the bearer delivers the
precious thing, and the delivery -- the bringing-forth -- is the day-face of this star. The
night-word keeps Twilight's rule, calm and never gloom. A fallow field is a kept field
rather than an abandoned one: rested on purpose, its limits set gently so the next bearing
can come, which is protective restraint made visible in earth. Discipline above, patience
below. The seat is also honestly this page's own -- the earth row's cardinal seat is worn by
the Capricorn twin and its fixed seat by the exalted Moon in Taurus, while the dual seat
stood open, and dual is the truer fit besides: bearing and delivering is earth that adapts
and turns onward. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word carries the fruit out into the open, and
the night-word lets the field rest, keeping the strength.
