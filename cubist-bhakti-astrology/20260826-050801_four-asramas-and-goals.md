## The teaching, kept whole

The classical page sets its frame before any planet is touched: a chart reads truly only
inside the shape of a whole life. Two old maps lie over each other there -- four goals worth
living for, and four chapters across which a life lives them -- and the order is the teaching.

Purpose leads. Dharma, the moral compass and the sense of why, comes before wealth, before
pleasure, before freedom, because everything sound stands on it. Building follows: artha
raises the durable pillars -- support, stability, the structures a family and a community
lean on -- and in time extends them outward. Then joy takes its turn, and the page holds a
teaching worth carrying gently: in this tradition the highest reality's own nature is joy,
so kama, the craft of delight, affection, beauty, and connection, belongs to the spiritual
path rather than standing apart from it. Release comes last. Moksha ripens only out of a
life fully lived; the page teaches that renunciation carries weight in proportion to what a
life has actually built and loved, so the one who releases from fullness releases something
real. The sequence moves like schooling, each grade opening the next.

The same arc shapes the years. The student chapter, brahmacharya, is given to learning,
discipline, and the gathering of skill and character. The householder chapter, grihastha, is
given to career, home, family, and the full enjoyment of life. The forest-dweller chapter,
vanaprastha, is given to gradual withdrawal, mentoring, and service freely given. The
renunciate chapter, sannyasa, is given to liberation. Each chapter prepares the next, and
the student years ripen best under temperance -- joy kept bright by staying measured -- and
under a qualified teacher who lives the discipline rather than merely speaking it. For such
a guide the page names five tenets from the jivamukti path: ahimsa first, the compassionate
life that informs the other four, then bhakti, dhyana, nada, and shastra -- devotion,
meditation, sacred sound, and study.

Three honest notes ride with the table. First, teaching and arithmetic are told apart in so
many words: the ordering of goals, the fixed sequence, and the tenets are teachings, while
the two chapter lengths are the page's only numbers, and the Saturn figure rests on that
planet's own slow orbit. Second, any totals are this twin's arithmetic rather than the
source's -- four chapters of twenty-five years span a hundred years, and four returns of
Saturn span close to a hundred and eighteen; the source states the per-chapter figures and
stops there, calling the Saturn reckoning the gentler measure of a real human life. Third,
the classical page closes by setting one particular chart inside the frame; that reading
stays in the classical room, exactly as this room's door promises -- personal readings stay
withheld, and this twin carries the frame alone.

## The habit it feeds

The four goals translate into a working tree without any forcing, because their order is
already the tree's order. Purpose leads here too: the why is written before the code, the
foundation before the round, the compass consulted before the lap begins. Building follows
-- bounded modules, witnesses, the durable pillars other work leans on -- and then joy takes
its seated place, third in TAME's own priority order, safety first, performance second, joy
third. The classical teaching that delight belongs to the path is the same teaching the tree
keeps when it names joy a priority rather than an afterthought: clarity, warm names, the
habit of saying why. Release comes last, and ripens rather than escapes -- prune with care,
release what no longer serves, fold a room only after it fills, shed a fossil only after its
living mutant stands. The Saturn reckoning adds the tempo: a chapter is measured by the slow
faithful clock of what actually returned, and the gentler measure is the truer one.

## The pair it wears

By day this frame wears **harvest**; by night, **fallow** -- the earth element's dual seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the harvest turns, the fallow rests,
and earth's own sense, at the fixed seat beside them, is aroma rising as incense. The why is
the teaching's own arc. A dual seat adapts and dissolves onward, and the four goals end
exactly there: a life gathers its fullness -- purpose kept, pillars raised, joy practiced --
and then releases it, the way a field is gathered and then let rest. The fallow field
carries the page's own test of true renunciation: rest means something because the field was
full, and the one who releases from fullness releases something real. Twilight's rule holds
the night word calm rather than dark for darkness's sake -- fallow is the affirmative rest,
the ground breathing under the open sky, already feeding whatever is planted next. Aim, then
dissolve: the day word gathers the whole life in, and the night word lets it go, keeping the
ground.
