## The teaching, kept whole

The classical page lays one map in three layers, and the layers line up. Each cardinal
direction carries an element; each element carries one of the four great aims of a human life;
and each pairing comes home in a cardinal sign of the zodiac. East holds fire, and fire holds
dharma -- right order, the harmony that lets a family and a society cohere -- seated in Aries.
South holds earth, and earth holds artha -- the material means of a life -- seated in
Capricorn. West holds air, and air holds kama -- pleasure, beauty, the sweetness of living --
seated in Libra. North holds water, and water holds moksha -- contemplation, and the
liberation it opens toward -- seated in Cancer. The page then closes the circle: these four
cardinal seats are, in this teaching, the very reason the elements stand where they do around
the whole wheel. The compass of aims is written into the zodiac itself.

The page argues one placement at length, and it is water's. Water stands for the contemplating
mind because water is the surface in which things are seen, and its condition pictures the
mind's condition: turbid water is a clouded mind, the vast restless ocean is a passionate and
powerful mind, and a clear still lake is the settled mind of a sage. Contemplation is the
doorway to liberation, so the north and its waters carry moksha. Two practices follow. A
person beginning a work may face the direction of the aim they seek -- a small devotional
gesture that lines intention up with element. And a chart may be asked which of the four aims
it leans toward, so a reading becomes a rounded picture: duty and means, delight and depth,
each honored in its own time.

## The habit it feeds

The compass translates into a working tree without any forcing, because a tree already lives
by aims it rarely names. Dharma is the order the work keeps -- the laws, the invariants, the
rules a round honors before it opens. Artha is the material fruit -- the module shipped, the
tool that runs, the means the tree gathers. Kama is the joy the style guides guard -- prose
worth reading, names warm to the hand. And moksha is the still reading: the survey, the
measurement, the witness whose surface is calm enough to see the tree truly in. The page's
water image lands here exactly -- a green witness is the clear lake, a churning half-measured
round is the turbid water, and settling the surface before trusting the reflection is the
whole discipline of measurement beats memory. The facing gesture becomes the align pass: stop,
name which aim the next round serves, and turn toward it before the first file opens. A
chapter can be asked the chart's own question -- where its fire, earth, air, and water gather
-- and a tree that answers honestly keeps all four in balance, chapter by chapter.

## The pair it wears

By day this teaching wears **river**; by night, **tide** -- the water element's cardinal seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the river cuts and water's own sense, at
the fixed seat beside it, is taste, sweetening to nectar after dark. The why is the page's own
weighting. Of the four placements, water's is the one the source argues in full -- the mirror,
the lake, the doorway to liberation -- and the cardinal column is the teaching's whole
subject, four cardinal signs holding the wheel in place. So the twin wears water at cardinal.
By day the river is a life moving through its aims, cutting a course through duty, means, and
delight, each bank shaping the water that shapes it back. By night the tide is the fourth aim
at work: the sea drawing the river home, the moon-pulled turning that asks for no effort and
misses no drop. Aim, then dissolve -- the day-word cuts toward the sea, and the night-word is
the sea receiving, calm and whole, the way moksha stands at the north of the compass
receiving the other three.
