## The teaching, kept whole

Ashlesha belongs to the Nagas, the divine serpents of the deep, and its symbol is the coiled
snake. The word itself means intimate contact -- entwining, adhering, clinging, holding close.
A serpent wraps its whole length around what it loves, and so the star gives love with depth
and endurance: the capacity to hold a person or a purpose entirely, to carry great weight for
the sake of the beloved, and a charisma others feel before they can name it. The classical
page calls these real gifts, and keeps them so.

The old story tells the rest. The mother of the serpents carried jealousy and cunning, and
asked her children to join a deception. Some obeyed her. Some refused -- they would hold to
truth even against their own mother -- and those became a separate, luminous branch of the
Naga race, keepers of deep mysticism and true spirituality. So one body carries two coils,
and the life-lesson of this star is simply which way one winds: toward secrecy and clever
self-protection, or toward loyalty, devotion, and the sacred.

The Bhagavata Purana then shows the coil at its highest. Ananta-Shesha, the endless serpent,
bears the worlds upon his hoods and forms the resting couch of the Lord -- all that strength
turned wholly into service. Kaliya, proud and venomous, meets Krishna as a dance upon his
hoods, surrenders, and leaves blessed rather than destroyed. And the gopis hold the Supreme
close by the sheer force of their love, wanting nothing for themselves -- the embrace made
pure devotion. The serpent's power is real; in the hands of love it becomes the couch of God.

## The habit it feeds

The two coils translate into a working tree without forcing, because a tree also holds things
for years. The embrace is maintenance: a module kept, a history carried whole, a promise a
reference makes and keeps -- loyalty with one's whole length is what a living repository asks
of its keepers. The choice of winding is daily and small. The same cleverness can coil inward,
into code that guards its author's secrets, or outward, into code held plainly in service of
its readers; one body, two coils, and each commit winds one way. The charm teaching lands on
the front doors: a README draws readers in, and the art is to draw them toward their own joy
-- name what the thing rewards, want nothing back -- since a door that pulls toward the
author's gain tightens into a trap, and the serpent bites its own tail. And ahimsa bounds the
whole: the power to hold, never to harm. A named bound is an embrace of exactly that kind --
it shelters what it wraps, and it knows where to stop.

## The pair it wears

By day Ashlesha wears **love**; by night, **longing** -- the water element's dual seat in the
fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where love flows onward and its dark twin deepens to
longing. The why is the star's own word. Ashlesha means intimate contact, and the page's true
north is the gopis, whose embrace is love in its purest devotion -- so the day-word is not an
ornament here; it is the subject. The dual seat carries the redemption too: a dual word adapts
and dissolves onward, and that motion is exactly the higher coil -- the grip that draws others
toward their own joy and then releases, love that flows onward rather than closing into
possession. The night keeps Twilight's rule -- calm, never gloom. Longing is the embrace
opened after dark: the beloved out of reach and the love undiminished, the coil loosened
without ceasing to hold. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word wraps close, and the night-word lets
the beloved go, keeping the love.
