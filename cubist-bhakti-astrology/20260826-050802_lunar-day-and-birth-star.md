## The teaching, kept whole

The lunar craft here is a threshold craft. A lunar day belongs to whichever star holds the Moon,
and the Moon keeps walking, so one day can open under a star at sunrise and hold its neighbor by
the hour a birth arrives. The classical page reads exactly such a day, kept anonymous: it opened
under Ashvini, the swift twins, and by early afternoon the Moon had crossed into Bharani, the
bearer. The birth-star stands firmly in Bharani, where the Moon rested at the birth itself; the
day's opening star remains Ashvini all the same. The page's quiet claim is that both gifts are
held -- the day itself moved from one to the other, and a chart may keep everything its day
touched.

The reading rests on an old lens. Varahamihira's encyclopedia devotes a chapter to the natures
of those born on each star's lunar day, and the page lays its two portraits over the four stages
of a life -- student, householder, forest-dweller, renunciate. Ashvini's portrait is the morning
gift: vitality, skill, healing, able trade, and the drawing of companions -- the very qualities
the student stage asks for. Bharani's portrait is the maturing gift: fierce, disciplined,
responsible, and dealing in precious stones -- strength that carries the three later stages, a
building that bears something of worth, a service that carries weight for others, and at the
last a release that ripens only after a life of real carrying. The page names its own method in
so many words: a synthesis woven from classical threads, offered as one way of seeing rather
than a fixed forecast.

anonymous in the source and stays anonymous here, exactly as both rooms keep personal readings
withheld.

## The habit it feeds

The crux translates into a working tree without any forcing: a bounded unit of work is allowed
to hold two natures, and the honest record names both. A chapter opens quick and companionable
-- survey, learning, the short bright gathering of skill -- and matures into carrying:
disciplined building, witnesses shown in the open, work borne on behalf of whoever comes next.
Averaging the two into one label would lose the day; the log instead writes where the threshold
fell, so morning and afternoon each keep their own name. The deeper half of the habit is the
baton. Quickness wants somewhere to go, and strength wants something to carry: learning that
never becomes building evaporates, and building that never releases hoards. So the arc runs
whole -- learn, build, serve, release -- and the close of a round is part of the round, the way
the page's fourth gift ripens only after the carrying is truly done.

## The pair it wears

By day this teaching wears **harvest**; by night, **fallow** -- the earth element's dual seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the harvest turns and earth's own sense,
at the fixed seat beside it, is aroma resting as incense. The why is the page's own arc. The
day-word is the bearer's afternoon: learning ripened into yield, something precious carried out
of the field and into open hands -- the harvest is the day the carrying pays. And the dark twin
keeps Twilight's rule -- calm, never gloom. Fallow is the release the bearer earns: the field at
rest after a full bearing, held dark on purpose, its richness gathering for a sowing it does not
yet name. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word carries the precious thing home, and the night-word
lets the field go quiet, keeping the ground.

## Gratitude

The portraits of those born on Ashvini's and Bharani's lunar days belong to Varahamihira's
Brihat Samhita, chapter 15, verses 26 and 27; the interpretive depth of both stars comes through
the teaching of Vic DiCara; the four-stage frame of a life is held from the Vedic tradition
through the Srimad Bhagavatam; and the two-star reading itself is the classical room's own
synthesis, named as such there and thanked as such here.
