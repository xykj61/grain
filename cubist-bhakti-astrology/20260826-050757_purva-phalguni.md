One star, four faces, held together the way a cubist canvas holds a face: the teaching as the
classical room keeps it, the counts the page itself carries, the habit those counts feed in a
working tree, and the pair of words the star wears by day and by night. Bhakti names the love
the reading serves -- and this star teaches how that love is grounded before it is enjoyed.

## The teaching, kept whole

The name teaches first. Phala means fruit, the result of an action; guna means the perceivable
quality of a thing. Phalguni is therefore the perceivable part of the fruit -- the peel, the
skin, the shell -- the surface that carries the fruit out into the world. So this is the star
of the fruit-bearer: creative, generative, gifted at giving the inward and unseen a form the
senses can meet. Art lives here naturally, since art is exactly that crossing.

Then the pairing. Purva Phalguni is one half of the tradition's first star-pair: two adjacent
stars holding one name and one symbol between them, and the symbol is the bed -- love,
marriage, union. Purva means the first, the approach, the way in; Uttara means the following
and the higher. Purva holds the spark and the bright first days of attraction, and its
strength is ugra -- fierce, initiating. Uttara holds the settled bond, the long keeping, and
its strength is dhruva -- fixed, enduring. The spark belongs to one star, the tended fire to
the other.

The classical page then names a subtlety plainly, and this twin keeps it whole. Two old and
trusted sources seat the pair's gods differently: the elder ritual tradition places Bhaga,
god of treasured and delightful things, in Purva, with Aryaman, god of promises, in Uttara;
the nakshatra teaching the room follows reverses them. The source holds both orderings as
deliberate, and resolves them by use: ritual and timing keep the elder seating, while
interpretation reads Aryaman in Purva. So the reading here leads with Aryaman.

Aryaman is the god of promises, vows, friendship, and devotion, his name grown from a root
meaning true, trustworthy, dedicated. He is invoked in every wedding, because a wedding is
the largest promise a community witnesses. His teaching is the ground of the whole star:
trust comes first, before pleasure, before the treasures of love -- and commitment stands as
the ground love deepens in, rather than the price love pays. Friendship rests on service,
service on a gentle selflessness, and affection -- praised in the Rig Veda as supreme
happiness -- sweetens even the difficult mile. The tradition's last word here names affection
the essence of beauty itself. Bhaga, in the pair's other half, names what the trust grows
toward: the delight that a kept word makes safe to hold.

## The habit it feeds

Aryaman's teaching arrives in a working tree already wearing work clothes, because the tree
keeps a rule in nearly his words: references are promises. A path cited is a word given; a
doc that names a behavior has promised the code beside it. The star's deepest principle --
keep the promises made, and make only the promises that can be kept -- is the bounding
discipline read as devotion: a budget named at construction is a promise sized to its
keeping, and a claim shipped with its green witness is trust built before delight. The
fruit-bearer's gift feeds the same loop from the other side: the inward idea earns its
perceivable surface -- the README at the door, the small round that lands today, the shape a
newcomer can see and feel. And Purva's ugra strength sets the tempo of beginnings: light the
spark cleanly, so the keeping half -- the long bond, the maintained module -- receives
something true to hold.

## The pair it wears

By day Purva Phalguni wears **love**; by night, **longing** -- the water element's dual seat
in the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where love flows onward and water's own
sense, at the fixed seat beside it, is taste. The why is the star's own symbol. The bed of
union is love's seat, and the star's closing teaching makes beauty love's own work. The dual
seat fits the purva half precisely: a dual word adapts and dissolves onward, and Purva
Phalguni is the half of the pair that leads onward -- the first stage flowing toward Uttara's
full flowering. And the dark twin keeps Twilight's rule -- calm, never gloom. Longing is the
night register of the spark: attraction with its lamp lowered, the reach toward what trust
has made safe to want. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word gives love its form, and the
night-word lets the form rest while the pull remains.
