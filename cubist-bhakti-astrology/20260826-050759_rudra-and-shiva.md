One story, four faces, held together the way a cubist canvas holds a face: the teaching as the
classical room keeps it, the counts the page itself carries, the habit those counts feed in a
working tree, and the pair of words the story wears by day and by night. Bhakti names the love

and says so at the door.

## The teaching, kept whole

The classical page opens on one being wearing two names, with a whole path stretched between
them. Rudra is the being as he arrives: born of a creator's anger, drawn from between the
brows when patience finally gave way, landing loud, named for his own crying. Shiva is the
title the same being earns -- the calm one, the auspicious -- once yoga has taught him to hold
his own fire. At birth he asks for three things at once: a name, a place, and work to do. He
receives all three, and then, untamed, he creates in his own likeness until his fierce
offspring crowd the world. The counsel that reaches him is a single word: discipline. Hold the
fire. Return what was disturbed. Wait for the right time. He agrees, honors his father, and
walks into the forest to practice -- and the practice is where the second name begins.

The page then turns the one being twice more. Seen from the practical, first vision, he is the
destroyer, the force by which what exists is unmade; seen from the matured vision, he is the
serene master who holds that force in perfect control. The image the teaching keeps is milk
meeting a single drop of lemon: the substance stays one, and the name changes with what it
touches. And at the arc's far end waits the correction of a stereotype -- the master's mountain
home turns out to be a paradise of lotus lakes, sandalwood, and unbroken music, with the teacher
seated in peace beneath a great tree. Mastery and joy live together; the calm at the center is
what makes the beauty safe to hold.

words. First, the names: the title's two is a teaching -- it counts the poles of the journey,

## The habit it feeds

The arc is already a method, so the translation asks for no forcing. Rudra is the red as it
arrives: something wrong, named loudly, the moment it is born -- and the tree's own discipline
agrees that the crying comes first, since a red books the allocation before new work opens.
The counsel is the method's second move. Hold the fire means the fix stays bounded rather than
spawning fierce offspring of its own -- a hurried patch that creates in its own likeness soon
crowds the tree with new reds. Return what was disturbed is the repair proven, and wait for
the right time is the gate honored. Then comes the forest: the fix closes in quiet practice,
a witness run on metal, rather than in louder crying. The tree even keeps a destroyer of its
own -- the sanctioned removal words, debride and shred -- and holds them exactly as the
teaching holds Rudra: the force real, the calm structural, a checkpoint laid first, a named
target, an explicit word before anything is unmade. And the mountain home corrects the same
stereotype here that it corrects there. Discipline is a warmer thing than deprivation; a
well-held tree stays a pleasure to work in, and joy keeps its seat in the order -- third,
after safety and performance, and still present at the table.

## The pair it wears

By day this story wears **rockstar**; by night, **hermit** -- fire's cardinal seat in the
fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where the rockstar ignites and fire's own sense, at
the fixed seat beside it, is vision. Sibling pages wear this pair for the fire they read; this
page wears it because the pair's own shape is the teaching. One seat holds a light word and a
dark word, the same substance in two registers -- which is one being wearing two names, the
milk and the curd in the palette's own grammar. The day word is Rudra's: cardinal fire
arrives first and unbidden, loud from its opening breath, refusing to let a wrong pass
unremarked. The night word is Shiva's, and the fit is nearly literal -- the hermit is the one
who walked into the forest to practice, and the tradition seats him there still. The dark
twin keeps Twilight's rule by the source's own surprise: the hermit's home is lotus lakes and
unbroken music rather than a bleak cave, dark euphoria rather than gloom. Aim, then dissolve:
the day word cries the true thing out loud, and the night word lets the crying go, keeping
the fire as a steady lamp.
