One counsel, four faces, held together the way a cubist canvas holds a face: the teaching as
the classical room keeps it, the counts the page itself carries, the habit those counts feed
in a working tree, and the pair of words the counsel wears by day and by night. Bhakti names
the love the reading serves -- this page reads a counsel of devotion as itself an act of
devotion, and says so at the door. One honesty rides ahead of everything: the classical page
speaks to one native, and her chart stays in that room's own keeping. This twin lifts only
the teachings any reader could weigh, and leaves the reading where it stands.

## The teaching, kept whole

The counsel begins with Aryaman, the keeper of promises, and his whole teaching fits in one
line: trust comes first. Fidelity, truthfulness, devotion, and patience are what make love
deep, safe, and lasting -- the soil, rather than the fence. From that soil the page unfolds a
chapter and two doors.

The chapter is the student years, and its counsel is temperance. The old word is celibacy,
which names only what is set aside; the warmer name the page teaches is patient wholeness,
the undivided heart. Love's bright energy is gathered and kept whole through these years, so
that in time it can be given fully to one devoted bond -- the page's own images are a river
held to one channel, which runs deep, and a flame held in one lamp, which burns steady. The
Bhagavata tradition frames the present age as one where desires scatter and dharma thins, so
gathering is the wise answer to the weather.

Then the two doors, each named fully honorable and fully whole. The first is the path of the
apsara -- the celestial dancer: a devoted, disciplined, single-hearted life given to beauty
and art, luminous and free, complete without marriage or children. The second is the path of
the householder: one devoted partner, cherished and trusted for life, and the family that may
grow from that bond. The page asks for no choice now; it asks only that both doors be known
open. Should the household door open, three counsels follow: consider welcoming home a child
already here, adoption named as a radiant way rather than a lesser one; choose a partner who
keeps promises, read past the first spark with a wise guide; and remember that a supported,
secure, happy person radiates that happiness through the whole home. The close holds it all
lightly -- counsel to weigh, never command, the choosing always the native's own.

## The habit it feeds

The counsel translates into a working tree without any forcing, because the tree already
lives by its first line. Trust comes first: in this tree a reference is a promise, a doc's
claim is checked rather than believed, and a witness run GREEN on metal is a kept word. That
is Aryaman's teaching wearing working clothes. The undivided heart is scope discipline -- the
river held to one channel is one keystone per round, the finishing edge that ships one real
thing before naming the next, energy gathered rather than scattered across ten open laps.
Patience is the ratchet: what turns on touch, sweeps down over chapters, and rewards the long
tending over the loud sprint. And the partner counsel is how the tree chooses what it binds
to -- a collaborator, a dependency, a tool is read past its first spark to its record of kept
promises: signed commits, green witnesses, a word held across chapters. The five tenets even
name the tempo of a good bench -- care taken as ahimsa, devotion as bhakti, steadiness as
dhyana, deep listening as nada, study as shastra -- a working day shaped like a devoted life.

## The pair it wears

By day this counsel wears **love**; by night, **longing** -- the water element's dual seat in
the fifteen-pair grid of `kyri/receipt.rye`, where love flows onward and water's own sense,
at the fixed seat beside it, is taste. The teaching chose its row itself: its central image
is the river held to one channel, and the river is water's cardinal seat -- so the counsel
stands in water by its own words, and within the row the dual seat is truest, because a dual
seat holds two honorable adaptations of one element, exactly as the page holds two honorable
doors of one devoted heart. The day-word is the householder's door and the apsara's art
alike: love given fully, flowing onward to partner, child, or beautiful work. The night-word
is the student chapter, and it keeps Twilight's rule -- calm, never gloom. Longing here is the
undivided heart itself: love gathered and held whole in patient reserve, sweet rather than
sad, a lamp tended in a quiet room while the one it will be given to is still on the road.
The aether row stood open for this pass, and nada's deep listening knocks at its door -- yet
one tenet of five is a window, and the river is the house. Aim, then dissolve: the day-word
gives the gathered gift away, and the night-word gathers it again, keeping the promise.
