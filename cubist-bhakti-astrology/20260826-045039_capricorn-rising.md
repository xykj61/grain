## The teaching, kept whole

Capricorn is the crocodile's sign, marked at the Sun's lowest turn of the year, and the
classical page gathers it from a cluster of strokes: low to the ground and lean, spirited and
real, strong in speech and heavy to move, unembarrassed and quietly hungry for regard. The
strokes cohere around one paradox held gently at the center -- a sign of the earth's own
fertility ruled by the planet of lack, so that great strength and a private wish to be seen
share one steady frame. Low means down to earth, glad among fundamentals, unafraid of the
dirty work; real means the sign grows powerful exactly when it stays honest and plain, and
weakens when it reaches for ornament. Its speech runs direct, forceful, strangely
captivating, and it treats a promise as a law -- which is why its bluntness lands with
weight, and why others feel its seriousness before it asks them to.

Heaviness is the key the page turns twice. A heavy thing at rest is hard to start, and a
heavy thing in motion is hard to stop -- so the sign can look unmotivated until it moves, and
immovable once it has chosen its direction. Detours frustrate it, delays drain it, and it
loves the finish line more than the winding road; its famous endurance is the jaws that hold
until the task is done. Kindness, the page teaches, gives it a clear road and an honest
start. Beneath the scales lives one tender place: a quiet need to be known as good and
right, a reliance on others' respect sitting oddly inside a nature otherwise indifferent to
approval. The remedy points inward -- a self-realization that fills the lack from within, so
the flag of one's own worth need never be waved. At its best the sign is tireless, honest,
quietly magnetic, famous almost without trying, and warm toward the flawed and the ordinary
because it sees clearly that everyone is flawed. Its love stands free of perfection, the
page says, and that is a real and generous love.

## The habit it feeds

The heaviness translates into a working tree without forcing, because it is already a
discipline. A heavy mover wants a clear road and an honest start, so a round is scoped
before it opens: the plan drawn, the seam named, the finish line visible from the first
line. Mid-lap pivots cost a tree exactly what detours cost this sign, and the cure is the
same -- love the finish line, and ship the one real thing before naming the next. The
promise kept as law is the reference kept as promise: a path cited is a path that exists,
and a claim closes on a witness run on metal rather than on enthusiasm. Realness governs
the prose beside the code -- plain grows strong, ornament weakens -- and the tender place
has a working remedy too. A green witness is regard answered from within: the module knows
it is right because its proof ran, whoever is watching, so the flag stays folded while the
work stays warm toward the flawed and the ordinary -- the imperfect module, the half-grown
room -- faithful to the long work that others abandon.

## The pair it wears

The truest seat for this sign is the earth row's cardinal chair -- grain by day, mist by
night -- and the sibling page on the sign's own nature already wears it, honestly and first.
So this page takes the earth row's dual seat, and the fit is its own rather than a
consolation: by day **harvest**, by night **fallow** -- `kyri/receipt.rye`, earth row, dual
column, where the row comment reads *harvest turns*. The source marks the sign at the Sun's
lowest turn of the year, and that turn is the earth's own: the crop carried in, the field
bare, the year pivoting in the dark. Harvest is the finish line this sign loves, said in
the ground's own word -- the long work brought home, the jaws released at last because the
task is done.

And fallow is the remedy said as a field. A fallow field looks like lack and is
replenishment -- worth restoring itself from within, quietly, with no crowd watching and no
flag raised. That is Saturn's sadness met by the earth's wish to bear fruit, kept calm:
dark euphoria rather than gloom, the low point of the year already holding the strength of
its climb. Aim, then dissolve -- the day word drives for the finish line, and the night
word lets even the finish line go, keeping the ground.
