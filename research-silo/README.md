## What starseeding is

Starseeding is **cross-constellation, cross-chain cross-pollination**: a point in one constellation wears an **outfit** — a name, a role, a temperament — that a point in another constellation can recognize and adopt. A seed carried from one sky and planted in another, growing true in new soil. The vision is already sketched in the expanding-prompts — the 3×39 baton passports and dividers (`../expanding-prompts/20260810-044453_the-3x39-baton-passports-dividers-and-starseeding.md`) and the handoff-baton checkpoint (`../expanding-prompts/20260810-025942_the-handoff-baton-vision-checkpoint.md`). This silo is where the research that feeds it gathers.

The council **d27** is starseeding's first garden: fifteen galaxies, three stars and nine planets each, a galaxy leading a circle of twenty-seven — one seat for each of the **27 nakshatras** (`../active-designing/yonder/20260810-082206_nakshatra-outfits-for-the-council-d27.md`). When the sky grows its outfits, each seat wears a nakshatra, and a nakshatra worn in one constellation can be recognized in another. That recognition, carried across chains, is starseeding.

## The dharma layer — nine over twenty-seven

Beneath the 27 lunar mansions sits their **essence**. Borrowing the **D9 (navamsa)** chart — which in the Vedic frame is *the chart of the true self*, the way the clock ticks beneath the face it shows — the 27 nakshatras group into **nine navamsa triads**, each a **dharma**: the quality so inseparable that, removed, the thing would no longer be itself (learned with gratitude from **Vic DiCara**; the dharma-essence talk is siloed at `../classical-vedic-astrology/studies/life-frame/20260705-171012_dharma-the-essence-of-a-thing.md`, and the ninth-chart reading at `../classical-vedic-astrology/readings/brooke/20260705-020812_the-ninth-chart-and-the-shape-of-dharma.md`).

Nine dharmas, nine themes, nine navamsa triads over the 27-nakshatra calendar — the same nine that give the **Twilight** register its palette:

| # | Navamsa theme | Nakshatra triad | Dharma (essence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **buried paintings** | Ashwini · Bharani · Krittika | the first making, kept |
| 2 | **tar to turquoise** | Rohini · Mrigashira · Ardra | poison lifted to light |
| 3 | **the mirror misaligned** | Punarvasu · Pushya · Ashlesha | honest self-seeing |
| 4 | **twilight meteorology** | Magha · Purva Phalguni · Uttara Phalguni | the in-between made home |
| 5 | **the loom** | Hasta · Chitra · Swati | connection made visible |
| 6 | **night witnesses** | Vishakha · Anuradha · Jyeshtha | attention that guards |
| 7 | **the impeded stream** | Mula · **Purva Ashadha (Apas)** · Uttara Ashadha | the obstruction that sings |
| 8 | **the fertile black** | Shravana · Dhanishta · **Shatabhishak (Varuna)** | presence in absence |
| 9 | **descent of the angel** | Purva Bhadrapada · Uttara Bhadrapada · Revati | grace in the crossing |

The two water nakshatras already pinned to **Basin** land inside the two water themes — Purva Ashadha (Apas, the unconquered waters) in *the impeded stream*, and Shatabhishak (Varuna, the hundred healers, the deep that heals) in *the fertile black*. The calendar now rhymes at three depths: the **27-slot rankings rota** (`../context/rankings.kyri`), the **council d27** of nakshatra outfits, and the **nine dharma themes** that group them. The nakshatra names are traditional Vedic astronomy — public and old; nothing here is personal.
